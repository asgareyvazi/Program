     1- SCOPE

    The following procedures are intended to provide concise but definitive steps to

     follow in operations involving the milling of lateral hole sections from a parent well

     using the Mechanical Set Whipstock System.

    The Mechanical Set Whipstock System is a mechanically set whipstock that requires

     a footing in the wellbore to “trip” from, setting itself in the casing. The wellbore

     footing can be anything that will support 15,000 lb for 5”, 20,000 lb for 7” & 30,0000

     for 9⅝” weight, such as a cement plug, Bridge plug, fish, or liner. Milling a window

     in casing using a whipstock usually consists of one run for setting the Whipstock in

     place and milling and completing the window through the casing wall and into the

     formation, while dressing the window to eliminate drag.

       3- Pre Operation procedure and consideration
         Pre run operation check list

    As part of the Pre operational Procedures,  all whipstock jobs should include a

     completed

          •  The anchor slip is in place, is in good condition, and is the correct size for the

           casing size and weight and the Allen screws are tight.

          •  The shear pins are properly installed and in good condition.

          •  The whipstock has not been tripped.

          •  The shear screw is the proper size, and it will make up into the Whipface.

          •  The shear screw will fit through the Rugby Mill hole.

          •  All mills are on location and the OD’s are correct for the casing’s size/weight.

          (Check mill OD’s with a gauge ring that is OD + 1/32").

          •  All downhole equipment has been properly measured and OD’s, ID’s, and

           lengths have been properly recorded.

          •  The slots on the gyro match and are compatible with the lug assembly on the

            orientation sub.

          •  The survey tools will pass through all drill string tool joints and drill collar

            ID’s.

          •  All rotary connections are compatible.

          •  All rotary shouldered connections are made up to the recommended torque.

        Making the Hole Preparation Run

        1- Make a run to ensure that the casing is clean and there is strong enough setting

           place for setting the anchor. The run should include the following (from the

           bottom):

          • A Full Gauge Drift Mill

          •  Casing Scrapper (Do not run casing scrapers across open perforations.)

          •  Cross Over Sub (Bit Sub with Float)

          •  String Stabilizer

          •  Pony Drill Collar or 1 Joint of Drill collar

          •  String Stabilizer

          •  Drill Collars

          •  Drilling Jar

          •  Drill Collars

          •  HWDP’s

          •  Balance of Drill Pipes To Surface

     Note: The wellbore must be clean and free of any obstruction to the desired

     setting depth for the Whipstock. Omission of this gauge run could result in the

    premature setting of the Whipstock later.

          •  Run this assembly in the well to the depth of the plug (Cement Plug).

          •  Tag the plug (Whipstock setting depth) two or more times with 15,000 lb for

            5”,

          •  20,000 lb for 7” & 30,0000 for 9⅝” Casing to verify that it is strong enough to

          •  Tally the pipe while running  in or out of the well  to  get an accurate

          measurement to the plug.

           the Bit Sub to the bottom of the drill pipe joint. Discuss the use of a float with

           the Company representative and install one if required.

         5. Make up the Mills.

      Window Mill in the case of using combination mills), using only the Rugby

        Mill below the single joint of HWDP. Depending on the casing size and grade,

      more than one Rugby Mill may be required to make multiple exits.

       5- Pre Running Safety meeting contents

          •  Lower the whipstock assembly slowly into the wellbore to prevent premature

            setting of the whipstock.

          •  Run the whipstock slowly, like a packer assembly, at a rate of 2 to 3 minutes

           per stand

          •  Do not turn string.

          •  Do not set down more than 4 K/LBS.

          •   Fill the drill string with fill up every 10 strands.

          •  Run slowly into the hole until approximately 30 feet off the bottom.Slowly

         move the whipstock assembly up and down from 5 to 20 feet to remove all

          •  trapped torque from the drill string.

          •  Lower the whipstock assembly slowly to the setting depth.

          •  close the BOP Shear/Blind ram when working on Whipstock.

          •  Do not drop anything in the wellbore when working over the wellbore.

          •  Use extreme caution when handling the whipstock, setting drill collar slips,

            safety clamps, etc. to prevent dropping the whipstock in the hole.

          •  Run the draw works in low. Pick up and set down the whipstock easily in

           the slips to prevent damage or premature setting.

          •  Ensure the lead tongs have most of the slack prevent jerking against the back-

         up tongs.

       6- Running in Hole procedure

   CAUTION: Proceed slowly and with caution while running the whipstock

    assembly through the BOPs, wellhead, liner top, and any other restrictive

     sections to prevent damage or premature setting. The difference between the

    whipstock OD and the casing ID is minimal, making it similar to a piston, so

    lower the assembly very slowly in these small bypass areas. DO NOT SET

   DOWN MORE THAN 6 K/LBS. IF TAKING WEIGHT CALL WHIPSTOCK

    SUPERVISOR.

          •  Run slowly into the hole until approximately 30 feet off the bottom

   CAUTION: Do not tag bottom until the following steps have been completed and

     the proper direction has been established. Tagging bottom too soon can cause

    premature setting of the whipstock.

          •  Slowly move the whipstock assembly up and down from 5 to 20 feet to remove

              all

          •  trapped torque from the drill string.

          •  Record up and down free weights.

          •  Run survey tools on wireline to the UBHO sub if using a survey.

          •  Take survey readings.

          •  To orient the whipstock, rotate the drill pipe to the right in ¼ turn increments

           (no more that ½ turn at a time) until the whipstock assembly is oriented in the

            correct position.

          •  Take another survey, making additional corrections as needed and lowering 4

            to 5 feet after each increment.

   CAUTION: A crooked hole may require several readings and corrections to

    remove all of the trapped torque from the drill string. Work slowly and carefully

     in order to achieve proper orientation.

     This Model of Whipstock, because it is hinged, must be set within 30° on either

     side of the low side to prevent the whipstock’s whipface from falling across the

     wellbore.

          •  Continue taking readings until 3 sequential reading are within a 5° range before

          assuming the direction is correct.

          •  After reaching and verifying the proper direction, call a meeting with the

           customer,  driller, and survey/MWD company representative to review the

            orientation of the whipstock’s Whipface once more.

          •  Continue the procedure if everyone is in agreement.

          •  Raise the survey tools at least 150 feet up the drill string to be above the recoil

           shock.

          •  Lower the whipstock assembly slowly to the setting depth.

       7- Setting procedures

          Continue lowering the whipstock until the required weight is reached to shear

           the shearpin. This shearing releases the tripbar and compressed spring, which

            in turn drives the slip up into the setting position between the casing wall and

           whipstock.

          •  Apply weight 15,000 lb for 5”, 20,000 lb for 7” & 30,0000 lb for 9⅝” on

          anchor to prove that it is set.

          •  After the whipstock has set, continue slacking off while adding weight to shear

           the shear screw on the Rugby Mill. A sharp drop in the pipe occurs when the

           shear screw shears.

          •  Raise the drill string approximately 1 to 2 feet.

          •  Set the drill string back down to make sure the shear screw has sheared. Repeat

               if necessary.

          •  Mark the depth on the pipe each time. It should read the same each time.

          •  Set the pipe back down with approximately 10,000 lb of pressure to ensure that

           the Whipface is over against the casing wall.

          •  Raise the drill string so that the Rugby Mill is 5 to 10 feet above the top of the

           whipstock.

          •  Lay down the necessary drill pipe to pick up the kelly.

          •  Displace the Mud in the well (if needed) and check mud properties for proper

           rheology for steel removal.

          •  The milling operation can now begin.

        Running Milling Assembly and Opening the Window

        This section assumes that the Rugby Mill has been sheared from the whipstock

       and raised 5 to 10 feet above the whipstock.

     NOTE: Install Ditch Magnets

         1. Make up the kelly to the drill string.

         2. Record string weight up and down.

         3. Engage the pumps, slowly increasing to the planned circulation rate, and

        record pump pressure and rate.

         4. Engage the rotary and slowly increase the speed between 20 and 40 RPM.

         5. After the rotary has stabilized, slowly increase between 50 and 75 RPM

        (depending on the mill size).

         6. Record the following information:

     Free rotary torque

     Pump pressure

     Pump rate

     Free weight – up and down (string weight with zero weight on mill)

         7. While rotating, slowly lower the drill string until the Rugby Mill contacts

        the whipstock and the casing wall, causing an immediate and substantial increase

         in rotary torque.

         8. Mark the kelly. This is the starting point for the Rugby Mill run.

         9. After making the initial mark on the kelly, raise the Starting Mill 3 to 5

          feet.

        10. While rotating, slowly lower the drill string again to see if the increase in

         rotary torque is indicated at the same place. Work slowly and carefully; repeat if

        11. After marking the kelly with the correct starting point for the Mill, begin

        the matter with a HPOGC Supervisor.

        14. Monitor returns for cuttings during milling operations and pump high

         especially important if the window is being milled in a lateral section.

         into the formation AND when the Dress Mill is centered at the bottom of the

       window.

        torque or drag are indicated.

        17. Stop the rotary and slide the Mill assembly through the window several

         times. If any weight or drag is indicated, start the rotary and ream back and forth

        through the window until weight or drag is eliminated.

       8- Abnormal Conditions

        Whipstock stuck

          •  Try to release the whipstock with 20000klb overpull in 7" hole and 30000klb

           over pull in 9-5/8" hole.

          •  Close an annular preventor hydrill and apply 500-1000 psi pressure in annulus

            to remove casing collapse.

          •  Apply weight on bit.

          •   If no success, shear screw and pull out the whipstock out of hole with fishing

            tool later.

       No success in passing the whipstock through liner lab

          •  Come back to neutral point and turn string 1/4 round in clockwise direction and
             try to pass through liner lab again.

       The packer does not set with designed setting pressure

          •  Release the pressure and apply the pressure again.

          •  Pull out of hole to surface and check the tools.

         Pressure drop during the milling operation

          •  Twisting off the string or mill:

          •  In this case the torque will be constant and progress will be stop.

          •  Pressure drop due to string washing out:

          •  In this case the standpipe pressure will decrease gradually.

          •  Pressure drop due to breaking the nozzle plugs:

          •  In this case the pressure decreases suddenly and no change in other parameters

            will happen.

          •  Pressure drop due to washing out the nozzles:

          •  In this case the standpipe pressure will decrease gradually.

         Note: In all the above items pulling the string out of hole to surface and
         checking the tools will be necessary.

       No progress during milling

          •  Change  the  drilling parameters,  for example increase or decrease RPM,

            increase WOB, decrease GPM.

          •   If you observe no progress in 2 hours, pulling the string out of hole to surface

            will be necessary.

        Observing complete loss during milling

          •  Don’t continue milling till curing the complete loss.

          •   If the client insists in continuing the operation, due to working with low GPM,

           stop the milling operation for 30 minutes after each hour of milling to cool the

            mill and prevent from thermal cracking.

      POH string due to failure of whipstock:

          •  Do not shock the string while put and pick up slips
