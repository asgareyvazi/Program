1  Introduction

     1.1    Program Introduction

        The South Azadegan Oil field has been discovered by Well AZN-001, drilled in 1999.
       This well drilled to a total depth of 4,643m evaluating all the possible hydrocarbon bearing of
       the Cretaceous sequences. So far up to, 39 wells have been drilled in the South Azadegan Oil
        Field.
         Carbonate reservoir of Sarvak formation of the Middle Cretaceous age, was penetrated
      and evaluated in all the initial 6 exploration wells (AZN-001, AZN-002, AZN-003, AZN-
       004, AZN-005 and AZN-006) and has been broadly developed in Azadegan Oil Field.
      Moreover hydrocarbon potentials of the clastic oil bearing zones of Kazhdumi and Gadvan
      Formations were also evaluated in the early exploration phase of the field. Clastic oil bearing
      zones of Gadvan formation has only been developed in the crestal area and southern parts of
       the Azadegan Oil Filed.

     1.2    South Azadegan Structure

       As the Previous exploration/delineation drilling in Azadegan oil field (Figure 1) has
      proved presence of a very prolific hydrocarbon bearing sedimentary column, particularly
       within the Cretaceous sequences. The Fahliyan Formation was divided into 6 zones, named
       as Fah-0, Fah-1, Fah-2, Fah-3, Fah-4 and Fah-5, of which Fah-1, 2, 3, 4 and 5 bear light oil.
      The average thickness of the Fahliyan, based on well data, is 299m. The oil bearing zones of
       Fahliyan only developed in south part of the field around Well AZNS-004 and AZNS-006. 4
        wells, AZNS-004, 006, 010 and 011, penetrated Fahliyan oil bearing zones.

      Fahliyan Formation
    The Fahliyan Formation in south Azadegan Oil Field is generally composed of a sequence of
limestones,  off-white  to  cream  to  light  brown  wackestone-packstone,  occasionally  detrital,
fossiliferous and in places with intergranular and micro-vuggy. The Upper parts of the section, with
over 105 meters in thickness, as has been designated as Upper Fahliyan as has low reservoir quality.
    The Lower Fahliyan lower section is composed of clean, light cream to light brown limestone
with interbeds of dolomitic limestones. Towards the base the lithology changes into more argillaceous
limestone.

     1 STRUCTURAL GEOLOGY:

      The Azadegan oil field is located at 80km west of Ahwaz and the Azadegan Field is
 an elongated anticline, trending in N-S direction, belongs to Arabian Trend which  is
 different from “Zagros Trend”. There is no outcrop for Azadegan structure and  it was
 detected by 3D seismic interpretation.

     2 LITHO-STRATIGRAPHY DESCRIPTION:

      The  stratigraphy sequence of  the  field includes Aghajari, Gachsaran, Asmari,
 Pabdeh,  Gurpi,  Ilam,  Laffan,  Sarvak,  Kazhdumi,  Dariyan,  Gadvan  and  Fahliyan
 Formations, from top to bottom. This sequence in well no. AZN-10, the deepest up to now
 drilled well of the field, continues with Garau, Gotnia, Najmah, Sargalu, Alan, Mus and
 Neyriz formations.
   Fars Group
    Fars group originally divided into two formations, Aghajari and Gachsaran formations.
   Aghajari Formation
     Depth: Surface to 1101m     Drilled Thickness: 1101m   Age: MIOCENE-PLIOCENE
 Lithology: This Formation begins with light cream, light gray, yellow, reddish brown,
 quartz, subrounded, good  sorted  Sandstone.  This  formation  is  mostly  consisting  of
 red marl interbedded with sandstone and red claystone layers. The Red claystone increase
 toward the base of the formation. The Sandstones are variably consolidated and generally
 light brown, light gray in colour, fine to medium grain size, generally fine grained, rarely
 coarse grained, generally  well  sorted  and  dominated by  sub-angular to  sub-rounded
 quartz  grains,  loose, friable to moderately hard, slightly calcareous. The Marls are red
 brown to brown and pale red brown, generally sticky and slightly silty, soft to firm and
 locally very low calcareous grading to claystone. The Red claystones are brown, moderate
 to dark brown, generally soft, sticky and amorphous, locally some calcareous grading to
 marl, generally non silty and occasionally mottled. A fauna of brackish to freshwater was
 observed, together with more marine fauna.

   2. Lesson learned

   Most of problems in south Azadegan field occurred in 3th and 4th interlayer of Gachsaran Group due

to presence of sticky Red and Brown Marl. Also, reorganization of first interlayer of Gachsaran which

into account cap rock of Asmari reservoir is so important to prevent lost and stuck problem through low

pressure pore formation pressure.

    Further shale and clay swelling in Bangestan Group is customary.

    Understanding and anticipating drilling problems, understanding their causes, and planning solutions

are necessary for overall-well-cost control and for successfully reaching the target zone. According to

report of nearby wells, the following items has been considered to drilling the new well:

    Bit balling is common problem. A rough bit surface will increase the surface area and increase the

adhesive forces. When the mud flow stops, or if a bit with poor hydraulic design is used, an electrostatic

force may cause clays to stick in the bit surface. With several work such as increase flow rate, Pump a

caustic pill, Spin the bit as fast as possible can be prevented this problem.

    Stuck  is another problem in the previous wells. Stuck can be classified under two categories:

differential and mechanical. In the nearby wells, it can be said that Mechanical type wasn’t happened

except stuck 13 3/8” casing in AZN 047 at 765m (Aghajari formation and got free with 250 Klbs

overpull). More stuck in the mentioned wells often is associated with complete loss, so differential type

may be happened. In AZN 047, string got stuck with 71 PCF mud from 1928m to 1945m (Pabdeh

formation) and got free with 40 Klbs Over pull. Another stuck in this well was happened with 76 PCF

mud at 2675m to 2714m (Sarvak Formation) and got free with 60-100 Klbs over pull.

    Tight hole is a common problem in the mentioned wells and usally for curing this problem, mud

weight is increased. The serius tight hole was reported in Jahrum/Pabdeh and Gurpi. It should be noted

that when tight hole is cured, mud weight don’t excess from critical value.

   The costly problem that may be happened in future wells is twist off string. Twisting off is ocurred

when the drill string parts by failing catastrophically under the torsional stress. This may happen if the

8  Formation or Pore Pressure and Mud Weight
Primary Well Control relies on the hydrostatic pressure of drilling fluid to balance the formation pore

pressure. In order to counteract any minor pressure reductions on bottom due to swabbing effect when

tripping, it is customary to maintain a hydrostatic column with a pressure slightly exceeding the pore

pressure of the exposed formation. This overbalance, safety margin, or trip margin, Ptm, varies

depending on circumstances but it is generally of the order of 200 psi for conventional operations.

The required density Pf to control a formation at a depth, D, with a pressure, Po, is given by: Pf =

(Po+Ptm), or Df = (Po+Ptm)/D

To gas cut or contaminated mud, Df = Do + (0.07-0.15) sg,

To oil and/or salt water contaminated mud, Df = Do + (0.05-0.10) sg;

2-4- Cementing Procedure
1)  1) Clean up tanks and preparing chemicals for mixing fluid.

2)  Fill tanks with fresh water for mixing fluids.

3) Before finished running casings with company man approval, start mixing cement additives and

    preparing mixing fluids according to below cement additives mixing procedure:

         a. Add Antifoam to fresh water and allow mixing for 5 minutes.

        b. Add CaCl2 and allow mixing for 40 minutes.

         c. Add Dispersant and allow mixing until the cement operation will finish.

4) Before running casing, check the stinger and stab-in shoe & Install stab-in shoe to end of first

    casing.

5)  After running casing, install stinger to end of first 5" drill pipe.

6) RIH to bottom with 5" drill pipe, before setting the stinger, circulation mud.

7)  Set the stinger & circulation mud if observed flowing of mud in casing, try again to setting the

    stinger.

8) Hang drill pipe.

9) Pre job safety meeting with involved personnel.

10) Test cement line W/ 3000 psi.

11) Pump spacer.

12) Mix and pump cement slurry.

13) Displace cement with drilling fluid.

14) Sting out and check for float valve working.

15) Circulate to clean out inner string.

  3-4- Cementing Procedure
1)  1) Clean up tanks and preparing chemicals for mixing fluid.

    preparing mixing fluids according to below Lead cement mixing procedure:

         c. Add Low Temperature Retarder and allow mixing until the cement operation will finish.

4)  Tail cement mixing procedure:

         a. Add Antifoam and allow mixing for 5 minutes.

        b. Add Dispersant and allow mixing for 15 minutes.

         c. Add Low temperature retarder and allow mixing until the cement operation will finish.

5) Before running casing, check cement head specially plunger of released cement plug and put the

    top & bottom plug.

6)  After running Casing, install circulating swage, start circulation at least one casing cap volume.

7) Pre job safety meeting with involved personnel and arrange with mud engineer to measure drilling

    fluid & cement loss/gain during cement job.

8) Stop circulation, connect 13 3/8" cement head and cement line.

9)  Test cement line W/ 3000 psi.

10) Pump spacer then release bottom plug.

(5) KICK DETECTION PROCEDURE WHILE DRILLING

If there is an indication of a possible kick occurring, the Driller shall follow the kick detection procedures

outlined below:

Stop drilling with the pump onPick up top drive to a predetermined space-out positionShut off the pumps

and check for well flow by visual examination on the bell nipple or flow line.

1)   If the well is not flowing:

    Immediately notify the rig manager, Drilling Supervisor and Tool pusher.

    The Drilling Supervisor will decide whether to circulate bottoms up or resume  drilling. The

         penetration rate will be limited as long as the cause of the abnormal situation is undetermined.

        Always be aware that some hydrocarbons (gas or oil cut mud) may rise to the surface. Ensure that the

         proper monitoring and safety systems all in place.

6 H2S monitoring and prevention measure

    6.1  Monitoring H2S
             1) Somewhere on the well site is apt to accumulate H2S, such as mud pit, shale shaker,
              and drilling platform, should be installed one set of. Make sure H2S monitor and

               alarm system can be used.

             2) Alarm threshold  setting  for H2S warning  indicator: the  first warning  level  is
              10mg/m3, unable warning sound,  just show present value of H2S content  to
                 operators. The second warning level is 20mg/m3, when the H2S content in air exceed
             20mg/m3, H2S warning indicator can give an alarm automatically, and warning

              sound should be heard by everyone work in well site. The audible alarm should be

                  installed on Kelly board.

             3) Equip enough gas masks on drilling platform, shaker screen (total amount should

                 large than 24), and equip 12 sets of 30min positive pressure Air Aspirators，2 sets of

                 portable H2S monitor and appropriative H2S warning indicator. Arrange a person

                 specially assigned for taking charge all the protective apparatus. This man must do

                 periodical check to keep these equipment in a good condition.

             4) H2S monitor should be done weekly check and compulsory examination.
             5)  After drilling into the pay zone, strengthen the detection of H2S content in mud.
