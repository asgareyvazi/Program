1.  General Information

 1.1 Field Introduction
Azadegan South Oil Field is situated in West Karoun province on a large long-axis anticline with N-S
trending with approximate dimensions about 20 km width and 60 km length near to Iran-Iraq border, to
the east of Yaran Oil Field.
The trap area of the south Azadegan is limited to 43.2x17.2 km. The Azadegan Oil field was discovered
in 1999, when Well AZN-1 was drilled vertically down to the Cretaceous Fahliyan Formation with total
depth of 4,643m. The results of this well, revealed the reservoir characteristics of the field. Following to
the result of the well no. AZN-1, another 5 exploration wells was drilled in different parts of this huge
field down to Fahliyan Formation. In the later stage the well No.10 assumed as exploration well for
evaluation of deeper formations. This well was drilled in 2008, down to the depth of 5340m (Neyriz
Fm.), mainly to evaluate the Jurassic formations.
Based on the exploration wells results, carbonate reservoir of Sarvak Formation is broadly developed in
Azadegan Field. This formation was penetrated in all of seven exploration wells (AZN-1, AZN-2, AZN-
3, AZN-4, AZN-5, AZN-6 and AZN-10). Clastic oil-bearing zones of Kazhdumi Formation developed in
main anticline of Azadegan Field, extending from north to center of South Azadegan. The Clastic oil-
bearing zones of Gadvan Formation developed only in crest area and south parts of South Azadegan
Filed, which was discovered by AZN-1 in crest and AZN-4 in south parts. The carbonates of Fahliyan
Formation also developed in Azadegan field, but the oil reservoir of this formation developed in South
part of South Azadegan.
The Azadegan Field is an elongated anticline, trending in N-S direction, belonging to "Arabian Trend".
The stratigraphy sequence of the field includes AghaJari, Gachsaran, Fahliyan, Pabdeh, Gurpi, Ilam,
Laffan, Sarvak, Kazhdumi, Dariyan, Gadvan and Fahliyan Formations, from top to bottom. This
sequence in well no AZN-10, the deepest up to now drilled well of the field, continues with Garau,
Gotnia, Najmah, Sargalu, Alan, Mus and Neyriz formations.
The Dominant lithofacies of Sarvak Formation are Rudist-bearing packstone and partially grainstone,
which contains abundant bioclasts. Some other facies like matrix-dominated wackestone and mudstone
are also found.
The AZADEGAN  Oilfield  is Located  in Khuzestan Province and  is Approximately Located 80
Kilometers West of Ahwaz City, near to Iran-Iraq Border. It is in The Northwest of The YADAVARAN
Field and in The West of JUFEYR Field. The Average Monthly Temperature is 45ºc in summer, and
Highest Temperature is 52ºc. The Lowest Temperature is -4.4ºc in winter. Maximum Rainfall is 78 mm
in February, Winter Season.

1.7 Well History

1.7.1 Drilling History

     This well was drilled on 13 Jun 2015 by rig FATH-33 to produce oil from GADVAN
      formation as below operation summary:

      1.     26” Hole was drilled, Ran & Cemented 20” Surface Casing. 20" CSG was run &
     cemented by inner stab-in stringer with slurry to surface.

      2.    N/U CHH and BOP Stack, Performed Pressure Test, Drilled out float shoe.

      3.     17-1/2" hole was drilled. Run and cement 13-3/8” casing @ 1155.5m.

      4.    N/U CHS and BOP, Performed Pressure Test, drilled out float shoe.

      5.    12 1/4" Hole was drilled. Run and cement 9 5/8" casing @ 2477.5m.

      6.    N/U THS and BOP Stack, performed pressure tests, drilled out float shoe.

      7.    8 1/2" hole was drilled, 7" Liner was run & cemented from 2356m to 3799.5m. 7" L.
     Lap dry test was performed, no bubble was observed, 920 psi pressure build up was observed
      in the closing period.

      8.     Set two cement plugs & Rig was released on 30-Aug-2015.

      9.     Rig FATH-65 was spudded on 17 Jan 2017.

      10.   D/O Two cement plugs.

      11.    7" Liner lap dry test was performed, no bubble was observed, 113 psi pressure build
     up was observed in closing period.

      12.    Perforation was performed inside of 7” liner, Perforation interval: 3659-3666 m &
     3669-3678 m

      13.    Completion string was run and packer was set at 3575.34m.

      14.    Rig FATH-65 was released on 17-Feb-2017.

      15.    Acidizing was performed after releasing the rig. 30 bbls of 28% HCl was spotted by
    CT on perforation zone and then bullheaded into formation.

      16.    After unloading the well with diesel and nitrogen, it was observed the well was not
     flowing but annulus between production casing and tubing got pressure.

      17.   To check the status of reservoir interval tried to run Temperature, Pressure and Caliper
      logs in static and injection mode, but the operation of injection mode was not successful due
      to leakage in completion string and annulus pressure.

9.1.1  Challenges
     Hole instability issues
     Wellbore losses
     Shale
     Tight hole & well instability

9.1.2 Recommendations
      It is mandatory to monitor closely the rheological properties in order to ensure adequate hole
       cleaning, run Hydraulic calculation to ensure that mud carrying capacity is enough to meet this
         critical goal.
     Mud can be treated with additional shale inhibitor if deemed necessary to control shale reactivity
      and washouts.
     Optimize the procedure to fight the losses (see the guide at the end of this mud program) and be
       ready with adequate LCM material to mix LCM pill as quick as possible.
     The previous mud in hole must be dump, due to have enough surface volume, keep 300bbl heavy
     mud in mud pits as kill mud.
     The concentration of KCl in the system is insufficient; shale cuttings will be soft and mushy. If
       the concentration of KCl is sufficient, they will retain their integrity. As drilling commences the
       potassium ion becomes depleted and must be replenished. The rate of depletion provides an
        indication of how reactive the shales are. While drilling gumbo, the appearance of soft mushy
        cuttings at surface is a good indication that the potassium concentration should be increased.
       Other indicators include a drilled solids increase, a CEC increase and the occurrence of tight hole
       or mud rings.
     Since this system is very solids-sensitive, it is best to mix the system from scratch instead of
       converting an existing drilling fluid (containing drill solids) to a potassium chloride system.
     This system is very sensitive to solids and hardness contamination so the first step in mixing this
       system is to treat the hardness.
     Due to necessary of directional drilling, it is recommended to pump (at the first 20bbl Low Vis pill
       follow by 20bbl Hi Vis  Pill should be pumped every stand) for clean the hole. For better
       understanding of hole cleaning procedure based on hole angels please refer to technical appendix.
     Due to face with torque and drag, it is recommended to mix water base mud lubricant 1 – 3% vol.
       reduce torque and drag while drilling horizontal or vertical.
      In the directional drilling section, it is recommended to use Glass beads 2 – 3ppb as due to have
       torque and drag.

9.3.1  Solids – Free CaCl2 fluid system
This fluid is recommended to make from fresh water or is solutions made with water and the addition of
one or more electrolytes. These fluids do not contain either suspended particles or viscosifiers, which
mean neither filtration control nor viscosity control, are provided. Some Brine is corrosive and when
corrosion inhibitors are required, compatibility studies should be performed. Some brine is toxic and
precautions should be taken to prevent contact with unprotected skin. Brine solutions can be prepared to
give good inhibition of clays or shales in producing formations. In addition, common contaminants do
not affect the brines.

Salt solutions were formerly considered too corrosive to be used as work-over or completion fluids or as
packer fluids. The corrosion problem can be solved by adjusting the pH of the solution to above 9 (min)
to 10; however, pH measurements may be less important than measuring and controlling the p-alkalinity
of the filtrate; this value should be kept above 1cc. Lime be used for pH control.

Adjustment of pH, or alkalinity, does not necessarily ensure the solution to be corrosion resistant. It may
be necessary to use a corrosion inhibitor to achieve lower corrosion rates. In some cases, an oxygen
scavenger might be used in conjunction with a filming amine inhibitor.

9.3.2  Corrosion control
Corrosion control in completion fluids is important to protect tubular goods and equipment. Corrosion
can be limited by controlling the fluid pH at the neutral or alkaline level, and  treating  out  dissolved
oxygen.  In  severe  environments  such  as  high temperature or contamination with acid gases, the
addition of filming amines can limit corrosion to acceptable levels.

9.3.3  Filtration process
Filtration provides clean  fluids  that will prevent damage to the formation, the reservoir and  its
permeability. It does this by removal of solids and particulates from the fluid that may otherwise block
the open space within the rock formation containing the hydrocarbon fluid within the production zone.

Any solids and contaminates in the completion fluid will reduce its effectiveness. An efficiently filtered
and completely solid free completion fluid will add value to both the productivity and the dependability
of the well in the long term.

VIRA dual pod filtration cartridge unit is recommended for filtering brines and produced water in
completion, stimulation, and workover operations. The pods are connected by a manifold, providing
staged filtration capability. Staged filtration utilizes cartridge filters with different ratings to maximize
filtration efficiency. Cartridge filtration is designed for use with fluids containing a low percent by
volume of suspended solids. Cartridge filtration is not economic when fluids are contaminated with oil or
other substances.

Total Solids can be measured at the wellsite using a turbidity meter. Solids that are not added to the
system to enhance the performance of a brine are considered contamination. Contaminations include
formation clays, precipitates, and polymer residues, among other things. These contaminations can be
filtered at the well site using diatomaceous earth, a plate and frame press, and Five-micron to ten
absolute cartridges.

Note: a clear completion fluid should not be sent to the wellsite with an NTU (Nephelometric Turbidity
Unit) greater than 30 or a suspended-solid Concentration greater than 40 ppm and for fluid returning
from the well NTU should be less than 150.

9.4.1 Losses control procedure
Loss of fluid returns will lower the hydrostatic head of the drilling fluid in the wellbore, thereby inducing
a kick. The influx fluid will then flow to the surface or into the zone of lower pressure. Lost circulation
can occur in naturally occurring fractured, vuggy, cavernous, sub-normally pressured or pressure
depleted formations. Induced losses can occur from mechanical fracturing due to pressure surges while
breaking circulation. In all cases of lost circulation, attempts should be made to keep the hole full. The
hole can be filled with either light drilling fluid or water. A record of the amount of fluid pumped should
be made.

The concentration of LCM will vary according to losses. In the event of seepage losses, treat system with
a finer grade of LCM at 20 – 30 Kg/m³. In the event of moderate to high rate of well bore loss, prepare
pills at different graded concentrations and spot over thief zone. The concentration may vary between 18
to 70 lb/bbl. In order to avoid LCM plugging MWD tools a circulation sub should be installed in the drill
string. The MWD engineer is to advise on the size and concentration of LCM that can be pumped
through MWD Tools. Ensure that the drilling displace the LCM pill out of the string prior to shutting
down the mud pumps.

9.4.2 Mixing LCM Pills
The amount of LCM that can be added to a mud depends very much on the mud properties. High gels
and YP will help keep the material in suspension. Since LCM is generally of low gradient, excess
material tends to float on top if too much is added. The following procedure may prove useful for mixing
LCM pills and avoiding the problem of flotation.

    1. ENSURE the pill has enough viscosity to keep the low gradient material in suspension before
       addition of LCM. ADD appropriate viscosifier if required.
    2. ADD the material in stages e g. 5, 10, 15, 20 etc. ppb.
    3. STOP after desired concentration has been reached, or when  it is observed that the material
       cannot be held in suspension.
    4.  If a higher LCM content is desired, LEAVE the pill to soak for a period of time and then TRY
     ADDING more material.

GUIDELINE FOR FIELD ENGINEERS FOR SHALE INHIBITION

Shale Hydration takes two forms in water-sensitive shales: surface and osmotic adsorption. Surface
hydration occurs when a small volume of water is strongly adsorbed onto the planar surfaces of the clays
which causes little softening or swelling, but can lead to excessive stresses if the swelling is confined.

Osmotic swelling occurs when a large volume of weakly held water is attracted to the clay surfaces by
electrostatic forces. Osmotic swelling causes softening and significant swelling as the adjacent clay
layers’ hydrate water and expand. Osmostic swelling does not generate excessive stresses, even when
confined, and can be substantially reduced if Extremely difficult drilling conditions have led to more
systematic approaches to analysing wellbore instabilities.

One such approach that can be used as a guideline for the mud engineer involves three basic steps:

    1.  Monitor and analyze the fundamental failure mechanism.
    2.  Determine and understand the factors governing this failure mechanism.
    3.  Model the problem considering impact of proposed changes.

      9.4.3.1 MONITOR AND ANALYSIS THE FUNDAMENTAL FAILURE MECHANISM.

Monitoring of data and events on the rig is the first step of the process and involves:

     Collection, organization and processing of drilling data. Drilling data such as Rate of
       Penetration (ROP), Revolutions  per Minute (RPM), Weight-On-Bit (WOB), Bottom-Hole
      Assembly (BHA), torque, over pull Trip in Hole (TIH) and Pull Out of Hole (POOH), swab and
       surge, flow rate, mud weight, mud Rehology, etc. should be collected, organized and analyzed on
       a continual basis and compared to the symptoms of wellbore stability being observed. This will
       help evaluate performance and establish trends or any deviations and measure the effectiveness of
       treatments and changes.

     Formation characterization. The standard procedure in drilling operations  is to apply the
      knowledge gained from previous wells to subsequent wells drilled in similar geologic regions.
       Wellbore-instability investigation requires evaluation of shales during the drilling process. Since

────────────────────────────────────────────
────────────────────────────────────────────
           South Azadegan Oilfield Project

   Contract No:          AZNS-075 Workover Program                 Page 68 of 117

        rig site shale testing is limited due to equipment and time limitations, most evaluations are
       performed in a properly equipped laboratory.

       Laboratory  tests  are conducted  to evaluate shale  swelling,  dispersibility,  cation exchange
        capacity, hydration capacity, colloidal content, capillary suction time, shale hardness tests and
     mud compatibility properties.

    A number of methods have been used to perform rig site tests. These include tests as simple as
       the methylene blue test (CEC) measurement of shale; shale hardness measurements; hot-rolled
       dispersion testing with various mud formulations and additives; and sophisticated analytical
      measurements of various clay properties. It is particularly important to try and determine the
        failure mechanism: swelling and softening or  brittle spalling. Information from these  tests,
       current shaker observations and mud logger data are used to evaluate drilling fluid performance,
        adjust mechanical drilling parameters and making casing design decisions. The primary options
        to counter shale problems at the rig site are:

                 Change the drilling fluid density.
                 Change to an oil- or synthetic-base system.
                 Change to a more inhibited water-base mud.
                 Reduce fluid loss to very low levels and increase filtrate viscosity.
                  Utilize pore-plugging additives, emulsified insoluble materials or plastering agents.
                 Adjust mechanical drilling parameters.
                 Evaluation and analysis of instability symptoms.

Instability warning signs such as the presence of excessive cavings on the shakers for a given ROP, fill
on connections, tripping difficulties, torque, stuck pipe, etc. indicate that something is wrong down hole.
The magnitude or volume of failed rock should be estimated and recorded with the other drilling
parameters. The number and location of specific incidents should be recorded, such as pipe

sticking, fill or packing-off, reaming and re-drilling. All of this information can then be organized and
correlated with time and depth to detect the location and quantify the severity of critical sections in the
well.

 9.5 Directional Drilling

9.5.1  Drilling fluid properties & selection
It  is common knowledge among  drilling engineers and  drillers  that borehole problems can be
considerably more severe in a directional hole than in a vertical well. The problems are magnified as the
hole angle and horizontal displacement increases. Although most of these reactions are mechanical in
nature, much can be done to alleviate them by proper selection and maintenance of the drilling fluid. For
example, if a water-base fluid is being used, lubricants can be added to lower the coefficient of friction
between the drill string and borehole or casing. Inhibiting salts can be added to reduce the interaction of
the drilling fluid with the exposed formations, thereby improving borehole stability.

There are a number of drilling factors influenced by the drilling fluid, but the following are of primary
concern in drilling a horizontal well.

     Borehole instability
     Hole cleaning
     Torque and drag
     Formation damage
      Barite sag
     Hydraulics

9.5.2 Borehole instability
Borehole instability is one of the costlier, but relatively common problem associated with drilling oil and
gas wells. Not only can it impede drilling, it can result in loss of the hole. The term normally conjures
thoughts of borehole collapse with accompanying stuck pipe. This is one of the more critical reactions of
borehole instability, but it can manifest itself in subtler ways such as hole-fill, bridges, hole enlargement,
or hole size reduction.

These  reactions lead  to other associated problems including increased torque and  drag, logging
difficulties, poor cement jobs, and increased drilling days, all of which add significantly to the drilling
cost. It should be pointed out that the discussions to follow do not cover hole enlargement problems from
weakly consolidated sands, or rubble zones that erode from the hydraulic action of the drilling fluid.

9.5.3  Stabilization
The swelling activity is controlled by adjusting the electrolyte content of the fluid to minimize expansion
of the clays. Lime and gypsum-treated fluids have long been used for this purpose, but basically do not
satisfy the inhibition needs of the more troublesome shales. The potassium ion is an effective swelling

inhibitor and  is used extensively for  this purpose through the addition of potassium  salts. The
concentration of potassium utilized ranges from several hundred mg/L to over 60,000 mg/L. The most
cost-effective potassium concentration, however, appears to be in the 3000 to 7000 mg/L range. In
marine environments, seawater or seawater with additional sodium chloride, are good alternates to
potassium. The tendency for the shale to disperse or subdivide into discrete particles appears to be best
controlled by the addition of an adsorptive, high molecular weight water-soluble polymer.

One of the more effective polymers for this purpose is the partially hydrolysed poly acrylamide with
molecular weight in excess of 15 million. These anionic polymers adsorb on to the surface of the cuttings
and borehole wall to act as a binder or elastic coating to retard dispersion. Another approach to
stabilizing the hard brittle shales that contain natural or induced micro fractures is the use of water-
dispersible asphalt or Asphaltene. The addition of these type materials appear to plug the micro-fractures,
or perhaps even surface pore throats to retard further fluid invasion. This action has proven very effective
in certain shales and can be applied in most water base fluids.

9.5.4 Hole cleaning
Cleaning cuttings from a vertical hole has been covered extensively by numerous investigators. Hole
cleaning in a vertical hole is essentially a matter of overcoming the slip velocity of the cuttings with
sufficient annular velocity and carrying capacity of the drilling fluid. Although any velocity greater than
the settling velocity of the largest cutting will theoretically lift all the cuttings to surface eventually, too
low an annular velocity will lead to an undesirable high concentration of cuttings in the annulus.
Experience has shown that cuttings concentrations more than 5% by volume cause tight hole or stuck
pipe when circulation is stopped.

9.5.5  Cutting bed formation
Radial slip velocity increases with increasing angle of inclination, forcing more cuttings to the low side
of the annulus, which eventually results in the formation of a cuttings bed. reported that bed formation
was dramatic in the 40° to 50° angle with the bed sliding downward against the flow, resulting in a very
high cuttings concentration.

The fact that a cutting bed forms is a serious problem, but the instability of the bed in this critical angle
of the well makes the problem even more severe. High torque and drag resulting from the high cutting
concentration at the lower part of the angle can obviously result in stuck pipe or loss of circulation.

9.5.7  Short tripping
Another technique that has proven helpful in controlling problems created by the cuttings bed in the
high-angle portion of the well is to make periodic short trips. Tripping the bit through the bed not only
assures that the bottom hole assembly and bit can be pulled through it, but the action should agitate the
bed to the point that a portion will slide down the hole and subsequently be picked up when circulation is
commenced. The time intervals for these short trips will be determined by the torque and drag conditions
that exist in any particular well. Lacking specific well history, a general rule of thumb is to short trip at
least once for every 500 feet of new hole.

9.5.8  Drill string rotation
The industry is in total agreement that drill string rotation improves hole cleaning, especially in hole
sizes less than 17 ½”. The general opinion in the industry is that the optimum drill string rotation rate is
approximately 120 rpm.  It  is believed  that  the  drill  string  rotation improves hole  cleaning by
mechanically disturbing the cuttings bed on the low side of the hole. There could also be effects due to
skewing the velocity profile in the wellbore and the creation of Taylor Vortices. Encouraged the use of
rotary steerable systems.

9.5.9 Turbulence
Bottom hole assembly design becomes important because  the flow  restrictions  of some  of  the
components, such as the measurement-while-drilling tools or positive displacement motors, may not
allow sufficient flow to reach turbulence in the annulus. Turbulence can, of course, be induced at some
constant flow rate by decreasing the annular area. This may be more difficult to accomplish in a
horizontal well because of limitations on the design of drill strings and tool assemblies. An increase in
the effective viscosity of the drilling fluid will decrease the Reynolds Number, and thus increase the flow
rate required to obtain turbulent flow. Thus, a lower effective viscosity fluid may be desirable in the
horizontal well, but attention must also be given to cleaning the larger diameter portion of the upper hole
or possible washed-out sections where it is impractical to maintain turbulent flow.

It is interesting to point out a basic fact between static fluid pressure and dynamic fluid pressure. Static
fluid pressure is a function of true vertical depth, while dynamic fluid pressure, or ECD, is a function of
measured depth. Thus, the ECD will continue to increase, albeit slowly, even after the hole becomes
essentially horizontal. The fracture pressure could be reached if the hole was extended to sufficient depth
and the fluid density is close to the fracture gradient.

9.5.10 Hole cleaning guide line
The single most important factor related to hole cleaning in deviated wells  is flow  rate. During
directional drilling operations drilled cuttings will settle on the low side of the hole and form a stationary
bed if insufficient annular fluid velocity is used. The critical flow rate required to prevent cuttings bed
formation must be determined.

Typically, few hole cleaning problems exist in vertical or horizontal sections. Most problems associated
with hole cleaning are seen on deviated wells and occur in the 40 – 60-degree section where gravity
effects can cause cuttings beds to slump down the hole. Typical flow rates to aim for in ERD wells:

increasing fluid viscosity will improve hole cleaning and this is particularly effective if the Yield Stress

the drilling fluid rheological properties should be designed to avoid the transitional flow regime. For hole
sizes above 8 ½”, the annular flow is laminar under most circumstances. Therefore, it is necessary to
specify a minimum Yield Stress. The optimum level required is best established based on field data and
experience.

9.5.11 Torque & Drag
Overall friction can become so great in an extended-reach well or horizontal section that the drill string
can only be rotated with great difficulty. Needless to say, this condition is conducive to stuck pipe.
Obviously, this coefficient of friction will be significantly affected by the lubricating properties of the
drilling fluid.

Many of the causes of torque and drag are mechanical, while others are related to hole geometry. Several,
however, can be directly attributed to the drilling fluid. Since wall contact with the drill string will be
extremely high in a horizontal hole, it is imperative that the lubricating characteristics of the fluid be
considered.

9.5.12 Formation damage
The obvious reason for drilling a horizontal well is to expose more of the producing zone to the wellbore
for increased production. Thus, the importance of preventing formation damage during drilling and
completion of the horizontal well is just as important as it is in its vertical counterpart.

In fact, it may be more important since the increased cost of drilling the horizontal well must show
additional production for its justification. A 50% damage or permeability loss in a potential 500 bbl/day
horizontal well represents a considerably greater loss in production than a 50% damage in a 100 bbl/day
vertical hole through the same reservoir.

Sandstone production is by far the most susceptible reservoir rock to damage or permeability loss during
drilling and completion operations. Though formation damage may be caused by poor drilling and
completion practices, one of the primary causes is due to exposure to the drilling and completion fluid.

This loss, attributable to the fluid, can be caused by one or more of the following occurrences:

      Particle transport from the drilling fluid into the production zone, subsequently plugging pore
        throats.

9.5.13 Recommended fluid parameters
     Velocity – One of the major obstacles to overcome in horizontal and ERD drilling is hole
       cleaning. In vertical holes, cuttings have room to settle without causing problems. In horizontal
        wells, cuttings only have to settle 4+ inches to form a cuttings bed. Research and practical
       experience has shown that cuttings bed formation is analogous to sand dunes on an ocean beach.
      The bed moves in a wave fashion up the hole. If sufficient annular velocity is not present, the bed
       thickens until it reaches equilibrium, which usually occurs when turbulent flow conditions exist.
      With water, turbulent flow is easily obtained since viscosity is in the denominator of the turbulent
       flow equation, and water has a viscosity of 1. Fluids other than water require more pump output
        to approach turbulent flow conditions. Practical experience in other industries has proven that
       materials can be transported in water slurries if the fluid velocity is between 3 and 7 ft./sec.

     Rheological Properties – Ideally one would want a true thixotropic fluid to drill horizontally. In
        effect, two different conditions exist simultaneously. One occurs in the vertical portion of the hole

       having a high Yield Stress, or if the fluid is visco-elastic, the initial gel strength needs to be
        elevated. The opposite is needed in the horizontal section from a hole-cleaning point of view.
      Here one wants water for the reasons pointed out earlier. Compromises are the result. If casing is
        set into the zone of interest, or if formations up-hole are not a problem, then water may be
       possibly combined with occasional sweeps.

     Surface Apparatus – Special attention must be placed on the solids removal equipment at the
        surface. Solids removal efficiency must be continually monitored so that unwanted particulate
       material is not retained in the fluid system that may negatively affect fluid weight or filter cake
        quality, resulting in lost circulation or increased torque and drag. The cost of problems that may

 9.6 Hydrogen Sulphide Gas in Drilling Fluid

9.6.1  Description of Hydrogen Solphide Gas

Hydrogen sulphide is a colourless gas, with an offensive odour and a sweetish taste. It is soluble in water,
alcohol, oils, and many other solvents. It has a specific gravity of 1.1895 with reference to air. It is
considered a weak acid,  it is toxic to humans and corrosive to metals. Hydrogen sulphide can be
dangerous to personnel on the surface as it is extremely toxic to human and even animal life, and is
extremely corrosive to most metals as  it can cause cracking of  drill pipe and tubular goods, and
destruction of testing tools and wire lines. When hydrogen sulphide enters the borehole during drilling,
completion, or testing for hydrocarbons, it creates several very detrimental problems. These problems are
encountered regardless of the source of the hydrogen sulphide. Hydrogen sulphide has created unsolved
problems in the oil field.

9.6.2  Origin of Hydrogen Solphide

During oil and gas operations, hydrogen sulphide can be found as a component of formation gases,
dissolved in water, hydrocarbons, or even liquid sulphur. Thermal degradation of organic materials and
sulphate reducing bacteria (SRB) can create hydrogen sulphide along with other gases.

9.6.3 Problems caused by Hydrogen Solphide Gas

      9.6.3.3 EFFECTS OF HYDROGEN SULPHIDE ON DRILLING FLUID

The first positive identification of soluble sulphide in the drilling fluids must be taken as a possible
indication of H2S entering the fluid from some source. In the simplest case an increase in mud weight
may be sufficient to control minor influxes. If levels persist chemical treatment will be required. This can
take two forms; neutralization and scavenging. The two terms must not be confused. Neutralization of
H2S in drilling fluid by caustic soda or lime is a common field approach. This alkalinity maintenance
method has proved successful when small amounts of H2S enter the drilling fluid, for example, from
drilling up pore spaces containing the gas.

This  is not a suitable approach to the control on influxes of Hydrogen Sulphide. The alkalinity
neutralization of H2S is instantly reversible by reduction in pH. Should the pH of the fluid decrease due
to further influxes of H2S, CO2 or brine flows the sulphids may come out of solution as hydrogen
sulphide gas. To ensure that such influxes are handled safely,  it is essential to employ a sulphide
scavenger. This term refers to any additive which is capable of reacting with one or more of the sulphide
species and as a result, converting them to a more inert form. To be truly effective an irreversible and
complete reaction must occur. As a mutual equilibrium exist between the three sulphide species in
solution (H2S, HS‐ and S2‐) irreversible and complete removal of one serves to removal all three.

were drop into the acid region, sulphide will be converting back into the poisonous H2S form. For this
reasons H2S should always be precipitated by adding a source of Zinc such as Zinc Oxide.

Removal by precipitation with treatments of (ZnO): H2S + ZnO ZnS + H2O

if H2S gas detected in mud following action should be taken to:

     Immediately rise up the pH to at least 11.5 to 12 with caustic soda.
     Buffer the pH with lime.
     Begin treatment with Zinc oxide to remove sulphide (S2‐) from the system. (1 lb/bbl treatment of
       Zinc oxide removes approximately 1100 mg/lit of sulphide. No more than 2 lb/bbl of Zinc oxide
       should be used to pre‐treat the system for H2S) If it appears that H2S bleeding in (flowing) form
       the formation, the mud density should be increased to shut off the flow of gas into the well‐bore.

12.  Health, Safety and Environments

  13.1   WELL CONTROL
 All the drilling operation shall obey the requirement of Well Control Manual of MSA and contractor’s
 well control manual; under no circumstances should the deviation from well control policy be permitted
 without prior approval through MSA. If the contractor’s well control manual or anywhere has conflict
 with Well Control Manual of MSA, take Well Control Manual of MSA as the standard.

  13.1.1 WORK Requirement of Well Control Equipment
    With shut-in casing pressure lower than 1,000psi, while snubbing with annular preventer, drill tools
      shall have 18-deg shoulder tool joints, and the snubbing velocity shall not be greater than 0.2m/s.
     After shut off the annular preventer or ram preventer, with the shut-in casing pressure not greater
     than 2,000psi, drill tools are allowed to pull up and down with the velocity less than 0.2m/s,
     however, rotating or tool joint passing through are forbidden.
    While drilling through pay zone and thereafter, BOP function (close-open) test for the BOPs shall be
     performed regularly.
     Kill manifolds shall not be used for drilling fluid fill-in.
    The maximum allowed shut-in casing pressure shall be remarkably indicated on choke manifolds.
     All gate valves of well control manifolds shall be tagged with serial numbers, and be indicated with
      on/Off.
     In case shear rams were used, after shearing the drill pipe with shear rams, retrieve the upper sheared
       drill pipe, lower a drill pipe joint with TIW valve installed into the hole and close pipe rams on it to
      prevent the possibility of shear ram’s failure.

  13.1.2  Management of Well Control Equipment
    Well control equipment used by the drilling crew shall be managed and operated by designated
     personnel with clear post responsibilities.
    Well  site  shall have specified component storeroom and rubber element storeroom with  air
      conditioners  respectively. The temperature of storerooms  shall meet storage requirements of
     components and rubber elements.
    At least 1 set is available as back-up for every type ram assembly in the well site. Ram preventer
      side door seals and ram rubber, every type gasket and bolt etc. should be enough for backup.

 13.3    Flow Check Procedure
To avoid a kick situation, it is recommended that a flow check be conducted at specific times during the
drilling operation.
While drilling (any warning sign).
Prior to tripping the drill string.
While tripping (any warning sign).
While out of hole (continuously).
Trip out:
After pulling 5% of the drill pipe
At the midpoint depth of the wellbore
Prior to pulling the first stand of drill collars
After all the drill string is out of hole.
Trip in:
Upon reaching surface casing shoe
At midpoint of the wellbore

 13.4    Primary Control Reference
It is the responsibility of the Drilling Contractor to keep the hole full at all times by using all the
available equipment. The Drilling Supervisor shall monitor that the correct preventive practices are being
implemented and/or conducted at all times.
Extreme care shall be taken to monitor mud volume, drilling breaks and gas cut mud.
The MACP (Maximum allowable casing pressure) shall be confirmed by the Drilling Supervisor, either
after setting each new casing string or, whenever the density of the drilling mud changes.
The MACP shall be clearly written on a kick control sheet that will be posted near the choke control
panel.
The predetermined distance at which the Tool Joint is to be pulled above the rotary should be posted on
rig floor near the BOP control panel.
The Driller is responsible for carefully measuring and recording the slow pump pressure.
Awareness of these values is an important element in killing operations, in order to avoid formation
breakdown.

Main technical measures for prevention of kicking and blowout during tripping.
Before POOH, circulation duration of mud in wellbore shall not be less than one and a half cycles. The
observation time after wiper trip shall be no less than one and a half cycles. The density differential of
inlet and outlet shall not exceed 1.248 pcf.
Flow check shall be conducted regularly via trip tank as follows
Prior to POOH (keep string rotation at 10 rpm for preventing stuck pipe)
POOH to last casing shoe
Before POOH BHA through BOP stack
Note: Tripping should be shut-off immediately and flow check shall be performed later on when any
suspected well kick is observed.
During POOH, fill up hole every 3-5 stands of DP and every stand of DC. If the nozzle is plugged,
during POOH, mud shall be filled full for each joint or continuous filling shall be conducted and trip
sheet shall be recorded carefully.
When the bit is in the oil and gas formation and 300m above top of oil and gas formation, POOH speed
shall be controlled to maintain the excellent well building property and rheological property of mud to
avoid severe swabbing in the wellbore.
During RIH, the running speed shall be controlled to avoid the loss of circulation due to down hole
pressure surge.
During tripping, check and repair of the equipment shall be arranged at the time when running to the
casing shoe. if equipment have to be checked and repaired during POOH, due blowout prevention
measures shall be taken during and after the check and repair, drill string shall be run to bottom and
circulate for one and a half cycles. After everything is normal, drilling shall be resumed. Equipment
check and repair shall not be conducted for empty well condition.
Treatment measures for invasion of gas during normal drilling:
When invasion of gas is discovered, it shall be discharged with circulation in time. The gas invaded mud
shall not be pumped in the hole before eliminating gas.
Increasing mud weight shall be conducted when stop drilling and after invaded gas eliminated from mud.
Mud weight incremental of each circulation cycle shall be controlled within 3.121 pcf
"In-post" policy
"In-post" will be initiated after the surface casing is cemented.

During drilling, specific personnel shall be arranged to be "in-post", the mud logging personnel shall

"In-post" requirements:

For tripping operation, trip sheet shall be filled accordingly. If anomaly occurs, it shall be reported to the

Treatment of loss of circulation
Before cementing non-target zone or pay zone, if loss of circulation occurs during drilling, POOH shall
be carried to run open ended drill pipe for cement plug job if condition allows. During RIH, mud shall be
filled from annulus to balance formation pressure to avoid blowout.
Treatment of loss of circulation during drilling after production casing cemented
Mud level not at wellhead after loss of circulation. Stop drilling, pick up TDS or Kelly, stop pumps and
observe with moving the drill string and prepare well control related work. Each 10 minutes, mud with
density no lower than the drilling weight shall be reversely filled up from the annulus. Each time, the
filling up shall be 1~2m3, or continuous reserve injecting mud with small speed to maintain that the
column pressure of mud in wellbore is higher than the formation pressure. POOH to casing shoe or
safety interval for observation. Additional treatment shall be made as per the situation of loss of
circulation.
Mud level at wellhead after loss of circulation. Stop drilling, pick up TDS or Kelly, stop pumps and
move the drill string to observe with well control well prepared. When the well is static, pull the bit
upward to the casing shoe or safe interval for observation. If no anomaly is observed, circulate with slow
pump rate. The measures such as plugging back job, POOH or condition mud etc. shall be carried out as
per the situation of loss of circulation. If loss of circulation occurs to pay zone, mud weight shall be
acquired by static pressure. After pulling out to casing shoe and observing for one tripping cycle, when
no more loss of circulation occurs after running to bottom, drilling operation can be resumed.
Well control for E logging, cementing and DST
Before E logging, down hole condition shall be stable and static. If it takes long time for electrical
logging, one more condition trip shall be conducted between logging. Shooting nipple is required for
logging. Logging service shall be furnished with tools for cutting the cables.
Continuously fill the well during performing wireline logging, and keep closely monitoring for the well
via trip tank.
Before running casing, ensure the well is in static condition without any flowing. Pipe ram shall be
changed to casing ram with proper size. Casing circulating head or crossover sub between casing and in
used DP shall be kept at rig floor at all times during casing running.
The hydrostatic pressure shall be kept balance with formation pressure during whole cementing job to
avoid blowout due to loss of circulation, loss of balance for pressure and cement weight loss during
WOC.

13.  H2S Monitoring & Prevention Measure

  14.1    Monitoring H2S
 Somewhere on the well site is apt to accumulate H2S, such as mud pit, shale shaker, and drilling
 platform, should be installed one set of. Make sure H2S monitor and alarm system can be used. Alarm
 threshold setting for H2S warning indicator: the first warning level is 10mg/m3, unable warning sound,
  just show present value of H2S content to operators. The second warning level is 20mg/m3, when the
 H2S content in air exceed 20mg/m3, H2S warning indicator can give an alarm automatically, and
 warning sound should be heard by everyone work in well site. The audible alarm should be installed on
 Kelly board.
 Equip enough gas masks on drilling platform, shaker screen (total amount should large than 24), and
 equip 12 sets of 30min positive pressure Air Aspirators 2 sets of portable H2S monitor and appropriative
 H2S warning indicator. Arrange a person specially assigned for taking charge  all the protective
 apparatus. This man must do periodical check to keep these equipment in a good condition.
 H2S monitor should be done weekly check and compulsory examination.
 After drilling into the pay zone, strengthen the detection of H2S content in mud.

  14.2    Measures for H2S prevention
 Windsocks shall be set on the drill floor and in the monsoon direction or/and prevailing wind direction of
 Azadegan oil field, off well site. In emergency, employees must follow the evacuation procedure.
 Explosion-proof blowers shall be installed around the rig, shale shaker, and drilling fluid tanks to blow
 away toxic and flammable gas at the well site.
 The rig crew in the H2S area shall be furnished with H2S monitors and adequate protective apparatus.
 Everybody shall be able to use, maintain, and check the apparatus.
 The technicians of rig crew are responsible for H2S proof training and the tool pusher is in charge of the
 supervision and examination.
 The pH value of drilling fluid in the H2S area shall be kept over 9.5. Pay attention to measurement of
 H2S concentration in drilling fluid. The H2S concentration shall be kept at less than 50mg/cm3 by using
 sulphur removal agents and degassers.
 Personnel safety and protection shall be carried out according to SY/T5087 during operations in polluted
 areas where H2S content exceeds the critical concentration.
 When the rig crew makes decision to bleed and fire because well control cannot be performed due to well
  site conditions, operations shall be performed according to procedures in SY/T5087.
 Once blowout occurs, the incident shall be reported to the higher level department as soon as possible
 and firefighting trucks, ambulances, medical care personnel and technicians shall be on duty at the well
  site.
