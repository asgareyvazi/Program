1     Introduction and Background

        South Pars gas field is part of the largest known offshore gas field in the world. It extends in to
         Qatari territorial waters where it is called North Field. Statoil is operator for the offshore
        development of South Pars phases 6, 7 & 8. Petropars is partner in South Pars phases 6, 7 & 8 and
         responsible for the onshore plant project.

         Pars Oil & Gas Company (POGC) has defined scope of work in the Master Development Plan
       (MDP) for the South Pars field.

        During 2003, Statoil has suggested changes to the MDP in order to either: 1) reduce costs or 2)
         increase production. Many of these changes were brought forward in a Position Paper sent to
      POGC 03/05/2003 “SSPP/POGC/JA/L127”.

        This document describes the entire Reservoir Management Plan and documents the changes from
         the MDP. In addition some potential future optimalizations are described in this report.

2    Recommendations and Plan


        All changes from the MDP have been discussed and approved by POGC and the JMC. Statoil has
         the following recommendation for reservoir development of South Pars phases 6, 7 & 8.

         1.  Include K1 in the field development
         2. Open hole completion, with predrilled liner
         3. No initial stimulation or perforation
         4.  Drill and complete the wells to approximately 2100 m horizontal displacement with an
             inclination of 50° at TD
         5.  Wells to be drilled to approximately 50 meters above Gas Water Contact (GWC)

       The simulation results show that same production ceiling can be achieved with fewer wells in
        combination with multibranched wells. This is discussed in chapter 9.

3     Conclusion

         All changes will result in a lower CAPEX and higher production compared to MDP. Assuming
       3*10 wells, cumulative condensate production will increase from 932 to 1035 million bbl.
        Cumulative Gas production will increase from 29,8 to 33,3 Terra Scf. The undiscounted value of
         extra condensate is more than 2000 million USD.

4    Geology

4.1   Introduction


       The late Permian Dalan Formation and the early Triassic Kangan Formation are the main gas
         reservoir on the South Pars Field. Oil is discovered in the more shallow Cretaceous Formations.
       Kangan and Dalan Formation are divided into five major sequences called Upper Kangan and
        Khuff 1, 2, 3 & 4 (K1, K2, K3 &K4).

        Khuff consists mainly of dolomites, limestone and minor anhydrites. Seven major depositional
        environments are identified, spreading from evaporitic/sabkhas flats to offshore settings.


4.2   General Regional Geology and Setting

         In the late Early Permian, the Arabian Plate is interpreted to have undergone a major phase of
          crustal extension resulting in continental separation. Progressive thermal uplift is interpreted to
        have occurred along the present-day Zagros Suture culminating in the regional ‘pre Khuff
         unconformity’. In the Late Permian, continental spreading took place as the Neo-Tethys Ocean
          started to form.

       Movement on major faults produced substantial, thickness variations within the Lower Dalan.
       Movement on these faults ceased, however, following the deposition of the base middle anhydrite.
        Major shallow water carbonate platform sedimentation subsequently prevailed across the entire
        Arabian Plate, lasting until the late Toarcian.

         Reservoir zone K1U, the topmost 23-37 m of the sequence, consists of dolomite with some minor
        mudstone and anhydrite stringers. This uppermost section is generally tight although thin stringer
        with some better reservoir properties appears.

         Reservoir zone K1M consists predominantly of grainstone, mudstone and wackestone with
         subordinate dolomite stringers. Anhydrite nodules appear in the uppermost and lowermost part of
         the zone. Reservoir zone K1M is regarded as the productive part of K1, with an average porosity
         of 13 %. The average permeability is 8 mD. The reservoir properties are best developed in well
        SP-9 and SP-11, while in SP-10 they are relatively poor.

         Reservoir zone K1L consists of dolomitized grainstone, wackestone and mudstone. Anhydrite
        appears as isolated nodules and as stringers at the base of the section. K1L has a very low
         permeability, in average less than 1 mD.


     K 2  (thickness 42-45 m)
      K2 is divided into reservoir zone K2U, K2M and K2L. The base of K2L, is interpreted to
         represent the base of the Triassic sequence. The two uppermost are the better one and almost
          identical in the three appraisal wells SP-9, SP-10 and SP-11. Reservoir zones K2U and K2M
         consist of some porous dolomite and limestone with good reservoir properties whilst the K2L
         consists of cemented limestone with very low permeability.



     DALAN FORMATION

     K 3 (thickness 119-122)
       The K3 member marks the top of the Permian sequence on South Pars. K3 is divided into three
         reservoir layers, K3U, K3M and K3L respectively.

         Reservoir zone K3U consists of porous grainstone, mainly dolomite, minor limestone with a
         gradational base into tight rock.

         Reservoir zone K3M is about 80 meters thick and consists of tight dolomite, some limestone at
         the base.

         Reservoir zone K3L consists interbedded dolomite and limestone, predominantly wackestone but
         also some packstone and grainstone. Some thin layers within K3L have very good reservoir
          properties, however tight anhydrite stringers occur at the base of K3, marking the K4 boundary.

       Khuff 4 (thickness 149-152 m)
       The K4 is divided into three reservoir layers, K4U, K4M and K4L. The two uppermost reservoir
        zones are regarded as the most productive one.

         Reservoir zone K4U consists of dolomite and some limestone and have good porosities. However
         the permeability is lower than expected because of the oomoldic texture (isolated pores).

         Reservoir zone K4M consists of oolitic limestone, predominantly grainstone but both wackestone
        and mudstone layers becomes more common closer to the base.

       The upper most part of reservoir zone K4L is defined by a 1-5 meters thick mudstone to
        wackestone, and represents a widespread flooding surface. K4L is a major unit of porous
         grainstone, interbedded with wackestone and mudstone. The lowermost part of K4L consists
        predominantly of dolomite with a high proportion of grainstone, but transitional from good to
        poor rock properties below. Cementation by anhydrite increases towards the base of K4L.




4.4   Depth Maps


        Structural depth maps
       The basis for the geological model is POGC’s depth maps, received by Statoil spring 2003. The
       maps were digitized and imported into IRAP RMS, which is the geological modelling software
        used on South Pars phases 6,7&8.

       The maps were only slightly adjusted in the three appraisal wells on phases 6, 7 & 8. The other
         well positions were correct for well SP-1 to SP-12.The structural depth maps is stored as a
         separate project in Statoil’s database.

       The K1, K2, K3 and K4 structural depth maps were checked in the well positions for all wells.
         Isochors were made for each reservoir zone (table 4.3.1)based on the observed thicknesses in the
         appraisal wells. The thicknesses are very constant over the whole field.

       The coordinate references system is: INT 24, UTM zone 39, central meridian 51-degree east.

4.5   Gas-Water-Contact (GWC)


       The gas water contact on South Pars varies over the field, it is deepest in southeast, defined by SP-
       5 (GDT at 3185 m TVD), and the most shallow are observed in SP-4 (GDT at 2783 m TVD,
     WUT 2816). This indicates a depth difference of around 400 meters from east to west over a
         distance of approximately 45 km.

5     Static Reservoir Model

5.1   Introduction

        This Chapter describes how the reservoir simulation model is constructed.

5.2   Geological input

       The reservoir simulation model is built based on the geological model with:

                 a) Top reservoir map.
                b) Layer model.
                 c)  Petrophysical parameters for all layers.
                d) Modelled GWC-map.

       The geological model contains 12 layers, as is described in Chapter 4. In the vertical direction this
           is too coarse to model dynamic movements in bottom part of the reservoir and possible water
        encroachment accurately.

       The reservoir simulation model contains 22 vertical layers as described in Table 5.4.

5.6   Adjustment of Permeability

         Permeability shown in Table 5.1 appears to be too high compared to production data from other
        phases in South Pars. The permeability has been multiplied with a factor of 0,7 to reduce
         permeability so that resulting flow data corresponds with actual flow data from other phases.

         Permeability is one of the most sensitive parameters when producing gas from South Pars. Data
          will be adjusted when production data from phases 6, 7 and 8 are available. Sensitivities by
         increasing or decreasing permeability have been performed, discussed and reported in Chapter 8.

5.7    Vertical Permeability

         Vertical permeability has been set to 0,03 * horizontal permeability in the Reference case.
          Sensitivity calculations show that this parameter is not crucial.

5.8    Vertical Flow barriers

         In addition to vertical permeability, vertical flow restrictions across main formation zones are
        modelled. In Eclipse this is modelled by multiplying the transmissibility with a factor less than 1.
         Interfaces between K1 - K2, K2 - K3 and K3 - K4 have been multiplied with a factor of 0,001.

5.9  PVT

       The simulation program Eclipse 100 is set up with black oil (oil, water and gas) where gas can be
         dissolved in oil (condensate) and oil can be dissolved in gas (condensate), including hysterics.

         In reference case, richness of gas in K1 is modelled to 32,9 Stb pr mmScf and 39,0 Stb pr mmScf
          in K2 to K4. Dew points are modelled to 330 bar in K2-K4 and 320 bar in K1.

        During production the initial GOR is approximately 4600 Sm³/Sm³, the end of plateau GOR is
       5800 Sm³/Sm³ and end of simulation GOR is 7650 Sm³/Sm³ when K1 through K4 is developed
          together.

       With a GIIP of 1909 Sm³ (67.4 T Scf), oil potential (condensate) is 408 million Sm³ (2570 mill
          bbl).

6    Dynamic Reservoir Model

6.1   Introduction

       The Eclipse model has been built as realistic as possible. In addition to the reservoir model a
         production network from the wells to the topside and further to shore through the pipeline is
        modelled.

      No injection schemes are studied.

6.2   Onshore Facility and Platform efficiencies

     A yearly total production rate from all tree platforms of 2700 million Scf per calendar day (76,5
         mill Sm³/cd) is set. This is based on a stream day rate of 3000 million Scf and expectation of 90 %
        uptime to compensate for planned or unplanned downtime of land facility, platforms and
          pipelines.

6.3   Well Efficiency

         In addition to onshore facility, pipeline and platform capacities, wells will be shut down for
        maintenance, testing or other services. A common approach is that, on average, the platform must
        be able to reach the production target with one well out of service. For example with 10 wells on a
         platform, 9 wells should be able to meet the production target.

        Well efficiencies are, in Reference case, set to 90 %.

         All flow calculations, from reservoir, via pipes to land facility, are correctly modelled based on a
          field rate of 3000 million Scf/d and assuming that 10 % of the well capacity is unavailable.


6.4   Modelling of pressure drop in wells and pipelines

         Results from simulations clearly prove that tubing length and size plays an important role. Efforts
        have been made to simulate with correct tubing, including length and diameter. A software
        program named PROSPER has been used to generate input to Eclipse (*.VFP files).
      Many different files have been created and used for short, medium and long wells with various
         tubing sizes.

8.3.1    Vertical communication between K1 and K2

           If the vertical barrier between K1 and K2 is more “tight” than expected, more resources are lost,
         but visa-versa if less “tight”. Then the gas from K1 will be produced anyway if K1 is not opened.

         Reference-case has an expectation of 0,001 of reduced transmissibility between K1 and K2.
         Especially if K1 is not developed, transmissibility between K1 and K2 is of vital importance for
          field performance.

8.4.6    Effects of Well Capacities

      Maximum gas handling capacities pr platform is 1200 mill Scf/sd. MDP-case therefore has 10
        wells and 120 million Sm³/sd as max capacity, or visa versa.

        Should number of wells be less than 10, well capacity needs to be increased, as follows:

                    -   For a 3*8 well scenario, a well capacity of 150 mill Scf/sd is needed
                    -   For a 3*9 well scenario, a well capacity of 134 mill Scf/sd is needed
                    -   For a 3*10 well scenario, a well capacity of 120 mill Scf/sd is enough

           If drilling is stopped after 3*9 wells, and well capacity are not adjusted, it would influence gas
        production plateau and all other production parameters. This is because 9 wells *120 mill Scf/sd,
        reduced under assumption that 10 % of well potential is unavailable, cannot take total production
         to 3000 mill Scf/sd. (3*9*120*0,9 = 2 916).

9.4   Multi branch wells

          Statoil is looking into the possibility to drill the last two or three wells per platform as multi
        branch wells. This would be with two well bores through the reservoir per well.

        This will meet the MDP request of 10 drainage points in the reservoir per phase. In this case only
       8 wells will be brought to the surface but 2 or 3 wells will have two draining points in the
          reservoir.

         Simulations have been carried out with the basis of 3*8 wells drilled to a horizontal length of
       2530 m at TD. Three (or two) of these wells on each phase have been replaced with multi
        branched wells.

        Every multi branched well enter the top reservoir (K1) 1500 meter horizontal length from
         platform. Then the A-track is drilled to TD turning slightly to the right at 42° inclination. The B-
         track is sidetracked from top K1 and turned to the left, and drilled to same TD and same
          inclination.
