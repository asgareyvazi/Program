# TASK AND RESPONSABILITIES OF THE DRILLING FLUID ENGINEER
It is the on-duty mud engineer’s job to report, every morning, on the events that occurred the previous day, by presenting a summary of activities, the chemical and physical mud specifications, consumption, and costs.
Each day the mud engineer prepares a short technical report (Drilling Daily Mud Report, or DDMR, in electronic format), which contains mud data, the volumes involved, consumption and costs; and forwards it to the head office via fax or email. Mud analyses are performed at least once a day, and three times a day at most, depending on rig-site operations; for instance, if drilling operations have been going on for 24 hours without interruption, there will be three analyses. In addition to this, the technician records these data in the Interval Recap and Final Recap drafts. In case mud pre-packaged by the customer is received, the mud engineer provides for identifying its rheological and chemical-physical specifications, and adds the necessary corrections if any. To this end, a “Received Mud Analysis” Form should be completed (AFR Form).
Also, the mud engineer provides for the drafting of a Final Recap (bimonthly) and/or an Interval Recap, mentioning all issues that have arisen, the remedies adopted and the rationales for discrepancies vis-à-vis the initial program.
The mud engineer manages and is in charge of the rig-site warehouse; providing detailed input on incoming and outgoing product operations (downloads from DDMR or re-entries) to the Inventory List (MMG-2/1 Form), which is forwarded to the main office in Rome daily, by fax or e-mail. Each section of the form is completed, except the “Product Code”, and “Stowage Area” columns.
The technician is also in charge of the operation and management of VIRA equipment present (Centrifuges, Silos), reporting to the Main Office on extraordinary maintenance operations and/or equipment failures if any, and requiring intervention by expert technicians (completing the Maintenance and Work (Man/Lav) Form). With regard to Centrifuges, the technician compiles, every day, the Daily Centrifuge Report, and forwards it to the main office.
At the end of the shift, the outgoing technician provides for the preparation of the Handover Form, for handing the responsibilities over to the incoming
technician, informing the latter on the real and current situation of activities at the rig-site. If it is ever necessary that two mud engineers (senior and junior) be present at the rigsite, the Handover Form is completed only by the senior mud engineer, whereas the junior engineer just goes through it, and takes note of the handover details.
The mud engineer is responsible of drafting the Monthly Performance and Consumption Ticket (Form Ticket), approved by the customer’s representative.
At the end of the shift, the mud engineer provides for forwarding documents to the office (or sending by certified mail) regarding the service during the period he/she has been in charge of activities, and namely: all DDMRs or equivalent forms specifically required by the customer, the monthly performance and consumption tickets, the end-shift and/or the end-stage reports, the warehouse operations, the accompanying documents and the equipment maintenance and treatment sheets, if there are any at the site.
It is the Tech & Logistics Dept.’s responsibility to collect and record the day's events on the Drilling Daily Mud Report Form, with special regard to technical and economical specifications. In case the contract requires consignment or consumption supplies, the person in charge will provide for reporting consumption and operability in the Monthly Ticket.
It is also the Tech & Logistics Dept.’s responsibility to support the rig-site logistically, by providing for the resupply with correctives and other needs, and making sure that expert technicians are present at the site if necessary (interventions on equipment and machinery).
With regard to restocking order for additives, these are decided according to the program function established, and the needs that might have arisen. After communicating with the office in Rome, the mud engineer provides for notifying the site customer’s representatives, whereas the in-house personnel coordinates, with customer’s personnel, the procurement and transportation of material. If it is not the customer’s responsibility to provide for transportation, the base technicians take care of setting it up, by contacting the approved carriers in a timely manner, and according to what is set forth in the program established with the rigsite.
The Tech & Logistics Dept. also provides for rig-site inspections according to the circumstances, in order to assess the operating situations, the maintenance status of VIRA equipment, the observance of safety regulations and the management of the warehouse. A specific report on the inspection is drafted, and a copy forwarded to the Quality System Manager.
The Tech & Logistics Dept. provides for collecting all documents sent from or present at the rig-site, including the mud program, in an appropriate drilling file, as well as for collecting data and preparing the conclusive draft of the final report regarding the drilling in question.
COMPLETION FLUID SECTION
The completion fluid for this section will be fresh water treated with a scavenger, inhibitor and biocide.
Solids-Free CaCl₂ Fluid Systems
This fluid is recommended to make from fresh water or is solutions made with water and the addition of one or more electrolytes. These fluids do not contain either suspended particles or viscosifiers, which mean neither filtration control nor viscosity control, are provided. Some Brine is corrosive and when corrosion inhibitors are required, compatibility studies should be performed. Some brine is toxic and precautions should be taken to prevent contact with unprotected skin. Brine solutions can be prepared to give good inhibition of clays or shales in producing formations. In addition, common contaminants do not affect the brines.
Salt solutions were formerly considered too corrosive to be used as work-over or completion fluids or as packer fluids. The corrosion problem can be solved by adjusting the pH of the solution to above 9 (min) to 10; however, pH measurements may be less important than measuring and controlling the p-alkalinity of the filtrate; this value should be kept above 1 cc. Lime be used for pH control.
Adjustment of pH, or alkalinity, does not necessarily ensure the solution to be corrosion resistant. It may be necessary to use a corrosion inhibitor to achieve lower corrosion rates. In some cases, an oxygen scavenger might be used in conjunction with a filming amine inhibitor.
Corrosion Control
Corrosion control in completion fluids is important to protect tubular goods and equipment. Corrosion can be limited by controlling the fluid pH at the neutral or alkaline level, and   treating   out   dissolved oxygen.  In   severe   environments   such   as   high temperature or contamination with acid gases, the addition of filming amines can limit corrosion to acceptable levels.
The completion fluid for this section will be Local water treated with a scavenger, and inhibitor.
Pit management will be a key in the successful completion of this interval and sufficient time for planning and preparation should be allowed.
All valves should be checked for leakage beforehand and defects identified to be reported and rectified by drilling contractor.
The mud pits which are to be used to mix the completion brine, should emptied and cleaned adequately and associated lines flushed with water
The rig pumps are to displace all clean up pills as well as the CaCl₂ at intended weight. Prepare spacers and wash pills as per program in the tanks. Ensure that sufficient spacer is prepared to account for any dead tanks volume.
Prepare all inhibited CaCl₂ before starting the displacement process. After preparing the brine should be filtered through filtration unit and store in a clean pit before displacement.
Filtration Process:
Filtration provides clean fluids that will prevent damage to the formation, the reservoir and its permeability. It does this by removal of solids and particulates from the fluid that may otherwise block the open space within the rock formation containing the hydrocarbon fluid within the production zone.
Any solids and contaminates in the completion fluid will reduce its effectiveness. An efficiently filtered and completely solid free completion fluid will add value to both the productivity and the dependability of the well in the long term.
VIRA dual pod filtration cartridge unit is recommended for filtering brines and produced water in completion, stimulation, and workover operations. The pods are connected by a manifold, providing staged filtration capability. Staged filtration utilizes cartridge filters with different ratings to maximize filtration efficiency. Cartridge filtration is designed for use with fluids containing a low percent by volume of suspended solids. Cartridge filtration is not economic when fluids are contaminated with oil or other substances.
Total Solids can be measured at the wellsite using a turbidity meter. Solids that are not added to the system to enhance the performance of a brine are considered contamination. Contaminations include formation clays, precipitates, and polymer residues, among other things. These contaminations can be filtered at the well site using diatomaceous earth, a plate and frame press, and Five-micron to ten absolute cartridges.
Note: a clear completion fluid should not be sent to the wellsite with an NTU (Nephelometric Turbidity Unit) greater than 30 or a suspended-solid Concentration greater than 30 ppm and for fluid returning from the well NTU should be less than 150.
Displacement Process:
It is recommended to circulate and condition the mud prior to displacement and to spot the open hole section to conditioned mud or a solids-free fluid loss pill. Circulate and condition the mud as needed prior to pumping the clean out pills to optimum displacement properties).

Displacement Procedure
Pre-Displacement Steps
1. Run bit and dual or tandem scrapers.
2. Circulate and condition mud. Reduce funnel viscosity as much as possible while maintaining adequate rheological properties to clean hole and prevent settling of solids. Maintain critical annular velocity to assure that liner tops are thoroughly cleaned prior to displacement.
3. Rig up equipment for displacement of completion fluid. All tanks, circulating lines, and pumps to be used in the displacement should be cleared of residual mud.
4. Insure sufficient space is available to store displaced mud.
