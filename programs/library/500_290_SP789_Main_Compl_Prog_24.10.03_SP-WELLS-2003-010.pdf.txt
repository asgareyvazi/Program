1      Introduction


1.1     General Information

       The "Main Completion Program" (MCP) covers the field specific topics for the well
        completion and clean-up operations of the South Pars (SP) phases 6,7&8 wells.

       The MCP gives an overview of the subjects related to the well completion including the
        running of the pre-drilled liner, and the clean-up of the wells. The back-up completion solution
           is to run and cement a 7” liner prior to perforation and possibly azidicing. Details regarding
         selected equipment and special procedures will be found in the Contractors Operational
        Procedures and/or in the individual Well Completion/Intervention programs. The MCP is
         positioned between the controlling documents and the single well program. Individual Well
        Completion/Intervention programs will in case of conflicts have priority over the MCP.

       The MCP is a Technical Description (TD) in the Statoil Governing Document hierarchy.
        However, any dispensations shall be verified by main office Drilling & Completion
        Department and endorsed by the South Pars Drilling Manager.

        All well completion operations will be performed in accordance with Statoil requirements and
         regulations. TEK PE B&B has verified this MCP according to Statoil internal procedures.

        Revisions of the MCP will be updated as required.


1.2     Organization and Responsibilities

       The SP Wells Manager has the overall responsibility for the drilling, completion and clean-up
          activities in the South Pars field and reports to the Project Director. Figure 1.1 shows the South
        Pars Wells Organization.


1.2.1   Onshore

       The South Pars Wells project team is an integrated sub-surface team consisting of a
        Geology/Geophysics and Reservoir (2 G&R) group and a drilling/ completion (D&C)
        engineering and operations group. The engineering and operations team for D&C is located in
       Dubai (UAE), logistics/base at Kish Island (Persian Gulf) and 2 G&R group in Tehran (Iran)
       and Stavanger (Norway). Wells Manager is located in Tehran.

2      Field and Platform Information


2.1     Introduction

        South Pars is a gas-condensate field located in the Persian Gulf. This field is named South Pars
       on the Iranian side of the border and North Field on the Qatari side. The North Field has been
       on production for some years.

       The reservoir formation is the Triassic, Permian Khuff formation, which consists of shallow
        water carbonate sediments. The Khuff formation is divided into four main units, K1, K2, K3
       and K4. Top reservoir is at approximately 2680 m TVD MSL at the most shallow, Phase 6 .

        South Pars 6, 7 & 8 will be developed with three wellhead platforms, normally unmanned
        during the production phase. There will be 16 well slots on each platform, of which 10 are
        planned utilized initially.

      Two jack-up rigs equipped with cantilever, will be in operation to drill and complete a planned
          total of 27 production wells and to tie back three predrilled appraisal wells (SP9, SP10 and
        SP11). It should be noted that adjustments in total number of wells might take place.

      As per present plan, the first jacket installation is planned for December 2003, with the last two
         jackets installed back-to-back right after the first. The two jack-up drilling rigs will
        immediately after the jacket installation be moved in place next to the jackets.
         Drilling will continue until late 2004 when the topsides are planned to be installed. The two
        jack-up rigs will then be moved between the three phases/jackets to enable topside installations
        with minimum loss of rig time. The rest of the wells will then be drilled through the topside.
       The drilling and completion program are planned completed by the end of 2006.

       The wells are planned drilled with an inclination between 20-60 degrees. The typical gas
        producer will be drilled to just above the gas-water-contact, which is at approximately 3130
     mTVD MSL in K4. The casing program is planned to be 26” x 18 5/8” x 13 3/8” x 9 5/8”, and
         the reservoir completed with a 7” 13% Cr non-cemented predrilled liner. The completion is
        planned to consist of a 9 5/8” production packer and 7” tubing with a sub surface safety valve.
       At present the preferred completion design is commingling of all for reservoir zones, K1, K2,
      K3 and K4 by running a predrilled uncemented 7” liner across the reservoir section. This is
        expected to provide sufficient productivity and optimized recovery. Perforation and acid
         stimulation can be applied in case of unsatisfactory productivity. After completion the wells
         will be cleaned up through the x-mas tree and a well testing package on the jack-ups. The wells
         will then be shut in until production start up.

3     Reservoir and Geology


3.1     Regional Geology

       The field is located on the Qatar-South Pars Arch, which is a north-south elongated, regional
          flexure, considered to be the recent expression of movement on a major basement suture. The
          field is shared by Iran and Qatar (where it is known as the North field). Gas in the field is
          relatively sour and contains ca. 0.5% (5,000 ppm) hydrogen sulphide.


       The eastward closure of the anticline cannot currently be defined on structure maps and, from
         the initial appraisal results,  it appears that the field has a slightly tilted gas-water contact,
        which is deepest towards north-east. The gas leg is believed underlain by an active aquifer,
        which provides a natural water drive. This water drive seems to be strongest in the west. This
        western bias to aquifer activity may explain the observed variation in fluid contact across the
           field.

       The primary gas producing reservoir units in the field are contained within the late Permian
        and early Triassic Khuff formation. The reservoir sequence comprises limestones, dolomites
        and anhydrite, which are characterised by poor to moderate (commonly 5-20 %) interparticle
          porosity. The development of effective reservoir layers is mainly the result of development of
        secondary porosity by dolomitisation.

       The productive sequence comprises four primary reservoir layers, Khuff-1 to 4. Gross
         reservoir thickness is between 400-500m.

     K1  (thickness 107-115 m)
       The top K1 is picked below a high gamma ray spike. The topmost 23-37 m of the sequence

          tight although thin stringers with some better reservoir properties appears. Reservoir zone 2 or

        subordinate dolomite stringers. Anhydrite nodules appear in the uppermost and lowermost part
        of the zone. Reservoir zone 2 is regarded as the productive part of K1.

        This second reservoir interval, marking the base of the Triassic sequence, displays a
         characteristic low flat gamma ray profile as well as a rather erratic sonic response. K2 is
        divided into three reservoir zones (4,5 and 6) where the two uppermost are the better ones.
        Reservoir zones four and five consist of porous dolomite and limestone with good reservoir
         properties whilst the lowermost part of K2 consists of cemented limestone with very low
         permeability.

     K3 (thickness 119-122)

3.3     Reservoir & Production Strategy

       The production strategy for South Pars phases 6,7 and 8 is to commingle all the four reservoir
        zones, K1, K2, K3 and K4 to produce and thereby deplete the reservoir pressure. Initial
        production from K1 was not in the original plan (MDP issued by POGC), but reservoir studies
        have shown that the current revised strategy will increase the reserves as well as the recovery.
         In addition, completion of the wells will be easier.

       The production wells will be drilled in a circular pattern around each of the three platforms
        with a maximum deviation of up to 60O. The platform will be designed to produce 1,2 billion
         scf/d from 10 wells with a planned production plateau at 1.0 billion scf/d. The planned
        production is 100 MMscf/d from each well in average. The phases 6,7 and 8 are expected to
        maintain the production plateau for approximately 20 years.

      When the field development phase is finalized, National Iranian Gas Company will take over
         the production operations.

       The number of wells on each platform can be limited to eight or nine if the wells prove
         sufficient productivity.

       The wells will produce without cutting formation water since the wells will be terminated 40 to
       60 meters above the gas-water-contact. However, a small fraction of water will condense in the
        well stream due to pressure and temperature loss. The platforms are equipped with a Free-
       Water-Knockout-Drum designed to operate at 126 bar. Each platform supplies the gas
        including condensate through a 32” dedicated pipeline to the onshore gas processing facility.

          Initial well-testing will be performed in conjunction with the clean up flow to the jack-up rig
          after the completion operation. The platforms will have permanent test separators for
         periodically well testing. No downhole instrumentation is planned for.

5.6     Well Backflowing

        Immediately after the installation of the upper completion the well will be cleaned-up by
        flowing it to the test equipment on the rig. The wells will be “kick started” to the maximum
         rate limited by the handling capacity of the testing equipment and specifications given by the
          rig. The purpose of this "kick start" is to maximise the clean up effect by generating maximum
       draw down instantly.

         Kick-starting a well means having 1) a near incompressible fluid in wellbore, 2) sufficient
        underbalance and 3) a non-burning fluid at top of wellbore. All these three conditions must be
         available prior to opening a well.

      When choke/valves are suddenly opened on surface and pressure completely brought down to
         zero, the entire pressure drop is transferred down the wellbore and the entire formation will
         "see" same pressure drop. This is because fluid in wellbore is incompressible or near
         incompressible (pressure wave travels with speed of sound which is much higher for non
        hydrocarbon liquids). Because fluid at top of well is non-burning (Nitrogen, water or similar),
             it can be routed straight to burner with no obstruction or reduction in flowrate. Hence, well
         pressure can be maintained at minimum to keep maximum underbalance for first few seconds
        and minutes of flow.

      By doing so, the entire exposed formation will, by experience, release its mud, mud filtrate or
        mud-debris from pore systems adjacent to wellbore. Therefore the different quality from
         varying layers will have less opportunity to make an uneven inflow causing less productive
        zones to remain uncleaned or non productive.

       From experience, by doing above, the entire or near the entire hole volume of mud will be
        dragged out of well before hydrocarbon is produced.

           Initial flow both in reservoir and at surface will be extremely high if sufficient underbalance is
         achieved. However, as the light fluid is produced out of hole, and mud is flown into vertical

        Supplied by: Baker Oil Tools
       The Flapper Lock Open Tool (FLO) is a wireline run tool which is used to permanently lock
       open the TRSCSSV. The FLO tool is used in the event of DHSV malfunction. After the DHSV
        has been permanently locked open, it may accept a Separation Sleeve or a Wireline Retrievable
         Insert Safety Valve provided communication of the fluid in the interior of the valve has been
        accomplished.

      Two wireline runs are required to activate the FLO tool. On the first run the FLO is landed and
        locked into the nipple lock profile of the DHSV. Withdrawal of the Wireline Running Tool,
       which is attached to the Slide Assembly of the tool, allows a Lock Ring of spring steel to be
         released into the Flapper Cavity of the DHSV, permanently holding the flapper in the open
         position.

     A second run retrieves the FLO tool from the locked open TRSCSSV. The tool is designed
        such that the Lock Ring cannot be released into the flapper section of the TRSCSSV if the
       Flow Tube is not in the fully closed position.

8      Fluid


8.1     Introduction

        Fluid operations under a normal well completion are related to well cleaning, displacement to
        packer fluid and conditioning of the 8 ½” reservoir section drilling fluid.


8.2    Temporary Abandonment

       The SP wells will most likely be temporary abandoned after having drilled the 12 1/4" section
       and set the 10 3/4" x 9 5/8" casing. The wells will be displaced to seawater, inflow-tested and
        plugged back with a shallow set plug.

       The cemented and tested casing together with the shallow plug will serve as barriers during
       TA.


8.3      Drilling Fluid

       The 8 1/2" reservoir section will be drilled with a water based non-damaging KCL drill-in fluid
         or a Sodium Chloride based Flo-pro system in order to minimize the formation damage. Final
        decision will be made after testing both mud types on core samples.


8.4     Well Cleaning

       The drilling mud used in the reservoir section is Water Based Mud (WBM). In order to reduce
         the amount of slop/waste it is recommended to pump the long way (down string - up annulus)
        during well cleaning/displacement.

       Well returns, during well cleaning, prior to running tubing, must have stabilized and preferably
        reach a cleanliness less then 100 NTU turbidity.


8.5     Packer Fluid

        After well cleaning the well is displaced to packer fluid. The packer fluid will be inhibited sea
         water.

         In operations where packer fluid can be lost to the formation it is important that the actual
        packer fluid has a low potential for formation damage. All fluids/brines that can be exposed to
         the formation shall therefore be qualified through tests for formation damage potential.

9     Well Clean-up and Production Test Equipment


9.1     Introduction

        This section gives a brief outline of the well clean up and production test equipment to be used
        onboard the rig.


9.2     Well Testing / Clean-up of Wells

        Special considerations shall be taken when planning the well clean up and well production
         testing programs. A general requirement for the South Pars development is that a pre-test
        meeting is to be scheduled to discuss the proper routing and handling of well fluid interface /
         slop that comes in return during the clean-up phase. Note that slop tank is available.

         In the well completion period, the surface well testing equipment will be used when the well is
        flowed to surface to collect produced hydrocarbons. The general routing of the well fluids
        during the clean-up phase will be through the x-mas tree to the testing choke manifold, heater,
         separator and directly to the burner boom. Alternatively a Multi Phase meter will be used.
        Eliminating the need for heater and separator. When the well interfaces that do not burn are at
         surface, the routing of these fluids will be through the testing choke manifold, separator, stock
         tank, transfer over to slop tanks or through the rig system. At this point all slop can be treated
       on the rig and dumped overboard if the oil content is less than 40 ppm.
