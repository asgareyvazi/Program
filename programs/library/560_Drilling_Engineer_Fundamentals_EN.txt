## 1. SEDIMENTARY LAYERS OF THE EARTH

- The earth's surface (land and sea) is made of sedimentary layers from
  ~5 km to ~20 km depth; deeper layers are older.
- Layers are named with general international names (Precambrian,
  Cambrian, Oligocene, Cretaceous, etc.) and locally with native names.

## 2. IMPERMEABLE AND PERMEABLE LAYERS

- Permeable layers: allow fluid movement (porosity & permeability).
- Impermeable layers: act as seals (e.g. marl, anhydrite, salt beds).

## 3. POROSITY & PERMEABILITY

- Porosity: void space in rock; Permeability: ability to transmit fluids.
- Layers containing salt water, oil and gas are trapped between
  impermeable seals.

## 4. FORMATIONS WITH DIFFERENT PRESSURES

- Normal, abnormal (over-pressured) and sub-normal pressure regimes.
- Hydrostatic pressure of a fluid column: P = 0.052 × MW × Depth (psi).

## 5. BALANCING AND OVERBALANCING FORMATION PRESSURE

- Balanced drilling: mud hydrostatic ≈ formation pressure.
- Overbalance: mud hydrostatic > formation pressure (safe margin).
- Underbalance: risk of influx / kick / blowout.

## 6. ISOLATING DIFFERENT PRESSURE LAYERS

- By running casing and liner strings and cementing them in place.

## 7. CASING HEAD HOUSING & WELLHEAD

- Wellhead system: casing head housing, casing spools, hangers, BOP
  stack, X-mas tree.

## 8. CEMENTING BEHIND CASING

- Primary cementing, types of cement slurries, additives, displacement.

## 9. CASING SELECTION — GRADE & WEIGHT

- Grade defines yield strength (e.g. V-150 = 150,000 psi yield).
- Wall thickness varies with weight per foot (e.g. 9-5/8" N-80 43.5 lb/ft
  vs 58.4 lb/ft).
- Wall thickness can be derived from weight-per-foot and OD.

## 10. DRILLING PARAMETERS — RPM, WOB, PUMP RATE

- Rotating speed (RPM), weight on bit (WOB), pump rate (SPM/GPM).

## 11. PUMP OUTPUT & CIRCULATION CALCULATIONS

- Strokes per pump, GPM, lag time of cuttings to the shaker.
- Mud volume in square tanks; hole volume (pipe & annulus).
- Mud weight adjustment by adding additives (and vice versa).

## 12. PRESSURE & TEMPERATURE GRADIENTS

- Pressure gradient and temperature concepts; downhole conditions.

## 13. TORQUE CALCULATIONS

- Torque of a string rotating free, milling, drilling in vertical wells.
- Torque in deviated wells (higher drag/friction).

## 14. LOSS, FLOW AND BLOWOUT

- Lost circulation (loss), flow (influx) and blowout — the primary
  warning signs and the importance of early detection.

## 15. FUNCTIONS OF DRILLING FLUID (MUD)

1. Transport cuttings out of the hole (suspension when circulating stops).
2. Control formation pressures (hydrostatic column).
3. Form a thin impermeable filter cake on permeable walls (fluid loss).
4. Support the wellbore wall and prevent collapse.
5. Control formation fluids and prevent flow into the well (blowout).
6. Keep drilled solids suspended.
7. Support part of the string weight (buoyancy).
8. Carry geological information to surface via cuttings.
9. Control corrosion of drill string in contact with the well.
10. Assist drilling by jetting through the bit nozzles.
