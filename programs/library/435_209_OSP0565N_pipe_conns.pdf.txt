EQUIPMENT


   RL-4S AND RL-4C THREADED CONNECTORS

TECHNICAL DESCRIPTION


   Vetco Gray’s RL-4S and RL-4C threaded connectors greatly reduce rig time to run
   casing strings by providing quick stabbing and make up. The RL-4S is available in 16",
   18-5/8", 20", 22”, 24" & 26". The RL-4C is available in 16", 18-5/8" and 20".

   RL-4S & RL-4C connectors feature a four-start thread, which provides for full make up
   with between one quarter and one half turn of the suspended joint. An elevator shoulder
    is provided for standard “box up” running. The connectors can also be run “pin up”
   depending on handling equipment used. A positive full make up indication is provided
   on both models. While the RL-4S and RL-4C are similar in geometry they are not
   interchangeable and therefore cannot be made up to each other.

   Primary locking of the connectors is established by the thread form which is self locking.
  An optional, integral, cut-to-fit secondary locking feature is available on the RL-4S only.
   The RL-4S uses a four slot style secondary lock feature in the pin and box sections;
   while the older RL-4S used a grooved section that runs around the entire circumference
    of the box and slots in the pin. The secondary lock should be used for driving and
   long-term tieback applications and may be used on the float shoe and collar joints, in
   place of thread lock compounds. In addition tabs should also be used for high current
   and loading applications.

ROUTINE OPERATION


                   PRE-OPERATIONAL CHECKS

    1.  Remove protectors. Clean both pin and box members thoroughly.

    2.   Inspect the threads and metal shoulder surfaces. Any joints with damaged
        connectors should be clearly marked and stored separately, for later inspection
       and repair.

    3.   Inspect the installation and replace any damaged or improperly installed o-ring
         seals.


                             NOTE:


  If inspection is made on the pipe racks, do not move/handle pipe before reinstalling
 protectors.


    4.   Lubricate both pin and box with recommended lubricants as specified above.

                    RUNNING PROCEDURE


                            NOTE:


Record joint part number and serial number as string is being picked up and run for future
reference.


   1.   Pick up a joint with casing elevators or handling tool, stabilize over previous joint
      and remove the pin  protector. Carefully stab  into preceding  joint so the
        anti-rotation slots on the pin are positioned midway between the anti-rotation slots
         is aligned with the one on the stab zone on the box. (See drawing 3406)

      1A For dual seal connectors, the pressure relief plug must be removed prior to
           stabbing and re-installed after the connector is made up.

   2.   Slack off all the weight on the joint before rotating. The stand off between the
       top of the box and the indicator shoulder on the pin should be ¼".

   3.   Apply tongs to the pipe directly above the connection, not the box or pin, and rotate
        slowly. The joint should rotate approximately 135o. Worst case stabbing alignment
       could result in connectors having to rotate as much as 180o to make up. Low
       torque is experienced until the last few degrees of rotation. At this point the torque
          will rapidly increase to torque as per the table in the back of this procedure. Refer to
         this table for maximum and minimum torque values. It is a good practice to verify
       the torque by setting the tongs at 90o to the line pull.

              SECONDARY ANTI-ROTATION DEVICE

   Primary locking of the connectors is established by the thread form which is designed to
   be self locking. Two distinct secondary anti-rotation systems have also been provided:
   4 Slot and Continuous Groove/3 Slot. Both systems are primarily identified by the O.D.
    profile of the box connection.


                            CAUTION:


  Pin connectors with 4 slots are interchangeable with the box connectors with a
 continuous groove. Box connectors with 4 slots are interchangeable with the pin
 connectors with 3 slots but the secondary lock cannot be activated.





                            CAUTION:


 When the RL-4S connector is put in applications where connectors are likely to be
 exposed to high cyclical loading, vibration or torque in the opposite direction of
 make up, then it is recommended that tabs be set. These applications include but
 not limited to: tieback strings, driven strings, float shoe and collar joints and
 strings which are likely to be run in areas where high currents are anticipated.


4 SLOT SYS TEM (INTRODUCED MID 1990)

   The RL-4S connector is supplied with 4 slots in both the pin and box (same as RL-4
   system). Each lock tab (secondary lock) provides approximately 400 ft/lbs resistive
   torque per inch of nominal pipe diameter. This value is over and above the resistance
   value provided by the torque applied to the connector. On a 20" connector this will give
   8000 ft-lbs of torque resistance per engaged anti-rotation tab, 32,000 ft-lbs if all 4 tabs
   were to be actuated.

   The locking tool is cartage actuated and is available in kit form. There are two types of
   adapters for the tool to shear and depress the tab into the pin slot. (These are the same
   as the RL-4 system.)

Hilti Adapter

     If anti-rotation tabs (secondary locks) are to be set, place the cocked locking tool into
   position with the narrow mandrel (D) inserted through the box window and into the
   square slot on the pin connector (E). The broad mandrel will rest on the box/pin indicator
   shoulder.

   For a right-hand connector, move the tool to the left until the narrow mandrel touches
   the side of the pin slot. Press the tool firmly and squarely against the connector, depress
   the safety latch and pull the actuator.





  Remove the tool and check the results. One end of the tab should have sheared and be
    firmly seated in the pin slot (F). If it is not, re-cock the tool and perform the locking
   procedure again to fully seat the sheared end of the tab. Re-cock the tool and set
    additional tabs as required.

   For a left-hand connector the procedure above is the same except the tool is moved to
   the right until the narrow mandrel touches the side of the pin slot.

   The above procedure applies to a box up running plan. If the connector is run pin up,
   then the right-hand tool is moved to the right, and the left-hand tool is moved to the left.

   The correct lock can always be double checked by imagining the pin being
   unscrewed from the box and checking that the tab is preventing this movement by
   acting on the pin slot side.

Hammer Activated Shear Tool

  A hammer activated shear tool can be used instead of the Hilti tool. This tool performs
   the same task as the Hilti tool but a sledge hammer is used to hit the shear tool and
   shear the anti-rotation tabs. The part numbers for this tool can be one of the following:
   H50115, A30175-3, A30175-4, R30015-1, R30060-1 or R30315-1.

CONTINUOUS GROOVE / 3 SLOT SYSTEM


    Prior to 1990 the secondary locking system comprised of a single locking groove on the
   box and the slots in the pin. Pin connectors with 4 anti-rotation slots are compatible with
   the continuous groove, but if a pin with three slots is made up to a box with 4 shear tabs
   the tabs and slots will not line up and can not be used.

   Where the optional secondary lock is to be used, the actuation tool with the correct
   shear head RL-4S (RH) or RL-4S (LH) should be used. The standard tool is a portable,
   hand held, powder charge tool P/N 30133.

   The part numbers for the tools and shear heads are as follows. For further information,

   Department.

  On pin connectors manufactured before March 1988 featuring the double shoulder, the
    tool is placed on the connector so that the long blade on the face of the cutting head sits
    in the pin slot and the cutting blade sits in the grooved portion of the box.

   The blade on the cutting head is narrower than the slot; therefore, it is important that
   the cutting head be pressed against the correct side of the slot as shown in

RECOVERING


     If it is desired to recover the string by disconnecting at the mudline, a LH Connector
   should be used at the mudline. This will prevent back off at the wrong position in the
    string during recovery.

    1.   Support the surface string weight on the hook. Rotate the string to disengage the
        connector.

    2.   Pick up on the string as soon as the threads disengage. Continue lifting until the
         following joint can be suspended at the rotary.

    3.   Using a pry bar, pry the locking tabs outward on each joint until they clear of the slot
       on the pin.

    4.   Using casing tongs, break the connection, install pin protector and move the
        recovered joint to the pipe rack. Repeat for remaining joints.

    5.   Repeat steps 3 and 4 for the remaining joints.

POST- OPERATIONAL MAINTENANCE


   Post-operational joint maintenance is necessary only if the tool joints are recovered
    after use.

    1.  Wash the pin and box members with fresh water.

    2.   Clean the threads and shoulder area of both pin and box, and inspect for damage.
       Any joints with damaged connectors should be clearly marked and set aside.

    3.   Inspect and replace any damaged O-ring seals.

    4.   Lubricate the threads and install thread protectors.

    5.   Service shear tab tools (Hilti) as required.
