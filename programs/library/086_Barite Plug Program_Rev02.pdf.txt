   Barite Plug
Barite intentionally settled from barite-water slurry spotted in a drilling well is called a “barite plug”.

It is used mainly as a temporary plug to control gas kicks. A key feature of the barite plug is that any

weaker zones uphole are not exposed to increased hydrostatic pressure because the top of the pill is

generally below the weak zone.

   Application
Barite Plug could control downhole flow by: a) high density barite gravity settling (it is the main

method) or setteling by dehydration from filteration into a hard plug and b) providing required

bottomhole pressure to shut off  high pressure area while other low pressure zones tolerate an

appropriate pressure.

  Water Base Barite Plug Formulation & Considerations
Measure the water for plug volume taking into account line volumes and dead space of the mud tank
into tank.

Note 1: Use Fresh water instead of sea water or salt water for rapid settling.
Note 2: Minimum 50 m Barite plug length is recommended.
Note 3: Mixing lines, gun lines, stand pipes, hoses, etc which will be utilized during pumping should
be emptied and cleaned adequately. 1-2 bbl fresh water is recommended as spacer. Barite plugs will
not settle if drilling fluid contaminates the pill.

Note 4: Add 8 lb/bbl SPERSEEN CFL in Water

Note 5: Adjust pH to 9.5-10 by useing 1-2 lb/bbl CAUSTIC SODA

Note 6: Barite plug should be mixed and pump rapidly.
Note 7: It is highly recommended to be mixed using cement unit rather than tanks.

Note 8: Pilot tests of the slurry should be performed prior to running the plug. (The barite in the
slurry should settle at the rate of 50% volume in 10 minutes)

Note 9: Dead volume should be calculated for final volume preparation.
Note 10: The viscosity and yield point of the barite slurry must be kept low if the barite is to settle
and form a plug.
Note 11: The mixing and pumping of the barite plug must not be stopped once the operation has
begun. The drillstring can easily become plugged, greatly complicating operations.
Note 12: Use mud not water to chase the barite plug to prevent premature settling
Note 13: Once pumping begins, the slurry must be spotted and pipe pulled without delay to prevent
the pipe from becoming plugged or stuck.

Displacment Procedure:
a)Before starting to mix the plug, all connections should be pressure tested to 5000 psi from the
 mixing pump to the TDS valve.
b)Because it is necessary to keep the barite plug moving at all times while in the drill pipes, arrange
ment must be made to ensure that the rig pump can be used for displacement in case the mixing
pump fails or the flowline plugs.
c) Check the valves on the fill up line to displacement tanks to ascertain that they are not leaking.
d)Before starting to mix the plug, have one displacement tank full of mud so that displacement can
begin immediately after mixing the plug.
e)Barite plugs are normally jet mixed. Use the recirculating mixer, each tank has normally 50 bbl
capcty. Check weight with the mud balance that is graduated up to 135 PCF.
f)After mixing the desired amount of plug, begin displacing immediately using the cementing pump
 and displacement tanks.
g)The slurry should be displaced at a rate fast enough to produce a pressure at the stand pipe
 pressure gauge. Pressure loss between the mixing pump and rig floor makes the pumping pressure
inaccurate at the mixing pump. The heavier barite in the drill pipe will tend to fall, and it is desirable
to “keep up with it.
h)The operations office will decide if drill pipe is to be pulled out of plug.
i)If the decision is to pull the pipe out of the plug,
-pull as fast as possible, consistent with the amount of drag,
-rotate the pipe in the slips while standing back each stand,
-after pulling out above the settled plug, circulate out,
-wait 8-10 hours, RIH and tag plug to be certain that plug is holding.
