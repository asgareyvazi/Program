   1. Mansour Abad Oil Filed

Mansour Abad oil field is an asymmetric anticline located approximately 10km to the
Northeast of Behbahan city, in the northern part of the South-Dezful Embayment, in vicinity
of the Khuzestan Mountain Front Fault (Figure 1). This field was explored in 1976 and its
nearby fields are Khaviz, Gachsaran and Behbahan. This structure  is an elongated NW-SE
trending anticline with approximately 600m of closure at the Asmari level, and the field
dimension is measured about 3km by 21km.

     Figure 1: Structural setting of the Mansourabad Oilfield (Alizadeh et al., 2018)

   2. Geological Setting

The Mansour Abad Field is an asymmetric anticline, in which southwestern flank is steeper
than northeastern flank (Figure 2). It seems that reverse faulting played a great role in the
development of this oilfield as it is s eparated from adjacent oilfields by a system of faults.
Petroleum system of the field comprises two carbonate reservoirs (mainly limestone): Asmari
(Early Miocene) and Bangestan (including the Cenomanian-Turonian Sarvak Formation and the

Owing to coexistence of excellent source rock units, porous and permeable reservoirs,
efficient seals, and suitable structural traps, Dezful Embayment is defined as the most prolific
petroleum province (account for 8% of global oil reserves) in Iran of which hosts most of the
onshore  reservoirs of the country (Bordenave and Hegre, 2005). This embayment  is a
depression in the Zagros Fold-Thrust Belt that covers an area of 60,000 km2 in SW Iran,
bounded towards NW, SE and NE by Balarud fault zone, Kazerun fault zone, and Khuzestan
"Mountain Front", respectively (Fig. 1) (Sepehr and Cosgrove, 2004).

Based on previous researches (Berberian and King, 1981; Ameen, 1991; Alavi and Mahdavi,
1994; Berberian, 1995; Hessami et al., 2001; Homke et al., 2004; Sherkati et al., 2005; Shiroodi
et al., 2015), the main reason of the present structural setting of SW Iran is the collision of the
Persian and the Arabian plates. At the beginning of the Late Cretaceous, north-northeastward
movement of the Arabian plate started. The movements were accelerated during the Late
Miocene and Pliocene and are progressing southwestwards at the present time (Hessami,
2002; Sherkati and Letouzey, 2004).

Dezful Embayment was covered by a wide, shallow intra-cratonic sea during Phanerozoic,
while sea level fluctuation and low-amplitude subsidence variations had been changing its
condition repeatedly. From Permian to Early Miocene, the area is dominated by carbonates
consisting generally of high-energy oxygenated limestones, with breaks in sedimentation on
regional highs; low-energy argillaceous limestones and marls are present in depressions
(James and Wynd, 1965; Murris, 1980; Bordenave and Hegre, 2005). Most of the reservoirs in
the area are carbonates for which secondary processes including fracturing and dolomitization
play a significant role  in their petrophysical  behavior. Organic-rich source rocks were
deposited during the Middle Jurassic (Sargelu Formation), Neocomian (lower part of the Garau
Formation),  Albian (Kazhdumi  Formation),  Early Cenomanian  (Ahmadi  Member/Shilaif
Formation) and Middle Eocene/Early Oligocene (Pabdeh Formation).

Asmari and Bangestan reservoirs are often connected in the Dezful Embayment because the
Pabdeh and Gurpi marls are heavily fractured in the high-relief anticlines (e.g. in Pazanan &
Gachsaran oilfields, Bordenave and Hegre, 2005). But in Mansourabad oilfield, different
maturity and bulk properties of the Asmari and Bangestan oils demonstrate that fluid and
pressure of these reservoirs are not connected, because the dense Pabdeh and Gurpi layers
act as effective static barriers (Alizadeh et al., 2018).

   3. Stratigraphy

A brief description of the expected stratigraphic units to be drilled in Mansourabad typical
vertical well is presented in the following breakdown:

Gachsaran Formation

Deposition of the Gachsaran Formation is cyclic. This formation totally consists of a repetition
of Anhydrite, grey and red marl as well as salt and some limestone beds.

Asmari Formation

Asmari formation mainly composed of limestone and dolomite. This limestone is light grey,
tan, Archie type I-A interbedded with type II /II and Wackestone, I to Packestone by Dunham.
The dolomite  is sandy, Anhydritic and sometimes highly porous and fractured crystalline
carbonate. Microfossils in the carbonates included: Borelismelocurdica, Miogypsinoides,
Compantus, Elphidum sp. Rotalia.viennoti, and Eulepidina sp.

Pabdeh Formation

Pabdeh Formation is made of gray to dark- brown Argillaceous Limestone, marl and shale with
Planktonic fauna.

   4. Asmari Reservoir

The Oligo-Miocene aged Asmari Formation, one of the world’s most prolific reservoirs, is the
most important reservoir rock of Iran that hosts more than 90% of recoverable oil of the
country (Ghazban, 2007). It consists of 314m of resistant, cream to brown-weathering, well-
jointed limestone with shelly intercalations in type section. Its contacts with the overlying
Gachsaran and underlying Pabdeh are conformable.

Oligo-Miocene sedimentation was  initially controlled by the palaeo-topography  of the
preceding Eocene Jahrum platform. Following a major eustatic sea-level drop at the close of
the Eocene (e.g. Abreu et al. 1998; Sharland et al. 2001), shallow-water sedimentation
downstepped in this basin, while the surrounding carbonate platforms were exposed. The
Asmari Formation was deposited on this first order sequence boundary (base of tectono-
stratigraphic megasequence 11 of Sharland et al. 2001).

The whole limy nature of Asmari Formation changes into evaporite (the Kalhur Member; the
lower part of the formation) in southwest Lurestan and also in southwest of Khuzestan area.
The type section of the Lower Miocene Kalhur evaporitic Member was measured on the south
flank of Kuh-e Anaran (Lurestan), and composed of three units including a basal anhydite,
marls and thin marly limestones and massive anhydite. The member conformably overlies
marls of the Pabdeh Formation and is conformably overlain by limestones of the Asmari. The
Kalhur member is a tongue within the Asmari limestone which is confined to south western
Lurestan (Figure 3).  It is believed that the formation  is Early Miocene (Aquitanian and
Burdigalian) in age where the Kalhur Member is present (Bahrami 2000; Nayebi 2003). Van
Buchem et al. (2010) introduced the proposition of four types of Asmari reservoirs in the
region:  1)  silisiclastic dominated,  2) mixed carbonate  silisiclastic, 3) mixed carbonate-
anhydrite, and 4) carbonate dominated. Their lithological, stratigraphic and geographical
characteristics are summarized in Figure 4. Whereas their basic distinction is a lithological
classification, the lateral changes and geometries that characterize them can be predicted
using the sequence stratigraphic model. According to the above mentioned regions, Mansour
Abad field is situated in region-4 (carbonate dominated).

Throughout Lurestan and Khuzestan areas, the Asmari-Gachsaran contact apparently remains
at a relatively constant time horizon. However, in the Fars Area, the Gachsaran progressively
replaces the top of the Asmari from northwest to southeast. Conversely, within the Fars Area,
the base of the Asmari Formation is generally found at a fairly constant horizon while in the
Lurestan and Khuzestan areas it varies in age.

   16.    Geological Forecasts

 As soon as a  drilling  location has been  finally approved,  a geological forecast and
 recommendation will be prepared for the location. For this purpose, all the relevant geological
 data from  all available sources will be used to produce an accurate geological forecast.
 Necessary documentation  with the geological column showing the co-ordinates  of the
 location, expected formation tops and lithology of the succession to be drilled will be
 prepared. In case of formation forecast for a deviated well, a deviation plan with co-ordinates
 of the bottom of the well and displacements in respect to the surface location in "X" and "Y"
 directions should be calculated to accompany the recommendation.

 The geological drilling program for a well will be prepared, by adding to the geological forecast,
 additional  information  in respect to petroleum  engineering and  drilling environment,
 accumulated from the previous drilling activities in the preparing of the planned well. In this
 document, data in respect to the proposed casing setting depths, formation pressures, mud
 losses and high pressure zones, and any other information from the nearby wells that could
 be used by the drilling section, should be forwarded.
