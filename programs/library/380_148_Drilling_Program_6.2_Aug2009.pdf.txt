  1 INTRODUCTION

Prospect 6.2 is located in the Southern part of the Caspian Sea, approximately 180 km
North of Nowshahr and about 209 km East of the Astara border.

The South Caspian region represents a great depression, where the sedimentary deposits
reach to 20 – 25 km thick.

Prospect 6.2 is located in the centre of the deepwater Basin. The wildcat exploration well
Prospect 6.2-X1 is the first deep water, high pressure well to be drilled by Alborz semi-
submersible rig in Caspian Sea offshore Iran.

The well location is determined based on geophysical data.

The nearest offshore drilling well (Ferssman well No. 1) is located approximately 100 km
east of the 6.2 prospect proposed well location. The only available offset data are from
Shah Deniz Field and 3 offshore drilled wells (Khazar, Meghdad, and Meysam).

Regarding the great thickness of Neogene - Quaternary Sediments in the South Caspian
Depression, the stratigraphy column defined here covers the Neogene Sediments that
uncomfortably overlies the Lower Deposits

The 6.2 prospect structure main characteristics are as follows;
Length:            22 km.
Width:               7.5 km.
Vertical Closure:    650m at SC500 level.
Areal Closure:      130 sq km at SC500 level.
Water depth:        725m.

No fishing area is existed around this prospect, but care must be taken from the HSE
standpoint

The site survey will be performed prior to drilling. More information will be collected
before the drilling operation start. The site survey has to give information about the sea
floor condition, boulders and information about reflection amplitude that can represent
shallow gas.

Limited area knowledge concerning the possibility of shallow gas has therefore made it
necessary to prepare the top hole sections as shallow gas are in place. Due to the shallow
gas uncertainties all technical action to be taken ahead of setting the BOP.
The  likely range  for seawater  surface temperatures  is between 27o C and  6o C.
Temperatures at mid water depth range from 7.3o C to 6o C and temperatures at mudline
range from 7o C to 5o C. The Expected reservoir temperature is about 110O C. Gas-hydrates
might develop within shallower section. Therefore gas-hydrates might already exist in the
formation or it can be self-induced due to the drilling operation. The drilling program is
designed to handle gas-hydrates.

The main objectives for the well are:

prognosed at 5322 m BRT.

A shallower total depth will only be considered if severe hole problems are encountered
after the predicted reservoir section is penetrated.

The predicted well pressures are extracted from the pressure studies of the seismic data
processed during the South Caspian Basin studies. The objective of the study was to
estimate  the overpressure  distribution  in  the  basin.  This,  in  fact was  realized by
transformation of the velocity field into pressure, resulted displaying overpressure studies
along several seismic sections in the South Caspian Basin. One of these lines which
intersect with the prospect 6.2 (near the actual location of well 6.2-X1) was regarded as a
basis for the preparation of the pressure prediction chart. The following results were
gained by utilizing this profile:

Pressure in the well is hydrostatic down to SC440 level, except in shallow part (about 60m
depth below sea bottom), which a mild overpressure zone due to presence of gas pocket is
expected to encounter. Onset of hard overpressures (1.44 S.G.) occurs at near SC440
level. Overpressures within SC440-SC300 interval (in the lower part of this interval) are
more uniformly distributed, possibly indicating a good connectivity within reservoir
levels.

A total of 296 days are planned to complete and test the well.

The drilling operation in the South Caspian is to be conducted with minimum permitted
discharge of drilling wastes. Thus, drilled cuttings will not be discharged to the sea, but
will be collected, transported from the well site to the supply base, treated to remove toxic
residues as necessary, and finally recycled, reused or disposed of in an environmentally
safe and appropriate manner.

3  WELL GEOLOGICAL PROGNOSIS

3-1   Regional Geological Setting

The South Caspian Region represents a great depression, where the sedimentary
deposits reach to 20 ~25km thick.
Prospect 6.2 is located in the center of the deepwater between Amoudarya (in East)
&Cora(in West) sedimentary basins.
Regarding the great thickness of Neogene  - Quaternary Sediments in the South
Caspian Depression,  the  stratigraphy column defined here covers  the Neogene
Sediments which unconformably overlies the Lower Deposits.

The following succession of the sediments that obtained from the penetration of
Fersman& Shah Deniz Field wells and 3 offshore drilled wells (Khazar, Meghdad,
and Meysam), would be similar with well 6.2-X1.
Miocene:
The Miocene Sediments are deposited on a disconformity, overlying the Paleocene
beds. The Neogene rock units start with Tarkhan and Tchokrak horizons, that are red
brown marine deposits, with 150-250m thickness and include gypsum beds and rare
foraminiferae, macro fossils are as follows:
-  Cardium sp.
-   Oyster sp.
-  Modiulus sp.

Spanidontella bed (Karaganian Stage)
This key bed is about 100m thick and comprises of green grey clay, marl, sandstone
and numerous sandy calcareous shell-beds and forms an excellent marker between the
red marl of Tchokrak beds and the Pholas beds. Age of this shell bearing bed is
Middle Miocene.

Pholas bed (Konkian Stage)
This bed is about 50m thick and comprises clay sandstone and marl containing shells
of Pholas sp. conformably overlie the Spaniodontella pulchella beds.
Age: Middle Miocene (previously late Vindobonian).

Sarmatian Stage
In Mazandaran  province,  the  Sarmatian  beds  reach 350m  thickness  and  are
characterised by the following fossils:
-  Mactra vitaliana
-  Cardium obsoletum

The  Sarmatian  in  this  region  conformably  overlies  the  Pholas  beds  and  is
unconformably underlain by the Continental Series (Cheleken Series).
Age: Most upper Middle Miocene

Note1: The Upper Oligocene to Lower Miocene deposits is well known as Maykop
Sequence (source rock- in Azerbaijan Republic.)

Pliocene (Lower)
Cheleken Series:
In the type locality (Cheleken Peninsula-South East Caspian Sea, Turkmenistan Rep.),
the Series is consist of a thick sequence of red and brown sandstone and calcareous
clays, representing sub-aquatic continental deposits.

In Iran, the Cheleken Series is recognised as a thick section of brown clays, marls,
sandstone and Conglomerates, with Miocene and Cretaceous fauna (reworked) and
Planorbis thiolleri, Limnea bouilleri.
Remark: This continental red beds are known as a reservoir rock, within the South
Caspian Basin & their adjacent onshore area.

Pliocene (Upper)
Aghchagylian Stage:
In the type locality, the name refers to limestone, marl, clay and sandstone.
In Iran, the Aghchagyl Stage is represented as siltstone, claystone, rubbly mudstone,
conglomerate, and contains following fossils:
-  Cardium dombra
-  Mactra subcaspia
Maximum thickness of Aghchagyl Stage in North Iran (onshore wells) is about 700m.
Quaternary
Apsheronian Stage (Basal Quaternary):
In the type locality (Apsheron Peninsula, Baku Archipelago), the Stage is represented
by a thick section of sand, marl and shell beds containing following fossils:
-   Cardiidae
-  Monodacna
-  Adacna
-   Dreissensia
In the eastern Iranian onshore, a thickness of about 650m for both Aghchagyl and
Apsheron Stages (in well GO-3) is reported. The thickness of Apsheron Stage in well
Khazar No.1 is about 800m.
In Dasht-e-Moghan and Alamdeh regions, Apsheron Sediments include the following
fossils:
-  Melania rhodensis
-   Corbicula fluminalis
-  Melanopsis eurystoma

Bakuvian Stage
This Stage is used originally in marine terraces in the Baku region.  It contains the
following fossils:
-  Didacna sp.
-   Planorbis eichwaldi
-  Theodoxus pallasi
-   Cobicula fluminalis
In Mazandaran and Gorgan  drilled  wells,  this Stage  is represented by a poor
consolidated marine and brackish clay and sand deposits.

•  SC800, this horizon is considered as present day seabed.

•  SC700, this regional horizon is chronostratigraphically esitmated in the Apsheron
   Stage of the Lower Pleistocene (Basal Quaternary).

Reservoirs:       None identified
Characteristics:    Clinoform

•  SC600, the Regional Horizon is chronostratigraphically esitmated in the Akchagyl
   Stage of the Upper Pliocene. The SC600 is a downlap surface and has been
    identified  as  the Maximum  Flooding  Surface (MFS)  associated  with  the
    transgression that has been developed during SC500.

Reservoirs:  Locally high stand and transgressive sands are expected to have
developed in the SC500 to SC600 interval.
Characteristics:   Maximum Flooding Surface, Down lap Surface.

•  SC500, This significant regional horizon is chronostratigraphically situated in
    Pliocene, and lithostratigraphically marks top of Surakhany, which is the Upper
   most unit of the Productive Series.

This surface is traditionally regarded as marking top of the Middle Pliocene. The
   SC500 is a sequence boundary and low order transgressive surface, and is thus
    strictly speaking not a time line, but covers the whole period of the transgression.

Reservoirs:  Shallow marine delta top sands development in the SC400 to
SC500 interval. Characteristics:   Unconformity, transgressive surface, on lap
surface, and locally erosional truncation of sediments below.

   •   SC400. This  regional horizon  is  chronostratigraphically  situated  in  the
       Pliocene, and lithostratigraphically within the Productive Series.

Reservoirs:  Sand development (shallow marine, delta top sands) in the interval
from SC400 to SC440.
Characteristics:    Base of minor low stand.

   •  SC300.  This  regional  horizon  is  chronostratigraphically  situated  in  the
       Pliocene, and lithostratigraphically within the Productive Series. The SC300 is
       believed to mark a high frequency maximum flooding surface following a
       regional highstand.

Reservoirs:  The clastic Preriva and Balakhany sands, known from fields in
Azerbaijan are situated between SC200 and SC300.
Characteristics:    High Amplitude Event in north-west.

data processed during the South Caspian Basin studies. The objective of the study was
to estimate the overpressure distribution in the basin. This, in fact was realized by
transformation of the velocity field into pressure, resulted displaying overpressure
studies along several seismic sections in the South Caspian Basin. One of these lines

Pressure in the well is hydrostatic down to SC440 level, except in shallow part (about

pocket is expected to encounter.

Onset of hard overpressures (1.44 S.G.) occurs at near SC440 level.

Overpressures within SC440-SC300 interval (in the lower part of this interval) are
more uniformly distributed, possibly indicating a good connectivity within reservoir
levels

Historically the pore pressures have under estimated in the central Caspian Sea

9 DRILLING HAZARDS


9-1   Shallow Gas

The South Caspian Sea floor is well known for its shallow gas occurrences. A specific
site survey including shallow seismic will be performed on the well location to
minimize the shallow gas  risk. Nevertheless, possible shallow gas bearing and
relatively high pressure sand lenses might be encountered.

As a risk reduction measure, a 8½” pilot hole will be drilled riserless and with MWD/
LWD in the string, in order to be able to combat any shallow gas kicks, shallow water
flows and gas hydrates. It is important to ensure that specific shallow gas well control
procedures are initiated during drilling and tripping operations while drilling pilot
hole.


9-2  Low fracture gradients

In deepwater environment, a lack of overburden compaction forces results in low
fracture pressures throughout the well. Consequently, a very low margin between pore
pressure and fracture gradient typically exists. The net effect is more complex (more
casing  strings) to corresponding depth below the mudline. As a  risk reduction
measure, additional contingency liners have been made available.


9-3  Mud Volcano

Although more than 70% of the mud volcano of the world are located in the Caspian
Sea, no such phenomena is expected to be penetrated in drilling of this well, as per
seismic interpretations.


9-4   Swelling Shale

Swelling  shale  is expected  especially  in unconsolidated  intervals of  the upper
Quarternary section. As a risk reduction measure, all hole sections below the 20”
planned setting depth shall be drilled with SBM.



9-5  H2S

No H2S risk is expected in this well. However, comprehensive H2S monitoring and
alarms system will be in place. In BP Azeri field (Central Caspian Sea) during a
production test 1500 ppm of H2S was recorded.

9-6   Faults

The shallower section, down to SC 500 appears faulted on the seismic profiles.
Special care should be taken while drilling through the faulted zones, since they may
be associated with mud losses or fluid flows.


9-7   Shallow Water Flow

The rapid depositional environment may result in over-pressured sands as is typically
seen in deep-water environments. In deepwater areas Shallow Water Flows may be
encountered from over-pressured water reservoirs, at relatively shallow depths. When
penetrated during drilling, weighted mud is required to be pumped to balance the
shallow water flow pressures. Often the maximum mud weight used is limited by the
strength of the formations above and below the water reservoir.

Although shallow water flow would not be encountered as per drilling prognosis, but
still the danger of encountering this shallow hazard cannot be discounted. As a risk
reduction measure the capability of drilling 26” hole section with  riser will be
considered, in order to be able to use heavy mud weights to balance the shallow water
zone.


9-8  Hydrates

Gas hydrates normally, but not always occur in deep water environment in the top
sediments. Hydrates will normally occur in the sediments from the seabed down to
150 – 400 meter below seabed. If the hydrates melt while drilling, it can generate an
unstable formation. It is less likely to encounter hydrates problem while drilling due
to low temperature at seabed and drilling with cold sea water.

In top hole sections where in-situ hydrates may exist, the exothermic reaction of the
cement slurry setting may melt the hydrates and release gas into cement column. As a
risk reduction measure, if hydrates are encountered while drilling, foam cements will
be used which are effective in minimizing gas influx due to the mechanism in which
they maintain pressure on the formation with the inclusion of nitrogen.

15  PLUG AND ABANDONMENT/ SUSPENSION


A plug and abandonment/ suspension program will be issued in due time based on the final status
of the well.

When the rig leaves location it is a Company policy that the well is left in a safe condition. From
a well control point of view this means leaving mechanical and hydrostatic barriers in the well
and is the same as for land operations. The well can be suspended and prepared for future re-entry
or permanently abandoned.



SUSPENSION OPERATIONS

When a well is suspended the wellhead will be left in place with a corrosion cap installed. Before
leaving the site the dimensions and exact position of the wellhead must be recorded so that
shipping can be made aware if its presence.

A corrosion cap is made to fit over the protruding wellhead e.g. 18 3/4” wellhead housing and will
either be run on a wire line with ROV assistance or may be run on drill pipe.

The final operation is to conduct a sea bed survey with the ROV to ensure no debris is left on the
sea bed.



ABANDONMENT OPERATIONS

If a well drilled is to be abandoned, the casing strings will be cut at a specified depth below sea
bed by using either mechanical casing cutting tools or by using an explosive charge. The wellhead
will be recovered with surface conductor casing.

No open formation should have communication to the sea bed after abandonment. In the event
that annulus seals are suspect remedial work should be carried out. Every effort should be made to
recover the TGB if used and the sea bed in the vicinity of the well should be inspected for any
debris that may exist as a result of the drilling operations.

16.3 Conventional Coring

The objective of coring is to obtain a formation sample for geological or reservoir evaluation,
determine permeability, porosity, composition of the rock, and to conduct flow studies. because of
the valuable information which the cores provide, the drilling objective is to furnish the maximum
core recovery at minimum operational cost.


Coring Objectives

The main coring objectives are to acquire representative cores from expected reservoirs at post-
SC500 sequence (SC 500 to SC 520), and in sandy packages of pre-SC 500  ( SC 500 to
SC350).Additional short coring due to geological condition above mentioned depth(up to 1600m
TVD ss)may be required that determined by geology Dep.


Criteria for Coring

Coring point will be selected onsite by wellsite geologist based on gas shows and interested sand
packages. It is anticipated that cores will be cut in 12¼” x 14¾” and 8½” x 9½” hole sections.

Conventional coring package will be utilized and a maximum of 100 m of total core length is
expected to be cut in different suitable depths.

Rig site core Gamma Ray will be utilized for quick core interpretation. Fiberglass inner barrels
with pressure relief valves will also be used.

As the nature of the sands at this location is unknown, the soft sediment coring package and
procedures should be utilized. This will involve rigorous core handling procedures and controlled
trip rates.

Core runs will be attempted (27m core barrel or equivalent assumed)

Standard wax and/ or gypsum injection methods for core preservation might be required.

Careful transportation and handling of cores at all stages will be important to prevent damage.

Executive Summary

Prospect 6.2 is located in the Southern part of the Caspian Sea, approximately 180 km North of
Nowshahr and about 209 km East of the Astara border. The high pressure well is to be drilled in 725 m
of water to an eventual TD of +- 5300m TVD BRT.

The expected reservoir is located in the Cheleken Series, Sequence that is believed to represent the
Upper Pliocene.

The main objectives for the well are:

•   In spite of TD at 5300m in this well, it is still far to achieve Base of Cheleken.
•  To determine the presence and quality of SC440, and SC400 to Upper SC300, these horizons must
   be tested for the reservoir quality.





                              Fig 1: South Caspian Sea.


Limited area knowledge concerning the possibility of shallow gas has therefore made it necessary to
prepare the top hole sections as if shallow gas exists. Due to the shallow gas uncertainties all technical
action is to be taken ahead of setting the BOP.
The likely range for seawater surface temperatures is between 27o C and 6o C. Temperatures at mid water
depth range from 7.3o C to 6o C and temperatures at mudline range from 7o C to 5o C. The Expected
reservoir temperature is about 110O C. Gas-hydrates might develop within shallower section. Therefore
gas-hydrates might already exist in the formation or it can be self-induced due to the drilling operation.
The drilling program is designed to handle gas-hydrates. MIS Recommends using MEG (Mono Ethylene
Glycol) in case of facing to Gas-hydrates

Initially an 8 ½” pilot hole will be drilled to 1622m TVD BRT to check the formations to the 20” surface
conductor shoe depth for any shallow gas, other potential shallow hazards and locate a suitable shale
section for the 20” surface conductor shoe.

On successfully drilling the 8 ½” pilot hole, 36” and 26” holes  will be drilled to 878m TVD BRT and
1622m TVD BRT respectively. The 8 ½” pilot hole and both the 36” and 26” sections will be drilled
with sea water and High Viscosity Bentonite sweeps. Potential issue are Hole Cleaning, Bit Balling,
Shallow water flows and Hole Instability.

After the 26” hole has been drilled and the 20” casing has been run, a 17 ½” hole to a depth of +- 3122m
TVD BRT will be drilled. The planned recommended and programmed mud system is a Synthetic Oil
Based Mud, with a mud weight ranging from 1.15sg to 1.25sg. However an inhibitive water based mud
will also be programmed as a backup. Potential Issues/Hazards are Hole Cleaning, High Circulating
Pressures and Equivalent Circulating Density, Differential Sticking, Hole Instability, Lost Circulation
and Potential losses while running and cementing the 13 3/8” casing.

The 12 ¼” hole will be drilled to a depth of 4422m TVD BRT with a Synthetic Oil Based Mud, that, if
used on the 17 ½” section will be carried over from that section. The mud weight can range from 1.5sg
to 1.75sg.  Potential Issues/Hazards are are Hole Cleaning, High Circulating Pressures and Equivalent
Circulating Density, Differential Sticking, Hole Instability, Lost Circulation and Potential losses while
running and cementing the 9 5/8” casing.

An 8 ½” hole will be drilled to final TD of 5322m TVD BRT with the mud carried over from drilling the
12 ¼” hole. The weight of the fluid used to drill the 12 ¼” hole will be reduced to 1.55sg by a
combination of centrifuging and whole mud dilution. Coring will be carried out in this section and
Potential Issues/Hazards will be Hole Cleaning, Differential Sticking of the drill string and core barrel,
High Circulating Pressures and Equivalent Circulating Density and Contamination of the Core.

                     TECHNICAL APPENDIX


1. LOST CIRCULATION

INTRODUCTION

In order to cure or prevent lost circulation most effectively, proper planning must be done. If drilling
through certain formation is likely to give losses, treating the drilling fluid with LCM before entering the
potential loss zone may be recommended.

If total losses is expected when drilling into for instance a naturally fracture zone, or a pressure depleted
zone, a reinforcing pill should be mixed ready prior to entering zone and pumped immediately when
losses occur.

However in many cases, losses are not expected and the situation must be analysed before treatment
start. The result of the quantification and analysis of losses will decide the type of curing method to be
used. The type of drilling fluid will influence on the choice of materials to be used in the pills to be
pumped. The first LCM pill to be pumped is often a pill containing different types of particles to bridge
off the fracture. These materials are mixed directly into the drilling fluid, before pumped down hole and
will work immediately if successful.

For severe losses, the LCM pill will not always be able to seal off the fracture. In these cases the solution
will be a reinforcing plug such as Form-A-Plug, Gunk, or Cement. When drilling into known formations
with natural fractures, the solution can be to go directly to a reinforcing plug if experience has shown
that LCM do not work in sealing the fracture.

General:

Check with down hole tools supplier regarding maximum recommended concentrations and particle size
of LCM in pills that can be pumped through the down hole tools; otherwise circulation sub must be part
of the BHA design.

“Normal” loss situation, i.e. being able to maintain fluid column with drilling fluid available on the rig.

Severe loss  situation (no choice),  i.e. not able to maintain mud column over time. Also check
recommendation for maximum pump rate when pill passes through down hole tools.

Concentrations and particle size of LCM may be much higher than recommendations from tool supplier
depending on how serious the situation is.

Recommendations and limitations should be discussed with operator before start of the operation.

Always make sure that the whole volume of pill is pumped out of the string/bit.

Always make sure to pull above LCM pill after spotting it.

CURING LOST CIRCULATION

Lost circulation can occur at any time or depth during drilling or tripping in hole. It is important to
distinguish between down hole losses and surface losses. When a drop in the drilling fluid volume is
noted  it  is important  that the obvious  is not ignored. All possible surface loss points must be
investigated. The treatment of the losses depends on the severity of the losses, the type of drilling fluid in
use as well as the type of formation where the loss occurs.

SEEPAGE LOSSES (< 20 bbl/hr)

Losses up to approximately 3 m3/hr (20 bbls/hr) are normally considered as seepage losses. Seepage type
losses are frequently cured by the simple action of reducing or stopping the pump rate and allowing the
formation to heal and become sealed off by the development of a filter cake. The pump rate can be
gradually increased after the losses have stopped.

If the loss does not heal by it self and the amount of loss cannot be tolerated from an economical point of
view, LCM pill for curing the loss should be pumped.

Recommended concentrations of LCM in pills to be used is at least 50 PPB. The following formulations
are recommended. If some products are lacking on the rig these should be replaced by other products to
give the same total concentration.


                  SOBM drilling fluid ( 50 ppb pill )

                       CaCo3 M           5 ppb

23.1 Tops of Cement

   •  30” conductor pipe shall be cemented to seabed to provide support for the wellhead and BOP’s.
   •  20” surface conductor shall be cemented to seabed to provide support for the wellhead and
      BOP’s.
   •  16” drilling liner shall be cemented to the top of liner if the 20” conductor could not go all the
      way to this depth for any reason, which may be due to abnormal pressure and/or incompetent
       formations.
   •  13⅜” surface casing shall be cemented 150 m into 16” drilling liner or 20” surface conductor
       depending on whether 16” liner is required or not. The reason behind this is to effectively seal off
      and protect any fresh water formation and/or isolate incompetent formation as well as to simplify
       the abandonment in a later stage.
   •  9⅞” Production Casing shall be cemented 150 m into 13⅜” surface casing for the same reason
       mentioned above.
   •  7” liner shall be cemented to the top of the liner to provide zonal isolation over the zone of
         interest.

 25 WASTE MANAGEMENT

 The proposed exploration drilling program in the South Caspian is to be conducted with minimum
 permitted discharge of drilling wastes. Thus, drilled cuttings and drilling or completion fluids will not be
 discharged to the sea, but will be collected, transported from the well site to the supply base, treated to
 remove toxic residues as necessary, and finally recycled, reused or disposed of in an environmentally
 safe and appropriate manner.

 Cuttings will be collected from shale shakers and sent to vertical cutting drier by means of auger. The
 vertical cutting drier will reduce the retained oil on cuttings from 35% to approximately 5%. The dried
 cuttings is then collected in the cutting skips and sent to shore for final treatment and disposal.
