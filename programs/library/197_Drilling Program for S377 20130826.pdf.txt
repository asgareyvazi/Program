 3.1 General Formation Lithology Descriptions
1)  In a typical geological section, one massive oolitic to pellety limestone with minor contemporaneous
    brecciation in the basal part developed in Fahliyan Formation (James and Wynd, 1965). The Fahliyan
    Formation has been drilled in Well AZN-1, AZN-2, AZN-4, AZN-5 and AZN-6 and the Fahliyan
    Formation in Well AZN-1, AZN-2 and AZN-5 are mainly consisted of poorly fossiliferous, generally
    micritic limestone, often argillaceous, and marls with no porosity butextensive and thick porous
    micritic and chalky limestones were developed in Well AZN-4 and AZN-6.
2)  In the Gadvan Formation, a variety of lithological rock types such as carbonates, rich organic shales
   and sandstones were deposited in various environments. The lower part of the Gadvan Formation is
   composed of low-energy, but not very deep, micritic, often argillaceous limestone with echinoids and
    algal fragments. Two sandstone units were defined in the Gadvan Formation，lateral changes and
    especially discontinuity of the sandstone layers which are consist of fine clastics (shales and
    siltstones) may be expected.
3)  The thicknees of Khalij Member  is about 14m in the Azadegan Field, and main lightology is
    limestone, containing algal and echinoid debris. Although fair porosity values were observed, the
    limestone was very tight and has a very low permeability.The Dariyan Formation in the Azadegan
    Field developed grey to brown thick-bedded limestone which is rich in Orbitorina.
4)  In the Azadegan Field, the upper part of the Kazhdumi Formation is made up of dark-grey to
    dark-brown marls, the middle part developed light-grey, micritic to chalky limestone, the low part, is
   around40-60m in thichnessclean channel sands interbeded  argillaceous sands with streaks of shale.
5)  The Sarvak Formation developed a carbonate unit which was gradually overlying the Kazhdumi
    Formation, and  it was abruptly terminated by an erosion phase and covered by various  late
    Cretaceous sediments. A Typical geological section of the Sarvak Formation in Azadegan reveals
    that dark grey fine grained nodulary argillaceous limestone interbedded with thin marls in the bottom,
   and graded upward into a white-to-buff massive chalky limestone with siliceous nodules;it was
    developed a very massive tan-to-brown limestone containing abundant rudist debris in the middle
    part; the upper part is made up of rubbly limestone with red staining and leached zones of breccia
   and the top of Sarvak Formation is very ferruginous.
6)  The Laffan Formation developed a thin greenish-grey pyritic shale layer, capping the Sarvak
    Formation. It was encountered in the Azadegan wells and only ostracods were found. It is a cap rock
    for the upper part of the Sarvak reservoir.

7)  In the Azadegan Field, the Ilam Formation consists of grey micritic limestone, often argillaceous,
    rarely  pyritic,  limestones.  Microfauna,  mostly  pelagic,  including  rotalids  and  oligostegina
    assemblage, a few algae, bryozoa, and rudist fragments exist.
8)  The Gurpi Formation in the Azadegan Field consists of grey to dark-brownish, slightly argillaceous
    micritic limestone with Maestrichtian to Campanian aged fauna. At Well AZN-1, about 50 meters of
    chalky limestones were encountered in the middle of the Gurpi formation, which contains abundant
    fragments of rudists, bryozoa, corals, and Orbitoides.
9)  Pabdeh Formation is divided into two parts in the Azadegan field. The upper part is made of grey to
    dark-brown argillaceous limestones and marls. Planktonic fauna dates that this part was deposited in
    Oligocene. The lower part is composed of dark-brown, slightly argillaceous limestone. Pelagic fauna,
    mostly Globigerina and Globorotalia, indicates that this part was formed during late Paleocene to late
   Eocene in age. It seems that no early Paleocene Formation exists in the Azadegan area.
10) The Asmari Formation in Azadegan Field developed a massive sandstone interval, with a few thin
    argillaceous layers in the upper sections. Sands are  clear, fine to medium-sorted, and poorly
    consolidated. On the other hand, the lower sections consist of intercalation of sandy, chalky, slightly
    dolomitic high-energy limestone with sands layers.
11) The Gachsaran Formation with a thickness ranges from 400 m to 600 m, thicken towards the north.
    Deposition of the Gachsaran Formation is cyclic. The cycle began with a deposition of shallow-water
    marine carbonates, and then it was overlain by a thick evaporitic phase. The evaporites were capped
   by red to variegated marl, which represented the end of cycle.
12) The AghaJari Formation consists of a repetition of brown to grey calcareous sandstones, chert debris
   and gypsum-veined red marl. A fauna of brackish to freshwater was observed, together with more
    marine fauna.
 3.2 Key Reservoir Descriptions
   There are three key reservoirs that we need to focus on. Gross thickness of Sarvak-3 ranges from 16m
to 30m and averaged at 21.4m.The average thickness of net-pay zone in Sarvak-3 is 15.8m and the
Net/Gross ratio 1s 79%  .It is mainly composed of light brown or dark brown bituminous dolomitic
limestone, which is dominated by bioturbated and bioclastic packstones and grainstone. It is a main
oil-bearing zone with a better petrophysical property with respect to others of Sarvak Formation in the
south azadegan. The mean porosity is 16.8% and the mean permeability is 20.7 md and So is 89.1%. The
forecasted top depth is -2752m in S377.

6. Drilling History and Lessions learnt
 6.1 Drilling History

The Azadegan Oil field was discovered when Well AZN-1 was drilled in 1999 whose total depth is
4643m at the Cretaceous formation.
Before CNPCI (Iran), 40 wells (7 exploration wells, 2 appraisal well and 31 producing wells) have been
drilled in the Azadegan Field, among which 23 wells are located in the South Azadegan Contract Area, 5
wells (AZNS-026, AZNS-027, AZNS-028, AZNS-029 & AZNS-030) has been drilled by CNPCI (Iran)
and 2 well (AZNS-031 & AZNS-032) are currently being drilled.
Carbonate reservoir of Sarvak in the Cretaceous formation is broadly developed in Azadegan Field and
was penetrated in 6 exploration wells (AZN-1, AZN-2, AZN-3, AZN-4, AZN-5 and AZN-6). Classic oil
bearing zones of Kazhdumi Formation developed in main anticline of Azedegan Field from north to
center of South Azadegan. Classic oil bearing zones of Gadvan Formation developed only in crest area
and south parts of South Azadegan Filed, which was discovered by AZN-1 in crest and AZN-4 in south
parts.

 6.2 Lesson learned

Well AZNS-17H:
The 17 ½” hole section had been drilled to 1140m with 70-82 PCF W.B.M, the formation drilled is
Aghajari-Gachsaran, tight hole (1140-50m) was encounter when during the condition trip, wash and ream
several times this interval. Totally operation is smoothly, a proper mud weight in this section is important
for the well’s operations.
The 12 ¼”hole is drilled to 1479 m with 85-88 PCF W.B.M, the drilled formation is Gachsaran. Tight
hole was encountered during the condition trip, wash and ream several times this interval and increase
MW f/88 to 100 pcf. Totally operation is smoothly, a proper mud weight in this section is important for
the well’s operations.
While drilled 17 ½” and 12 ¼” hole section in Aghajari and Gachsaran formation observed bit and
stabilizer balled up.
The best approach is to use good fundamentals: an inhibitive mud, optimal hydraulics and a quality ROP
enhancer.
Cure bit balling
•   Work the bit by rotating fast off bottom and reciprocating the string to shake off the clay coating.
•   Reduce ROP for a short time.
•   Spot a walnut pill.
•    Circulation.
•   Fresh water.
•    If bit balling is observed while drilling, a detergent is usually added to disperse the balled clays.
•   The use of a small percentage of glycol also helps to reduce bit balling.
•   Consider fresh water pill.
•   Keep the API fluid loss as low as possible.

The 8 ½” hole is drilled to 1970m with 75 pcf salt water w/2-12 BPH losses. For the mud losses, a mud
system with LCM is required.
The 6⅛” hole was  drilled  to 3595.5m MD with 76 PCF O.B.M,  the formation  drilled was
Gachsaran-Asmari-Pabdeh-Ilam-Sarvak-Kazhdumi, the main problem is stuck pipe @ 3595.5m , MD in
Kazhdumi Fm. work on stuck with over pull, pipe got free and continue drilled to TD with 73 PCF
O.B.M so a proper mud weight in this section is important for the well’s operations.

4. Requirement of Drilling and completion Fluid
4.1 Formation or Pore Pressure and Mud Weight
Primary Well Control relies on the hydrostatic pressure of drilling fluid to balance the formation
pore pressure. In order to counteract any minor pressure reductions on bottom due to swabbing

Drilling Program                                                                               Page 19

────────────────────────────────────────────
────────────────────────────────────────────
                   SOUTH AZADEGAN PROJECT
                      S377H on Pad 50 Drilling Program
                                             Version: 3rd

effect when tripping, it is customary to maintain a hydrostatic column with a pressure slightly
exceeding the pore pressure of the exposed formation. This overbalance, safety margin, or trip
margin, Ptm, varies depending on circumstances but it is generally of the order of 200 psi for
conventional operations. The required density Pf to control a formation at a depth, D, with a
pressure, Po, is given by: Pf = (Po+Ptm), or Df = (Po+Ptm)/D
To gas cut or contaminated mud, Df = Do + (0.07-0.15) sg,
To oil and/or salt water contaminated mud, Df = Do + (0.05-0.10) sg;

4.3 Guideline for Mud Program
1)  This is a preliminary program. The contractor should provide Drilling Fluid Program based on this
    basic proposal.
2)  The lowest possible PV is essential for: low frictional losses & optimal hole cleaning.
3)  The Yield Point (YP) is calculated by subtracting the PV from the Fan reading at 300 rpm, giving a
    result in lbs/100ft2. YP = 2xR300 - R600. An optimal YP is essential for: carry capacity of drilling
    fluid (a rule of thumb is that YP = 0.75 x hole size in inches); stability of drilling fluid towards
    settling of solids (a rule of thumb is that YP = 9 x specific gravity) & the hole capacity of the drilling
     fluid.
4)  Optimally the 10’ gel value is 1.5 x the 10” gel value.
5)  Always maintain the readings of 6 rpm between 1.2 and 1.5 times of hole size in inches.
6)  Drill from Asmari to Sarvak, Formation, KCl polymer mud will be mixed for this section. Losses are
   common (even with the light mud weights used) in the Asmari, Sarvak formations. Sized CaCO3
    materials are used as lost circulation material. Mud weight shall be kept as low as possible. High pH
    shall be maintained to control any small amounts of H2S and CO2 gas. In the Sarvak Wells, lubricant
   may be required as the hole dictates. KCl Polymer mud will be transferred to CaCO3 based Drill-In
    fluid to drill the Sarvak reservoir. Mud weight shall be kept as low as possible. CaCO3 is the optimal
    weighting material and barite is back up as required.

7. Directional Drilling
7.1 Anti-collision measures
Anti-collision should be considered if more than one wells on the same pad. The distance between
every two cellars is only 15m. The risk of collision between two slots likely occurs in vertical section.
Anti-collision issue of long vertical section  is a big challenge and anti-collision measures are as
following:
1) From surface to kick off point: In upper formation, adopt pendulum BHA and low WOB and high
  RPM to guarantee drilling upper hole vertically.
2)  Single-shot and Multi-shot will be used to monitor and survey the well trajectory. Take survey of
    single-shot every 400-500 m, if the deviation is nearly or larger than required, then survey should be
    taken more frequently and well straightening measure should be performed. The multi-shot survey
    should be taken during the last trip and the survey interval of multi-shot is not more than 30m.
3)  After survey, the drilled well path should be compared to existing and other proposed wells. The
    distances between other wells and the drilled well are calculated at frequent depth intervals in the
     critical section. These calculations can be performed using the application COMPASS, WELLPLAN
    or NAVIGATOR. Survey uncertainty must also be computed both for the drilled well and the critical
    existing wells.
4)  Steering BHA (Motor and MWD) should be back-up. Run Steering BHA (Motor and MWD)
   whenever potential collision risk increased.
5)  Existing well survey data
   The well AZN-17  is the nearest  offset  well, which  is 68m  far away from  this  well,

         slack off weight is 30 Ton except liner weight, too much weight will damage the
        running tool.

2.2.3.3. Setting liner hanger
1)  RIH to design depth, start pump to establish circulation, if need, circulate one bottom’s up,
    remove off total gas, break off the TDS and drop the ball into the string.
2)  Re-make up TDS and space out. Drop ball and start pump with 2~3 bpm to chase the ball or
     limit the pump pressure less than 500psi until the pressure comes up sharply.
3)  Note: If the pressure comes up sharply, stop pump at once.
4)   Verify the weight and recalculate the extension distance whether it is suitable.
5)  Slowly pressure up to 200psi over designed set pressure hold 2 min;
6)  Slack off, check the weight of the hook, if the hook load drop down continuously, it is
    proved that the liner hanger has been activated, continue to slack off until set down 10~15T

    running string weight on the liner hanger.

     the liner hanger cannot be set, increase 100 psi per time to repeat the setting program. Until
     the pressure get same as the running tool releasing pressure.

2.2.3.4. Release the setting tool
1)  After the liner hanger is successful set, set down 5T running string weight on liner hanger.
2)  Pressure up to 200psi over designed, hold for 2min.
3)  Bleed off, pressure up to 500psi again, pick up 1m (cannot over 2m) to verify if released.
4)   If the liner weight has been lost, it indicates setting tool released successfully.
    Note: If hook load continuously increase, limit over pull lower 5T than the liner weight to
    keep the hanger function. (If too much over pull, the liner hanger maybe un-hung)

control equipment shall be recorded in the IADC daily reports and shall be signed by both the Drilling
Contractor's Tool Pusher and Drilling Supervisor on the well site. They shall also be recorded in the
Daily Drilling Report.

(3)  Primary Control Reference
It is the responsibility of the Drilling Contractor to keep the hole full at all times by using all the available

these values is an important element in killing operations, in order to avoid formation breakdown.

    Once per tour.

    Whenever changes occur in the dimension and characteristics of the string, BHA, jet size, jet
        plugged or jet lost, etc.
(4)  Secondary Control Reference

out. Space-out for all Pipe Rams and Annular Preventer must be known to the Driller at all times to keep
tool joints clear of annular preventer and rams.
(5)  Kick Detection Procedure While Drilling
If there is an indication of a possible kick occurring, the Driller shall follow the kick detection procedures
outlined below:
Stop drilling with the pump onPick up top drive to a predetermined space-out positionShut off the
pumps and check for well flow by visual examination on the bell nipple or flow line.
1)   If the well is not flowing:
    Immediately notify the rig manager, Drilling Supervisor and Tool pusher.
    The Drilling Supervisor will decide whether to circulate bottoms up or resume drilling. The
         penetration rate will be limited as long as the cause of the abnormal situation is undetermined.
        Always be aware that some hydrocarbons (gas or oil cut mud) may rise to the surface. Ensure that
         the proper monitoring and safety systems all in place.
2)   If the well is flowing:
    Close the well as per the Shut-in Procedure.
    Immediately notify the Wellsite Drilling Supervisor, rig manager and Tool pusher.
