1. INTRODUCTION

The South Pars Gas field is located in the Persian Gulf, at some 100 KM from shore. Four huge

condensate rich gas bearing reservoirs have been identified (K1, K2, K3 and K4) in this field. NIOC has

envisaged developing this field by phases of some 1000 MMSCFD wet gas production each. TOTAL /

Gas Prom  / PETRONAS have already developed two such phases (referred to as "Phase 2" and

"Phase3"). NIOC developed South Pars development "Phase 1".ENI  / PETROPARS consortium

developed Phases 4 & 5 and PETROPARS /STATOIL joint ventures developed phases 6 / 7 /8. Phases 9

& 10 are developing by NIDC / LG. In each contract, respecting phases are integrated in order to share

common  facilities and thus reduce the overall cost of the development. NIOC  is pursuing further

development under "Phase 11", "Phase 12" and" Phases 13 &14" and "Phases 15 and 16", and "Phases 17

and 18", "Phase19",  It is believed that developing the whole field will lead to 28 development phases.

This program details the plan which provides the basic support data involving, geological, reservoir and

drilling / completion information for the development of Phases 17 & 18 of South Pars development

project. The drilling will be done with two drilling jack up rigs but not limited to providing two jack up

rigs, One (1) vertical APPRAISAL wells and Twenty one (21) deviated development wells from two

separate platforms, each with eleven slots. Each drilling rig shall drill ten directional wells and one

appraisal wells and five (5) wells are on third platform, all wells must be perforated through all reservoir

layers. The remaining slots will be used for infill drilling if necessary to maintain the production plateau

in future. POGC plans to drill one (1) appraisal, delineation (DW) and drill twenty one (21) deviated

wells from two platforms.

3. HEALTHS, SAFETY AND RISKS


3.1. Health & Safety

   We always have time to rig up and operate safely: Take “time-out” if necessary
   Plan the job ahead don’t run afterwards
   Helmets and tools to be secured against falling into the hole/casing. Use hole cover / mats when
  working near rotary table.
   Perform pre-job meeting prior to running casing.
   Make sure  all areas involved  in casing  transport and handling  is being secured  to avoid
  unauthorized personnel into entrance areas.
   Focus on falling objects
  ▪   Focus to avoid loose items on rig-floor and all decks below in drilling module and well bay areas
  ▪    All equipment used above 2m has to be secured with wire
  ▪    All tools has to be suitable for use in derrick (use of wire)
  ▪    Sign-out list has to be filled out in drillers cabin (in/out)
  ▪       List with numbers in the toolbox at rig floor
   No work at two levels without a barrier between
  ▪    Important with work orders sign by driller and area responsible at TDAD
  ▪   Use barrier tape
  ▪   Keep people away
  ▪   Radio communication
  ▪   Respond back on communication
    Install hole cover on casing top between each intermediate operation
  Focus on experience / feedback from previous conductor installations regarding HSE, operation and
  handling of pipe.

5.1. Regional Geology
The South Pars Gas- Condensate Field and its southern extension, the North Dome, are located in the
interior platform of the Arabian Shelf structural province. The field is a part of huge NNE-SSW trending
Qatar- Fars Arch that is one of the major structural elements in central Persian Gulf area. It is also
relatively close to the Zagros Mobile Belt Mountain forelands located northwards. So the geological
history of the South Pars was as effected by events occurring in the quite stable Arabian Plate as by those
in the surrounding more active area.

Several reservoirs (oil and gas) were identified by the exploration and appraisal wells. The oil reservoirs
of the South Pars are in Khami and Bangestan Group formations. The Gas-Condensate bearing reservoirs
of this field belong to the Kangan and Dalan formations of Triassic and Permian age respectively. These
carbonate formations were deposited  in a shallow marine environment during a general marine
transgression that began in the middle of Permian and lasted until the early Triassic. The basal section of
Dashtak formation, which was formed throughout the lower, middle and upper parts of the Triassic
period,  is respected by argillaceous siltstone, shale and dolomite with anhydrite interbeds and the
remaining middle-upper parts mainly consist of dolomite intercalation.

Dashtak formation is known as good seal for Kangan-Dalan reservoirs.Within these reservoirs (Kangan
and Dalan which are equivalent to the Khuff Formation in Arabian nomenclature), there are four main
producing levels, which from top to bottom are K1, K2, K3 and K4 which are separated by anhydritic
layers. Each of these levels has been divided to several subzones (Totally 16 subzones – TEC 2003; 12
subzones – total 1999). A massive anhydrite body, known as Nar member, separates the K4 from the
underlying K5 member that exhibits poor reservoir characteristics.

6.1. Background from South Pars Phase 6, 7&8

A ‘continuous curve’ strategy through the 16” hole section has proven to be the most effective way to
maximize the drilling performance whilst still achieving the required inclination of approximately 45deg/
62deg inc by section TD.

In order to establish a drilling performance benchmark, the first 16” hole section was drilled utilizing a
conventional 11 1/4” low speed, high torque PDM and insert roller-cone bit. To maximum overall cost
benefit to the project it was decided to try an alternate directional drilling system comprising of an ultra-
high-powered, pre-contoured metal stator PDM in conjunction with a steerable PDC bit. This first trial
delivered a step-change in drilling performance with an ROP increase, excellent PDC bit steerability and
one bit/BHA for the section. On the basis of these results the decision was made to utilize this bit – motor
combination for the remaining 25 wells. Further minor refinements were made to the BHA and drilling
practices to enhance performance and gain consistency and a BHA build up rate experience database was
developed.

The budgeted time for this section was 12 days. The fastest 16″ section was completed in 4.0 days. Both
times include drilling, running 13 3/8” casing and cementing.

Turboback stabilizer

Turbobacks are short stabilizers which are placed between the bit and the drive sub of a motor. This
means that the stabilizer rotates at bit RPM.

They have reaming elements on  the  front edges  of  their  blades and  are  shorter compared  to
conventional stabilizers. This helps to minimize the bending loads on the bearing section of the mud
motor in curved hole sections during directional drilling operations.

Due to the additional guidance of the reamer blades hole spiraling is reduced and the borehole is kept
in gauge.  The reaming elements on the blades smoothen the borehole walls by removing possible
edges and offsets. The OD  of a Turboback stabilizer  is  typically 1/32" or 1/16” under gauge
compared to the  bit  size, which ensures both smooth sliding of the BHA, even in curved borehole
sections, and the required  contact between  the  blades and the borehole wall  to  ream  away
unwanted edges and offsets. Due to the improved borehole quality, sliding of the string is made easier
and hanging of the BHA is avoided. Both effects result in more constant WOB available with reduced
peak loads on the BHA. Field tests have proven that the use of Turbobacks significantly reduces the
required reaming time.

Several benefits can be accomplished with Turboback stabilizers:

   •  Added stability and protection to bit gauge

   •  Good borehole quality by wiping mechanical tight spots such as ledges

   •  Reduced spiral hole pattern seen when rotating with bent subs

   •  Prevention of bit and BHA whirl, which reduces the risk of MWD failures

   •  Reduction of torque and drag if used with motors

Recommendations

   •  Where overgauge hole  is  a  result  of  using  a  deflection  assembly,  consider  the  use  of
      Turboback stabilizers and/or roller reamers to smooth out ledges and tortuosity

   •   If using a motor, it is recommended to use a Turboback stabilizer to promote a smoother borehole
       wall and also to help stabilize the bit

   •    If Turboback stabilizers are used, they should be used from start to finish. Dropping them out for
       sections will require reaming of that section if they are run again

   •   If  Turboback  stabilizers  are  used  they  should  be  of  the  same  profile.  For example,
       changing from 3 blade to 4 blade may require reaming.

   •  Turboback stabilizers can also be used in rotary assemblies. There is evidence that they provide
      added stability and protection to bit gauge, and may stop "hourglassing"

8. MUD LOGGING SERVICES

Objective: Sequence characterization, well control and reservoir evaluation.

  Raw data summary:

   1)  Cuttings

   2) Gas curve

   3) Gas shows (non hydrocarbon components)

   4) Gas samples (from gas line)

   5)  Drilling data (ROP, torque, mud losses)

1) CUTTINGS

Objective: Litho-biostratigraphy, hydrocarbon detection and geochemical characterisation.

•  Acquisition techniques and sampling specifications

   1- Sampling rate: 5 m. 3 m if hydrocarbon shows occur

   2- Size and quantity suitable for lithological and fossils identification.

   3- Sets: 1 (one) series washed and oven-dried for stratigraphy. Quantity 250 cc [1 (one) series
  unwashed. Quantity 250 cc].

   4- Thin sections are required.

   5- Characterisation of drilling fluid needed: for both Head Space and Source rock, mud samples
   shall be collected every 200 m and preserved in special glass vials.

•  In case of oil shows on cuttings (and mud):

    1- Use cicloexane as solvent for standard oil shows analyses (Wood’s lamp).

    2- Use eptane as solvent for special analyses (QFT).

•  Operational recommendations and warnings

    1- Sedimentary lost circulation materials (limestone, etc.) may be mistaken for rocks constituents.

    2- It  is  strongly recommended  to  avoid using PDC  bits,  since  they induce  textural and
    mineralogical modifications on the rock and can affect oil show detection .

    3- Borehole instability (washouts and caving) produces bad depth matching due to wrong lag time

    4- WBM-Glycol system can cause cleaning problems

10. 4. Expected H2S Gas
Introduction
The presence of Hydrogen Sulfide in drilling fluids,  if not treated with caution, can be particularly
damaging to personnel, equipment and mud properties.

The sources of Hydrogen Sulfide are three-fold; it is present either in permeable formations as a result of
the degradation of Sulfure-containing organic materials or as a direct result of the thermal degradation of
drilling fluid products containing Sulphur, (examples are lingo-sulphonates and thio-carbamate biocides)
and finally as a result of the bacterial degradation of sulphates by Sulfide reducing bacteria (SRB) under
anaerobic conditions. The latter is usually encountered when re-entering old completed wells.

Effects
The first positive identification of soluble sulfides in the drilling fluids must be taken as a possible
indication of H2S entering the fluid from some source. In the simplest case an increase in mud weight
may be sufficient to control minor influxes. If levels persist chemical treatment will be required. This can
take two forms; neutralization and scavenging. The two terms must not be confused.

Neutralization of H2S in drilling fluid by caustic soda or lime is a common field approach. This alkalinity
maintenance method has proved successful when small amounts of H2S enter the drilling fluid, for
example, from drilling up pore spaces containing the gas. This is not a suitable approach to the control on
influxes of Hydrogen Sulfide. The alkalinity neutralization ofH2S is instantly reversible by reduction in
pH. Should the pH of the fluid decrease due to further influxes of H2S, Co2 or brine flows the sulfides
may come out of solution as hydrogen sulfide gas.

To ensure that such influxes are handled safely, it is essential to employ a sulfide scavenger. This term
refers to any additive which is capable of reacting with one or more of the sulfide species and as a result,
converting them to a more inert form. To be truly effective an irreversible and complete reaction must
occur. As a mutual equilibrium exist between the three sulfide species in solution (H2S, HS- and S2-)
irreversible and complete removal of one serves to removal all three.

To remove the problem it is necessary to precipitate the sulfide as an insoluble inorganic sulfide. Zinc or
iron compounds are usually chosen because of the relative inertness of the sulfides formed. Zinc source
(Zinc oxide or carbonate) has in the past been the preferred treatment. However, continuous treatment
with  Zinc  carbonate  will  produce  significant  undesirable  carbonate  concentration  (Carbonate

As above mentioned Hydrogen Sulfide is a poisonous and dangerous acidic gas encountered in many
formations. It can quickly deaden senses and can be fatal, even at low concentration. Hydrogen Sulfide is
characterized by its typical ″rotten egg″ smell. For safety reasons, it should be neutralized immediately
with caustic soda or lime to increase pH to >11.5 to form sulfide (S2-), then precipitated with a source of
Zinc.

Neutralization with caustic soda:

2 NaOH + H2S             2Na+ + S2- + 2H2O (at pH>11.5)

Neutralization with maintaining excess lime:

2 Ca(OH)2 + H2S           Ca2+ + S2- + 2H2O (at pH>11.5)

Converting Hydrogen Sulfide into just sulfide by increasing pH is not a permanent reaction. If the pH
were drop into the acid region, sulfide will be converting back into the poisonous H2S form. For this
reasons H2S should always be precipitated by adding a source of Zinc such as Zinc Oxide.

Removal by precipitation with treatments of (ZnO):

H2S + ZnO        ZnS + H2O

If H2S gas detected in mud following action should be taken to:

   1. Immediately rise up the pH to at least 11.5 to 12 with caustic soda.

   2. Buffer the pH with lime with 2 lb/bbl

   3. Begin treatment with Zinc Carbonate to remove sulfide (S2-) from the system. (1 lb/bbl treatment

   of Zinc oxide removes approximately 1100 mg/lit of sulfide. No more than 2 lb/bbl of Zinc oxide

   should be used to pre-treat the system for H2S).

   4. Utilize COR-MUSCLE at range of 0.5-1% vol.

If it appears that H2S bleeding in (flowing) form the formation, the mud density should be increased to
shut off the flow of gas into the well-bore.

Mud Weight

The starting mud weight will be 1.18 SG for the section - allow time for the cold mud to warm up and
shear in order to avoid excessive ECD. With this weight the fluid system will be above the collapse
pressure of the well bore and provide bore stability. The mud weight will be kept as low as possible from

Plastic Viscosity

Plastic viscosity should be maintained as low as possible by extensive use of solids control equipment,
coupled with whole new mud dilution.

API Fluid Loss Control

It is recommended that the API fluid loss be maintained at <5 ml at all the time. This may best be
achieved by the addition of HYDRO-STAR NF at 5-6 lb/bbl and MY-PAC LV at 1-1.5 lb/bbl.

MBT

Measuring of the MBT is the direct indication of formation reactivity. MBT of range of <15 lb/bbl is
acceptable. Above this range, mud Rheology will start to shoot up to uncontrollable levels. Using fine
screens & high speed centrifuges will help in maintaining lower values of MBT by discarding the fine
solids and consequently minimize the dilution rate.

Shale inhibition will be achieved by utilizing KCl at range of ±20 lb/bbl, HYDRO-CAP XP at range of
±0.5 lb/bbl and HYDRO-PLAST at range of 3 lb/bbl to pass troublesome shale and clay-stone layers.

10.10. LCM Pill Spotting Procedure

1.  After spotting LCM pill, pull the bit above the calculated top of the pill. Wait 30 minutes and monitor
    the annulus.
2.  After 30 minutes, attempt to fill the annulus, if not already full.  If the hole can be filled, close the BOP
   and squeeze 10 bbls of LCM pill into the formation without exceeding 200 psi.
3.  If the hole remains stable, continue to drill ahead.
4.  Should the LCM pill fail to reduce the losses or the hole cannot be filled, then mix and spot another 50
    bbls of LCM pill.
5.  Attempt to fill the annulus.  If successful, close the BOP and squeeze 20 bbls of LCM pill into the
   formation without exceeding 200 psi.  If a squeeze pressure develops, hold the pressure for 1 hour
    before staging the drill string back to bottom.
Notes:

  All LCM pills must be thoroughly mixed and homogenised before being pumped.

 When pumping an LCM pill through an MWD tool, the displacement rate must be reduced below

    that required to turn on the tool, e.g. less than 500 gpm in 17″ hole. The displacement rate is to be

   agreed with the rig site MWD service representative prior to displacing any LCM pill.

 Remove filter screens from the drill pipe and if necessary from the mud pump suction lines.

  •  Stuck pipe

An  increase  in hole angle reduces  the  transport performance  of  the  drilling  fluid. Hole
inclinations > 40° are critical because of cuttings bed build up and possible avalanching of the
cuttings bed. As shown below hydraulic graph, the only concerning can be for cutting bed.
Turbulent flow tends to have a greater capacity to carry cuttings beds in deviated well. Therefore
Low-Vis/ Hi-Vis pill as following procedure is highly recommended to be utilized whilst drilling.

Use of sweeps
If the correct mud properties are maintained and drilling practices include high rotary speeds then
the mud system will clean the hole. Once the mud system is right the use of sweeps only acts to
cause deterioration of the ideal mud properties.

In highly deviated wells mud rheology is already difficult enough to keep within specification
without the detrimental effect of sweeps being absorbed into the system. Furthermore, their use in
highly deviated wells has proven largely ineffective, regardless of the sweep design.

In a highly deviated well with the fluid flow along the top of the hole, even the most viscous of
pills will allow cuttings to fall to the bottom of the hole. Also as the pipe is rotated and the fluid
flow profile takes effect, mixing of the sweep with the drilling fluid is inevitable. The most
common result is that a sweep is never detected back at surface.

Try to avoid pumping Low-vis / Hi-vis pills when the hole is clean. The focus should be on
primary hole cleaning to minimise the formation of cuttings beds in the first place. In case of any
doubting on hole cleaning then pump tandem Low-vis/ Hi-vis pill to clean the hole.

Low-vis pills create turbulent flow (intended to stir up cuttings) in the wellbore but this has a
number of detrimental effects, then Hi-vis pills are likely to pick up large quantities of cuttings
and caving, which can result in pack-off. However it is recommended to utilize tandem pill for
each three stands has been drilled by pumping 15 bbls inhibited sea water (20 lb/bbl KCl) then
followed by 15 bbls Hi-vis pill to be carrying out the cutting that have been suspended when Lo-
vis has been pumped into the annulus.



Note: keep drill string on rotation at maximum allowed RPM whilst pumping low.vis /Hi.vis its highly.

11.5.Hole Cleaning Guidelines in Deviated well
There is no way, cuttings beds will form in high angle wellbores, regardless of how efficient the hole
cleaning practices are. How the cuttings are distributed in the hole will dictate the measures that are
required to move them.  Increasing mud rheology over than normal range to get better hole cleaning in
deviated well absolutely is a wrong practice which cause to increase the cutting beds and cuttings overall.
Here is some practices for drilling a well directionally as a guidelines to optimize wellbore stability and
hole cleaning as well. Management of the cuttings in the hole is a key to efficient drilling operations. A
wellbore does not have to be 100% clean, or free of cuttings to be “clean”. Every high angle wellbore will
have a cuttings bed of some kind. A cuttings bed that is clean for drilling is not necessarily the same as
that for tripping a BHA, running casing or running wire-line logs.

Good hole cleaning practices
Good tripping and drilling practices are critical to ensuring acceptable hole cleaning. The benefits of a
powerful, purpose built drilling rig can easily be negated by poor or inappropriate drilling practices.

Mud and rheology guidelines
Regardless of the mud type the overall objective is to maintain a pump-able fluid with low-end rheology
that is high enough to keep cuttings moving out of the hole. The use of 6 rpm readings as a primary
indicator of hole cleaning capability and maintaining low PV (plastic viscosity) for pump-ability is widely
accepted.

Flow rate Effect on Hole Cleaning
Pump at the maximum available flowrate at all
time. This will be limited by formation pressure
integrity. Use the ECD as a guide to maximum
flowrate possible without compromising hole
integrity.

Flowrate is the key parameter for hole cleaning
rate, as shown in the below figure. The faster
the pump rate the quicker the hole is cleaned, so
long as the rpm is sufficiently high, preferably
>120 rpm.

It is important to appreciate that as long as cuttings are moving up the hole at a faster rate than they are
being generated, then the hole is being cleaned. Flowrate will affect the rate of cleaning and allow faster
cuttings generation to be tolerated.

When discussing the desired flowrate for highly deviated, say 1000 –1200 GPM in 17-1/2″ hole, it is not
unusual to be told that such high flowrate will wash out the hole. Many people have concerns that
turbulent flow will result in erosion of the wellbore. This is a misnomer for several reasons. For all intents
and purposes, it is impossible to get turbulent flow in the drill pipe annulus, regardless of the flowrates

Torque and Drag monitoring
This is one of the primary methods of monitoring hole cleaning as the information is readily available and
easily interpreted on the rig floor.





Surface torque and drag monitoring involves taking torque, rotating string weight, pick and slack-off

weights at every connection. As the well is drilled deeper the values for up weight, rotating weight and

down weight will all slowly increase, as will the difference between them. When the up and down weight

lines diverge away from the predicted trends, i.e. up weight increases and down weight decreases it can

indicate build up of cuttings beds in the well and a requirement to stop and circulate clean. Examples of

these trends are shown in the figure below. Torque and drag modeling has proven to be an excellent tool

for monitoring cuttings bed build up but there are many actions that may be occurring that will not

necessarily show up or may be misinterpreted. Differential sticking, key seating and wellbore instability

effects should not be misinterpreted as cuttings build up.

Back-ream and pumping out
Although back reaming may be considered an appropriate practice in vertical wells and conventional low
angle deviated wells. Back reaming and pumping out of hole are not appropriate practices for high angle

wells.

Whereas tight hole in vertical wells is likely to be due to wellbore conditions, tight hole in highly
deviated wells is likely to be due to hole cleaning and cuttings. Back reaming and pumping out of hole
through a cuttings bed can very easily lead to packing off, stuck pipe and possible loss of the string and
wellbore.

Guidelines for precautionary back reaming
On occasion, it may be necessary to back ream in a highly deviated well for precautionary reasons.
     1. Prior to precautionary back reaming, the hole should be cleaned up with maximum flowrate
   and rotation.
     2. The is some danger that cleaning up the hole before back reaming may be seen as a waste of
    time, given that the hole is to be back reamed anyway. However, the intent of circulating clean is
     to get the cuttings level down to a more manageable, lower risk level, before commencing a
     relatively high risk operation (i.e. back reaming).
     3. Back ream slowly out of the hole with maximum allowable flowrates and pipe rotation. If
    flowrate is limited it may be advisable to back ream in stages with several stops to clean the hole to
    a safer level of cuttings or at least redistribute them away from the BHA.
     4. It must always be remembered that a potentially dangerous cuttings dune exists above the BHA.
   The driller should be vigilant for an indication (e.g. increased torque) that the BHA is being pulled
    into this cuttings dune and that a pack off or stuck pipe is imminent if appropriate action is not
    taken.

joint. The indicator mark will be even with the rotary table when the packoff is properly landed. The
indicator mark should be made at a distance from the top of the packoff R/R tool, which is equal to the
