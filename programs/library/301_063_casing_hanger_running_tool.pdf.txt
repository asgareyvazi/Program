TECHNICAL TOOL DESCRIPTION

  The standard and high capacity MS-700
   Pressure Assist Drillpipe Running Tool
  (PADPRT and HC-PADPRT), is designed to run
   the MS series casing hangers and also run, set
   and test either the MS-1, MS-E or the SG-TPR
   seal assembly in a single or dual trip.

   The tool consists of three basic systems:

   1) The Cam and Latch ring System:
   Locks/releases the tool to the casing hanger.

   2) The Smart Ring System: Ensures the
   seal assembly is in the correct position in
    relation to the casing hanger wickers prior to
   energizing with pressure.

   3) The Pressure Assist System: Energizes
   the seal assembly with the assistance of
   15,000 lbs (6800 kgs) of drillpipe landing string weight. The pipe rams or an annular
   are closed and the pressure above the tool’s bulk seal is pumped up to 3000 psi
   (207 bar) to fully set the seal assembly E-ring.

                    OPERATIONAL SUMMARY
    1.   Rotate stem to the right 4 turns to lock the tool to the casing hanger. Verify that
        connector (51) and three each of plug (52) are not installed in tool.
    2.  Once the casing hanger is landed in the wellhead, set down all casing weight and
       15,000 lbs (6800 kgs) of landing string weight.
    3.   Rotate the drillpipe to the right ¼ turn to ensure that the stem is free prior to
         circulating and cementing operations.
    4.   Circulate and cement as per program.

    5.   With 15,000 lbs (6800 kgs) of drillpipe landing string weight down, rotate the string
       4 to 4-1/2 turns to the right. Travel of stem should be 8".
    6.   Pressure to 3000 psi (207 bar) with the 15,000 lbs (6800 kgs) down on the tool to
        energize the MS seal assembly. This will require 0.03 barrels of fluid.
    7.   Overpull the landing string to 100,000 lbs (45,359 kgs) to release from MS-1 seal
        with 6-1/4" travel and maintain during the testing of the seal assembly and the
       BOP’s.
    8.   Test the seal assembly and BOP.

    9.   Relax the overpull to 5000 lbs and rotate the stem to the right 4 turns to release the
        running tool from the casing hanger.
   10.  Pull the tool to the surface and inspect the lead impression blocks to determine
       whether or not the seal has been properly energized.

ROUTINE OPERATIONS

                    PRE-OPERATIONAL CHECKS

  Make sure that the tool is properly configured with the proper parts installed for the
   intended wellhead position.


                             NOTE:

 The tool is traditionally prepared with the standard set-up, which is to have items 9, 10,
 and 29 fitted ready to run and set a MS seal on to 9-5/8" - 10-3/4" or 13-3/8" - 14" casing
 hangers. Any attempt to use (11) and (12) and (47) will cause severe damage to both
 the wellhead and tool. This tool requires a different setting sleeve adapter, support ring,
 and bulk rubber packer for running the 7" or 7-5/8" casing hanger into it’s normal position
 in the wellhead. For the 7" or 7-5/8" hanger position; the setting sleeve adapter (11) and
 support ring (12) will be blue in color and the bulk rubber packer (47) will have a red stripe
 on its OD with the red stripe installed down.

Tool Handling
    1.   Tool supplied loose - remove the HC/PADPRT from the tool skid using a lift-cap
      made up to the drillpipe top connection and move to the required location.


    2.   Tool supplied made up to casing hanger complete with pup - remove the tool and
       hanger assembly from the shipping basket using suitable rated slings and move to
        the required location.

   Using elevators on the drillpipe pup, pick up and tail-in to the drill floor.

   The following checks, assume that the tool has already been fully serviced and
   re-cocked.

HC / PADPRT


                             NOTE:

 If the HC / PADPRT was made-up to the casing hanger on the beach, the tool will already
 have been configured to match the hanger. Verify that the plug launcher is installed in the
 hanger.

    1.  Ensure that the tool has been configured correctly for the casing hanger about to
       be run, (13-3/8" and 9-5/8" vs. 7" adapter).Verify that connector (51) and three
       each plug (52) are not installed in the tool.
    2.  Check that the tool has been fully re-cocked by inspecting the limit pins and their
         positions. Two of the limit pins, 180o from one another should be fully into their
        respective long slots and each retained with a leaf spring and a socket head cap
        screw. The remaining 4 limit pins should be in their positions but not in short slots.
        This is easily recognized as the remaining 4 leaf springs are deflected and again
        held with socket head cap screws.
    3.  A welding rod can be inserted into the hole in the underside of the tool and a
       measurement taken to the bottom of the cam mechanism. The reading should be
         7-1/2" to 8".
    4.  Check that the length of the tool from the top of the setting sleeve to the casing
       hanger landing shoulder is approximately 57-3/8".
    5.  Check that the bulk rubber packer is the correct one for the casing hanger to be run
       and  is in good condition. Refer to H56070 (PADPRT) drawing and  bill for
        information or H56570 (HC-PADPRT).
    6.  Check the anti-rotation key on the lower body for correct operation and that  it
        springs back after being depressed.
    7.  Using a set of chain tongs, rotate the tool stem 1/8 of a turn right hand to ensure
         that the stem is free.
    8.  Inspect the tool joint threads and sealing face for damage.

    9.  Inspect the 4-1/2" 8rd non-upset box connection for damage, then make up the
         4-1/2" 8rd pin x 4-1/2" IF pin crossover to check for damage if applicable.
   10.  Inspect the seal on the lower body of the tool that seals in the top of the casing
        hanger.

                           CAUTION:

Vetco Gray strongly recommends that casing scratchers and centralizers not be
used. Field experience has shown that parts can break off when passing through
the BOP stack, lodge in the wellhead, or scar the wellhead bore, preventing the
hanger or seal from seating correctly, or causing an MS-1 seal failure due to
wellhead bore damage..

  14.  Pick up the hanger assembly and remove the master bushings. Run the hanger
      assembly down through the diverter and paint the joint above the tool with white
        paint. Refit the master bushings. Take a note of the string weight indicator reading
      and continue to run the hanger on drillpipe.
  15.  Prior to passing the hanger assembly into the BOP’s, open choke and kill lines and
      compensator if the string weight allows. If the string weight is more than the rating
        of the compensator, it should be adjusted to support the weight of the landing
        string as soon as the casing weight drops off. Take a note of the string weight
       again, land the casing hanger and check that the measurements are the same as
       taken in Step 10 with 15,000 lbs down. Mark the drillpipe at the RKB (mark 1).


                            NOTE:

The following steps refer to making marks on the drillpipe during the setting operation. It is
essential that these steps be followed in deep water and are advantageous in shallow
water.

  16.  With the calculated landing string weight, overpull 100,000lbs (45,359 kgs) and
        hold. Mark on the drillpipe with another reference point (mark 2). This mark will be
      used after the seal has been set and an overpull of 100,000lbs (45,359 kgs) is
       required during seal and BOP testing.
  17.  Set weight back down and adjust landing string weight to neutral (+ or - 5000 lbs.).
       Rotate drillpipe to the right ¼ turn to ensure that the running tool stem will move
         freely. Hold neutral weight during circulating and cementing operations.
  18.  Start circulation and cementing program. To assist in bringing the cuttings to the
       surface, pump through mud booster lines.


                            NOTE:

It is strongly recommended that a pump be lined up on the BOP choke or kill line which is
closest to the top of the HC / PADPRT, and that mud should be circulated through this line
for the duration of the circulating and cementing operation, until the cement plug has been
bumped. A boost to mud velocity at this point may minimize the risk of having returned
solids setting in or around the running tool/casing hanger interface, where they can have
a detrimental effect on the seal setting process.

  19.  With the cement job complete, break off the cement line and open the landing
        string to atmosphere. Apply a 100,000 lbs overpull to verify mark 2. This mark
       should be in the same location as in Step 16. Apply 15,000 lbs (minimum of 10,000
        lbs - maximum 25,000 lbs) of landing string weight down on the tool and rotate the
       running string an additional 4 to 4-1/2 right hand turns. Using the reference wire,
      check that the string has dropped and measures 8" to 8-1/4" with 15,000 lbs (6800
       kgs) still down. Mark the drillpipe (mark 3).
  20.  Pressure test the surface equipment to 3000 psi down against a closed BOP
         fail-safe in the choke or kill line that is going to be used to energize the seal.
      Record the volume pumped to achieve the test pressure.
  21.  Close a set of pipe rams or an annular and line the top drive up to the trip tank or
       disconnect the top drive to monitor for returns. Apply and hold low pressure of 500
        psi (35 bar) below the closed ram to ensure that the rubber packer is sealing in the
       wellhead. With no leak-off, increase pressure in increments to 3000 psi (207 bar)
      down the choke or kill line to energize and lock the seal in position. Observe the
      volume of fluid pumped and compare to the previous volume. If the seal is not
       properly positioned, test fluid flow will be observed through the landing string or an
       increase in the trip tank volume. If continuous returns occur, the tool’s packer can
      be unseated by over pulling the landing string to 5000 lbs (2270 kgs) and setting
       15,000 lbs (6800 kgs) back down on the tool. This will wipe the wellhead sealing
       area free of any debris.


                            NOTE:

The total volume pumped to achieve 3000 psi seal setting pressure should be that noted
in Step 20 plus 0.03 barrels. To prevent collapsed casing due to setting pressure leaking
past the annulus seal, pumped volume should be accurately monitored against this
calculated volume.

  22.   If flow continues, an additional 4 right hand turns should be made and the tool
       should be recovered with the seal assembly. To re-run seal, refer to appropriate
       section of this procedure. If the MS seal is not set fully in the seal pocket , sitting
       plus 3/8" or greater off the landing shoulder, the bulk seal will not energize and the
        test fluid will bypass through the tool main body, around the stem OD and vent
       through the stem ID. It is imperative that the drillpipe is open to atmosphere, full of
         fluid, and monitored during functioning and test with the HC / PADPRT. A small
      amount will be displaced during functioning. (Less than 1 gallon displacement
       returns is normal.)

                            NOTE:

Some operators displace seawater after cementing, leading to a condition where the fluid
in the drillpipe is lighter than the mud on the annulus side, and creating a pressure load
acting on the bulk rubber seal due to mud column weight. This will result in an increased
load required to de-energize the bulk seal, best handled as follows:

1. Before applying the overpull to release the tool, run the following calculation.
2. Determine the weight of drillpipe fluid & riser annulus fluid & compute as follows:
       Differential pressure = (Mud weight – Seawater weight) x Water depth x .052
     Example: Differential pressure = (11.8 #/gal – 8.6 #/gal) x 6000 ft. x .052 = 999 psi
3. Pressure down drillpipe based on the calculated differential pressure acting on the
bulk seal.
4. Apply the 100,000 lb. overpull specified above to release the tool.
5. Continue with the standard operation as normal.

  25.  Close BOP pipe rams and annulars as required and test. Test per requirements in
      1000 psi (69 bar) increments after initial low pressure test. Closely observe fluid

                   FIELD RE-COCKING PROCEDURE

GENERAL

   Re-cocking of the tool ensures that the proper orientation exists between the release
   thread and the position of the cam. The tool must always be re-cocked before use.

   This procedure presumes that the tool has just been pulled from the hole after making a
   successful casing run. The release thread is disengaged. The stem (1) and setting
   sleeve (5) assembly may be anywhere between fully down and the position where the
    limit pins are up against the top of the short slots. The cam is at or near the bottom of it’s
    internal travel and the Rupture Disc Assembly has been used to vent any trapped
   pressure as per Cautionary Notes of this procedure and reinstalled .

   Rotating the stem in this procedure will require 500 to 1000 ft-lbs (608 to 1356 Nm)
   torque.
    1.  Remove the six limit pins (16) and leaf springs (4) by removing their screws (42).

    2.   Lay re-cocking skid and the tool in a horizontal position with the anti-rotation key
        facing up and secured from rotating by screwing in 2 x ½" UNC bolts into the
        release thread nut (14).
    3.   While reacting against the release thread nut (14), slowly rotate the stem (1) to the
           left (as viewed from the top of the tool) UNTIL the casing hanger latch ring (45) can
       be seen just BEGINNING TO EXPAND. THIS IS AN IMPORTANT POSITION!
       The ring expansion can best be viewed by observing the opening in the lock ring
       near the anti-rotation key (13).
    4.   Place matching turn indicator marks on the stem (1) and release nut (14). Rotate
        the stem to the left 4 complete turns and align indicator marks. Mark the two thread
         starts on the release sleeve (43) and on the release nut body (14) or release nut
        thread sleeve (53) and align ready for engagement. Using the re-cocking skid, or
        other means, move the stem (1) towards the release nut (14) or release nut thread
        sleeve (53) until the release thread is bumped up. Maintain compression on the
        stem.
    5.  Rotate the stem to the left 8 to 9 additional complete turns. As turning begins, make
        sure that the release thread on the stem is moving up into the release thread nut
        (14) or release nut thread sleeve (53). Once the thread has started, the
       compression can be released.
    6.  Check that the casing hanger lock ring (45) is fully collapsed and that it is loose in
           its groove. Rotate the stem a maximum of ¼ turn to the right to free any bind that
      may be in the cam mechanism.

               DISASSEMBLY/ASSEMBLY PROCEDURE


                            NOTE:

This tool requires a different setting sleeve adapter, support ring, and bulk rubber packer
for running in the 7" wellhead positions than for running in other wellhead positions.
Ensure that the proper setting sleeve adapter (8 or 11), support ring (10 or 12), and bulk
rubber packer (29 or 47) are used and that the bulk rubber packer (47) is properly
installed with the red stripe down. See drawing H56070 or H56070(HC).


  For ease of assembly, this tool can be built as five separate sub-assemblies:
          Body Assembly
           Release Nut Assembly
             Setting Sleeve Assembly
          Lower Setting Sleeve Adapter Assembly
          Stem Assembly
   1.   Coat all interior surfaces with Jet-Lube Alco-EP 73 Plus. All threaded components
      must be coated with Never-seize or equivalent.
   2.   With the body (3) in a horizontal position, use shop air to blow out the four ports.

   3.    Install the cam (20) with Never-seize. Thread the cam until the end of the cam is
         just inside the end of the body (3).
   4.    Install the latch ring (17) into cam. Make certain the latch ring lobes engage the ID
        of the cam. The ID of the cam and the latch ring should be well greased.
   5.    Install the retaining ring (35) below the latch ring (17).

   6.   Stand the body (3) upright. Install two o-rings (32) on OD of upper body. Set the
      body sub-assembly aside.
   7.   Stand the setting sleeve (5) upright with the major ID up. Coat the ID of sleeve with
       grease.
   8.    Install the polypak (33) with the lips facing down (toward the smaller or closed ID).
      Ensure that the o-ring has been removed from the polypak.
   9.   Set aside the setting sleeve sub-assembly.

  10.  Place the support ring (10 or 12) over the seal releasing latch ring (48). Align and
     make up 12 socket head cap screws (49). Torque to 50 ft-lbs (68 Nm).
  11.  Grease the ID of the bulk rubber packer (29 or 47) and slide it over the reduced OD
        of the setting sleeve adapter (8 or 11). As noted on the drawing , the bulk rubber
       packer (47) (for 7" position) has a “red stripe” which must be installed to be facing
      down when assembling to tool.
