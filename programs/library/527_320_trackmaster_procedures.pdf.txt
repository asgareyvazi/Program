TRACKMASTER SYSTEM

1.0  ABSTRACT

           The Smith Services-Red Baron Group TrackmasterTM is a single trip sidetracking sys-
     tem for a cased well bore. The running assembly consists of the Mill, Whipstock Assembly,
      Running Tool and Bypass Valve. The anchor subassembly can be either a Packer, Retriev-
      able Anchor, Mechanical Set Anchor, Retrievable Packer or ThruBore Packer. The objective
       of the TrackmasterTM System is to accomplish the following seven steps in a single trip; run
      the assembly, orient the Whipstock Assembly, set the Whipstock Assembly, break the Mill
      from the Whipstock, mill the window, drill the rat hole and pull out of the hole. The result is a full-
       size usable window achieved within a minimum of milling time and a full gage rat hole for the
       directional drilling assembly.

          The Whipstock Assembly may be oriented and set at any depth. It should be located
      immediately above a casing collar to provide a smooth I.D. in which to set the anchor and mill
       the window. A surveying system can be run with the TrackmasterTM System if there is a preferred
       orientation for the sidetrack. When orienting in a specific direction and if a hole angle of 2º or
     more exists at the setting depth, then it is recommended that the Whipstock Assembly be set
       0º to 90º to the right or left of high side of the hole. It should not be oriented greater than 105º.

         When orientation is achieved, the anchor is set. Both hydraulically and mechanically
       set anchors are available. The Packer and ThruBore Packer are permanent anchors. The
        latter has an open interior diameter bore. After setting, a load is applied to the drill string to
       part the Break Bolt between the Mill and Whipstock. At this point, string rotation may begin for
        milling of the window followed by drilling of the rat hole.

          The Retrievable and Mechanical Set Whipstock Assemblies can then be retrieved
        after being used. The Whipstock Assembly above the ThruBore Packer can also be retrieved.
     Two retrieval methods are available, Die Collar Assembly and the Hook Retrieval Tool.

2.0  SCOPE

             This procedure provides instructions for sidetracking a well in one trip with the
      TrackmasterTM System. General guidelines are given for the milling operation. Because each
       job is different, the optimum weight and rotary speeds are best determined on the job.

4.0   PREPARATION FOR SIDETRACK

       4.1   Equipment

             Note:    Before running the Mechanical Set Anchor make sure that the Foot is
                         larger than the I.D. bore of the bridge plug for proper actuation of the trigger
                        release.

              4.1.1     Account for and inspect all equipment for possible damage during shipment.
                   Check condition of all connections, fittings and any hydraulic lines on the
                     Whipstock Assembly.

              4.1.2     Caliper and record the O.D. of the Mill and the dimensions of the bottom
                       hole assembly components.

              4.1.3        If the Running Tool is to be used, remove the piston. Check the condition of
                       the piston and O-ring. The piston will be reinstalled after the system has
                   been filled with hydraulic fluid.

              4.1.4    Check the fit of the Orifices (nozzles) in the Lead Mill nozzle housings. The
                      threads must be clean, free of dings and burrs and clear of obstructions.
                  Use the Bottom Thread Taps if rework is needed.

              4.1.5    Check for proper make-up of the Break-off Plug and Control Line Hose
                     Adapter to the nozzle housings without the O-rings. Remove them before
                          transferring the Mill to the drill floor. Also, check for proper make-up between
                       the Break Bolt and Retaining Screw.

       4.2   Well

              4.2.1      Prior to running the equipment, make a gage ring and casing scraper run.

              4.2.2    Make a casing collar locator run. The bottom of the Whipstock Assembly
                   must be set 2 to 6 feet above a casing collar. Record the depth on the job
                          report.

4.3    Milling Fluid Guidelines

       4.3.1    The drilling fluid must be in good condition, clean and compatible with the
                 formation surrounding the milled window.

       4.3.2       Drill pipe filter screens and suction pit screens should be used to remove
                       all forms of debris. It is a good practice to circulate bottoms
             up to ensure that all the drilling fluid is in good condition prior to milling.
                Debris in the well bore will be taken into the drill pipe through the bypass
                valve if one is used. Any debris that enters the drill String can potentially
              cause problems.

       4.3.3    Polymer drilling fluids, in good condition, do not require any special
                adjustments. For water based and oil based drilling fluids, a ratio of plastic
                  viscosity to yield point (PV/YP) as close to 1.0 or less is ideal for steel
                  cuttings removal.

       4.3.4    The optimum flow rate is 50 gpm per inch of casing I.D. with the minimum
               being 30 gpm per inch of casing I.D. Although turbulent flow can remove
                  steel cuttings from the hole faster than laminar flow, it can cause problems
               such as bird nesting of cuttings and a restrictive buildup of cuttings. Laminar
                 flow provides the best overall properties for removal of steel cuttings.
                 Therefore, laminar flow is preferred over turbulent flow for lifting the cuttings
                 out of the well. In optimum conditions, it is recommended to start with a high
                laminar flow. Small adjustments can be made in the flow rates, rotary speed
              and weight while monitoring the cuttings for size, shape and thickness.

       4.3.5     High viscosity sweeps can be pumped periodically based on the amount of
                  cuttings returned to the surface or at a minimum, pumping a high viscosity
             sweep with the "bottoms-up" circulation prior to pulling out of the hole at the
              end of the sidetrack.

       4.3.6     Ditch magnets should be placed in the first tank beyond the shale shaker
                   prior to milling to capture cuttings. This will decrease mud pump damage
              due to cuttings contamination. The magnets should be periodically cleaned.
              They should stay in for several days after the sidetrack to recover any
                  additional cuttings that were trapped downhole or until no more steel cuttings
                are collected.

5.0  TRACKMASTER MAKE-UP PROCEDURE

             Note:    The Bypass Valve should be tested prior to being run in the hole.

             Note:        If using a Whipstock Assembly with the Mechanical Set Anchor, proceed
                         to Step 5.3. The Running Tool is not used in the assembly.

       5.1   Pick up Mill with a lifting sub. Set Mill in rotary table with drill collar slips and safety
            clamp. Remove lifting sub.

       5.2   Use lifting sub and elevators to pick up Running Tool. Make-up the connection with
                 Mill. Lift this assembly out of the rotary table.

       5.3   Use an air tugger or hoist line to pick up Whipstock Assembly by the catline hook.
          Remove the Shipping Container from the Hydraulic Set Anchor or the Shipping Cover
            from the Foot of the Mechanical Set Anchor. Hang the Whipstock Assembly through
             the rotary table. Secure with drill collar slips and safety clamp. The safety clamp should
           be set below the lower Control Line Assembly fitting in the face of hydraulic Whipstocks
                      .

       5.4    Pick up Mill and install Orifices (nozzles) and O-rings. Apply thread locking compound
              to Break-off Plug and Control Line Hose Adapter. Shoulder each part and tighten.

             Note:     Break-off Plug and Control Line Hose Adapter can be broken off prior to
                      running if using a Mechanical Set Anchor.

       5.5   Slowly lower the Mill to the top of the Whipstock face. Align the Break Bolt and Mill.
          Use a thread locking compound on the threads before fastening. Install the Rubber
            Slug and Retaining Screw and tighten.

             Note:        If using a Mechanical Set Anchor, proceed to Step 5.7.

       5.6   Connect the Control Line Assembly to the Control Line Hose Adapter. Use Teflon tape
           on the threads. Tighten fittings at both ends.

       5.7    Pick up and remove the catline hook, drill collar slips and safety clamp. Lower assembly
           and set it in the rotary table and secure.

             Caution: Do not pull the anchor above the rotary table when it is hanging from the
                            mill.

             Note:        If using a Whipstock Assembly with a Mechanical Set Anchor, proceed to
                     Step 5.10.

6.0  RUNNING PROCEDURE

       6.1   Run the Whipstock Assembly

              6.1.1      Trip into the hole slowly with the hook on the block in the unlocked position.
                      Monitor weight indicator for hole drag and trip tank for proper fill up.

             Note:    Use caution while tripping the Whipstock Assembly into or out of the hole.
                  Sudden starts, stops or dropping of the drill string may damage the Break
                          Bolt.

             Note:    For a Whipstock Assembly with a Mechanical Set Anchor, trip within 50
                         feet of the bridge plug. Care should be taken not to run into the bridge
                      plug and cause the anchor to set.

              6.1.2    When the desired depth is reached, work the string up and down to
                     determine hole drag. Slack off to a neutral weight.

       6.2    Orient The Whipstock Assembly

             Note:        If orientation of the Whipstock Assembly is not required, proceed to Step
                          6.3.

              6.2.1     Survey the orientation of the Whipstock Assembly. Turn the assembly to the
                       required tool face and repeat as needed.

             Caution: Make sure that the Bypass Valve does not close. This will cause Hydraulic
                     Set Anchors to prematurely set.

       6.3   Set The Whipstock Assembly

              6.3.1     Hydraulic Set Anchor

             Note:    High pressures may be experienced during the setting procedure. All
                      personnel must be made aware and all necessary precautions taken.

                         6.3.1.1     Once properly oriented, verify that the assembly is at the setting
                                    depth. Place a reference mark on the kelly.

                 6.3.2.1     Once properly oriented, set the Mechanical Set Anchor by
                            lowering the assembly to contact the bridge plug with

                                 to the anchor should be held by the anchor. The anchor should
                             not return to the bridge plug.

               (½) the Break Bolt shear value while maintaining 3000 to 3500 psi. Hold

      6.6     Drill The Rat Hole

             6.6.1    Make several reaming passes through the window and back to bottom to
                      clean up any burrs. Check against possible fill.

                       the Mill to verify that the window's condition is acceptable.



7.0   DRILLING RECOMMENDATIONS

       7.1      It is recommended that a bit or stabilizer not be rotated down the face of the Whipstock.
           Doing so may damage the edge or catch on the Whipstock.

       7.2   Leave the hook unlocked on all trips out of the hole. Pass through the window very
             slowly on all trips. Pick up the desired drilling assembly and continue normal drilling
              operations.

8.2   Hook Assembly Retrieval Tool

       8.2.1   A Whipstock Assembly with a Hook Slot in the Whipstock face can be
                  retrieved with the Hook Assembly Retrieval Tool. The anchor can be either
             a Retrievable Anchor, Retrievable Packer , Mechanical Set or a ThruBore
                Packer. The tool is designed to be hooked into the slot in the Whipstock
              and then pulled to release the assembly.

       8.2.2   A typical BHA for retrieval is; Retrieval Tool, joint of drill pipe, surveying
               equipment, crossover, heavy weight drill pipe and drill pipe.

       8.2.3    Make up the Hook Assembly Retrieval Tool. Orient the reverse side of the
              hook tang to the orienting sub. Note the orientation and placement of the
                nozzles in the tool.

       8.2.4    Lower the Retrieving Tool to just above the Whipstock and record operational
                parameters. Orient and jet wash the Hook Slot in the Whipstock.

       8.2.5     Position the hook tang below the slot on the Whipstock face after completing
                the washing operation.

       8.2.6     Slowly work the string up and down the Whipstock face to drop the hook
               tang into the slot. Be prepared to work the string with minor alterations in
                the hook tang attitude to engage the slot. Set down against the bottom of
                the slot to confirm engagement.

       8.2.7    Once engaged, pick up and apply overpull to release the assembly. Increase
                the overpull to compensate for drag if necessary. If release is not achieved
                  at the anchor overpull load listed above, increase the overpull in stages up
                  to maximum load for the hook, also listed above. Do not exceed this load.
