INTRODUCTION


CONTROL OF OPERATIONS ON THE MOBILE DRILLING UNIT


The intent of this Introduction is to define the role and responsibilities of all personnel
and service companies who will be involved with the drilling, re-entry and completion
phases associated with the Donghae-1project.

The overall responsibility for the management will be KNOC Drilling Manager.

The MODU will, at all times, be under the control of the DRILLING CONTRACTOR,
who will report directly to KNOC, on all matters associated with the rig, operations
and it’s equipment.

The onshore Completion Engineer, assisted by support  staff, shall report to the
drilling manager. They will control all the completion and associated activities, write,
issue and implement programmes and authorise any changes (which must be
confirmed in writing unless it is an immediate safety related issue).

The day to day operational safety and marine management of the MODU will be the
responsibility of the rig’s Drilling Superintendent, with input from his Drilling Team and
the KNOC Company Representative.

The on site management and co-ordination of the Completions Programme is the
responsibility of the KNOC Company Representative.  He will be assisted in his
duties by the KNOC Completions Engineer who shall be responsible for co-ordinating
the activities of the completion and testing service contractors.

The  Rig  Superintendent  shall be  responsible  for  the  implementation  of  the
Completions Programme and control of activities on the rig in consultation with the
KNOC Company Representative.

The various completions support and service company representatives will report and
identify themselves to the KNOC Company Representative and the KNOC Company
Completions Engineer on arrival.

2.   OPERATIONS PRIOR TO PICKING UP COMPLETION



2.1   Assemble well control crossover from (5 ½”) 4 1/2” Vam Ace tubing x 4 ½” IF

     TIW valve.


2.2   Make up emergency hang-off tool and stand back in derrick.


2.3   The Tubing Hanger Running Tool (THRT) should be made up to tubing hanger

      as per Tree manft.’s instructions. (O & M Manual Volume)


2.4    All Completion brines will be filtered through the filtration equipment as per

      Completion Fluids Programme.



2.5   The Test Choke Manifold and Subsea Test Tree (SSTT) will be equipped with

       points to facilitate chemical injection (methanol or glycol) for prevention /

       dissolution of hydrates and the injection of de-emulsifying or de-foaming

      agents.



2.6   Pressure testing of surface lines and equipment lines will be made with the rig

     cement unit.


2.7    All pressure tests must be recorded on charts and witnessed by a KNOC

       representative.


2.8  A 10-minute stabilised period will constitute a good test.




2.9   Ensure  all metering devices and recorders are accurately calibrated and

      zeroed:


2.10   Differential pressure meters should be calibrated with a manometer.


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 16 از 97 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
2.11   Static pressure meters should be calibrated using a DWT.


2.12  Liquid meters are to be calibrated by flowing a measured volume through each

       respective meter. Two consistent results are to be obtained.


2.13  Check the condition and size of orifice plates to be used. Retain all calibration

       charts.


2.14  Check  electronic  data  acquisition  instruments  readings  against manual

       readings.

4.    TESTING OF DOWN HOLE ASSEMBLIES


4.1    All equipment assemblies are to be tested to 5,000psi after make up on shore,

     and  all plugs, valves and  drifts run through them. And where possible

      witnessed by a KNOC representative prior to shipment.


4.2    Installation of Isolation sleeve into tubing hanger. (On the surface) (As per

    OEM Manual)


4.2.1 Ensure running tool is redressed with new shear pins in accordance with

      manft. Procedures.

     *NB - Sleeve can be set when hanger is in either vertical or horizontal attitude.

4.2.2  Inspect outside surfaces of sleeve, replace existing ‘O’ rings with new ones

      from redress kit.

4.2.3  Inspect seal bores in tubing hanger for damage, dress out if there are any.

4.2.4  Pick up running tool and locate in the fishing neck of the sleeve.

4.2.5  Raise tool/sleeve assembly.  Locate into tubing hanger bore and land off on

      the No-G shoulder.

4.2.6  Jar down on spang jars to shear screws in running sleeve, which will engage

     No-Go expanding ring.

4.2.7  Continue jarring down until such time as shear pins in running tool go then

      recover tool.

4.2.8  Carry out visual inspection to confirm sleeve is set.

4.2.9 Be specific to what drifting needs to be carried out i.e.


        Note:-     T.H - drifted with 3.701 “ drift (4 ½ “ 15# tubing)

                  Also run all slickline plugs or standing valves through the hanger


4.3   Measure and record the outside diameter, inside diameter and length of all

       tools and  assemblies. Note  all  the measurements on  the  Completion

      equipment record sheet.

5.   TREE HANDLING OPERATIONS


5.1   General


     The lifting and handling of the tree’s will be under the direction of the

      Tree Manufacturers Engineer, but all aspects of the Tree Handling on the

    MODU   will  be  supervised  by  the  DRILLING  Contractor’s  Rig

      Superintendent.


5.1.1 The Horizontal trees will be picked up from the supply boat and landed adjacent

       to the moon pool, (for the purposes of shipment the tree connector is locked onto

       the shipping skid)  all trees should be shipped to the Rig as one offshore

       transportation.


5.1.2 The Horizontal trees will be lifted from the supply boat using a sling attached to

       the Lifting sub on the trees


5.1.3 The tree’s needs to be picked up and lifted in one smooth and rapid motion.


5.2   Tree 1 will be landed onto the deck .


5.3   Tree 2, and 3 will need to be set down on the laydown area, unlocked from

        their lightweight transportation skid, and then set down on the deck


5.4   Transfer from deck of the rig to transportation trolley will be carried out using

      the bridge crane attached to the transportation-lifting sub. After setting down

     on the trolley and transportation skid it will be chained down. The TRT running

       tool will be made up on the tree.


      Note: -Due consideration of the weather conditions must be taken into account,

       the KNOC representative  will have the  final decision guided by the  tree

      manufacturers operations manual, any decision must take into consideration all

       inputs especially that from the Tree Manufacturers engineers.

•  Rig heave should be a maximum of 1 Meter.
•  Pitch and Roll 1.50 (Double amplitude).

      Maintenance Guidelines


7.5   The key to a successful filtered brine displacement is preparation. Clean

      working practices and good housekeeping cannot be over-stressed when

      using completion brines. All tanks and lines to be used for brine preparation

     and storage must be completely free of mud and solids.


7.6    All pits and ditches to be utilised for the mixing and storing of brines should be

      scrupulously cleaned using fire hoses.


7.6.1 The brine pit internals should be washed down using a high-pressure

      washgun, including gratings, “I” beams and pit room walls. Persistent dirt

      should be manually removed.


7.6.2 The surface mixing and mud transfer lines should be flushed out using

      seawater and detergent until clean.


7.6.3 The brine pits should be squeegee dry and if possible lines opened to check

        for any solids left behind.


7.6.4 Where possible all unnecessary water hoses and lines should be blanked /

      locked off to prevent brine contamination.


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 35 از 97 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
7.6.5  All dump valves should have the seals and valve seats checked to ensure they

      are in good order. They should be greased to ensure a good seal and if

       possible manually guided and checked to ensure a perfect fit when closed.


7.6.6  All ditch gates should be sealed on either side with silicon, this should be

      replaced if the gate is opened.


7.6.7  All cleaning should be done well in advance wherever possible. It is always

       better to do more than necessary than delay the operation due to dirty

      equipment.


7.6.8   It is not advisable to use barite or bentonite to seal gates and dump valves.

     They do not always form an effective seal and contaminate the brines with

       solids.


7.6.9 Pump packings should be inspected for severe leaks, as should the slipjoint

      during operations with brine in the hole.


7.6.10 The mud engineer is to check all equipment and lines prior to any mixing of

      completion fluid.


7.7     Fluid preparation


7.7.1   Once the pits and equipment are cleaned to the satisfaction of the mud

        engineer, then seawater may be added to the brine tanks in preparation for

        the salt as per formulation.


7.7.2  The salt may be added in bulk bags via the portable mixing hopper, or in 50-

       kg sacks through the conventional hopper. Should the portable hopper be

       used, then every attempt should be made to ensure there is minimal

       backpressure against the hopper, as this will detrimentally effect its

       performance.


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 36 از 97 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
7.7.3  Once mixed the fluid should be allowed to stand without agitation before

          filtering, this will enable solids to settle and minimise filter cartridge usage.


7.7.4  The DE and twin pot cartridge filter units are used in series, depending on the

        condition of the mixed brine, newly mixed fluid should be all able to be

         filtered in series, through DE filter, then polished off using the cartridge filter.


7.7.5   Brine should be filtered from one tank to another in one pass.


7.7.6  When filtering, the brine should not be allowed to suck dry from the tank, as

         this will unduly plug the filters with residual solids in the suction loss.


7.7.7   Prior to the final displacement the brine should be treated with the required

         inhibitors.


7.8    Clean-up and Displacement


7.8.1 The hole should be displaced to seawater at the fastest possible rate, under

        controlled conditions. If possible sufficient mud should be retained on surface to

        re-displace to the well in the event an influx occurs.


7.8.2 Once displaced to seawater, the well should be observed for flow, prior to

        continuing with the clean up.




7.8.3  The following combination of pills should be circulated round at the highest

      pump rates dumping returns:

         1. 100 bbls high viscosity seawater.

         2. 100 bbls Detergent pill.

         3. 100 bbls Flocculant pill.

         4. 100 bbls high viscosity seawater.


7.8.4   Continue to circulate until the seawater returns are clean (< 30 NTU).

7.9.1   There should always be a stock of filtered brine to maintaining hole volume,

      and supply sufficient fluid to operate the downhole equipment.


7.9.2   Daily checks should be done to ensure that the brine has not been

        contaminated, and that the density remains constant.


7.9.3  The hole should then be displaced to filtered, inhibited brine.

8.    TCPG PERFORATING PROCEDURES

       Overview


      The purpose of this job is to perforate the 9 5/8ths casing with 6” guns, loaded

        with 12 spf HMX DP charges. A Time-Delay/Drop Bar firing head (TDF/DBF) is

         to be run at the top) section of the guns.(The Actual Firing Head to be

       Great care must be taken when applying pressure to the well, as the initiation

        pressure of the firing heads will be below the test pressure of the completion.

        All volumes for testing must be closely monitored to ensure that there is no

                  PART THIRTEEN


Chrome Tubing Handling and Running
INTRODUCTION

1.1    This document is intended to give guidance in the handling and running of 4
        1/2” Vam Ace chromium tubulars.

1.2    Any action during the storage, handling and running of Chrome tubulars
       which causes the formation of hot spots (localised hardening) can lead to
        stress cracking, embrittlement and corrosion.  It is therefore essential that
      more care be taken with these tubulars than is normally adopted for carbon
        steel tubulars.

1.3    At  all times when handling Chrome tubulars, contact with other metallic
       equipment, (slings, wire brushes. hammers, etc) which are not made of like
        material should be avoided.

SHIPPING

2.1    Chromium tubing ideally should be shipped to location in proprietary Handling
       systems that utilise combinations of steel and epoxy urethane to keep the
       tubes apart, immobile and free from damage. Threads will be coated with Jet
       Marine corrosion inhibiting storing compound or similar.
2.2    For these wells the tubing will be in the wooden shipping system.


RIG SITE PREPARATION AND INSPECTION

3.1    Before the tubulars are transferred from the boat to the installation,  it is
      recommended that the completion supervisor conducts a pre-job meeting
        with the  rig crew and  all other personnel involved  in handling chrome
        tubulars. An explanation of the required handling procedures and reasons for
       running chrome tubulars will highlight the crew's awareness.

3.2    Before unloading tubing ensure that threat protectors are tight and secure.
      Webbing Slings should be used for handling tubulars.

3.3    Rack pipe on non-metallic supports in sufficient number to accommodate the
       weight and number of joints and be properly spaced to prevent bending and
       thus avoid water accumulation. Ensure that the pipe does not come into
       contact with steel supports by separating each row with hardwood strips.

3.4    Pipe should be placed on rack with enough space to allow 360 degrees
        rotation without the tubing banging. Do not stack tubing higher than five tiers.


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 71 از 97 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
3.5   Remove thread protectors (use only wooden or plastic mallets), remove any
       desiccant packages and drift all joints using a 42" x 3.705” 4 1/2” non-metallic
           drift. Avoid hammering and other violent impacts to the tubulars.

3.6    Blow through the pipe with compressed air to clear away moisture and loose
        scale. Measure pipes and number each joint in consecutive order.

3.7    Clean the pipe threads and the thread protectors with hot water or a steam jet
        with detergent. Do not use diesel or paraffin as a cleaning agent. The thread
      compound will have difficulty adhering to the surface that has been cleaned
        with diesel or paraffin.

     NOTE:      Ensure thread protectors are thoroughly cleaned

3.8    Never clean the threads with a wire brush.   If needed, use a non-metallic
         bristle
       brush.

3.9   Have offshore thread inspectors perform deck inspection. Visually inspect
       pipe body, thread coating and the seal areas for any sign of corrosion or
      damage. Burrs or minor thread damage away from the seal area may be
       repaired using a file. Any joint that fails the inspection should be rejected. The
        rejected joint should be clearly marked with a red band and the reason for
        rejection painted on the joint.

     NOTE:       Seal areas should never be ground or filed. Where any
                   thread dressing has been required using a file or emery
                    cloth has taken place, thoroughly clean afterwards.

3.10     If the tubulars are to be run right away, dry off threads thoroughly on box. pin
      and protectors with clean cloth and compressed air.

3.11   Inspect the protectors and thoroughly clean   if they show any signs  of
       contamination.

3.12     If the tubulars are to be left for more than 2-3 days before running, apply a
         light even coat of API Modified thread compound to box end (after ensuring
       thread surfaces are dry) and replace the thread protectors. By applying the
       thread compound. The thread condition will not be able to be visually re-
       inspected when the casing is being run.  It is therefore imperative that the
       thread inspection is meticulously performed when tubulars are on the pipe
        rack.

3. 13    If the tubulars are to be left for more than one week at the rig site. Use Jet
       Marine or a light oil instead of casing compound to coat the threads.  This
       should be thoroughly cleaned off prior to running.

4.1   Move pipe to catwalk using webbing slings.

4.2     Pull pipe up V-door using single joint pick-up elevators on air tugger. With

     ENSURE THREADS ARE CLEAN AND DRY. Visually inspect box end

MAKING UP

5.1     Prior to Make-up of any Chrome tubular connection. the following procedural
       checks should be carried out with regard to connection monitoring system
        (including integrated control).

        1.     Calibrate the Integrated Control System with the power tong for speed
                control. In high gear. the setting should be 15 RPM. In low gear, the
                setting should be 3 RPM.

        2.     Calibrate the load cell to zero and shut output valves. This function will
             check that the load cell is fully operational.

        3.      Clarify that the turns encoder resolution is 1000 pulses per one
                rotation of the power tong rotor.

        4.    Check recommended tubular data is correct 4 1/2" 151b/ft 13% Cr L-
            80 Vam Ace. The parameters below are set  to ensure that the
            premium connection  will shoulder out below 3540  ft/lbs and no
              deformation of the primary seal face occurs as optimum torque is
              achieved. Thread torque build up prior to shoulder should be smooth.

                a.    Reference Torque = 10% of recommended optimum torque
                   (542 ft/bs).


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 73 از 97 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
                b.   Maximum Shoulder Torque 70% of recommended optimum
                    torque (3794 ft/lbs).
                 c.    Minimum Recommended Torque = 4880 ft/lbs.
                d.    Optimum Recommended Torque = 5420 ft/lbs.
                e.   Maximum Recommended Torque = 5960 ft/lbs.
                        f.    Minimum recommended turns for premium connection = 0.
                g.   Maximum recommended turns for premium connection = 1.5.
                h.    Reference dump torque = varies between 6  - 9%  of the
                recommended optimum torque (217 - 325 ft/lbs).

5.2    After stabbing, continue make up using   fluid grip tong with integrated
        control system. The computer gradually increases the tubular make up speed
         until the pre-set RPM value of 15 RPM is reached. If the torque exceeds the
        pre-set safety limit (reference dump torque 325-488 ft/lbs), the process halts.
       This will protect the tubular from excessive torque during the vulnerable stage
        of the initial make-up.

5.3    Running in: After the reference torque is reached, the computer controls the
      3 RPM setting until the shoulder point is reached.

5.4    Shoulder point: At the shoulder point, tests are conducted to determine that
              if the make-up conditions are within pre-set limits  i.e. maximum shoulder
        point is less than 3794 ft/lbs.  If these tests are acceptable, the make-up is
       allowed to continue. Otherwise the makeup is aborted in a controlled manner.
       This  will minimise the  risk of damaging the connection. The connection
       should be backed out using the integrated control system with the same
       present RPM parameters. The connection can be remade if acceptable.

5.5   Approach to target: To avoid overshoot of the optimum torque the computer
       decreases the RPM too less than one. When optimum torque is reached, the
       computer can either terminate the make-up by a controlled release of the
       torque or go into torque hold.

BREAKING OUT

      Breaking out mode: It is important to control the breakout speed, to prevent
       the connection rotating  at high torque,  this to avoid  galling. The speed
     demand is gradually increased from zero to a pre-set limit. Rigid control of
    RPM prevents rapid release of stored energy, thus avoiding galling.

6.1    The fluid grip tong with integrated control should be used for the complete
       break out cycle. An integral back-up should be placed on the coupling to
       prevent coupling back out. The back-up friction jaw should be placed on the
       bottom half of the coupling.

6.2   Do not maintain tension on the pipe when backing out connections. Once the
       connection breaks and one full turn is made, the power tongs will gradually
       increase to a controlled speed break out for the remainder of the thread.
       Maintain the pipe in a vertical, free turning position.

[ صفحه 74 از 97 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
6.3   Once the threads are disengaged  (the pin  will jump  in the box), spin
       approximately 1/3 of a turn before pulling out. Use a stabbing guide while
          lifting joint Out of the box to avoid seal damage if "jump out" occurs.

6.4    Replace thread  protectors before  laying out  or racking back  tubing. A
       decision will be made at the time whether racking back tubing is feasible
       dependent on rig capability and the operational plan. For example.  if the
        tubing will only be racked back for a very short time, standing back should be
       considered. If so, derrick fingers should be wrapped with rubber or wood. Pin
      and box protectors should be used.

6.5    Each broken connection should be thoroughly cleaned with a high-pressure
      washer and inspected for damage and re-greased.

                  SCSSSV TEST PROCEDURE


1.      Visually check valve for damage and record relevant valve information. Check
        that all connections are tight with set screws secure.

2.     Flush control line from test pump to remove air and connect to control line port
      on SCSSSV. Function test the SCSSSV on the pipe rack.. Record opening
      pressures and measure the amount of control line fluid bled back after closing
       the valve. Repeat operation three times and record results on test sheet.

 NOTE :    Use Marston Bentley HW540 water based glycol control line fluid.
                            Drift the SCSSSV 3.826 inches
             Record in the Tubing Retrievable SCSSV Test Report
            .

3.     Pick up SCSSSV and make up to completion string. Flush control line and
      make up to control line port using SCSSSV Suppliers Fitting.

       •     Pressure test control line to 7500 psi. (This is 2500 psi. above the
               anticipated maximum C/L pressure). Check the amount of fluid bled
            back after the pressure test and record on data sheet.

       •     Function test the SCSSSV three times. Record opening pressures and
                 fluid returns. Record data on sheet.

4.     Continue to run completion.

     NOTE : Maintain 5000 psi. control line pressure whilst running in

5.    Space out and install tubing hanger. Make up the control line to the underside
        of the tubing hanger using the Inconel fitting. Pressure test the control line
       through the hanger to 7500 psi. Function test at 5000psi the SCSSSV three
       times and record opening pressures. Check the amount of fluid bled back
        after each test and record same.

6.      After the tubing hanger is landed and set, function test the valve three times
       using the workover controls umbilical and record opening pressures. Test the
        control line to 5000 psi. Check the amount of fluid bled back after each test
      and record.
