2.0	Shah Deniz Project Introduction & SD A-03 Overview
Shah Deniz is a gas condensate reservoir in the Caspian Sea, offshore Azerbaijan. To date, three exploration / appraisal wells, namely SDX-1, 2 and 3 have been drilled on the structure. Three wells, SD A-01, SD A-02 & SD A-03 will be pre-drilled, at the E2 location, from the Istiglal semi-submersible rig and tied back to a jack up platform. Pre-drill wells SD A-01 and SD A-02 have now been drilled. SD A-03 will be the third pre-drill development well and should be spudded around 21st February 2004.
A further six production wells will be drilled from the platform starting in 2006. An additional five sub-sea wells will be drilled at a later stage from the E3a location to maintain plateau production. The primary objective for the first three pre-drills and six platform wells will be to produce SP2 and SP3 with re-completion potential in the Balakhany VIII.
The three reservoir targets are the Balakhany VIII and Sand Packages 2 and 3 (SP2, SP3) of the Upper Fasila. SP4, below SP3 will also be penetrated for production later in the life of field.
The reservoir section is at high pressure, between 9,250psi and 12,000psi, but low temperature. Maximum bottom hole temperature, at approximately 6000 m TVD, is around 100oC to 105oC. Stage 1 of the field development is based on a platform, plus sub sea facility, in the East Flank. These facilities are planned at locations, denoted E2 and E3a, in water depths of 101 m and 293 m respectively.
Fig 1.1 presents a plan view of E2 and E3a locations relative to the Shah Deniz structure and the location of the three exploration / appraisal wells, SDX-1, SDX-2 and SDX-3. SDX-1 is the closest offset to the platform location E2. Probable bottom hole targets are shown for both E2 and E3a wells, based on base SP3.  Note: SD A-01 is AL, SD A-02 is AE and SD A-03 is the AM target location.
Figure : Platform and Sub-Sea Locations Relative to the Shah Deniz Structure
The AM well has been designed to step out 2.3 km from the E2 location, in line with the plan to progressively increase step-out for each pre-drill well. The well is planned to deviate to the southeast to penetrate the reservoir section in a more crestal location than any of the previously drilled wells. E2-AM will penetrate the Top Balakhany 8A formation approximately 300m vertically updip from SDX-2 (the previously highest penetration point), and penetrate the base SP3 approximately 420m vertically updip from SDX-2.
E2-AM has been targeted to meet the objectives both of drilling an updip location with good reservoir properties and of extending our experience of drilling progressively more deviated wells.
The well has also been designed to drill to the defined target zone within a set of drilling constraints.  These drilling constraints have been deliberately chosen to allow graduated learning in the drilling of increasingly deviated and complex wells.  The drilling constraints used in the design of E2-AM were:
Drill the overburden section with an inclination angle not greater than 45 degrees
Penetrate the upper Sabunchi section with at least a 0.1 SG drilling window
Drill the SP1 to SP2 section with an inclination angle not greater than 20 degrees
The narrowest drilling window in the entire borehole occurs in the upper Sabunchi formation where sands have the highest amount of overpressure.  The well has been designed to allow at least a 0.1 SG drilling window between the pore pressure in the highest pressured Sabunchi sand and the Brumfield fracture gradient in the surrounding shales (figure 3).  The Brumfield fracture gradient used here is unmodified for tectonic stress (i.e. has not been elevated to account for compressive stress effects).  Although this window is narrower than that encountered in the previous predrill wells it is still wider than that drilled by SDX-2 (0.04 SG window).
The E2-AM planned trajectory drops angle in the Balakhany formation (by the top of SP1 (Balakhany 9).  Low inclination angles are considered necessary to maintain reservoir isolation by ensuring cement integrity around liners, and isolating high pressure SP1 sandstone from the lower pressure SP2 and Balakhany 8 gas reservoirs.  Low inclination angle also helps minimise borehole breakout through these zones.
Taking into account the constraints above, the well trajectory for E2-AM has been designed to penetrate one geological target (SP3) and one drilling target (SP1) to facilitate drilling the well safely. The well also penetrates SP1 with TD below SP4. This the four main targets and penetration points are as follows :-
Drilling Target : Top Sabunchi penetration point (on the se250 depth surface) which would allow a 0.1 or greater drilling window.
Top SP1 penetration point (on se284 depth surface) to indicate where inclination should be < 20
Primary Reservoir Target : Base SP3 penetration point (on se300 depth surface) to target the seismic amplitude anomaly for the SP3 sand.
Base SP4 penetration point.
The well trajectory is vertical from the surface to 30” conductor shoe at 182m MDBRT. It then maintains 4 inclination (away from the other template slots) until the KOP in the 16 ½” section. Below the KOP inclination then builds at 2 deg./30m until c.42 is achieved.  This angle is maintained in the 12 ¼” section through the Sabunchi and into the  Balakhany. Inclination is dropped in the 8 ½” section from c.42 to c.16 at 1.5 deg./30m by top of SP1, then maintained until the TD of the well.
Well E2-AM will also meet the additional objective of providing information on the possible presence or absence of boundaries and compartmentalisation in the crestal area of Shah Deniz (via MDT results which will give an indication of reservoir interference/communication).  Previous mapping over the crest of the Shah Deniz structure identified a number of apparent near- vertical discontinuities.  These could be interpreted as the result either of faulting or of poor data imaging (due to uncertainties in the velocity model used in migration).  One of the main objectives of the recent Pre-Stack Depth Migration (PSDM) reprocessing effort at Shah Deniz has been to determine whether the number of such discontinuities is reduced with further iteration of the velocity model. Recent results of PSDM reprocessing do show a significant reduction in the number of discontinuities.

Background Data for 16-1/2” & 12 ¼” Hole Sections
On SD A-03, as a result of it’s updip location, there is a tight (0.12 sg) Pore/Frac window, through the Sabunchi, just below the 13 3/8” shoe at the top of the 12 ¼” section. This results in SD A-03 being different to SD A-01,2 in the following ways.
The mudweight used in the 12 ¼” section is higher than normal due to the increased pore pressure at the top of the section. The chosen downhole mudweight of 2.05sg provides :-
Approximately 100 psi overbalance to predicted Pore Pressure (Swabbing Awareness is crucial).
Approximately a c.0.06sg ECD/Fracture Strength Window.
As a result of the above, at the base of the 12 ¼” section, the reducing fracture pressure (in the Balakhany) comes very close (0.04 sg) to the Equivalent Mud Weight (EMW) when circulating (ECD).  Accordingly, the 12 ¼” TD / 10” Casing shoe criteria has been made shallower to avoid catastrophic losses in the Bal 8A where the pore pressure regression occurs (as experience on SDX-2 in similar circumstances).
To allow the required higher MW to be used in the 12 ¼” section, placement of the 13 3/8” shoe, slightly deeper than usual, in the Sabunchi Horn (rather than above it) will be essential to get the required increased shoe strength (as shown by well SDX 2).
NB : If there is any doubt that the 13 3/8” shoe may not be in the Sabunchi Horn then a dedicated cleanout trip, with a BHA consisting of a 14 ¾” bit directly below a 16 ½” Ander-Reamer, will be made to open up the 14 ¾” rathole.
This will allow the shoe to be set deeper with a greater confidence that it is in the Sabunchi Horn. Correct placement of the 13 3/8” shoe combined with a satisfactory LOT (2.15sg), is a key aspect to the successful delivery of SD A-03 in that it will avoid having to use the 11 ¾” contingency liner option early in the execution of the well due to insufficient 13 3/8” shoe strength.
Since the 13 3/8” shoe is being ‘pushed’ slightly deeper into the Sabunchi Horn the mudweight required for the 16 ½” section will be higher than normal – 1.91sg Versus 1.83 sg DHMW.
Correspondingly, increased awareness/management of Surface/Downhole Mudweight, Overbalance, Pit Levels/Management, Kick Tolerance, ECD, Surge/Swab trends & Wellbore Stability will be crucial in order to avoid :-
Exceeding fracture strength which could cause losses.
Letting the well go under balance which could cause a Well Control situation.
To this end specialist engineers from “KSI” (a company which specialises in ECD mgt) will be on board during the end of the 16 ½”  section and during all of the 12 ¼” section with the sole objective of assisting the on/offshore teams in managing ECD during Drilling, Tripping & Casing Running/Cementing Operations.

A strategy to mitigate against excessive casing wear was put in place for SD A-01.  A baseline MFCT was run inside 10-3/4"x10" casing and a post drilled MFCT run after +/- 500 krevs.  The two logs indicated that virtually no wear had occurred during this time. During drilling and coring of the 8½" section metal returns from ditch magnets versus cumulative revs have been recorded.  This provides a qualitative measure of casing wear against time and provides a baseline against metal returns can be compared for SDA-02.
A final MFCT was also run the after 5-1/2” liner was set on SDA-01 to further evaluate actual wear during slow drilling 8-1/2” section to TD.  Again negligible casing was observed.
To date on SDA-02 (part-way through well suspension) significant indications of casing wear have not been observed although the Multi Finger Caliper Tool (MFCT) has not yet been run at TD so these results are not conclusive.
The above results from SDA-01 and SDA-02 and comparison of the SDA-01, 2, 3 trajectories, indicate that the 8½" section on SDA-03 can be drilled without casing wear protection. However, should metal returns seem excessively higher than expected casing wear protection will be needed as a contingency.
Recommendations for SDA-03
Do not run a baseline MFCT on the production casing.  This proved to be of only limited value for SDA-01.
Ensure awareness raised on rig with respect to criticality of casing wear and influencing operational factors (RPM).
Ensure ditch magnets are used while drilling the 8 ½” hole section. Ditch magnets should be monitored on a 12 hourly basis and results recorded for rig site trend analysis.  Results should be directly compared with those from SDA-01 to look for strong deviations from this trend.
Run a MFCT log at TD as final check and record of casing wear. Compare results to those from SDA-01 and 02.

19.0	Cement Program
The detailed cement program completed for SD A-02 remains as the baseline for SD A-03. For SDA-03 extensive work has been done in reviewing the 22” cement jobs on SDA-01 and 02, resulting in a different slurry recipe for SDA-03 in an attempt to eliminate gas and SOBM flow from the wellhead ports.
The end of well reports for SD A-01 and 02 includes job evaluations and lessons learned, and will be available for consultation.  Included in this Well Program are design summaries of each cement job.  These contain information on depths, hole size, fluid design, properties, volumes, flow rates, centralization, and product and equipment needs.
A final CemCADE™ simulation and cement job program will be issued before each cement job, using real well and fluids data.
Finally, after the job is executed, all data will be compiled into a thorough end of well report document, including lessons learned and areas for improvement in future operations.  A job report template can be found in Cement Program Appendix and in the Drilling Operations Manual.  This must be completed by the offshore cementer and sent to the engineer in town after every cement job to ensure all data is captured.  This will aid in the preparation of complete and useful job evaluations.
Design Methodology
In simple terms, the objective of a casing cement job is to remove the mud in the annulus and replace it with cement, thus providing an annular pressure seal.  Poor displacement mechanics will lead to incomplete displacement of the mud and this will leave a continuous mud channel across zones of interest, thereby permitting undesirable communication between zones.
Key factors affecting mud removal are -
mud, spacer and cement rheologies and densities
mud conditioning before cement placement
hole size, shape, angle, and condition
centralization of the casing
displacement rates
Many of the above factors are inter-related and adjustments in one have an impact on other factors.  Where relevant the above factors will be discussed in the specific casing section.
To enhance the possibility of success in meeting the key objective of mud removal, an advanced software package called WELLCLEAN II™ has been used.  This software provides a 2D representation of the wellbore, taking into consideration the factors listed above.  A visual image is collected during the placement simulation that provides an indication of the fluid profiles and the degree of success anticipated in removing the mud from both the narrow and wide side of the casing.  This model only considers mechanical aspects of displacement, and not any chemical interactions or incompatibilities between fluids.
From the CemCADE™ simulation results, it is clear that the most important factors in cementing the critical reservoir section on Shah Deniz are the size, condition and shape of the open hole.  Hole ovality was an issue on the exploration wells, particularly on SDX 3 (SD-TN-DE-020).  The ovality is directly attributable to tectonics and mechanical instability of the wellbore.  Ovality makes centralization and mud removal difficult and is therefore a major issue for cementing success.  Every step must be taken to eliminate it.  Also, if an overly large hole is drilled then a successful primary cement placement will clearly be impossible.
Schlumberger policy for thickening time is:  “The minimum thickening time for a cement slurry is the greater of the following:
1.5 times the job placement time
job time + 2 hours”
This policy has been followed in all designs.

SUMMARY OF SITE CONDITIONS AT PROPOSED SHAH DENIZ E2 PLATFORM
23.0	Challenges, Risks and Contingencies
23.1	Shallow Gas
There is a high frequency of shallow gas well control incidents in the Azerbaijani sector of the Caspian. Seismic interpretation of Shah Deniz identifies a high risk of shallow gas on Shah Deniz. The platform location is selected in an area of negligible/low risk of shallow gas and a high-resolution site survey has been run to confirm this. Pilot hole concluded shallow gas risk as negligible. This was confirmed in SDA-01.
23.2	Mud Volcanoes
There are a number of mud volcanoes on Shah Deniz. The risk of mud volcano activity affecting drilling operations is very low. Historic mudflows can be seen from seismic and may be encountered while drilling but should not cause significant drilling issues.
23.3	Shallow Water Flows
The rapid depositional environment may result in over-pressured sands as is typically seen in deep-water environments. Shallow water flows were experienced on SDX-3 and SD-6 where pressures of 1.49sg were experienced at 741m MDBRT in SDX-3 and at 716m MDBRT in SD-6. Sands were intersected by SDX-1 at around 707m MDBRT where they were drilled with a pilot hole and seawater. Although the sands flowed, causing tight hole, only minor gas percolation was observed and the sands were therefore initially interpreted as normally pressured. However, circumstantial evidence could be interpreted to suggest that the “Q” interval may be overpressured by up to 220 to 300psi at the SDX-1 location. These sands can be mapped by seismic and are laterally extensive, indicating similar pressures are present at the platform location. High percentage connection gas (17%) was observed at 752m (Q-7 sand) in SD A-01, which suggests that this interval is overpressured by up to 480psi.
Shallow Faults
Seismic analysis has identified significant shallow faulting on Shah Deniz. Faulting on and around the promontory, where E2 is positioned, can be divided into three distinct regimes as follows:
Ring faults are located to the west of the location and extend to depths greater than 1200m. Their closest surface expression is ~130m away.
Shallow detachment faults (controlling seafloor subsidence) are on the east flank and appear to tip out into the “Q” at ~700m. The closest shallow detachment faults are ~350m from the platform location .The platform site is selected to avoid these shallow faults.
The third group of faults are at an intermediate level. This intense zone of normal faults extend from approximately the running sands at ~600m down to the lower Apsheron at ~1200m. These faults extend across the promontory with a NW to SE orientation and are downthrown to the east.
‘Q’ Sands, 22” Cementing & 19 ½” Hole Well Control
SDX3 experienced significant problems due to a shallow high pressure running sand. Control of this running sand was lost due to riser less drilling. The development wells have a 22’’ surface casing set above the top of the ‘’Q” interval.  The “Q” interval will then be drilled with BOP control.
In SDA-01 the intent was to set 22” casing above the “Q” interval sands such that sands Q1 to Q4 could be drilled with a weighted mud system in the subsequent hole section. Drilling 26” hole to 612m TVDBRT, however, “Q-1” was penetrated. After having cemented the 22” casing, and prior to drilling out the shoe, a flow was observed at the wellhead. Such an observation implies possible flow from the “Q-1” interval. A low LOT provides evidence for a channel in the cement.
To minimise the risk of this re-occurring in SDA-02, the 26” hole was TD’d 10m above the top of the “Q-1”. To further ensure a better cement job, centraliser numbers were also increased.  However on SDA 02 a lower-than-expected LOT was again experienced and gas bubbles (and SOBM, later on during the 16 ½” section) were observed from wellhead ports.
To cater for the observed low LOT’s at 22’’ shoe and corresponding Well Control aspects & concerns raised by the BP Reps, the following measures will implemented:
Field wide dispensation for kick tolerance below 25 bbls (BP policy) if required.
Review well control procedures & detailed Risk Assessment for subsequent 12 ¼’’ x 19 ½’’ section
From initial investigations on SDA 02 it seems that the observed gas may be coming from an interval exposed in the 26” section and that the source of the SOBM may that lost on SDA 01 (ie. breeched across at the 22” shoes). However it should be noted that investigations are still ongoing as to the source of the SOBM (ie. SDA 01 or SDA 02) and the true answer will probably not ever be known. Regardless, this is unacceptable from well integrity and environmental aspects. To this end the cementing procedure for the 22’’ casing is under review for SDA 03.
Working up the above counter measures, to these Well Control & Environmental issues, may result in an increased likely hood of running the 18” liner.
Mudweight/WBS/Kick Tolerance/ECD Management
The key area on SDA 03 where management of the all the above aspects will be essential is through the tight (0.10 sg) Pore/Frac window, through the Sabunchi, just below the 13 3/8” shoe at the top of the 12 ¼” section. Due to the tight Pore/Frac window (0.1sg) at the top of the 12 ¼’’ section (through the Sabunchy) the required mud weight for this section on SDA –03 will be higher than SDA 01/ 02. (2.05sg DHMW V’s 2.00sg normally).
Following comparison of ECD (with this increased DHMW) to fracture strength throughout the 12 ¼’’ interval, it has been necessary to make the TD shallower to reduce the risk of losses. The risk associated with the resulting longer 8 ½’’ section are considered to be more manageable than if 12 ½’’ TD had not be changed.  Correspondingly management of ECD will be crucial too in order to not ‘use up’ the small margin between MW and Frac.
To allow the required higher MW to be used, placement of the 13 3/8” shoe, slightly deeper than usual, in the Sabunchi Horn will be essential to get the required increased shoe strength (as shown by explo well SDX 2). To achieve this 16 ½” TD/ 13 3/8” shoe criteria, within the 80m MD Sabunchi Horn thickness, the relative spacing of 14 ¾’’x16 ½’’ BHA and rathole have been reviewed and modification of the BHA is NOT deemed necessary due to thickness of the Horn.
Tectonic Stresses
Analysis of caliper data from the exploration / appraisal wells indicates large amounts of breakout within shales above and within the reservoir. Interpretation suggests tectonically stressed formations below the Surakhany Anhydrites. The resulting breakout could cause drilling and casing running challenges in deviated wellbores. The tectonic stresses could also cause long-term deformation of casing strings.
23.8	High Collapse Pressures
High pore pressures, in both the overburden and reservoir, result in many casings strengths being driven by collapse rather than burst pressures. Within the overburden, pressures are highest within the Sabunchi, up to 12,950psi. Within the reservoir, pressures are highest within the water bearing sand SP1, up to 14,150psi.
23.9	High Burst Pressures
High downhole pressures also create high burst pressures whilst drilling, in the event of well control or during production with high Well Head Shut in Pressures of up to 11,000psi in extreme case.
23.10	Reservoir Depletion
The platform process facilities are being designed to give a minimum Well Head Flowing Pressure (WHFP) of 2200psi giving a final bottom hole flowing pressure of around 4000psi. However, potential future installation of compression on the platform could reduce WHFP further.  The production casing and liner are therefore designed for full depletion to 2000psi bottom hole pressure, equivalent to 0.1psi/ft dry methane to surface from 20,000ft.
Given that, within the reservoir, the pore pressure in SP1 is as high as 14,150psi, the production liner will need to isolate differentials of up to 10,150psi. This may potentially increase to 12,150psi, if compression on the platform is introduced.
23.11	Depth Variation
Across the Shah Deniz structure, formation depths vary greatly. On the flanks, depths to formation tops are greater than on the crest. Flank wells generally have higher absolute pressures but lower gradients, compared with crestal trajectories.
At the E2 platform location, water depth is 100.9m, but increases moving towards the crest of the structure.
23.12	Deep Reservoir Targets
Deep reservoir targets in the order of 6,100m TVD will give rise to long hole sections. For some trajectories, this may result in torque and drag becoming an issue. Drilling times will also be great, with each well potentially taking between five to six months to drill to target depth.
23.13	Slim Clearances
The number of casing strings and contingency liners result in extremely tight clearances. Flush and semi flush integral connections have been selected to accommodate this problem.
24.0	Overview of Exploration / Appraisal Wells
24.1	SDX-1, 1Z, 1Y
SDX-1 was the first well drilled to the reservoirs on Shah Deniz. The well was in a mid flank position on the east flank of the structure and was a gas discovery. Numerous problems were encountered whilst drilling, as follows:
Tight hole in the Surakhany formation, probably due to tectonics, not recognized as such at the time.
Higher than anticipated pressures in the Surakhany and Sabunchi formations led to several kicks.
A very large pressure regression in the Balakhany formations leading to drilling with high overbalances.
Thin high pressured sand, SP1, sandwiched between two low to moderately pressured reservoirs.
Two sidetracks (SDX-1z, SDX-1y) due to mechanical sticking. The first was probably due to differential sticking or tectonics whilst the second was due to poor directional control leading to stuck logging tools and junk in the hole.
Two changes of rig, one planned, to utilize newly refurbished high pressure rated rig and the second due to generator problems on the newly refurbished rig.
24.2	SDX-2, 2Z, 2Y
The second well on the structure, SDX-2, was also drilled in the east flank, but in an up dip location. This too was a gas discovery. The well location proved to be an extremely difficult area at which to drill. This was mainly due to a tight pore / fracture pressure window and a lack of planning time. Key issues were as follows:
Only one casing string set on depth as planned.
Losses and gains led to the premature setting of the 16”, 133/8” and 95/8” casings and the 7” liner.
11¾” contingency liner had to be run.
Severe lost circulation near to the base of the Surakhany and again at Top Balakhany, due to a tight pore-fracture window.
Over 19,000 barrels of mud were lost.
Numerous kicks, including one incidence of cross flow associated with one of the lost circulation episodes.
BOP problems.
Logging problems.
The well was spudded before SDX-1 had been completed and therefore before learning from the earlier well could be incorporated in the planning.
24.3	SDX-3, 3Z, 3Y, 3X
SDX-3, the third well on the structure, was drilled on the northern flank in a down dip location. It was a dry hole. Despite the number of sidetracks this was an easier well to drill. Problems encountered were:
Presence of a shallow overpressured sand led to remedial cementing on the 20” casing.
Three stuck pipe incidents leading to sidetracks. All appear to be directly attributable to tectonics.
24.4	SDA-01
SDA-01, the first of pre drilled development wells on the structure, was drilled in a mid flank position  on the east flank of the structure. Key learning’s to date include:
Greater than anticipated pressure and associated gas from 750m,  “Q”-4, sand.
22’ cement channeling that led to flow from wellhead grout ports.
Mechanical failure of simultaneous drill and under-reaming of 14.75 x 17.5” interval.
Greater than anticipated pore pressure measured by MDT in top Surakhany.
Greater than anticipated pore pressure and associated gas from Pre Fasila sands below SP-4.
24.5	SDA-02
SDA-02, the second of pre drilled development wells on the structure, was drilled on the east flank of the structure to the NW of the platform location. Key learning’s to date include:
Overall excellent performance to date.
22’ cement job may have channeled which led to flow from wellhead ports. Source of flow may in fact have been SDA-01.
Hughes Christensen DOSRWD Eccentric Pad tool sustained damage when used to drill out 22” shoe – dedicated drill-out trip planned for SDA-03.
No repeat of Ander-reamer mechanical failure as seen on SDA-01.
