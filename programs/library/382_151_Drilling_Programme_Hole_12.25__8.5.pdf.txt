1. INTRODUCTION

The South Pars Gas field is located in the Persian Gulf, at some 100 KM from shore. Four huge

condensate rich gas bearing reservoirs have been identified (K1, K2, K3 and K4) in this field.

NIOC has envisaged developing this  field by phases of some 1000 MMSCFD wet gas

production each. TOTAL / Gas Prom / PETRONAS have already developed two such phases

(referred to as "Phase 2" and "Phase3"). NIOC developed South Pars development "Phase

1".ENI / PETROPARS consortium developed Phases 4 & 5 and PETROPARS /STATOIL

joint ventures developed phases 6 / 7 /8. Phases 9 & 10 are developing by NIDC / LG. In each

contract, respecting phases are integrated in order to share common facilities and thus reduce

the overall cost of the development. NIOC is pursuing further development under "Phase 11",

"Phase 12" and" Phases 13 &14" and "Phases 15 and 16", and "Phases 17 and 18", "Phase19",

It is believed that developing the whole field will lead to 28 development phases.

This program details the plan which provides the basic support data involving, geological,

reservoir and drilling  / completion information for the development of Phases 17 & 18 of

South Pars development project. The drilling will be done with two drilling jack up rigs but not

limited to providing two jack up rigs, One (1) vertical APPRAISAL wells and Twenty one

(21) deviated development wells from two separate platforms, each with eleven slots. Each

drilling rig shall drill ten directional wells and one appraisal wells and five (5) wells are on

third platform, all wells must be perforated through all reservoir layers. The remaining slots

will be used for infill drilling if necessary to maintain the production plateau in future. POGC

plans to drill one (1) appraisal, delineation (DW) and drill twenty one (21) deviated wells from

two platforms.

4. HEALTHS, SAFETY AND RISKS
4.1. Health & Safety

 F We always have time to rig up and operate safely: Take “time-out” if necessary

 F  Plan the job ahead don’t run afterwards

 F  Helmets and tools to be secured against falling into the hole/casing. Use hole cover /

  mats when working near rotary table.

 F  Perform pre-job meeting prior to running casing.

 F  Make sure all areas involved in casing transport and handling is being secured to avoid

  unauthorized personnel into entrance areas.

 F  Focus on falling objects

  §   Focus to avoid loose items on rig-floor and all decks below in drilling module and well

  bay areas

  §   All equipment used above 2m has to be secured with wire

  §   All tools has to be suitable for use in derrick (use of wire)

  §   Sign-out list has to be filled out in drillers cabin (in/out)

  §       List with numbers in the toolbox at rig floor

 F  No work at two levels without a barrier between

  §   Important with work orders sign by driller and area responsible at TDAD

  §   Use barrier tape

  §   Keep people away

  §   Radio communication

  §   Respond back on communication

 F   Install hole cover on casing top between each intermediate operation


  Focus on experience  / feedback from previous conductor installations regarding HSE,

  operation and handling of pipe.

8.1.1. Discussion
12 ¼” Interval will be drilled through Surmeh, Neyriz, Dashtak, Evaporate, Sudair, Aghar and
finally 9 ⅝” casing will be set 20 m MD inside upper Kangan which is a thin shale streaks and
are distinctive and easy to pick. Top of Upper Kangan has been estimated at 2664 m TVD. The
9 ⅝” casing shall cover all the Aghar shale formation which causes to be in safe side for mud
weight whilst drilling in Kangan reservoir.

Shoe track and cement of 13 ⅜" casing will be drilled with sea water then the hole will be
displaced with HYDRO-FOIL Gen 1.

HYDRO-FOIL Gen 1 (KCl/ PHPA/GLYCOL HYDRO-PLAST Polymer) mud system
necessitating a minimum requirement of 9.4 – 12.1 PPG   Losses could be experienced in
porous layers such as Surmeh formation. The fluid for this section is one designed to give
maximum inhibition to the formations in particular Kazhdumi and enable drilling through the
troublesome shales and marls. This inhibition will be achieved with KCl at 25-30 lb/bbl,
concentration, HYDR-CAP XP at range of 0.3-0.5 lb/bbl, HYDRO-PLAST at range of 3-5
lb/bbl and CUMULUS CPG at range of 2-3% by volume.

For mud weight at range of 9.4 PPG KCl at range of 25 lb/bb and NaCl at range of 20 lb/bbl
will be utilized as weighting agent. For mud weight more than this NaCl can be added in active
system to get saturated base fluid, after that Barite will be utilized to get desired mud weight
as hole condition dictates.

Hydro-Star NF at 5-6 lb/bbl and MY-PAC LV 1-2 lb/bbl will be used to control the API fluid
loss below 4 CC/30 min and keep the filter cake as thin as possible on the bore hole. Fluid loss
should be tighten and keep below 3 ml across the shale and marl beds in Neyriz, Sudair and
Aghar formations.

It is necessary to keep API fluid loss whilst drilling in this interval lower than 4 ml/30 and
tight it across the Shale formations to be lower than 3 ml to improve the hole stability in this
troublesome shale formations. A minimum range of HYDRO-PLAST at 3 lb/bbl should be
kept in active system whilst drilling in Neyriz, Sudair and Aghar shale formations.

HYDRO-CAP XP is a versatile polymer which is easy to mix. The main function of PHPA is
to encapsulate cuttings to prevent them from hydrating. PHPA systems provide maximum
inhibition by maintaining at range of 0.3-0.5 lb/bbl excess active product in the system.
HYDRO-LUBE at range of 1.0-1.5% by volume shale be utilized in a deviated well to achieve
sufficient lubricity in the system. The use of hydrocarbons will not be considered due to
environmental factors.
Barite is the only candidate to get mud weight as per hole condition dictated in this interval.
pH shall be controlled by consumption of Caustic Soda on range ±9.5

Mechanism of Shale Stabilization

The two methods of shale inhibition from the HYDRO-FOIL GEN 1 system are potassium
ions and Cloud Point Glycol.

   •  The cation exchange of ions, most commonly Potassium, with the shale can reduce
       hydration and swelling of shales.
   •  Cloud point Glycol (CUMULUS CPG) by the clouding effect of the glycol which
       prevents  pore  pressure  transmission.  The   stabilization  mechanisms  combine
       synergistically to provide inhibition close to that of non aqueous fluids.
At a specific temperature (cloud point) a water soluble glycol starts to change from soluble to
insoluble, forming a thermally activated emulsion (TAME). As the temperature increases the
glycol starts to come out of solution and the liquid begins clouding. Eventually the single
phase liquid separates into two separate phases.
Cloud point is a function of the following variables:

  ü  Salinity of the solution (Increasing in salinity will affect to decreasing in clouded point
      temp).
  ü Molecular weight and type
  ü Type of electrolyte
  ü Concentration of the glycol (Increasing in glycol concentration will affect to decreasing
       in clouded point temp).

Note: clued point temp shall to be kept higher than flow line temperature and lower than
bottom hole temperature.

Research has concluded that the glycol is associated by hydrogen bonding to the reactive sites
of clays. During this adsorption process, water is displaced and ordered structures of glycols
are formed. There is little depletion of the glycol from the fluid when drilling, the glycol
returning to solution as the cutting moves up the wellbore and the fluid temperature drops
below the cloud point. This supports the theory that the bonding between the clay and the
glycol is transitory, and there is some mechanism for association and disassociation due to
changes in the environment.

8.1.2. Expected H2S Gas
Introduction
The presence of Hydrogen Sulfide in drilling fluids,  if not treated with caution, can be
particularly damaging to personnel, equipment and mud properties.

The sources of Hydrogen Sulfide are three-fold; it is present either in permeable formations as
a result of the degradation of Sulfure-containing organic materials or as a direct result of the
thermal degradation of  drilling  fluid products containing Sulphur, (examples are lingo-
sulphonates and thio-carbamate biocides) and finally as a result of the bacterial degradation of
sulphates by Sulfide reducing bacteria (SRB) under anaerobic conditions. The latter is usually
encountered when re-entering old completed wells.

Effects
The first positive identification of soluble sulfides in the drilling fluids must be taken as a
possible indication of H2S entering the fluid from some source. In the simplest case an increase
in mud weight may be sufficient to control minor influxes. If levels persist chemical treatment
will be required. This can take two forms; neutralization and scavenging. The two terms must
not be confused.

Neutralization of H2S in drilling fluid by caustic soda or lime is a common field approach. This
alkalinity maintenance method has proved successful when small amounts of H2S enter the
drilling fluid, for example, from drilling up pore spaces containing the gas. This is not a
suitable approach to the control on influxes of Hydrogen Sulfide. The alkalinity neutralization
ofH2S is instantly reversible by reduction in pH. Should the pH of the fluid decrease due to
further influxes of H2S, Co2 or brine flows the sulfides may come out of solution as hydrogen
sulfide gas.

To ensure that such influxes are handled safely, it is essential to employ a sulfide scavenger.
This term refers to any additive which is capable of reacting with one or more of the sulfide
species and as a result, converting them to a more inert form. To be truly effective an
irreversible and complete reaction must occur. As a mutual equilibrium exist between the three
sulfide species in solution (H2S, HS- and S2-) irreversible and complete removal of one serves
to removal all three.

To remove the problem  it is necessary to precipitate the sulfide as an insoluble inorganic
sulfide. Zinc or iron compounds are usually chosen because of the relative inertness of the
sulfides formed. Zinc source (Zinc oxide or carbonate) has in the past been the preferred
treatment. However, continuous treatment with Zinc carbonate  will produce  significant
undesirable carbonate concentration (Carbonate contamination) which can adversely effect on
drilling fluid rheology and fluid loss. See the below table:

8.1.12. Hole Cleaning Guidelines In Deviated well
There is no way, cuttings beds will form in high angle wellbores, regardless of how efficient
the hole cleaning practices are. How the cuttings are distributed in the hole will dictate the
measures that are required to move them. Increasing mud rheology over than normal range to
get better hole cleaning in deviated well absolutely is a wrong practice which cause to increase
the cutting beds and cuttings overall. Here is some practices for drilling a well directionally as
a guidelines to optimize wellbore stability and hole cleaning as well

Management of the cuttings in the hole is a key to efficient drilling operations. A wellbore
does not have to be 100% clean, or free of cuttings to be “clean”. Every high angle wellbore
will have a cuttings bed of some kind. A cuttings bed that is clean for drilling is not necessarily
the same as that for tripping a BHA, running casing or running wire-line logs.

Good hole cleaning practices
Good tripping and drilling practices are critical to ensuring acceptable hole cleaning. The
benefits of a powerful, purpose built drilling rig can easily be negated by poor or inappropriate
drilling practices.

Mud and rheology guidelines
Regardless of the mud type the overall
objective is to maintain a pump-able fluid
with low-end rheology that is high enough
to keep cuttings moving out of the hole.
The use of 6 rpm readings as a primary
indicator of hole cleaning capability and
maintaining low PV (plastic viscosity) for
pump-ability is widely accepted.

Flowrate Effect on Hole Cleaning
Pump at the maximum available flowrate
at  all  time.  This  will  be  limited  by
formation pressure integrity. Use the ECD
as a guide to maximum flowrate possible without compromising hole integrity.

Flowrate is the key parameter for hole cleaning rate, as shown in the below figure. The faster
the pump rate the quicker the hole is cleaned, so long as the rpm is sufficiently high, preferably
>120 rpm.

It is important to appreciate that as long as cuttings are moving up the hole at a faster rate than
they are being generated, then the hole is being cleaned. Flowrate will affect the rate of
cleaning and allow faster cuttings generation to be tolerated.

ROP
Historically, there have been two different schools of thought on drilling ROP in high angle
hole sections. Some choose to drill at maximum instantaneous ROP and then perform remedial
hole cleaning operations as required. Alternately some nominate a safe ROP at which the hole
can be kept clean as it is drilled ahead.

High instantaneous ROP and remedial hole cleaning are likely to result in periods when the
well unloads cuttings at a rate that cannot be handled by the containment system.

Isn’t necessary to be concern regarding hole cleaning at high ROP. Hole cleaning can be
achieved with even with ROP at rate 15 m/hr.

Torque and Drag monitoring
This is one of the primary methods of monitoring hole cleaning as the information is readily
available and easily interpreted on the rig floor.





Surface torque and drag monitoring involves taking torque, rotating string weight, pick and
slack-off weights at every connection. As the well is drilled deeper the values for up weight,
rotating weight and down weight will all slowly increase, as will the difference between them.

Back-ream and pumping out
Although back reaming may be considered an appropriate practice in vertical wells and
conventional low angle deviated wells. Back reaming and pumping out of hole are not
appropriate practices for high angle well bores when tight hole is encountered or as a primary
hole cleaning tool.

Back reaming and pumping out of hole are not only considered to be very inefficient but can
also be very risky on highly deviated wells.

Whereas tight hole in vertical wells is likely to be due to wellbore conditions, tight hole in
highly deviated wells is likely to be due to hole cleaning and cuttings. Back reaming and
pumping out of hole through a cuttings bed can very easily lead to packing off, stuck pipe and
possible loss of the string and wellbore.

8.1.13. Guidelines for precautionary back reaming
On occasion, it may be necessary to back ream in an highly deviated well for precautionary
reasons.

١.  Prior to precautionary back reaming, the hole should be cleaned up with maximum flowrate
   and rotation.
٢. The is some danger that cleaning up the hole before back reaming may be seen as a waste
   of time, given that the hole is to be back reamed anyway. However, the intent of circulating
   clean is to get the cuttings level down to a more manageable, lower risk level, before
   commencing a relatively high risk operation (i.e. back reaming).
٣. Back ream slowly out of the hole with maximum allowable flowrates and pipe rotation. If
   flowrate is limited it may be advisable to back ream in stages with several stops to clean the
   hole to a safer level of cuttings or at least redistribute them away from the BHA.
٤.  It must always be remembered that a potentially dangerous cuttings dune exists above the
  BHA. The driller should be vigilant for an indication (e.g. increased torque) that the BHA is
   being pulled into this cuttings dune and that a pack off or stuck pipe is imminent  if
   appropriate action is not taken.

9.1.10. Good hole cleaning practices
Good tripping and drilling practices are critical to ensuring acceptable hole cleaning. The
benefits of a powerful, purpose built drilling rig can easily be negated by poor or inappropriate
drilling practices.

Mud and rheology guidelines
Regardless of the mud type the overall objective is to maintain a pump-able fluid with low-end
rheology that is high enough to keep cuttings moving out of the hole. The use of 6 rpm
readings as a primary indicator of hole cleaning capability and maintaining low PV (plastic
viscosity) for pump-ability is widely accepted.

Flowrate Effect on Hole Cleaning
Pump at the maximum available flowrate at all time. This will be limited by formation pressure
integrity. Use the ECD as a guide to maximum flowrate possible without compromising hole
integrity.

Flowrate is the key parameter for hole cleaning rate, as shown in the below figure. The faster
the pump rate the quicker the hole is cleaned, so long as the rpm is sufficiently high, preferably
>120 rpm.

It is important to appreciate that as long as cuttings are moving up the hole at a faster rate than
they are being generated, then the hole is being cleaned. Flowrate will affect the rate of
cleaning and allow faster cuttings generation to be tolerated.

This is one of the primary methods of
monitoring   hole   cleaning   as   the
information  is  readily  available  and
easily interpreted on the rig floor.

Surface  torque  and  drag  monitoring
involves taking torque,  rotating  string
weight, pick and slack-off weights  at
every connection. As the well is drilled
deeper the values for up weight, rotating
weight and down weight will all slowly
increase, as will the difference between
them. When the up and down weight
lines diverge away from the predicted trends,  i.e. up weight increases and down weight
decreases it can indicate build up of cuttings beds in the well and a requirement to stop and
circulate clean. Examples of these trends are shown in the figure below.

Torque and drag modeling has proven to be an excellent tool for monitoring cuttings bed build
up but there are many actions that may be occurring that will not necessarily show up or may
be misinterpreted. Differential sticking, key seating and wellbore instability effects should not
be misinterpreted as cuttings build up.

Back-ream and pumping out
Although back reaming may be considered an appropriate practice in vertical wells and
conventional low angle deviated wells. Back reaming and pumping out of hole are not
appropriate practices for high angle well bores when tight hole is encountered or as a primary
hole cleaning tool.

Back reaming and pumping out of hole are not only considered to be very inefficient but can
also be very risky on highly deviated wells.

Whereas tight hole in vertical wells is likely to be due to wellbore conditions, tight hole in
highly deviated wells is likely to be due to hole cleaning and cuttings. Back reaming and

Guidelines for precautionary back reaming
On occasion, it may be necessary to back ream in an highly deviated well for precautionary
reasons.

   ١.  Prior to precautionary back reaming, the hole should be cleaned up with maximum
    flowrate and rotation.
   ٢.  The is some danger that cleaning up the hole before back reaming may be seen as a
    waste of time, given that the hole is to be back reamed anyway. However, the intent of
    circulating clean is to get the cuttings level down to a more manageable, lower risk level,
    before commencing a relatively high risk operation (i.e. back reaming).
   ٣.  Back ream slowly out of the hole with maximum allowable flowrates and pipe
    rotation. If flowrate is limited it may be advisable to back ream in stages with several stops
    to clean the hole to a safer level of cuttings or at least redistribute them away from the
   BHA.
   ٤.   It must always be remembered that a potentially dangerous cuttings dune exists above
    the BHA. The driller should be vigilant for an indication (e.g. increased torque) that the
  BHA is being pulled into this cuttings dune and that a pack off or stuck pipe is imminent if
    appropriate action is not taken.

9.1.12. Completion Phase
 Discussion
Clear, solids-free brines are the most commonly used fluids in completion and workover
operations to minimize formation damage whilst perforation job and completion phase to
improve production rate.
Completion and workover fluids are specialized fluids used during well completion operations
and remedial workover procedures. These fluids must control not only subsurface pressure
with density, but also must minimize formation damage during completion and workover
operations.
The use of fluids that cause minimal formation damage can result in dramatically improved
production. Most reservoirs are sensitive to any fluids other than those contained in them
naturally.
All surface pits need to be cleaned and flush all lines, then prepare inhibited NaCl or CaCl2
Brine at 11.6 ppg as operation dictated to displace hole to be ready for completion the well. In
case of required completion fluid to be more than 11.6 ppg then CaBr2 will be utilized to get
desired brine weight.
Corrosion:  Salt solutions are often highly  corrosive. Dissolved oxygen  is the primary
corrosive agent in sodium-, potassium-, calcium-chloride- or bromide-brine base completion
fluids. The solubility of oxygen in these brines decreases as saturation with the  salt  is
approached. Anyhow corrosion inhibitor should to be adding in completion fluid to minimize
the risk of corrosion.
pH: Corrosion  is accelerated  in acidic environments (pH <7) and retarded  in alkaline
conditions (pH >7) . As the pH (pH = -log [H+]) is increased, the concentration of H+ will be
reduced, which may slow the corrosion rate. Conversely, decreasing the pH increases the
concentration of H+ ions, which increases the cathodic reaction and may increase the corrosion
rate (acids tend to dissolve metals faster).
Normally, a pH of 9.5 to 10.5 is adequate to mitigate most corrosion. In some cases, a pH as
high as 12 may be required. High pH values (>10.5) neutralize acid gases and lower the
solubility of corrosion products.

25. After correct amount of cement has been pumped, drop drill pipe wiper plug and begin
pumping displacement fluid.
26. Reduce pump rate to 1-2 barrels per minute prior to drill pipe wiper plug reaching setting
tool. This wills permit0bservation of the pump pressure increase when drill pipe wiper plug
latches into liner wiper plug and shears from the slick joint.

   NOTE: Make note of the pressure increase and displacement volume after shear out.
    Correct calculated displacement if necessary and complete displacement of cement.

27. When the liner wiper plug latches into the ball catch landing collar, apply pressure to liner
wiper plug to seat to ensure that plugs have bumped and are latched. Bleed off pressure and
check for bleed back to ensure floats and plugs are holding.
28. Pull string minimum 150fl above liner top and circulate conventionally.
29. Pull out of hole with drill pipe and lay down setting tool. When running tool is out of hole,
check for damage. If possible flush outside and inside of setting tool assembly. Lay down,
grease and pack for shipment.
30. Complete job report, noting additional data such as shear pressures, final displacements,
torques, etc. Ad improvement and recommendations if any was identified.

Circulation pressures

The max circulation pressure mentioned in  this procedure  is 70% of the hanger shears
pressure. Normally as low as 700 psi. The hanger can only be present when the differential
pressure at the hanger exceeds the nominal shear pressure. This means that for circulation
down through the running string pressures far above this at surface will not shear the hanger
(due to pressure losses during circulation). However, if the liner bridges off the hanger will see
the full circulation pressure. The consequence of a preset hanger depends on the situation, if
higher circulation pressures are required at surface, this could be considered.

MECHANICAL SETTING OPERATION

1. To set the pocket slip liner hanger mechanically, the entire liner weight must be set on
bottom.

  Note    : float shoe should have side ports to permit the liner to be set on bottom and
  maintain circulation. Right hand rotation will shear the brass shear screws. It takes a
   total of 15 turns (max. 21 turns) to fully activate the mechanical setting action in the
                                         setting tool.

2. Pick the liner up off bottom to the desired shoe depth. Downward movement will cause the
slips to engage the casing wall and hang the liner. (Since the slips are already in contact with
the hang off casino wall, there is Very little down movement needed to hang the liner and no
hanger body rotation is rewired.
3. Slack off the entire liner weight on to the housing body slips continue to slack off 5,000 to
10,000 lb. Of drill pipe weight.
4. When you are ready to release your running tool, rotate the drill string 15 to 20 rounds to the
right at the setting tool (it takes a total of 31 turns to completely release the setting tool from
the hanger).
5. Continue to pick up on the setting tool to pull the setting tool out of the hanger body and as
you continue to pickup, the slick joint will pull out of the drillable cement bushing. At this
point the setting tool assembly will be completely free of the liner hanger assembly


  Remember that the mechanical setting procedure is only to be used as a backup setting
                                  method
