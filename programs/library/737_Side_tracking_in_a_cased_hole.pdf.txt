────────────────────────────────────────────
         Background

Side tracking from a cased hole takes longer and
normally requires more service companies than
in open hole (logging, cementing, fishing).


A whipstock set wrong or at the wrong depth may
require to start all over.


Side tracking may be performed on new wells or
as a remedial action on an old wells.


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 3 از 45 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
     Reasons for cased holes ST

• Exploration hole redrilled as development
  well
• Hole is abandoned
• Intermediate casing landed too low
• Collision risk when exiting the shoe
• Targeting a new zone
• Stuck pipe or fish extending to the shoe
• Damaged casing
• Open hole below shoe cannot be fixed


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 4 از 45 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
   The specific questions to ask

1. Does the well need to be plugged back?
2. What deflection tools are available?
3. Is the well a reentry drilled with a different rig?
4. Will a casing or liner be run later?
5. Was it done before on the same field?
6. Is there a good CBL / VDL
7. Shape of the casing? (Usit log, collapsed casing)
8. Rig power to perform any operation?
9. Orientation technique available?


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 5 از 45 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
Does the well need to be plugged?

The lower part of the well may not be abandoned.
Completion tools allow producing several zones
even if this was not the initial plan.
A well should not be drilled with another branch
opened (Losses, well control, formation damage).

[ صفحه 6 از 45 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
What are the deflection tools available?

A window is achieved with a whipstock.

The technique known as “section milling” simply
 removes an entire section of the casing.

The whipstock length (7 to 15 ft) is longer when
 counting the undercut length above and the rathole.

A section milled interval is usually from 35 to 80 ft.

────────────────────────────────────────────
    Techniques of side tracking
• Section milling (a section of casing is milled out)
• Casing whipstock (a steel wedge shaped device)
• Punching a hole with a mill. (TTRD or CTD)

────────────────────────────────────────────
 Is the well drilled with a different rig?
   Considerable care is needed with depth.
1. Older logs may show RKB yet the rotary table
   height was used.
2. The rotary table height may have been erroneous.
3. The driller’s depth may have been off.
4. The 1 ft per 1000 allowance (on the tally) can
   quickly add up to a substantial amount.

  A Side track on a new well with the same rig is
   not be a problem unless the string is changed out.
 A packer can be used for depth correlation.

  Will a casing or liner be run later?

A liner is set with a hanger (Packer and/or Slips).
50 ft or more above the window may be needed.

There may be DLS limits for the completion pumps
(ESP, PCP) or other completion jewelry.

Normally, in a 9 5/8 casing, fishing tool company
do not like setting a whisptock in a DLS over 4.

        Is casing in good shape?
• Casing corroded or partially collapsed.
 (PDM or full gauge stabilizers may not go)
• Punching a hole will weaken it. Eliminating a
section may be dangerous in an unstable spot.
• Going through two strings of casing is easier to do
with a whipstock than with “section milling”.

[ صفحه 13 از 45 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
   Does the rig have the power?

Re-entry is often associated with smaller holes.
Smaller holes are associated with smaller rigs.
Rigs used on reentry may not have the hydraulic
power necessary to set a whipstock.
- A smaller hole requires both power and flexibility.
- A rig freshly mobilized may have DPs with
scale or rust inside.

────────────────────────────────────────────
   Orientation technique available?

A cased hole ST usually requires to be oriented.
On a deviated well with a known direction:
-Orientating with a magnetic tool is not a problem.


On a straight hole:
- Gyro orientation is almost inevitable.
The job is often as good as the initial orientation.

────────────────────────────────────────────
    ST with a classic rotary BHA

Side tracks can be performed with classic rotary
BHAs but preferably on deviated wells.

- Section milled windows: Conceivably, a
pendulum or a Build BHA is usable.

- Whipstock: Only a rotary build BHA can be run.
A pendulum may hit the well further below.
Stabs should not be rotated across a window.

────────────────────────────────────────────
     Side track with Motors
A motor can side track in the shortest distance.
Adjustable bends, high speed power sections,
short bearing sections and power sections are
some of the benefits available.
-Section milled windows: Side tracking has
been performed in less than 30 ft with a motor.
On a poor cement plug a motor should be chosen.

 - Whipstocks: If casing tracking is feared, a
     motor should perform better.


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 19 از 45 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
Side track with Rotary steerables
  Rotary steerables are usable in cased holes but
  with some limitations.
  - Section milled windows:
  Requires a longer window (60 ft plus with X5)

    (Risky on a mediocre cement plug)
  - Whipstocks: Two main risks
   1- Casing tracking
   2- Rotating stabs across the whipstock.

    Burying all stabs in new hole before running the RSS is the solution.


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 20 از 45 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
  Rotary steerables and Whipstocks

With a whipstock set 30° right one can assume some
right hand walk in the rathole.
(The mill may tend to jump the whip face)
The best is to continue drilling 15 to 30 feet with the
same orientation as the whip on a 100 % setting.


DLS may be reduced with low RPM and pumps.
(You may have to choose between two evils)

────────────────────────────────────────────
          Section milling

A 35/40 ft window was the norm with
conventional tools. The knives of a section mill
tools would die out soon after milling a casing
collar especially with hard steel (P110).
A cut initiated 5/10 ft below a casing collar would
produce a 35 ft section.
Section milling is dependable but time consuming
compared with whipstocks.

  DLS caused by Whipstocks
DLS produced is a point of controversy.
In a 7” casing DLS ranges from 12 to 16 deg.
Whipstocks have an angle between 2 and 3.5°
depending on brand and hole size.

           DLS given by manufacturers, assume a
                  straight hole above and include a
               couple of feet above and below.
                Consequently, the apparent DLS may
                   just be 10 or less on a 30 ft survey.

[ صفحه 31 از 45 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
Whipstocks: “Two trips” or “one trip”

Economic pressure favors “one trip systems”.
It seems “one trip systems” are more prone to milling
the whipstock rather than the casing.

Milling accidently the whipstock is more likely if:
- Whipstock set in excessive DLS (above 4 in a 9 5/8 csg)
- Whipstock set in a heavy and hard steel casing
- Whipstock set at high inclination


────────────────────────────────────────────
[ صفحه 32 از 45 ]
────────────────────────────────────────────
     Whipstocks field problems
• Starting off course may be a bigger problem in a
  cased hole versus an open hole.
• In a cased hole, it takes longer to know.
• Except for proximity issues or landing TVD,
  starting 90° off on a straight hole should OK.
• Starting more than 90° off may result in hitting the
  casing below and ruining a bit.
• With low DLS it may take over 200 ft to obtain a
  clean survey (at the DNI).
• Normally 1/8th wear on the mills is acceptable.

────────────────────────────────────────────

 Whipstock orientation deviated hole

• On a deviated well, 30° left or right may be OK.
• Anything above 30° (especially when crossing the
  high side) may put the well in jeopardy.
• The portion of the casing below could be hit or
  scratched as gravity will “pull” the BHA and bit.


  In a nutshell, casing tracking may be induced.

   Cement plug considerations

Setting a plug across a section should be easier.
There should be no risk of losses or slippage.
Contamination risk should be reduced.
In case no underreamer is used,
older cement chunks could fall in.
In the case of a whipstock,
a cement squeeze may be needed
before cutting a window.

           Tricks and Tips
To evaluate the cement (section milled window):
- PDM: Tag some weight (no pumps, no rotary)
- RSS: Tag some weigh (rotary off, pumps on)

PDM stalling or peaks of torque indicate problems.
(BHA not getting off or steel is in the way).

Inclination check shot surveys allow to confirm
initial whipstock orientation.
When backreaming, tell the driller where to stop.
This way a stabilizer will not catch accidently.

────────────────────────────────────────────
           Conclusions
• Use all the info you have about the well
• Beware of old logs (depth and CBL)
• Motors have better Side track capability.
• Rig Power and flexibility should be known
• Do not hesitate in taking check shots
• Directional driller should be involved in cementing
  operations as well as window cutting operations.
• Look at the side track like a fishing job.
• Do not hesitate to use Mud logging info
