1. Design Basis

N111E is proposed as a vertical appraisal well to appraise the petrophysical characters of Sarvak,

Kazhdumi and Gadvan formation in the south-east of North Azadegan Field. N111E drilling program is

based on:

1)  Drilling Geological Design of N111E.

2) FEED Drilling and Completion in North Azadegan Field.

3)  The Iranian Petroleum Standards (IPS) and Iranian Laws and Regulations.

4)  International Standards of Drilling Program.

5) SY 5333-1996, “Drilling Engineering Design Standard”.

6)  Exploration history of AZN3, AZN5 and AZN7 etc. adjacent wells.

N111E will be a vertical hole to proposed total depth of 4200m (TVD), with the basic information as

1.2. Structure

The Azadegan Field is located approximately 80 km west of Ahwaz city, in the west - south west Dezful

Embayment, parallel to the border of Iran-Iraq. It is situated in the northwest of the Yadavaran Field

(Kushk & Hossainiyeh) and in the west of Jufeyr Field.





                            Fig 1.2-1 Geographical Location Map

The Azadegan Field  is located on a large long-axis anticline trending in N-S direction with an

approximate length of 60km and width of 20km, based on OWC intersection with 2D-Seismic depth

map closed to the top Sarvak. Taking the line of 2km north to AZN7 as northern boundary, the trap area

of the North Azadegan is limited to 11×16km2.

1.3. Potential Drilling Risks

Up to now, only 2 wells (AZN3, AZN7) have been drilled in North Azadegan Field, AZN3 is located in

the centre and AZN7 in the north of this field. But there is an adjacent well AZN5 in the south of North

Azadegan Field, which is located in the western flank of anticline. Since no well has been drilled in

eastern flank of the anticline in this field, it is hard to describe the structure of Sarvak, Kazhdumi and

Gadvan formation in more detail, and drilling risks of N111E are based on the AZN3, AZN5 and AZN7

etc. adjacent wells.

1)  Some minor or middle fractures exist in sandstone and limestone, partial or total lost circulation

    was encountered from offsets mainly below top of Asmari, max was 139m3/d, average was

    10-15m3/d. Total loss for AZN6 well is about 1590m3.

2)  High pressure aquifers exist in Gachsaran and Gadvan formations to cause kicks and high salinity

      salt water cut.

3)  Anhydrite/gypsum or salt exists in Gachsaran formation mainly cause tight hole and stuck drill

      string.

4)  High concentration H2S /CO2 sour gas and  salinity  exist  in Sarvak (H2S:0.03mol%, CO2:

    1.65mol%), Kazhdumi and Gadvan formations mainly to cause drill string and casing corrosion

    and drilling crew harmed or poisoned.

5)  Abnormal high pressures exist in Gachsaran or Dariyan/Gadavan formation, so high quality well

    head and well control equipment and management will be requested.

6)   Formation consists mainly of limestone below top of Pabdeh to produce low ROP in drilling

     operation.

Moreover, the combination of H2S and CO2 is more aggressive than H2S alone, since casing corrosion

and rig personnel risks are frequently found in oilfield environments. So problems about corrosion and

rig personnel safety are big challenges for drilling in North Azedagan Field.

The contractor should be familiar with the drilling history (incl. experienced drilling hazards) of existing

exploration wells. Appropriate contingency planning needs to be developed to adequately deal with

potential similar hazards in this well.

3.4. Drilling Operation Procedure


3.4.1. Pre-spud Preparation

Ensure that the following has been completed prior to spud:

  (1)  Have safety / operational meeting with all personnel before moving on equipment.

  (2)  Rig completely is rigged up and all major components function is tested. Equipment shall be

        installed smoothly, positively, consistently, completely, firmly, effectively and jointly according

       to the installation standards. Crown block, rotary table and wellhead shall be calibrated to make

       sure that they are aligned, and the error shall be less than 10mm.

  (3)  Monitoring equipment functional and calibrated:

     a)   Weight indicator, etc.

     b)  Pump stroke indicators installed on all pumps

     c)   Pressure gauges (pump, stand pipe, manifold) with backups available

  (4) Mud system filled, fluid can be diverted and tanks emptied without leaks.

  (5) Low pressure mud system tested.

  (6)  Shakers work correctly and

     a)   Decanting angles are correct.

     b)   Screens are checked for proper sizing and spares.

     c)   Ensure screens for different sections are on location.

  (7)  Rig is supplied solids control equipment.

  (8)  Adequate supply of mud products on location for surface hole (lost circulation).

  (9)  Drill string is drifted on the racks.

  (10) Drill collars are inspected on location.

  (11) Record joint count of all drill pipe and collars on location (to be maintained throughout the well).

  (12) Surface casing threads are cleaned.

  (13) Check all wellhead equipment (including flange, washer, test plug, O ring, wear bushing, bolt,

       nut, valves etc.)

  (14) Effective volume of the reserve tank shall be no less than 320m3. Enough water shall be in place

       for lost circulation.

  (15) Weighting materials such as ferrum powder etc shall be available at well site all time during the

       operation.

  (16) Communication facilities: 3 telephone lines on site (one each for supervisor and contractor, one

       fax line shared by both), walkie-talkie for special operations, and HUB, Modem, LAN line etc

      should be prepared for internet connection.

  (17) Flare pit and blow out pit shall be at least 150m away from well site.

  (18) 30” conductor pipe shall be set about 15m below bottom of cellar and cement.

  (19) Install bell nipple, flow line and mud charge line.

3.4.2. 26″ Surface Hole with 20″Surface Casing

Drilling Procedure for 26” Hole

  (1)  E-mail or phone spud notification to Drilling Superintendent 24 hrs before expected spud time.

  (2)  Pre-spud, shall hold the tool box talk. Every body on duty should well know objective of the job,

      method – step to the job, responsibility, hazards related to the job and the worksite, hazards check

          list, hazards control method.

  (3) Make drilling fluid for 660.4mm hole according to drilling fluid program.

  (4) M/U STB and BHA, RIH.

  (5)  Drill 660.4mm surface hole to +/-100m, bit change and drilling parameter as per the drilling

       condition encountered.

  (6)  Periodically circulate as necessary to clean hole of cuttings, pump Hi-Vis as required.

  (7)  Increase viscosity to prior to casing point. Make wiper trip and if there is no fill, then circulate to

       clean well bore, pump Hi-Vis to spot the bottom & POOH for logging and casing.

  (8)  Logging as per geological program.

  (9) Run 20” casing string to bottom. Install centralizers on the casing as cementing program. Use

        float shoe, and install float collar on top of the second joint of casing. Tack weld and thread lock

         first 3 joints.

  (10) Install the wellhead. Pressure test to 80% of casing collapse strength.

  (11) Nipple up 20” BOP and test as BOP testing procedure.

  (12) Mud should be prepared in pits for 17-1/2”section.

  Note:

  Report hole conditions in detail on daily drilling report, noting depth and severity of any hole

  problems (tight hole or mud losses) and a description of any sloughing (size, shape and quantity).

  This information will assist in optimizing future locations in this area since spudding.

Potential Surface Hole Problems

Caving and lost circulation are possible. Ensure an adequate supply of LCM and water.

Survey Requirements

Run Totco or multi surveys with slick line unit every 150m or bit trips.

3.6.3 Lost Circulation while Drilling

Lost Circulation is a big challenge for this N111E well, which is generally meant to some degree of

whole fluid losses. The situations through which whole fluid losses may occur are wide in both scope

and number.  Whole mud losses may occur into open granular porosity, vugular porosity, rubble or

transitional weathered porosity, or static or dynamic fracture porosity.   Further, losses may be induced

into normally competent matrices through pressure increases brought about through poor drilling

practices, excessive ECD, high surge pressures, annular “pack-off”, annular cuttings loading, or well

control situations.

With the exceptions of dynamic fractures (those that vary in size or propagate dependent upon the

pressure applied to them), most static loss situations can eventually be addressed through the principle

of geometric bridging.  That is, the placing of a particle or semi-solid agent of sufficient size to become

“caught” in the loss network.  Other smaller particles then become caught upon the original large

particle, and so on, until the original loss scenario has again become one of addressing “seepage” losses

through the newly deposited bridging network.

 The primary function of the fluid is to allow the pay zone to be drilled with a minimum of formation

damage.  That is, maximum productivity must continue to be the main focus of operations, even while

combating losses.

As a result of the above, remedial treatments for losses must be chosen based upon their compatibility

with the concept of undamaged return permeability.

In the event  that the  lost  circulation material has the  potential  to cause impediment of return

permeability, either through a direct effect upon the formation face, OR through an effect upon the

completion treatment or equipment, the availability of a secondary recourse is desirable; in this case,

3.10. Health, Safe and Environment

HSE is an important part on location and must be obeyed by contractor as below:

(1) Contractors must adherence to the standards and regulations of Health, Safe and Environment

constituted by the local government and international standards.

(2) Contractors’ execution regulations must adherence to the HSE standards, report these execution

regulations to management then execute.

Because of H2S in North Azadegan, some measures for H2S prevention as below:

Measures for H2S prevention

1) Windsocks shall be set on the drill floor and in the monsoon direction off well site. In emergency,

employees can evacuate upwind.

2) Explosion-proof blowers shall be installed around the rig, shale shaker, and drilling fluid tanks to blow

away toxic and flammable gas at the well site.

3) The rig crew in the H2S area shall be furnished with H2S monitors and protective apparatus in accordance

with SY/T 5087. Everybody shall be able to use, maintain, and check the apparatus.

4) The technicians of rig crew are responsible for H2S proof training and the tool pusher is in charge of the

supervision and examination.

5) The pH value of drilling fluid in the H2S area shall be kept over 9.5. Pay attention to measurement of H2S
concentration in drilling fluid. The H2S concentration shall be kept at less than 50mg/cm3 by using sulfur

removal agents and degassers.

6) Personnel safety and protection shall be carried out according to SY/T5087 during operations in polluted

areas where H2S content exceeds the critical concentration.

7) When the rig crew makes decision to bleed and fire because well control cannot be performed due to well

site conditions, operations shall be performed according to procedures in SY/T5087.

8) Once blowout occurs, the incident shall be reported to the higher level department as soon as possible and

firefighting trucks, ambulances, medical care personnel and technicians shall be on duty at the well site.

9) After the blowout is controlled, H2S concentration should be measured in the places where H2S is possible

to gather. The personnel are not allowed to access until the H2S concentration  is below the  critical

concentration.


Note: More details refer to the relative standards.
