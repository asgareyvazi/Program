# General Information
This Document Covers the Drilling of four wells in Siah Makan oilfield intend to Bangestan Producer and Delineation goals.
Note: The program explains the group 1 wells completely and due to vertical wells of group 2 and group 3 just cementing volumes, casing design, well montage and required information is reported with details.
According to delineation targets of these wells, it is planned to do F.B.DST at first 50m of Asmari in location E012 and W065 and also first 80m of Bangestan in location W065. Also in W014N location coring will be done in Sarvak zones 8,9,10 and 11, approximately 272m.
- Locations W014N (B) and W036N (B) Shall Be considered to produce from the Bangestan reservoirs. Therefore, to accomplish this goal 18 5/8" surface casing will be set at 150m. The 13 3/8" intermediate casing will be run to 2677m into Gachsaran formation. The 9 5/8" production casing should be run in Cap Rock at 3313m. The 7" liner will be run in Ilam Fm. at 4314m and 5" liner will be run in Kazhdumi formation at any depth that will be notified base on log evaluation results. The total depth of these wells will be approximately 5057m.
- Location E012 as delineation / producer well from Pabdeh Formation by a vertical well. If the Pabdeh formation is not suitable for economic oil production, drilling will be continued and the well will be completed as Bangestan well.
Drill the well vertically down to total depth of 4424m (-4242m). 18 5/8" surface casing will be set at 150m. The 13 3/8" intermediate casing will be run to 2380m into Gachsaran formation. The 9 5/8" production casing should be run in Cap Rock at 3076m. The 7" liner will be run in Pabdeh Fm. at 3555m and 5" liner will be run in Sarvak Fm. Zone”7” Base on log results.
- Location W065 (B) as delineation / producer well from Bangestan Formation by a vertical well.
Drill the well vertically down to total depth of 5132m (-4928m). 18 5/8" surface casing will be set at 150m. The 13 3/8" intermediate casing will be run to 2955m into Gachsaran formation. The 9 5/8" production casing should be run in Cap Rock at 3183m. The 7" liner will be run in Ilam Fm. at 4364m and 5" liner will be run in Kazhdumi Fm. at any depth that will be notified base on log evaluation results.

# Introduction
Siah makan oilfield is located 10Km. south of the Bibi Hakimeh and West of the Kilur karim Field. SI-1 is about 230 Km. southeast of Ahwaz and 125 Km North of the coastal city of Bushehr. This field structure is mapped geophysical anticline about 16 km. long and 10km. wide.

# Well Information

# Well Objectives
The primary objectives of drilling the four wells in Siah Makan oilfield are:
To produce oil from Bangestan (W014N, W036N, E012 (second scenario), W065) and Pabdeh (E012 first scenario) reservoirs.
To develop the more information in Asmari and Bangestan reservoirs Including two F.B.DST in first 50m of Asmari reservoir (E012, W065) and one F.B.DST in first 80m of Bangestan reservoir (W065).
To evaluate the Sarvak formation. (272m coring in Sarvak formation in well W014N).
To evaluate production capability of Pabdeh formation by acidizing and flowing the well (E012).
To evaluate the Bangestan group reservoir by open hole logging included full suite log, CDR/GR and image log in all wells and RFT in three wells (W036N, E012, W065).

# Reservoir Data
The Asmari and Bangestan formations are the main reservoirs.

# Geological forecast

# Formation Description
A brief description of the expected stratigraphic units to be drilled in Siah Makan typical vertical well has been presented in the following breakdown.
Aghajari Formation
The Aghajari Formation consists of red and grey silty marls that interbedded with sandstone.
Mishan Formation
The Mishan Formation is mainly consistent of grey Marl and limestone. Which are grey, tan and white, argillaceous to silty and crystalline.
Gachsaran Formation
Deposition of the Gachsaran Formation is cyclic. This formation totally consists of a repetition of Anhydrite, grey and red marl as well as salt and some limestone beds.
Asmari Formation
The top of the Asmari formation is marked by limestone Anhydritic and oil stained. At the top of the middle Asmari Formation is a sandstone which is medium-coarse grained sucrosic, dolomitic and oil stained. Anhydrite and Anhydritic limestone is also present and below, dolomite and dolomitic limestone are present and interbedded with grey to red-brown shale.
Pabdeh Formation
The Pabdeh Formation is mainly consistent of marly limestone. The top of the Gurpi Formation is gradational from argillaceous limestone to calcareous shale.
Ilam Formation
The lithology of the Ilam Formation is consists of Archie type I and I/III limestone.
Surgah Formation
The lithology of the Surgah Formation is consists of limestone.
Sarvak Formation
The lithology of the Sarvak Formation is consists of limestones, locally dolomitic and interbedded with marly limestone and bituminous shale.

# Program Outline

# Drilling Summary
It is planned to drill and complete vertical well to delineate and apprise the Pabdeh formation and Bangestan group reservoir. Also producing from these reservoirs is other main objective of this plan.
26" Hole section:
The 26" hole section will be drilled from surface to ±150m as top hole. For drilling the hole, it will be used 26" bit with 63-76 pcf Pre-Hydrated Bentonite mud for hole cleaning. Also PHB pill will be used to clean the hole and since Bentonite has the best cake around the well bore, it will make a good cake for hole stability. According to previous wells in this field, complete loss is possible in this section. The 18 5/8" casing will be set at ±150m and it will be cemented. It is very important to harness of pipe for prevention of movement upward. N/U 20-3/4" 3k C.H.H and 20-3/4" 3K Annular BOP and Single Rams.
17 1/2" Hole section:
The 17 1/2" hole section will be drilled from ±150m to ±2677m in Gachsaran formation before entering to high pressure zone. In this section will be used 64-96 pcf Bentonite mud /salt water/ Inhibited salt water / Inhibited polymer mud for hole cleaning. The 13 3/8" casing will be set at ±2677m and it will be cemented in one stage. According to previous wells in this field, loss, flow, tight hole and stuck are possible in this section.
N/Up D.S.A pack off 20-3/4” 3K x 16-3/4” 5K and then C.H.S 16-3/4” 5K x 13-5/8” 5K. N/U 13 5/8" 5K BOP’s Stack (13 5/8" 5K Annular Preventer + 13 5/8" 5K Double Rams + 13 5/8" 5K Single).
12 1/4" Hole Section:
This section will be drilled from ±2677m to ±3313m through Gasharan formation to top of Asmari in Cap Rock. For drilling this section will be used 130-137 Pcf salt saturated polymer mud. Then 9 5/8" production casing will be run ±3313m and cemented to surface. According to previous wells in this field, flow and tight hole are possible in this section.
N/U D.S.A pack off 13-5/8” 5K x 11” 10K and then T.H.S 11” x 11” 10K. N/U 11” 10K x 13-5/8” 10K DSA and 13-5/8" 10K BOP’s Stack (13 5/8" 5K Annular Preventer + 13 5/8" 10K Double Rams + 13 5/8" 10K Single).
Radial Cement Bond log will be run to evaluate the quality and bonding condition of cement behind 9-5/8” production casing.
8 3/8" Hole Section:
This section will be drilled from ±3313m to ±4314m through Ilam formation. For drilling this section, Oil base mud / Inhibited salt water / Inhibited CaCO3 polymer mud with 70-90 pcf MW will be used. After drilling to liner point, 7" liner will be run and cemented in two stage due to using Silica flour in tail cement. According to previous wells in this field, flow and tight hole are possible in this section.
Liner lap will be test as positive test with applying 2000 psi over pressure on mud column hydrostatic and negative test with dry test tools as 2000 psi differential pressure against formation pressure below liner lap.
- According to delineation targets of these wells, the F.B.DST from first 50m of Asmari Fm. will be done in E012 and W065 wells.
6 " Hole Section:
This section will be drilled from ±4314m to ±5057m. This interval is included Bangestan group and Kazhdumi formation. Therefore, the main problem in this interval can be predicted as fluid loss, tight hole and stuck pipe due to sensitive and instable shale. For drilling of this section, Oil base mud / Inhibited salt water / Inhibited CaCO3 polymer mud with 79-90 pcf MW will be used. After drilling to Liner point, 5" Liner will be run and cemented.
Liner lap will be test as positive test w/ applying 2000 psi over pressure on mud column hydrostatic and negative test with dry test tools as 2000 psi differential pressure against formation pressure below liner lap.
- At well W014N, coring will be done from zones 8,9,10 and 11 of Sarvak Fm. (272m)
- Also in W065 location, F.B.DST from first 80m of Bangestan group Fm. will be done.

# Sequence of Operation

# 26" Hole Section

# General

# Sequence of Operation
Make up 26" BHA as per BHA design, drill formation down to 150m (18 5/8" casing point in Aghajari formation). Use LCM pill if encountered heavy losses. If LCM could not cure loss then use cement plug(s) to cure it.
Note:
- TD should be adjusted in such a way to have 2 meters of rat hole under casing shoe.
- The exact TD will be determined based on 18 5/8" casing tally.
- Do not forget to strap pipe while POOH.
- Drop Totco prior to POOH to evaluate the verticality of hole.
- Use following parameters to drill this hole section:
WOB = Maximum available
RPM = 90-130
GPM = 800-1000
At TD, Circulate and clean hole. POOH, P/U 26” bit , RIH and condition the hole to section TD. Check for any fill. CBU on bottom. POOH. If observed any indication of drag, run back to bottom. Wash and ream to make sure that the hole condition is good.
R/U 18 5/8" casing running gear. Perform Safety meeting. Run ±150m of 18 5/8", 87.5 ppf, J-55, BTS casing with guide shoe and stab-in collar to bottom as per casing program. Casing shoe should be adjusted in such a way to have 2 meters of rat hole and enough stick up (1.5 m) on rig floor. Fill casing by non LCM fresh mud joint by joint.
Note:
- Take care when running casing not to set down excessive weight if tight hole or ledges are encountered.
Run stinger on 5” DP’s using the “C” Plate and 5” x 18 5/8" centralizer. Sting in. M/U and test cementing lines. Circulate for one casing annulus volume. While circulating, check the DP’s annulus for any return. Use all DP’s weight on the stinger, if observing any minor leakage through DP’s annulus. Cement casing as per program. Any minor adjustment regarding the corrected depth will be done in due time. Displace with mud. Sting out. Check the float. Circulate to clean the DP. POOH.
Note:
- Based on the results obtained from cementing, decision will be made to perform top jobs.
Rest the 18 5/8" casing on the elevator until cement surface samples get hard. Check for casing to be center inside 30" conductor pipe. In case of any problem, report immediately.
If there is no cement return to surface, a top-up job should be performed to fill the annulus, stage by stage until cement reaches the surface. Use the same weight as the primary cement slurry. Ensure adequate X-over for connecting line pipe to cement line is available. The cement should be accelerated with max. 2% calcium chloride and no other additives. Wait for the top job cement to get hard.
Cut 30" conductor pipe from cellar base. L/D and remove it. Rough cut 18 5/8" landing joint and L/D same. Dress casing neck suitable to install and to weld casing head housing.
Install and weld casing head housing 20 3/4” – 3000 psi on casing stub and test the sealing of welded casing (limit pressure to a maximum of 80% of the collapse strength of the casing or maximum 500 psi). Outlets to align with cellar stairs.
Note:
- Before installing the casing head housing, open all the test and plastic packing ports and clean inside it with water and air.
- Do not forget to check the wear bushing with CHH and tie down flange before N/U BOP’s.
Install tie down flange plus 20 3/4”x3000 psi spacer spool. N/U 20 3/4” 3000 psi BOP’s and related lines. N/U bell nipple. Test BOP’s as per NIOC policy to 2000 psi pressure.

# Sequence of Operation

# General

# Sequence of Operation
Make up 8 3/8" BHA as per BHA design (Junk Sub Included). RIH to top of collar. Work on junk sub, Circulate, clean hole, POOH. Run Radial Cement Bond Log from 9 5/8" casing. P/U new bit, RIH to bottom. Drill ahead down to liner point at 4314m. Circulate, clean hole. Perform wiper trip to last casing shoe.
Note:
- Adjust mud weight according to nearby wells and open hole condition.
- Perform BOP's test on each 7 days as per NIOC policy.
- Section TD maybe changed based on formation tops detected by well site geologist.
- Do not forget to strap pipe while POOH.
- A material balance to determine the theoretical concentrations of additives in the mud should be calculated and reported to WSS. Significant deviations from programmed values should be explained.
At section TD, CBU, sweep the hole cleaned, perform wiper trip. R/U wire line equipment. Run Full Suite log. Run Image log. Run CDR/GR log.
Condition hole prior to liner job. Due to setting of 7” liner as off bottom, fill the rat hole with high vis pill to prevent of dropping cement. R/U Liner running tool. Run 7" liner (32 ppf, P-110, Vam / 29 ppf, P-110, Vam) as per program. P/U and run liner hanger and accessories. Follow liner string with 5” DPs to top of Ilam formation (Run drift inside 5” D.P & perform SLM while RIH with liner).
M/U cementing line. Set the liner as per program. Release setting tool. Cement liner as per program. Set liner packer (If Available). Pull out setting tools 15 std’s, Circulate to clean the string. POOH with liner setting tools while L/D excess 5” DP on floor.
Clean well to liner lap (±3253 Mt) with 8 ½” bit #RR. P/U 6" bit and new BHA (4-3/4" DC & 3-1/2" DP), RIH, Clean and drill out cement (if any) to top of LDC. Circulate, clean hole, POOH to 3200m. Closed top rams and perform liner lap pressure test (2000 psi for 30 minutes) and Dry test liner lap if liner top packer did not use. (In case of any leakage, firstly perform squeezing cement behind liner if unsuccessful then tie back the liner to surface)
Note: Dry test program will be offered in due time.
- Radial cement log will be performed under 1000 psi inside 7” liner and if necessary, repair of cement behind liner will be done.
- According to delineation targets of these wells, the F.B.DST from first 50m of Asmari Fm. will be done in E012 and W065 wells.
- Full suite log of Asmari and Pabdeh formations will be done in E012 (first 80m of Pabdeh) and W065 wells.
- Image log of Asmari and first 80m of Pabdeh will be done in E012 well.
- RFT (40 points), using dual packer (4 point) and fluid type determinant tool (6 point or interval) will be performed in E012 well.
L/D last BHA (6” DC) and P/U new BHA (4-3/4” DC). Perform BOP and related equipment pressure test.

# 6" Hole Section

# General

# Sequence of Operation
Make up 6" BHA as per BHA design. RIH to top of float collar. Drill out plugs, float collar and cement to 2m above shoe. Displace hole by 79-90 pcf Oil Base Mud. DESIGN FACTORS
The design factors have been captured from Exxon Production Research Co. (EPR) standards for uniaxial (conventional) and triaxial (von Mises, Hill) casing and tubing design.
Minimum specified yield strength should be derated for elevated temperatures
Collapse resistance is based on tubulars with nominal wall thickness. However, a 10% allowance for wall loss due to casing wear is accounted for in the design factor for protective strings subsequently used for production.
Different collapse design factors may be required for casing and liners subjected to non-uniform external pressure loading (e.g., flowing salt)
Burst resistance design factors apply equally to both the pipe body and the connection design ratings (internal yield pressure). However, a 10% allowance for wall loss due to casing wear is accounted for in the design factors for surface and protective strings. Note that the design factors for the connections (internal yield pressure) on these strings should remain as used on production strings (no coupling wear). Also, connection design ratings are based on nominal wall for burst resistance.
Tension capability of pipe body is based on tubulars with nominal wall thickness.
Design factor may be reduced to 1.333 if the connection meets the appropriate EPR qualification requirements.
For API tubulars, the burst resistance and calculations of the triaxial stress should be based on 87.5% of nominal wall thickness. For non-API tubulars, the burst resistance and the calculation of triaxial stress should be based on minimum specified wall thickness. However, a 10% allowance for wall loss due to casing wear is accounted for in the triaxial design factor for surface and protective strings.
Collapse design factor
A collapse resistance design factor of 1.0 is required for surface casing, protective casing, and protective liners since these strings are not usually subjected to
collapse loads. Normally, the pressure is greater inside these casing strings than
outside. All other tubular strings require a collapse design factor of 1.125. If
protective casing or liners ultimately serve as production casing or liners, a design
factor of 1.238 is required to include a 10% allowance for wear that may occur
while drilling through these strings.
Burst Design Factor
There are three categories of design factors for burst resistance:
No Anticipated Shut-In Surface Pressure: These design factors are typically used for wells that are not expected to be exposed to shut-in surface pressures and represent minimal risk.
Standard Wells: These design factors are used for normal pressure (i.e., 9.0-lb/gal pore pressure) development wells where pressures and operating conditions are well established.
Critical Wells: These design factors are normally used for wells that meet any of the following criteria:
Wildcats, field wildcats, or outpost wells where pressures and operating
conditions are not well established
Wells located in or near marine environments (i.e., offshore, waterway,
river crossing)
Wells requiring protective casing in abnormal pressure transition zones (i.e., greater than 13.0-lb/gal mud weight)
Wells requiring either H2S tubular design or wells that may encounter gas bearing formations suspected of containing greater than 500 ppm H2S, unless in a mature area with past history of trouble-free operations and a maximum pressure of less than 3000 psi
Wells located in areas with ultra-sensitive environmental concern that could result in considerable adverse publicity and/or very high cost
Wells located within 1/2 mile of a densely populated area (community or small settlement) or heavily traveled road, railroad, waterway, or airport
Leak-Resistance Design Factor
A leak-resistance design factor is applied to the connections of tubing, casing, and
liner strings that are required to hold pressures at some point during the life of the
well. A design factor of 1.0 is required for leak resistance.
When designing any tubular string, the lower of the leak resistance and burst
pressure working ratings determines the maximum internal pressure working
rating of the string.
Tension Design Factor
Tension design factors are used to calculate tension working ratings and are
applied to all casing and tubing strings. There are two categories of design factors for tension:
Pipe Body
These design factors are applied to pipe-body design ratings. Pipe-body design
ratings are always based on the minimum specified yield strength of the pipe-body material (i.e., pipe-body yield strength). A design factor of 1.333 is required for the pipe body of casing and tubing.
Connection
These design factors are applied to connection design ratings (i.e., joint strength). Connection tension design factors differ for casing and tubing. Tubing connection design factors are based on the tubing connection design ratings which use the minimum specified yield strength. Thus, tubing design factors for the connection are the same as for the pipe body (i.e., 1.333). Casing connection design ratings  are based on the ultimate strength (i.e., fracture, jump-out) of the casing. Since this design rating basis can result in failure loads higher than pipe-body yield, the tension design factor for casing connections is higher (i.e., 1.5) than that of the pipe body.
Compression Design Factor
The compression design factor applies to strings that are subjected to large
compressive loads. These are normally surface casing strings, but may be
conductor or structural casing strings in some cases. A design factor of 1.333 is
required in these cases. The compression design factor applies to both the pipe
body and the connection. When designing a casing string for compression, the
lower of the pipe body and connection working ratings determines the
compression working rating for the string.
Triaxial Design Factor
A design factor of 1.25 is used for triaxial designs for tubing, production casing, and production liners. For surface casing, protective casing and protective liners, a design factor of 1.375 is used to provide an additional 10% allowance for wear when drilling through these strings. Uniaxial design factors must also be satisfied. The von Mises equation is used to calculate triaxial stresses in carbon steels. For coldworked CRA materials that exhibit yield stress anisotropy, the Hill equation is used to calculate triaxial stresses. For CRA materials, the minimum specified yield strength must be derated for elevated temperature. For carbon steels, a similar temperature derating should be considered for temperatures greater than 300°F. The same design factors apply for both equations. Triaxial stress calculations are based on minimum specified wall thickness.

BURST LOAD CASES
Gas Kick Profile; This drilling load case creates an internal pressure profile that simulates the maximum pressures imposed on the current string while circulating a gas kick to the surface. The following assumptions are made:
The Driller's Method is used to circulate the kick influx out of the wellbore. That is, the mud density used to circulate the kick out of the wellbore is the same as the mud density in use at the depth from which the influx originates. The Driller's Method, in comparison to the Wait-and-Weight or Engineer's methods, results in higher casing pressures during circulation of the kick influx from the wellbore, and is therefore used to ensure a conservative analysis.
The mud density does not vary with temperature and pressure.
The effect of annular friction pressure losses is ignored. This is consistent with a normal kill procedure conducted at a slow pump rate.
Kick volume and kick intensity was assumed 100bbl and 0.5ppg, respectively.
Surface Protection (BOP); This drilling load case is used as a criterion for the design of the upper portion of the casing. This load case is appropriate when used in conjunction with the Gas Kick Profile load case, the former serving as the basis for burst design in the upper section of the string and the latter serving as design basis in the lower section. The Surface Protection (BOP) load case is based on full displacement to gas, with a surface boundary condition equal to the surface pressure that would result from fracture pressure at the shoe above the deepest open hole interval for which the current string is exposed to drilling loads and a seawater gradient back to surface. This load case develops relatively high design pressures at shallow depths, while providing a more conservative design pressure at shallow depths than that which would obtain from the Gas Kick Profile load case. This load case represents no actual physical scenario; however, when used with the gas kick criterion, it ensures that the casing weak point is not at the surface. Typically, the gas kick load case will control the design deep and the surface protection load case will control the design shallow, leaving the string's weak point somewhere in the middle.
