TECHNICAL DESCRIPTION


   The BOP Isolation Test Tool is used for testing the BOP stack while the stack is landed
   on the Vetco Gray 18-3/4" MS 700 wellhead housing. The tool seals in the wellhead
   housing bore and isolates the casing string/casing hanger seal from the BOP cavity test
   pressure.

   The Isolation Test Tool functions and tests to 15,000 psi (1035 bar) with or without the
   Nominal Seat Protector (NSP), wear bushings or casing hangers in place.
   Tool functions are both vertical (inches) and rotational (284o). Vertical stem movement
   collapses the tool, energizing the bulk seal and expanding the ‘C’ ring when needed.
   Rotational action allows the tool to be run in the extended position preventing premature
   seal or ‘C’ ring energization.

   The tool is preset in one of two positions by engaging the anti-rotation pin in one of two
    slots. One position is for testing with the nominal seat protector in place where weight
   expands the ‘C’ ring into the wellhead cam tool profile. The second position is for
   pressure  testing on the wear bushing or hanger and the wellhead without anything
   landed on the load shoulder, while the ‘C’ ring remains retracted on the tool.

  A bulk seal on the OD of the tool is energized between the tool and wellhead bore during
   collapse of the tool.

   Test pressures are limited to the working pressure of the wellhead system, i.e. 18-3/4"
   system 10,000 (690 bar) or 15,000 psi (1035 bar), 16-3/4" system 10,000 psi (690 bar),
   by the BOP stack system operating pressure or by the capabilities of the component
   upon which the tool is landed, whichever is less.

ROUTINE OPERATION


                   PRE-OPERATIONAL CHECKS

    1.  Remove the protectors. Thoroughly clean and inspect the tool joints and all other
         exteriors of the tool. Deburr as required. When examining the outer surface of the
          tool, note any marks or grooves from previous use. This is recommended in case
        resistance is encountered when running or retrieving the tool so that further
        examination of new marks may be of value to determine the problem.

    2.   Examine the sealing element on the OD of the tool and replace if necessary.

    3.   Pick up the tool at the stem upper tool joint (4-1/2” IF) and suspend vertically.
       Ensure that the upper section properly retracts from the lower section with a
        standoff of 3-1/8” between upper and lower sections and that it will rotate freely to
        the left. Slack off all suspended weight; rotate the stem to the right 7/8 turn to
        confirm that the stop plate will rotate freely within its detent groove and that it
        properly drops into the slot in the inner body. A strap wrench around the lower body
      may be required. A simple way to move the Tool from Long slot to short slot is to
         position the tool stop plate in mid way of the detent groove. This will allow the lower
       body to move either way to change from long to short.


                             NOTE:


 This should only be done when the tool is in the short slot. If the tool is in the long slot and
 weight is applied downward while in the un-jayed position, it will expand the C ring and
 make it difficult to get the tool sections separated, the lower section will have to be
 anchored to the floor to get them apart.


    4.   With the tool in the running position, examine the polypak seal on the OD of the
       upper body and replace as required. Ensure that the lips of the seal are facing up.

    5.   Check the standoff dimension of 3-1/8" between the upper and lower sections of
        the tool, per sheet 2 of 3 drawing H56027-3. If the dimension is out, then the lower
       body/stem threads have backed off and need to be re-tightened to ensure proper
        pre-load of the seal.

    6.   Lightly grease all external surfaces of the test tool prior to running.

Testing on Nominal Seat Protector

   Requires proper placement of the anti-rotation pin onto the long vertical groove located
    in one of the flow-by slots. This allows C ring expansion to carry the test pressure load
   through the housing rather than the NSP (see Dwg. 3254 on page 3). Refer to drawing
   H56027-38 for pin orientation. A notch has been milled into the diameter above the
   polypak seal and visually aligns with the NSP long slot and anti-rotation pin when the
    tool is in the NSP position.

Test ing on Casing Hanger, Wear Bushing or Load Shoulder on Hous ing

   Actuation of the ‘C’ ring is not required in this operation; hence the inner body must be
   rotated and locked in the position indicated for the wear bushing on the inner body (Ref.
   Dwg. H56027-38). The indicator notch will be 22.5o to the right of the anti-rotation pin
   when the tool is set in the casing hanger/wear bushing position.

RUNNING PROCEDURE


General

   With downhole or below rotary operations, tools with instructions such as “weight down”
   or torque values are used to assist in the proper function of the product as well as
   provide definitive signals to the operator at the rig floor. Rig position, hole condition,
   motion compensator sensitivity, etc. are factors which affect function and can affect the
   weight required for a successful performance. In addition, water depth can also play a
   tremendous part in providing accurate feedback at the rig floor. As a general point,
   weights and torques in this procedure are expressed as values anticipated in water
   depths ranging to 1200-1500 ft. Working in water depths in excess of this will cause a
   movement toward the higher part of the range and in cases exceed the range.
  Remember that these values are needed to ensure proper function and provide solid
   feedback.

    Drillpipe may be hung off the lower connection to decrease trip time. Hang off capacity
   should not exceed 250 kips or capacity of the tool joint which is screwed onto lower
   connection, whichever is less.


                              CAUTION:


 Caution should be taken not to exceed the maximum load of 40,000 lbs weight
 down while in the running position or tool damage could occur. Any pipe weight
 below the tool must be taken into consideration.

    In any case, where doubt arises, do not hesitate to contact your local sales or service
   representative.

Test ing on Nominal Seat Pro tec tor for all Sys tems

Test ing on Tag Shoulder on Fullbore Sys tems ONLY

   The running, handling, set and testing procedures are similar for all wellhead positions;
   however, the tool anti-rotation pin is positioned in a different slot for the nominal seat
   protector which allows the ‘C’ ring to expand. Refer to the pre-operational checks and
   drawings to ensure the tool is oriented correctly.

   The ‘C’ ring on the BOP Isolation Test Tool must be actuated and engaged into the
   wellhead cam tool profile prior to pressure testing against the sealing element, in order
    to properly transmit the test pressure forces through the high pressure wellhead
   housing.

    1.  Make up the Isolation Test Tool to a stand of drillpipe.

       Ensure that the stem and upper body sub-assembly is fully extended and rotated
         to the left, properly detenting the tool and fully exposing the flow-by path through
        the test tool.

        For the NSP only: Confirm that the anti-rotation pin is engaged in the correct slot
         position for seating on the NSP. This ensures expansion of the ‘C’ ring. This can
       be checked by verifying the dogs expand the ‘C’ ring during tool collapse and the
         indicator mark aligns with anti-rotation pin.

       The tool can be functioned at the rotary prior to running by using the setting
        procedures to verify tool is functioning correctly. Reset tool prior to running.


                             NOTE:


 Minimum weight down (tool weight) should be used when checking the tool at the rotary
 while the tool is in the long slot. Closing the gap 3-1/8” could cause the tool to become
  stuck. A spacer can be used and placed between the two sections to prevent them from
 collapsing all the way. It only takes a slight downward movement (¼”) to see the ‘C’ ring
  start to expand.


                            CAUTION:


  Failure to properly set the tool position will result in high retrieval forces for the
 NSP or swedging the NSP into the wellhead.


    2.   Measure in the hole when running to verify that the test tool lands in the proper
         position by comparing present water depth/wellhead position calculations with the
         location of the tool.

    3.   Prior to the test tool entering the BOP, open the motion compensator and adjust it
         to support the weight of the running string. Open the choke and kill lines. Do not
         rotate the tool to the right until it is landed.

  4.   Slowly lower the tool in the wellhead housing until a drop in load is observed
     (maximum 40,000 lbs [18,140 kgs]) which indicates the test tool has reached the
      proper position in the wellhead housing bore. Reconfirm that the tool has fully
      landed by comparing previous measurements. Close the choke and kill lines.

  5.   Set a minimum of 10,000 lbs (4536 kgs) maximum 15,000 lbs (6806 kgs) of the
       running string weight on the nominal seat protector. Open the running string to the
      atmosphere. Apply RH rotation to obtain one turn at the tool. Evidence of the turn
      can be seen through monitoring the drill string weight and a torque build up of
      3000-5000 ft-lbs.

     When testing the NSP, proper actuation of this tool is dependent of “free travel”
       (dropping of the stem) under load. As the drill string is rotated, the string will drop
      approximately 3". As the string begins to drop, increase the weight down to 20,000
       lbs (9100 kg) minimum to 60,000 lbs (27,216 kg) maximum. This dynamic action
       assists in testing on the NSP and enhances expansion of the “C” ring into the cam
        profile of the wellhead. Lifting the tool off of the NSP keeps the test pressure load
      from being exerted on the NSP. The 20,000 lbs (9100 kg) is desirable weight at the
       running tool; therefore allowance should be made for passive compensators.


                            NOTE:


The total downward movement of 3" indicates the tool has fully energized the “C” Ring
into the wellhead cam profile, energized the primary wellhead sealing element in the
wellhead bore and engaged the upper body polypak seal which seals off the annulus
flow-by.


  6.    Fill the landing drillpipe string with water once the tool has landed and set. When
        testing, the open running string provides a visible monitor that seals on the test tool
      have fully seated and are not damaged. Damaged seals or incomplete travel will
       allow the test fluid to bypass the test tool and travels up the ID of the running string.
           If bypass fluid is visible, lift the tool and repeat step 5 with increased weight down.
       Further bypass indicates seal damage and the tool must be retrieved  for
       inspection.

  7.   Test the BOP per standard rig procedures.  It is recommended that  full test
      pressure is achieved in increments of 1000 psi (69 bar) or less and that fluid
      volume and pressure are closely monitored.

  8.   Bleed off the test pressure; open the pipe rams or the annular preventor and
       retrieve the isolation test tool with straight upward pull.

  9.   Proceed with standard rig drilling procedures.

Test ing on Wear Bush ing on all Systems

Test ing on Load Shoulder on Fullbore Sys tems ONLY

   The running, handling set and testing procedures are similar to the NSP; however, the
    tool anti-rotation pin is positioned in a different slot. The slot orientation allows the ‘C’
    ring dogs to align with the slot and thus not expand the ‘C’ ring. The test pressure loads
   are transmitted through the tool directly onto the wear bushing, hanger or housing
   shoulder.

    1.  Make up the Isolation Test Tool to a stand of drillpipe.

       Ensure that the stem and upper body sub-assembly is fully extended and rotated
         to the left, properly detenting the tool and fully exposing the flow-by path through
        the test tool. Tool can be functioned at rotary table prior to running.

    2.   Measure in the hole when running to verify that the test tool lands in the proper
         position by comparing present water depth/wellhead position calculations with the
         location of the tool.

    3.   Prior to the test tool entering the BOP, open the motion compensator and adjust it
         to support the weight of the running string. Open the choke and kill lines. Do not
         rotate the tool to the right until it is landed.

    4.   Slowly lower the tool in the wellhead housing until a drop in load is observed
      (maximum 40,000 lbs [18,140 kg]), indicating the test tool has reached the proper
         position in the wellhead housing bore. Reconfirm that the tool has fully landed by
       comparing previous measurements. Close the choke and kill lines.

    5.    Fill the landing drillpipe string with water once the tool has landed and set.

    6.   Set a minimum of 10,000 lbs (4536 kg) maximum 15,000 lbs (6804 kg) of the
        running string weight on the WB or casing hanger. Open the running string to the
       atmosphere. Apply RH rotation to obtain one turn at the tool. Evidence of the turn
       can be seen through monitoring the drill string weight.

    7.  When testing, the open running string provides a visible monitor that seals are
        energized and are not damaged. Damaged seals or incomplete travel will allow the
         test fluid to bypass the test tool and return up the ID of the running string. If bypass
          fluid is visible, lift the tool and repeat step 5 with increased weight down. Further
       bypass indicates seal damage and the tool must be retrieved for inspection.

    8.   Test the BOP per standard rig procedures.  It is recommended that  full test
        pressure is achieved in increments of 1000 psi (69 bar) or less and that fluid
       volume and pressure are closely monitored.

    9.   Bleed off the test pressure, open the pipe rams or the annular preventor and
         retrieve the isolation test tool with straight upward pull.

POST-OPERATIONAL MAINTENANCE


    1.   Inspect the tool for marks, scratches, etc. which could be an indicator of any
         interface problems when the tool was in the hole.

    2.   Extend the test tool per standard procedures and wash thoroughly with fresh
        water.

    3.   Inspect the primary sealing element and the upper body seal and replace as
        necessary.

    4.   Re-jay the tool and lightly grease all sliding and exterior surfaces prior to storage.
