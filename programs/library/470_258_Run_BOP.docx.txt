

### Running the BOP:
Purpose:
The purpose of this procedure is to ensure smooth and safe operation when running the BOP- Stack on Riser.
Scope:
The following procedure describes the required activities, which need to be carried out prior and during the running of the BOP- Stack on Riser.
Responsible Persons:
OIM / Toolpusher / Subsea Engineer / Driller / Crane Operator
Procedure:
Safety Precautions and Preparations:
Hold a Toolbox meeting. Review the procedure and JSA (Job Safety Analysis).
Acquire weather forecasts and confirm a suitable weather window is available for the operation.
The rig is to be moved 30m off location while running the BOP.
Permit to work fully approved and signed with recommendations.
The clear-call system between the rig floor and the moonpool is to be operational before the job begins.
Work vests (correctly donned and secured) must be worn when working in or near the side of the moonpool.
PPE worn at all times.
Safety harnesses attached to fall arrestors must be worn when working from the spider beams or from heights.
Protectors must be correctly secured before any riser is removed from the catwalk.
Never run an air winch down through the rotary to the moonpool for man riding.
All air hose connections must be secured with "R" clips and whip checks
Lifting gear fully inspected (check for appropriate colour coding, damage, malfunction, etc).
All non essential personnel to keep clear of the drill floor, raised catwalk and riser storage areas.
Stand clear in the moon pool when the riser is moving through the rotary.
Ensure good communication between driller, POD reel operator and the crew working in moon pool.
The BOP stack should not be run if vessel motion is outside operating limits for BOP handling, or weather window is not sufficient to run and land the BOPs. Crane limitations are a maximum of 20 knots wind speed and maximum of 1.5 degrees (full amplitude) pitch and roll for handling the slip joint and landing joint.
Serial numbers of the riser and the position of each joint of riser run will be recorded while running so that the riser record book can be updated.
Record the string weight after each joint is run to assess the buoyancy condition.
Ensure there is no build up of scale or loose debris in the riser prior to running
Check while running in the condition of the straps securing the buoyancy modules, if required change out, change out before lowering through rotary.

BOP / LMRP preparation to be carried out between wells:
During previous drilling phase the following checks to be done prior to running the BOP:
Open all the ram bonnets and clean out the cavities.
Check wear of the bonnet, seal face and main body of rams.
Check for damage/wear of the ram piston rods and seals.
Check the ram rubbers for damage and change as required.
Pressure test all the BOP hydraulic actuators to 250psi and maximum operating pressure except the annular close functions which will only be tested with pipe in them to 250psi and 1500 psi.
Change the bonnet gaskets. Oil the bonnet faces and close them. Torque the bolts to 5000ft/lbs.
Visual checks on the annular preventers; change the elements as required.
Adjust the pre-charge pressure on the subsea accumulators including the annular surge bottles.
Check the pre-charge pressure on the surface accumulators’.
Perform function test from both yellow & blue pods.
Function test the wellhead and LMRP connectors and the kill and choke mini connectors.
Operate the rams with 500 psi and check for easy of movement.
Check the two (2) electrically operated pumps on the BOP control unit (calibration and
auto start).
Visually check the BOP and LMRP wellbores for wear or key seating.
Pick up the BOP/LMRP with the BOP transporter forklift. Clean and grease the connectors. Clean the ring grooves and install new gaskets on the LMRP/BOP skid test stumps.
Install the test plugs and pressure test the BOPs. Rams and fail safes to 300psi for 5mins and 15000psi for 15mins. Annulars to 300psi for 5mins and 7000 (70% of SWP) for 10mins.
Remove the test plugs.
Check that all tools and equipment have been removed from the BOP.
Riser Preparation:
Check the seal condition on all connections (riser, kill, and choke and booster line).
Check the riser seal-subs and pin and box ends for wear or damage.
Check the wellbore of the riser pins and boxes for wear or key seating.
Check riser buoyancy modules for cracks or damage. Ensure the retaining straps are tight and in good condition.
Ensure the buoyancy end stops are secure.
Check the condition of the POD hose clamps and hydraulic conduit clamps.
Check the riser telescopic joint packer record to ensure it will hold for the total duration of the well - pressure test the same.
Check the diverter equipment and support ring.

Personnel Requirements:
Preparation:
Service and check the catwalk, drill floor and moonpool air hoists including the man riding winches and check the wires are in good condition.
Service the riser handling and riser test tools.
Grease the riser spider and check the gimbals hydraulic system.
Have sufficient air built up in the air accumulator bottles.(Sub Sea to check)
Check/service all grub screws on the riser protectors.
Clear the rig floor of any equipment not needed for riser handling.
Clean and oil or grease the BOP transporter.
Assure that Position and Riser Angle Indication Beacons are charged and tested.
I) Run the BOP:
Move the rig off the well head.
Lay down the mouse hole.
Inform the BCO and Central Control Room that the BOP is about to be transported to the well centre.
Skid the BOP to well centre with the BOP transporter. Unlock the wellhead connector from the skid test stump and retract the ring gasket retainers.
Lower the retractable test-stump, remove the old ring gasket from the test stump, grease the connector and clean the ring grooves.
Install a new AX ring gasket on the test stump, extend the test-stump and lock the ring gasket retainers.
Grease the connector, clean the ring groove, remove the cover from the mandrel and install a new AX ring gasket.
Pick up a stand of drill pipe in the elevators and run it down through the BOP ensuring the tool joints are clear of the rams.
Perform a complete BOP function test on both pods. Pull the stand out of the BOP and function test the shear rams.  Record the time taken and fluid volume for each function.
Perform an EDS function test.
Rack back the stand then change to the 500ton bails and elevators. Do not connect the link tilt cylinders.
Unlock the top drive pipe handler.
Remove the rotary table insert bushings, master bushings and adaptor ring. Take measures to prevent personnel from falling through the open rotary.
Install the Gimbal, riser spider and control console in the rotary.
Pick up the termination joint with the crane. Place it on the drill floor with the box end towards well centre, check the stabs and seals, change as required, oil/grease the stabs as required and set it in the spider. Remove the riser handling tool if used
Put the first joint of riser and pick it up.
Engage Hydraulic Running Tool (1500Psi max) or make up the running tool bolts (Cameron RF 7500ft/lbs).
Set up reels for running BOP, centre all BOP functions at panel except for pod locks and select one pod and use this reel mini panel. Put riser connector to lock, BOP connector to unlock and both secondary to vent, open the isolation needle valve and set regulator to 1500 psi.
Stroke up guide line tensioners and pod line tensioners to mid stroke.
Orientate the riser joint and stab it onto the termination joint in the rotary. Slack off the full weight of the riser joint to ensure proper engagement with the previous joint. Make up the bolts (Cameron RF 7500ft/lbs).
Check the riser adaptor box and the kill and choke stabs are clean. Change any damaged seals and oil/grease as required
Run in and stab the termination joint and first joint onto the riser adaptor. Make up the bolts the coupling bolts (Cameron RF 7500ft/lbs).
Take a test lift and re-torque the coupling bolts.
Pick the BOP up clear of the BOP support dogs and retract them.
Unlock and retract the BOP transporter.
Record the flex joint, LMRP and BOP slope indicator readings.
Run the BOP in the water with all kill & choke fail safes and isolation valves in the close position.
Record the weight of the BOP assembly, termination joint and first slick joint.
Close the riser spider and slowly land the riser in the spider and back out the riser handling tool bolts, and then remove the riser running tool.
Install the riser testing tool and make up the bolts (Cameron RF 7500ft/lbs). Pressures tests the kill and choke lines to 300psi-low and 15000psi for 10 minutes respectively (as per API recommendations).
ii) Run Riser Joints from the Aft Side Pipe Deck:
Lift the riser joint from the storage rack onto the catwalk with the portside crane using a two leg spreader and tag lines.
Shackle the crane and the catwalk air winch to the forward end and a drill floor air winch to the aft end.(Air winch only required if weather does not allow normal operation)
Ensure good communication between the crane operator and air winch driver with the deck foreman in clear view.
Pick up the joint with the crane and the drill floor air winch sliding it up the door ramp and keeping it slightly below horizontal, controlling it with the catwalk air winch.
Land the flange on the drill floor and remove the protector as required. Check the riser, kill, choke and booster line stabs. Change any damaged seals and oil/grease as required.
Install the hydraulic riser running tool and apply the required pressure (1500Psi), check if tool is complete engaged.
Rotate the elevators to face aft and lift them with an air winch, at the same time lowering the blocks to swing the elevators out over the riser running tool.
Lower the elevators onto the riser handling tool and latch them securing the latch with rope.
Remove the drill floor air winches from the riser joint and the elevators.
Pick up the joint with the elevators slacking off on the catwalk winch, adjusting the height with the crane to bring the box end into the drill floor and continue picking up until the joint rests.
Remove the crane and the catwalk air winch.
Check the riser pins and boxes and the kill, choke and booster line stabs. Change any damaged seals and oil/grease the stabs as required. Install Riser Seal Sub in joint which is in the Spider, assure seals are in good condition and lightly oiled.
Swing the joint into well centre, orientate the kill, choke and booster line stabs as required and stab the joint.
Slack off the full weight of the riser joint to ensure proper engagement. Make up the bolts (Cameron RD 7500ft-lbs).
Take a test lift, re-torque the bolts and then take the weight, open the spider and inform the moonpool the string is ready to run in.
Lower the string slowly and stop to install the clamps as required.
Fill Kill and Choke lines while P/U the next joint of Riser.
iii) Pressure Test Kill and Choke Lines while Lowering BOP:
All high pressure testing is covered by the permit to work system. Prior to pressure testing a PTW (permit to work) and JSA (Job Safety Analysis) will be raised and signed by the OIM. Pressure testing is defined as applying pressure to a sealed item of equipment for the purpose of testing the integrity.
The permit must describe the equipment to be pressure tested and its location on the rig. It is the duty of the permit controller and the persons involved in the task to check that the testing does not clash with other operations.
Hold a Toolbox meeting. Review the procedure, PTW and JSA.
Upon receipt of the permit, the rig site copy must be displayed at the work site, with the equipment testing procedure and JSA.
The equipment testing procedure and JSA should also be displayed at the pressure test pump.
Check the calibration and test certificates for the high-pressure pump unit to be used.
Ensure there is a PRV (pressure relief valve) on the test pump. Check the rating.
The OIM/ Tool Pusher will check the PRV certification and that it is set within the MWP (maximum working pressure) of the lowest rated piece of equipment.
Ensure all pumps in the system to be tested have been electrically and mechanically isolated. All local and remote controls will have “Do not operate: isolated equipment” signs displayed.
Ensure any test pressure does not exceed the maximum working pressure of the equipment to be tested. Verify that the equipment to be tested is in a suitable condition to be tested and is fit for purpose.
All temporary lines must be snubbed to suitable fastenings.
All barriers will be in place and checked, to keep non essential personnel away from test area.
The pressure testing medium will normally be water (seawater or fresh). If any other medium is used ensure it is safe to use under pressure and additional procedures are in place. Use additional PPE (personal protective equipment) as required.
Operation:
Ensure a permit to work has been made out for the task. This must be checked at shift changes.
Ensure barriers are in place to prevent non essential personnel entering the area. The pressure path from the test pump to the equipment to be tested must also be barriered as required.
Check all the connections have been correctly made up and the pressure lines have been snubbed to a suitable anchorage.
Ensure there is a relief path, should the pressure pass the equipment being tested.
Make a P.A announcement that pressure testing is about to commence. This announcement must specify the area the testing is taking place and all associated areas.
Instructions between the test pump operator and the equipment being tested must be clear and concise and there must be open lines of communication i.e. Radios or telephone.
The instructions given to the test pump operator must include the line up being employed, the equipment to be pressure tested and the pressure to be applied. It is important to specify the above items as it enables the test pump operator to annotate the test chart correctly and avoids confusion.
The test chart must be suitable for both low and high pressure tests (two pens or two chart recorders).
Flush the line to confirm the correct line up. If any above normal pressure is seen, the flushing will be shut down.
Commence pressure testing.
Apply a low pressure test to the test pump and pressure line against a closed valve.
When applying the low pressure test do not increase the pressure above the test pressure and bleed down.
The low pressure test will be to 200-300 psi and following stabilization a 5 minute straight line on the calibrated test chart must be achieved as per API RP53 although this may be extended to ensure a straight line is achieved.
Following the low pressure test increase the pressure at a steady rate to apply the high pressure test.
Do not apply a pressure greater than the required test pressure and if the pressure drops increase it back up to the test pressure.
Following stabilization a 10 minute straight line on the calibrated test chart must be achieved as per API RP53 although this may be extended to ensure a straight line is achieved.
In the event of a leak always bleed the pressure off and get this confirmed by the test pump operator. Do not work on or attempt to tighten any fittings that are under pressure.
Make hourly PA announcements during the pressure testing operations. This practice ensures that all personnel, not involved with the operation are informed of the ongoing hazard.
At the conclusion of each test, bleed the pressure off, and confirm before lining up for the next test
If any equipment is to be tested off the critical path, i.e. stabbing valves or Gray valves, this should be carried out using the  test pump in an area away from rig floor if possible. This area should be clear of any other equipment.
Testing will be carried out in the same manner as in the above procedure.
Post Task Actions:
When all pressure testing has been completed ensure all the lines are bled down to zero before any rigging down.
At the conclusion of all testing make a PA announcement informing personnel that the high pressure testing is complete.
Rig down all equipment and remove all barriers placed to prevent personnel access.
Any machinery isolated for the testing will be de-isolated.
Review the procedure and JSA.
Tidy up all tools and rig down any lines.
Sign off and return PTW to permit control area.
iv) Pick Up the Telescopic Joint:
Ensure good communication between drill floor, pipe deck, crane operator, spider deck area and pod reels (Radios).
The telescopic joint is normally stored on the Starboard Side aft board side of the pipe deck.
Jump the ROV and check the TV monitor on the rig floor is receiving a good picture. Position the ROV and carry out a riser, BOP, slope indicator, and wellhead inspection.  This inspection should be witnessed by the Company Representative.
Lift the telescopic joint directly from the support to the drill floor with the main block of the starboard crane. Install the catwalk air winch on the aft end and a drill floor air winch on the fwd end.
Ensure good communication between the crane operator and air winch drivers with the deck foreman in clear view.
Lift the telescopic joint to the drill floor with the crane sliding it up the door ramp as far as possible, controlling it with the drill floor and catwalk air winches.
Land the packer box on the drill floor and remove the pin end protector.
Using a drill floor air winch, install the hydraulic riser running tool onto the slip joint and make up the bolts (Cameron RF 7500ft-lbs).
Rotate the elevators to face aft and lift them with an air winch while lowering travelling blocks to swing the elevators out above the riser handling tool.
Lower the elevators onto the riser handling tool and latch them. Secure the latch with rope.
Remove the drill floor air winches from the elevators and telescopic joint.
Ensure good communication between the driller, crane operator and air winch driver with the deck foreman in clear view.
Lift the telescopic joint with the elevators, lowering the deck crane at the same time, until the box end of the telescopic joint is resting on the catwalk with the outer barrel resting against the top of the V door.
Remove the top sling and spreader. Reposition the crane to the box end of the telescopic joint and pick it up restraining it with the catwalk air winch.
Flush through the kill, choke and booster lines to confirm they are clear. Check all the gooseneck stabs. Change any damaged seals and oil/grease as required.
Continue picking up the telescopic joint with the elevators and crane allowing the box end to swing in towards the top of the door ramp with the catwalk air winch and adjusting the height with the crane.
When inside the door hold the box, remove the crane and catwalk air winch.
Check the seals in the riser and kill and choke line boxes.
Continue to hoist the telescopic joint with the elevators, and swing it in to the rotary. Remove the protector from the riser joint in the rotary.
Orientate the kill and choke lines and stab the box onto the pin in the rotary.
Slack off the full weight of the telescopic joint to ensure proper engagement with the previous joint. Make up the coupling bolts (Cameron RF 7500lbs-ft).
Take a test lift, re-torque the bolts and then take the weight, open the spider.
Inform the crew in the moonpool etc the string is ready to run.
vi) Kill Choke Line Pressure Tests:
A permit to work must be in force prior to commencing all high pressure testing.
The kill, choke and booster lines are normally tested every 3 joints. The first test must be done once the termination joint and the first riser joint are run.
Land the riser on the spider and remove the riser running tool (if required).
Fill up the lines with water until free of air.
Install the riser plug testing tool.
Connect the testing manifold to the testing unit which must have a pressure recorder suitable for high and low pressure tests.
Before connecting the testing manifold to the test plugs flush through the lines then
make up the connections. Make up the coupling bolts (Cameron RF 7500lbs-ft).
Check the line up and pressure test the line up to testing manifold.
Pressure test the kill and choke lines to 300 psi for 5 min and 15000psi for 10 min. All tests performed against the kill & choke isolation valves.
Bleed off the pressure and remove the riser test tool.
vii) Land the BOP:
Notify the moonpool and the ROV personnel before lowering the telescopic joint to land the inner barrel mandrel on the spider.
Back out the bolts and remove the riser handling tool.
Pick up the landing joint (slick joint) from the aft pipe deck and install it on the slip joint.
Make up the coupling bolts (Cameron RF 7500 lbs-ft). Take a test lift and re-torque the bolts.
Pick up the riser string, open the riser spider and notify the moonpool and the ROV personnel before lowering the slip joint.
Lock the load ring onto the telescopic joint with the rotating dogs (6). Visually confirm all six dogs are locked. The SSE will witness this operation.
Once the load ring is locked to slip joint, take weight of the load ring (20klb over pull) and remove the safety slings from the load ring. Unlock the load ring support dogs.
With ROV observing, lower the telescopic joint to a working level for installing the kill, choke and booster line goosenecks and the hydraulics to slip joint.
The last clamps should be installed on the telescopic joint so that they will be just below the splash zone when BOPs are landed. (Two clamps on each side). Install support saddles and hot line hose on the goosenecks.
Using the drill floor and cellar deck air winches lower the kill line, booster line and choke line goosenecks (in that order) from the forward port side of the drill floor and stab them into the receptacles on the telescopic joint, install the lock blocks and retainers.
The kill, choke and booster lines can be pressure tested at this point to ensure the gooseneck seals are good before landing the BOP.
The riser tensioners must be pressured up well in advance (24 hrs minimum).
Adjust the riser tensioner and CMC pressures as required for landing the BOP (2/3 on MRT`s and 1/3 on DSC).
Spool off sufficient cable and conduit hoses from the reels to allow for landing and heave considerations.
With the BOP 5-10 meters above well head depth move the rig to final position the BOP above the wellhead.
Position the ROV to observe the wellhead and the guide posts. Confirm the wellhead connector is open, and the ring gasket is in place.
Move the rig into position and land the BOP with 50klb down weight.
Re-position the ROV to observe the wellhead connector position indicator. Lock the connecter confirming visually with the ROV.
Perform a pick up test with100klb over-pull.
Adjust the riser tensioners as required.
Record the slope indicator readings with the ROV. Record the ship and ROV headings.
Close the shear rams and pressure test the AX gasket to 500 psi.
Set neutral weight at the telescopic joint lock dogs.
Unlock the telescopic joint hydraulic latch and use the DSC to pull back the landing joint and stroke out the inner barrel. Land the crossover in the spider.
viii) Install the Diverter:
Back out the coupling bolts and tail out the landing joint . Install a protector on the pin in the rotary.
Lay down the landing joint to the Starboard Side After Maindeck.
Pick up the diverter from the pipe deck with the crane using slings of the correct length to keep the diverter horizontal. Care to be taken not to crush the flow line seal hydraulic piping. Use the catwalk and drill floor winches to control the load.
Land the diverter as far as possible into the drill floor with the handling tool towards well centre.
Rotate the elevators to face aft and lift them with an air winch at the same time lowering the blocks to swing the elevators out over the riser diverter handling tool.
Lower the elevators onto the handling tool and latch them securing the latch with rope.
Holding the diverter back with the catwalk winch lift it with the elevators and crane initially and then lower the crane and catwalk winch to swing the diverter.
Remove the crane and catwalk air winch.
Remove the protector from the joint in the rotary.
Stab the diverter onto the telescopic joint inner barrel mandrel and make up the bolts (Cameron RF 7500lbs-ft).
Remove the ball joint clamp.( If applicable).
Pick up the diverter and inner barrel and open the riser spider.
Extend Diverter Hanging Dogs and confirm visually the 4 dogs are extended.
Orientate, run in and land the diverter in the diverter housing.
Install the hydraulic manifold.
The hydraulic supply at the diverter manifold should read 2800 - 3000 psi.
Diverter element pressure should read 0 psi.
Slip joint pressure should read 0 psi.
The control valves should be as follows:
Diverter element  =  Open
Diverter Lockdown Dogs   =  Unlock
Insert Packer Lockdown Dogs =  Block (centre)
Set manifold pressure to 1500 psi.
Put diverter lock down dogs to "lock" and verify the 4 dogs are extended.
Take a 10klb over pull
Set the diverter element to 600 psi then change this function to "remote".
To remove the diverter handling tool turn the mandrel 41/2” turns to the left. Remove the diverter handling tool. Take measures to prevent anyone falling through the rotary.
Flush all lines with sea water (minimum one internal volume).
Fill the riser with sea water and energize the telescopic joint upper packer with sufficient pressure to make a seal (typically 50 to 100 psi) and the flow line seals to 200psi.
Check the telescopic joint packer and flow line seals are holding and there are no leaks on the hydraulic manifold.
Rig down the riser spider and gimbal.
Install the adaptor ring, master bushings, insert bushings and hole cover.
Pick up and install the mouse hole.
RIH the BOP test tool and install the diverter packer. Pressure test the BOP.
Post Task Actions:
Subsea Engineer to monitor fluid used by BOP.
Secure the Riser and BOP bridge cranes.
Sign off all permits.
After the BOP has been run, remove all handling gear and return it to its storage area.
