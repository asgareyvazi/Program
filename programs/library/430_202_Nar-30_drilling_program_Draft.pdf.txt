Introduction


     1.1    Program Introduction

           Nar anticline is located in coastal Fars, Northeast of Kangan and Northwest of west
             Assaluyeh anticlines. This structure extended Northwest – Southeast direction. The
           Nar anticline is nearly asymmetric and has 28 Km length and 5 to 8 Km width at
              surface.  Its area closure on top of the Kangan Formation  is about 160 square
              kilometers.

Pre Spud


      Pre-spud checklist distributed in contractor rigs. The checklist will be performed based on

      the aforementioned checklist. In addition to the checklist, following matters should be

      checked:

    Check & observe the alignment of the rig. This check will be performed in four stages;

           rig levelling, derrick verticality, derrick alignment with rotary table, rotary table

         alignment with conductor pipe. To do this pick up 3 joints of 9 1/2” DC and check the

           verticality of the derrick.

    Mechanical facilities for mud treatment such as :

         Desander, Desilter, mud cleaner, proper size of shaker–screens, hoppers, mud guns &

          mixers, should be all available on rig and functioning properly.

     All mud tanks, mud transfer lines & connection should be checked by filling them with

         water & cross wellhead circulation.

    Mud engineer laboratory should be equipped with a complete set of testing equipment.

        The calibration should be done under supervision of well site drilling supervisor

          before starting the operation.

     Weight indicator, rotary speed gauge, any smart drilling instrumentation, Stroke

          meter, pressure gauges on manifold and in the drilling console, loss / gain gauge and

         any other gauges should be checked and calibrated.

     Stand pipe manifold valves & connection must be checked & pressure tested.

     All motors should be checked under load prior to start drilling.

    Check & control mud stock & cement stock every Day & if there is any necessity  to

          request mud material, cement or any drilling tool, it should be prepared & requested

           at proper time.

     Air drilling package must be installed & ready for using in the case of need.

       Note: Fill up pre-spud check list and send it to drilling office.

2  Geological Overview

       The Nar-30 is located on Northeast flank of Nar anticline, about 341.4m of east of
       Nar-17 and 387m of northwest of Nar-21. This well will be drilled to total depth of
      2808m about 150m in Lower Dalan. The FBDST will be carried on in Lower Dalan


3  STRATIGRAPHY OF NAR STRUCTURE

       The stratigraphical succession in Nar - 30 will be normal from surface (Dariyan
       Fm.) down to total depth of 2808m in Lower Dalan as follows:


      Dariyan Formation (surface to 117m)
       This Formation consists of buff, white, organic detrital, porous Limestone in
        the upper
        part and grey to dark gray, pyritic, argillaceous Limestone in the lower part.
       Age: Aptian

     Gadvan Formation (117 to 182m)
      The Gadvan Formation starts with grey to greenish grey, sticky, calcareous
       Claystone or Marl and consists of buff grey to dark grey, argillaceous, buff,
       yellowish cream, organic detrital Limestone with interbeds of grey to greenish
        grey, sticky, calcareous Claystone or Marl in the middle and upper parts.
       Age: Barremian -Aptian.

      Fahliyan Formation (182 to 332m)
       This Formation consists of buff to white, soft, chalky, and buff to light cream,
       organic detrital Limestone and some thin beds of beige, fine to medium
       grained, crystalline Dolomite in the lower part.
       Age: Neocomian


      Hith Formation (332 to 397m)
       This Formation consists  of white, hard,  crystalline Anhydrite beds with
       generally interbeds of buff brown, organic detrital, porous and buff grey,
        crystalline, calcareous anhydritic Dolomite.
       Age: Upper Jurassic

     Surmeh Formation (397 to 1155m)
       This Formation  starts  with  beige and  buff, moderately  hard,  coarsely
        crystalline,  saccharoidal,  porous,  partially  anhydritic  Dolomite.  This
       formation  consists  of  an  alternation  of  Dolomite  with  the  above
        characteristics and white, hard, crystalline anhydrite in the upper part and

Kangan Formation (1943 to 2153MD)
Kangan Formation consists of light grey, off white, pale brown, grey, some
argillaceous & chalky Limestone, grey to light brown, some argillaceous
Dolomite with some black, dark grey, greenish grey, red brown Shale beds.
The Kangan Formation is divided to following units:

      Unit A:
      The upper part of Kangan Formation consists of grey to buff grey,
       micro crystalline, mottled, glauconitic, slightly anhydritic, dense ,hard
      and grey to buff grey, organic detrital, pseudo oolitic Dolomite with
        interbeds of red brown and greenish grey Shale.

      Unit B:
       This  unit  consists  of  buff  grey  and  light  brown,  crystalline,
       saccharoidal, porous, glauconitic, calcareous Dolomite to dolomitic
       Limestone and grey to buff grey, organic detrital, oolitic, porous and
       grey to brownish grey, micro crystalline, dense, slightly argillaceous,
        in part whitish grey, soft, chalky Limestone.


      Upper part of Unit C:
       This part of unit consists of a thin bed of greenish grey and dark
       brown,  silty, moderately hard Shale, buff grey to brownish grey,
        microcrystalline, hard and light cream to light grey, in part soft,
       chalky Limestone .
       Age: Lower Triassic

    necessary. Check flag sub for proper functioning.

 Choke manifold

  All chokes, lines, valves and manifold components which are exposed to well pressure should
  have a working pressure rating equal to or greater than the Bops. The minimum recommended
  size for all the choke lines and valves is 3”.The minimum working pressure ratings are as
  follows:
     5000 psi for upstream and down stream of the chokes.
     1500 psi down stream of choke manifold valve to burning valve.
    All valves should be full opening types.

 Choke manifold should be equipped with at least four lines.
     One line should be capable of bringing the well return directly to the shaker tank and
          should be equipped with two gate valves.
      Three other  lines should be equipped with adjustable chokes, two gate valves
          upstream and an erosion nipple at the down stream.
      At least one choke should be remotely operated.


 BOP testing policy
1.BOP stack will be pressure tested at its working pressure on test stump prior its installation
2.After installation on the wellhead the following tests will be carried out:

      Pipe rams and annular BOP shall be tested with open end cup testers or test plug to a
         low pressure of 300 psi and to a high pressure equal, at least, to the maximum
           anticipated well head pressure.
      Blind/shear rams shall be tested using plug tester to the same pressure as stated for
          pipe rams. During the normal drilling operations, blind/shear rams will be function
            tested only unless otherwise dictated by local laws and regulations.
      Should a plug tester not available, blind/shear rams will be tested against the casing
          each time a new casing string has been set, prior to drilling out the cement, in this
          case the testing pressure will not exceed 1500 psi( except for the 20” casing)
     Under no circumstances, the maximum test pressure shall exceed 70% of the rated
          working pressure of the lowermost pressure rated items  ( parts of equipment,
          wellhead, casing, etc.)

  Pressure control equipment must be appropriately and regularly tested according to laws and
  regulations, if any. Test procedures shall also be provided by the drilling contractor, approved
  and authorized by company. Testing of equipment should be carried out during non-productive
  time whenever possible and be properly planned to ensure that all necessary equipment is
  available, properly installed and in efficient operating condition. All pressure tests shall be
  performed by drill water. Down stream valves should be fully open while testing upstream
  valves.

 The company well site supervisor and drilling contractor tool pusher shall witness all pressure
  tests. Should any of the tests identify faulty equipment, drilling operations must be suspended
  and the well secured, if needed, until the faulty equipment is repaired or replaced.

3. Slow Pump Rate
 The Slow Pump Rate (SPR), or Slow Circulation Rate (SCR), is a reduced pump rate which is
  used for a well killing circulation. As a general rule, the slow pump rate (sometimes called the
  kill rate) will be of the order of one third to one half of the normal drilling circulation rate. A
 pump rate of approximately 1-3 bbl/min is usually suitable.



 The factors which govern the choice of the slow pump rate include:
 a. The rate at which kill weight mud can be mix.
 b. The danger of fracturing the formation at the casing shoe.
 c.  Pressure limits on the circulating equipment.
 d.  Pressure limits on the mud gas separator, choke manifold and choke.
 e.  The ability of the choke operator to cope with pressure control.

 Good drilling practice requires that the slow pump rate pressure be checked and recorded
  regularly by the Driller. The following points should be noted with regard to the recording of
  the slow pump rate pressure:

        1.  Check and record the slow pump rate pressure:
           a.At least once each shift or tour.
           b.After any change in mud density or viscosity.
             c. After each 300-500ft of hole made.
           d.After any change in nozzles or bottom hole assembly.
             e. After any change in pump liner size.
                 f. After casing is set.
              g. Just before drilling in to any known or suspected transition zone.

2.  Never record the slow pump rate pressure when circulating bottoms-up.
3.  Record two slow pump rates for each pump.
4.  Record the slow pump rate pressure from the gauge used to kill the well – the choke panel
   gauge, not gauge on the driller’s console or the standpipe manifold.
5.  With differently sized pump, record the slow pump rate pressure at the same pressure for
   each pump and not at the same stroke rate.


4. Flow Checks
Check for flow on all drilling breaks below a depth to be designated by the Company Man. The
Driller has the responsibility to consider any unexpected change in drilling rate to be a drilling
break. In addition, the Company Man will define a “drilling break” for each section of hole,
dependent on the geology of the section. If a flow check is positive, shut the well in and commence
well control procedures.

   5. Shut In Procedures
      All shut-ins will be “hard”. This means the ram or annular preventer will be shut in on spaced-
     out pipe against a closed choke while a drill pipe shut-off valve will be installed (if tripping)

     SHUT IN PROCEDURES WHEN OVERFLOW OCCURS


1. OVERFLOW OCCURS WHILE DRILLING:

    1) Choke manifold set-up, chokes closed, hydraulic valves open;
    2) Send out alarm signals stop Top Drive (or rotary table) and pumps. Close internal BOP in top
        drive;
    3)  Pick up a joint out of the rotary table while drilling with Top Drive;
    4) Close ram BOP;
    5) Report the case the rig manager and drilling supervisor and Company Man as soon as
       possible;
    6) Open hydraulic valve (if necessary to monitor casing pressure);
    7) Keep on observing, record standpipe pressure, casing pressure.


2. Overflow occurs while making a trip (DP)

    1) Send out alarm signal, stop POOH or RIH operation, lock the string connector immediately;
    2)  Connect string safety valve (quarter-turn);
    3)  Close ram BOP;
    4)  Report the case to the rig manager and drilling supervisor and COMPANY MAN as soon as
        possible;
    5) Open hydraulic valve (if necessary to monitor casing pressure);
    6) Keep on observing, record standpipe pressure, casing pressure.


3. Overflow occurs while making a trip (DC)

    1) Send on alarm signal. Connect safety valve (1/4 turn) and back pressure valve;
    2) Make up drill pipes and RIH as soon as possible;
    3)  Close ram BOP;
    4)  Report the case to the rig manager and drilling supervisor and COMPANY MAN as soon as
        possible;
    5) Open hydraulic valve (if necessary to monitor casing pressure);
     6)  Keep on observing, record standpipe pressure, casing pressure.

4. Overflow occurs in open hole

    1) Send out alarm signals;
    2)  Try to run the drill pipe with float valve if the situation permits;
    3)  Connect Kelly or top drive after running drill pipe, close pipe ram BOP;
    4)  Report the case to the rig manager and drilling supervisor and COMPANY MAN as soon as
        possible;
    5) Open hydraulic valve (if necessary to monitor casing pressure);
    6) Pay attention to observe the change speed and change value of standpipe pressure, casing
        pressure.


5. Overflow occurs while running casing

    1) Send out signals, let the casing seat in the elevators of rotary table or in the spider as soon as
        possible;
    2)  Try to run more casings if the situation permits, connect circulating head and a Kelly (no
        Kelly if run casing with Top Drive);
    3)  Close annular BOP;
    4)  Report the case to the rig manager and drilling supervisor and COMPANY MAN as soon as
        possible;
    5) Open hydraulic valve (if necessary to monitor casing pressure);
    6) Pay attention to observe the change speed and change value of standpipe pressure, casing
        pressure.


   6. Kick Circulation Procedure
    Except in unusual circumstances, a Constant Bottom Hole Pressure method of well control will
    be used for controlling kicks. The normal Constant Bottom Hole Pressure methods of well
    control are: the Wait and Weight, Drillers and Volumetric methods.  If a kick occurs in
    combination with severe losses, bull heading may be approved by the Drilling Manager.


   7. Closed In Drill Pipe Pressure

    When the well is closed in, the bottom hole pressure will rise until equal to formation pressure.
     As the drill pipes (and annulus) are in communication, the closed in drill pipe and the closed in
     casing (annulus) pressure will also rise and stabilize (in the absence of migration gas).

    The closed in drill pipe pressure at this time indicates the amount of under balance of the
      hydrostatic pressure in the drill string relative to the formation pressure. It is assumed that the
       drill string is filled with a column of clean drilling fluid of equal density from the rig floor to the
       bit, i.e., a known hydrostatic pressure value. In well killing operations, the drilling fluid density
       is increased by the equivalent value of the closed in drill pipe pressure.

  Until circulation begins, if there is gas in the well, surface pressure will continue to rise due to

8. Measuring Closed In Drill Pipe Pressure When There Is A Float In The
   String

 To determine the closed in drill pipe pressure when a back-pressure valve (float) is in the drill
  string, drill pipe pressure should be increased slowly in 50 or 100psi increments using the
  smallest pump available.

  After each stage of increasing the drill pipe pressure, the casing (annulus) pressure should be
  monitored for a change. If the casing pressure does not change (increase), the float has not
  opened, and the closed in drill pipe pressure is less than the under balance of the hydrostatic
  pressure in the drill pipe.

 When casing pressure is seen to rise, pumping should be stopped immediately. The current
  closed in drill pipe pressure, minus any increase seen on the casing (annulus) pressure gauge,
  is the amount of under balance of the hydrostatic pressure in the drill string relative to the
  formation pressure. This is the value to be used when calculation the kill weight mud.
