## SUBJECT

Step-by-step practical procedure for installing and putting into service
the B.O.P control system (accumulator unit, pumps and manifold) for use on
the rig.

## INSTALLATION STEPS

### 1. Positioning the Accumulator & Pump Unit

- Place the accumulator and pump unit at a safe distance from the well
  (100–150 ft from the rig in a safe area), so that personnel can operate
  the control valves in case of fire or a well kick.

### 2. Pipe Work (Rig-up of Connections)

- The pipes should preferably be run in a rack (like tubing), with proper
  size and wall thickness for the control-unit connections.
- Connections must extend from the unit to the BOP stack using strong
  1-inch (heavy) pipes.
- Use swivel joints where flexibility of the lines is required.
- All lines must be flushed (cleaned with water/air pressure) before use.

### 3. Open the Unit Valves

- Fully open the drain valve of the accumulator and pump unit.
- Fully open the flow isolation valves of the accumulator bottles.

### 4. Nitrogen Pre-charge of Accumulator Bottles

- Open the guards on the pre-charge (nitrogen) valves of the bottles.
- Install the charging assembly and nitrogen pressure gauge on the
  bottles, as per the figures.
- Screw the gauge assembly onto the bottles and tighten the lock nut on
  the bottle valve; loosen two to three turns so the gauge shows the
  pressure.
- The indicated pressure should be within 10% of 1000 psi (1000 psi ±
  10%). If the pre-charge pressure shown has changed due to ambient
  temperature variations, it is acceptable.
- Connect the pre-charge gauge assembly to the accumulator nitrogen
  bottles; open the bottle valve slowly (to avoid damaging the valve
  core) until the gauge reads 1000 psi, then close the nitrogen bottle
  valve.
- If the accumulator pressure is more than 1000 psi, relieve the excess
  pressure through the drain valve until the accumulator pressure
  decreases to 1000 psi; then close the bottle valve and tighten the
  lock nut on the accumulator models E, G and T; open the charging
  assembly handle upward.

**Note: Always use nitrogen gas for the pre-charge — never oxygen.**

### 5. Filling the Hydraulic Oil Reservoir

- Fill the oil reservoir tank through the 4-inch fill opening on the
  hydraulic control-fluid tank until the fluid level is 10 inches above
  the tank bottom (K-50 or K-90 as applicable).
- Do not use fuel oils in place of hydraulic oil.
- Close all air valves to the air pumps and the air bypass line; also
  close the electric pump motor starter.

### 6. Commissioning & Pressure Test

- Start the unit, verify pump operation (air and electric), check for
  leaks at all connections.
- Charge the system to the required operating pressure; verify
  accumulator bottle pressures are as per specification.
- Operate each BOP function (open/close) from the control panel and
  confirm correct response.
- Record all pressures and settings in the unit log before the BOP stack
  is pressure-tested.
