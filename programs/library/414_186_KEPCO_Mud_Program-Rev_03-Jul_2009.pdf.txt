Executive Summary

 Prospect 6.2 is located in the Southern part of the Caspian Sea, approximately 180 km North of
Nowshahr and about 209 km East of the Astara border. The high pressure well is to be drilled in 725
m of water to an eventual TD of +- 5300m TVD BRT.

The expected reservoir is located in the Cheleken Series, Sequence that is believed to represent
the Upper Pliocene.

The main objectives for the well are:

       •   In spite of TD at 5300m in this well, it is still far to achieve Base of Cheleken.
       •  To determine the presence and quality of SC440, and SC400 to Upper SC300, these
          horizons must be tested for the reservoir quality.





                              Fig1: South Caspian Sea.


Limited area knowledge concerning the possibility of shallow gas has therefore made it necessary to
prepare the top hole sections as  if shallow gas exists. Due to the shallow gas uncertainties all
technical action is to be taken ahead of setting the BOP.
The likely range for seawater surface temperatures is between 27o C and 6o C. Temperatures at mid
water depth range from 7.3o C to 6o C and temperatures at mudline range from 7o C to 5o C. The
Expected reservoir temperature is about 110O C. Gas-hydrates might develop within shallower
section. Therefore gas-hydrates might already exist in the formation or it can be self-induced due to

Initially an 8 ½” pilot hole will be drilled to 1622m TVD BRT to check the formations to the 20”
surface conductor shoe depth for any shallow gas, other potential shallow hazards and locate a
suitable shale section for the 20” surface conductor shoe.

On successfully drilling the 8 ½” pilot hole, 36” and 26” holes  will be drilled to 878m TVD BRT and
1622m TVD BRT respectively. The 8 ½” pilot hole and both the 36” and 26” sections will be drilled
with sea water and High Viscosity Bentonite sweeps. Potential issue are Hole Cleaning, Bit Balling,
Shallow water flows and Hole Instability.

After the 26” hole has been drilled and the 20” casing has been run, a 17 ½” hole to a depth of +-
3122m TVD BRT will be drilled. The planned recommended and programmed mud system is a
Synthetic Oil Based Mud, with a mud weight ranging from 1.15sg to 1.25sg. However an inhibitive
water based mud  will also be programmed as a backup. Potential Issues/Hazards are Hole
Cleaning, High Circulating Pressures and Equivalent Circulating Density, Differential Sticking, Hole
Instability, Lost Circulation and Potential losses while running and cementing the 13 3/8” casing.

The 12 ¼” hole will be drilled to a depth of 4422m TVD BRT with a Synthetic Oil Based Mud, that, if
used on the 17 ½” section will be carried over from that section. The mud weight can range from
1.5sg to 1.75sg.  Potential Issues/Hazards are are Hole Cleaning, High Circulating Pressures and
Equivalent Circulating Density, Differential Sticking, Hole Instability, Lost Circulation and Potential
losses while running and cementing the 9 5/8” casing.

An 8 ½” hole will be drilled to final TD of 5322m TVD BRT with the mud carried over from drilling the
12 ¼” hole. The weight of the fluid used to drill the 12 ¼” hole will be reduced to 1.55sg by a
combination of centrifuging and whole mud dilution. Coring will be carried out in this section and
Potential Issues/Hazards will be Hole Cleaning, Differential Sticking of the  drill string and core
barrel, High Circulating Pressures and Equivalent Circulating Density and Contamination of the
Core.

 POTENTIAL MUD RELATED PROBLEMS AND HAZARDS.

Hole cleaning: Pump hi-viscous mud more frequently and of greater volume.

Hole cleaning: Increase pump rate.

No drill water: Ensure gel is pre-hydrated at high concentrations. Use guar gum to alternate
the gel based viscous sweeps.

Hole instability: Displace to a weighted hi-viscous gel mud.

Bit Balling: Pump Drilling Detergent Pills.

Gas Hydrates: Using of MEG is recommended. It should be done based on client instruction.

Mud composition

A - Mud Composition:           PreHydrated Bentonite Viscous sweeps

Drill water                   :     Less than 200 mg/l Cl- for gel hydration.

Caustic soda              :       Alkalinity control.
Soda Ash                   :     Hardness control / Alkalinity control.
Bentonite                    :       Viscosifier.


Mixing Procedure:

  1.   Fill the tanks with the required volume of drill water.
  2.  Raise the pH to 9.5-10 with additions of caustic soda.
  3.  Treat out any hardness greater than 200 ppm with soda ash.
  4. Add 30-35 lb/bbl of Bentonite and allow hydrating for as long as possible (at least 6 hours).
     Processing with a shearing device will improve yield.

  2.   Fill the tanks with the required volume of sea water.

  5.  Mix 0.2 to 0.3 ppb Citric Acid to improve yield of Guar Gum.
  6.  Treat regularly with Biocide if pills to remain in pits for any length of time.
  7.  Pills should not be kept for more than 6 hours in the surface tanks. These will lose viscosity rapidly
     over time.

1. LOST CIRCULATION

INTRODUCTION

In order to cure or prevent lost circulation most effectively, proper planning must be done. If drilling through
certain formation is likely to give losses, treating the drilling fluid with LCM before entering the potential loss
zone may be recommended.

If total losses is expected when drilling into for instance a naturally fracture zone, or a pressure depleted zone,
a reinforcing pill should be mixed ready prior to entering zone and pumped immediately when losses occur.

However in many cases, losses are not expected and the situation must be analysed before treatment start.
The result of the quantification and analysis of losses will decide the type of curing method to be used. The
type of drilling fluid will influence on the choice of materials to be used in the pills to be pumped. The first LCM
pill to be pumped is often a pill containing different types of particles to bridge off the fracture. These materials
are mixed directly into the drilling fluid, before pumped down hole and will work immediately if successful.

For severe losses, the LCM pill will not always be able to seal off the fracture. In these cases the solution will
be a reinforcing plug such as Form-A-Plug, Gunk, or Cement. When drilling into known formations with natural
fractures, the solution can be to go directly to a reinforcing plug if experience has shown that LCM do not work
in sealing the fracture.

General:

Check with down hole tools supplier regarding maximum recommended concentrations and particle size of
LCM in pills that can be pumped through the down hole tools; otherwise circulation sub must be part of the
BHA design.

“Normal” loss situation, i.e. being able to maintain fluid column with drilling fluid available on the rig.

Severe loss situation (no choice), i.e. not able to maintain mud column over time. Also check recommendation
for maximum pump rate when pill passes through down hole tools.

Concentrations and particle size of LCM may be much higher than recommendations from tool supplier
depending on how serious the situation is.

Recommendations and limitations should be discussed with operator before start of the operation.

Always make sure that the whole volume of pill is pumped out of the string/bit.

Always make sure to pull above LCM pill after spotting it.


Information regarding reservoir conditions/properties, i.e. permeability or pore size, should be gathered from
the Operator. Discuss with Operator possible formation damage  if introducing LCM in a reservoir section
before usage.

CURING LOST CIRCULATION

Lost circulation can occur at any time or depth during drilling or tripping in hole. It is important to distinguish
between down hole losses and surface losses. When a drop in the drilling fluid volume is noted it is important
that the obvious is not ignored. All possible surface loss points must be investigated. The treatment of the
losses depends on the severity of the losses, the type of drilling fluid in use as well as the type of formation
where the loss occurs.

SEEPAGE LOSSES (< 20 bbl/hr)

Losses up to approximately 3 m3/hr (20 bbls/hr) are normally considered as seepage losses. Seepage type
losses are frequently cured by the simple action of reducing or stopping the pump rate and allowing the
formation to heal and become sealed off by the development of a filter cake. The pump rate can be gradually
increased after the losses have stopped.

If the loss does not heal by it self and the amount of loss cannot be tolerated from an economical point of
view, LCM pill for curing the loss should be pumped.

Recommended concentrations of LCM in pills to be used is at least 50 PPB. The following formulations are
recommended. If some products are lacking on the rig these should be replaced by other products to give the
same total concentration.

reduced drilling fluid weight and/or pump rate.
