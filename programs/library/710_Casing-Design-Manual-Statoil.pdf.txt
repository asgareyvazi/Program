1      Introduction


1.1     Objective

Objectives of this document are:
• To clearly identify responsibilities of the Design Engineer and the Design Engineer’s Supervisor,
    related to the process of designing, selecting, installing and retrieval of casing.
•  To specify the minimum set of requirements to be fulfilled in the design, selecting, and planning of
    casing installation process.


1.2    Warrant

Document is warranted in Statoil Area Requirement, “Drilling, Well & Production Activities” (AR03)
(Statoil group/All locations/On-and offshore) [2].

This document is published and followed-up by T&P-UGT-BBT-BRL Tubulars and Material Group.
Dispensations and non-conformances shall be handled according to “Non-conformities and
dispensations” (WR0011) (Statoil group/All locations/On-and offshore) [3].

2    Work process requirements


2.1     Planning of Casing Design Works

As casing design is a vital and integrated part of the overall HSE&Q activities with regards to
establishing and operating oil and gas wells, the design process is designed to cater for the
requirements relevant to these activities.
In order to secure proper relation between well and casing criticality, and design resources and
requirements, a Design Category scheme has been established, where Design Category 1 constitutes
requirements for the simplest types of wells, and Design Category 3 for the most complex. Design
Categories are defined by means of reservoir temperature and shut-in wellhead pressure.

Casing and down hole equipment shall be of such diameter, wall thickness, material quality and
strength, and shall be manufactured and installed in such a manner that it will withstand the pressures,

2.1.1    Responsibilities of the Design Engineer

To produce and document a casing design that ensures the string's pressure containing and structural
integrity in the drilling, testing and production, and abandonment phase in a way that:

•   Fulfill all HSE, technical and operational requirements both on local regulatory and company level,
    in accordance with requirements found in this document.
•  Provides the optimal economical design based on all applicable inputs such as well, field and
   company requirements.
•  Clearly documents the design basis used, and the load cases evaluated, deviations and operational
    limits.
•  Withstand all relevant well control scenarios.

Consider and include all other relevant design inputs that may or may not influence the casing and/or
liner design, and such the well integrity.


2.1.1.1  Design Engineer Competence

The Design Engineer(s) shall only accept tasks that she/he is fully competent with, or shall utilize
assistance from other competent personnel, or in an “on-the-job training”-situation under competent
surveillance.
Competence in this matter shall span all aspects of the design work included in this document.


2.1.2   Competence related Responsibility for the Design Engineer’s Supervisor

Design Engineer’s Supervisor is responsible for overseeing and securing Design Engineer’s
competence via one of the alternatives described in 2.1.1.1.

2.1.3   Computing Tools for Casing Design

The following computer software shall be used for casing design works:

•  Landmark's StressCheck™: For Design Category 1 and 2 casing design.
•  Landmark's Wellcat™: For temperature calculations in Design Category 2 and complete casing
    design in Design Category 3-wells, and for Annulus Fluid Expansion calculations (AFE).
•  Maurer Eng.'s CWear™: For casing wear simulations.
•  For calculating kick tolerances: Kick Calc. (See “Drilling & Completion Operation” (WR0436) [6]

Landmark's Wellcat™ may also be used for Design Category 1 and 2 wells, then offering reduced

Casing Design, table Minimum Safety Factors/Design Factors, note 3.)


2.1.4   Planning of Casing String Installation and Retrieval


2.1.4.1  Casing string preparations and installation:

All casing installations shall be planned and performed according to manufacturers and company
recommendations, covering all activities from pre-shipping preparations at supply base, pre-running
inspections at rig site, and application of thread lubricant and makeup.
Information can be found in the Statoil document “Statoil Standard Casing and Tubing Program for
Operations on the Norwegian Continental Shelf” (TD0024) [4], the DBR Rørdatabase [5], and in
various manufacturer issued publications available from T&P-UGT-BBT-BRL.
Where manufacturer and company recommendations are deviating or in conflict, company
recommendations shall prevail.

A torque/turn monitoring and recording computer shall be used to monitor, record and evaluate
makeup of all premium connections and non-premium when recommended by relevant connection
design owner unless other features for ensuring an acceptable makeup process is provided.
The system shall be of acceptable accuracy, with measuring inaccuracy of less than applied torque +/-
1%, and be in "calibration valid" status.
Trace ability between computer recordings and connections shall be planned for and ensured.

If retrieved casing are intended for re-use, the casing retrieving process shall be planned so that
retrieving process does not jeopardize the planned re-use.

3.3     Inputs to Design of Casing Strings

Design work shall be based on auditable information related to the wells or field in question.

It is fundamental for the quality of the simulation models that the engineer performing the design work
is updated and is fully understanding the drilling and testing process, wells objective(s) and service, its
completion strategy and details with regards to equipment and operations, its operational strategy and
expected lifetime.
The following factors shall be evaluated and used as basis for the casing design.

Application of the Well
•  Well type (platform, sub-sea, land).
•  Well objectives (exploration/appraisal, production, injection, annular cuttings injection/annular
    production).
•  Well evaluation program (logging, testing etc.).
•  Future use of well.
•  Life time prognosis.
•   Artificial lift (Gas, ESP etc).

Formation Properties
•  Geological prognosis.
•  Shallow gas.
•  Pore pressure.
•  Gas Gradient in different zones.
•  Temperature prognosis.
•  Hole integrity information, such as expected fracturing and collapse behaviour, chemical
    stability/sensitive shale.
•  Location of permeable and potential loss circulation zones.
•  H2S- and CO2- bearing intervals.
•  Location of squeezing salt and shale zones.
•  Reservoir invasion and damage.

Well Design
•   Directional plan including dogleg severity.
•  Mud system chemical/physical tolerances, base fluid density including any salt gradient.

3.4     Casing, Liner and Liner Packer Design Test Pressure Determination


3.4.1    Validation of Casing Integrity

Pressure testing of casing hanger, casing, liner and liner packer is the only acceptable method for
verification of a string’s ability to serve as a pressure restraining vessel.


3.4.2   Design Test Pressure Determination, Casings, Liners and Liner Packer


3.4.2.1  Testing of Casing and Liners

For casing design purposes, the test pressure measured at wellhead level, and fluid density shall
produce an internal casing differential pressure equal to, or higher, than maximum expected
differential pressure at all depths, covering all relevant casing operations, services and load cases
described in this document.


3.4.2.2  Testing of Liner Packers for Production Casing/Liner service

For testing of liner packer(s) located below production packer, test pressure and fluid density shall
produce a differential pressure no less than the higher of:
•  Formation fracturation strength at casing shoe, or at potential leak path, +70 bar, or
•  Actual differential pressure at liner packer(s), that may or will occur during production/injection
    tubing testing.


3.4.2.3  Testing of Liner Packers for Surface or Intermediate Casing/Liner service

For liner packer testing, test pressure and fluid density shall produce a differential pressure no less than
formation fracturation strength at casing shoe, or at potential leak path, +70 bar.

3.4.4    Test Pressure Determination Uncertainties

3.5     Casing Sizes and Setting Depths:

After having received and understood the information mentioned in paragraph 3.3 the design engineer
shall determine casing setting depths and number of casing/liner strings based on the information
available.


3.5.1   Approach to the Setting Depth Selection, all Design Categories

•  Check the information in 3.2 for setting depth criteria dictated by formation anomalies, formation
    evaluation, shallow gas, production /injection requirements etc.
•  Determine preliminary setting depths based on information mentioned above, mud density, pore
    pressure, fracture gradient and ECD/EMW requirements/guidelines.
•  Formation strength between packer and reservoir formation shall be of sufficient strength to
    withstand any pressures that may occur in the said area if casing integrity is lost.
•  When performing detailed casing design in StressCheck™ and WellCat™ it shall be checked that
    the required kick tolerance volumes can be handled. Alternatively, KickCalc shall be utilized to
    calculate minimum kick volumes for the design in question.
•  The “Limit to Frac at Shoe”-principle for casing burst load design shall be used.
•  Adjust setting depths if necessary, or proceed with deviation request to finalize setting depths.

Setting depth of production casing in Design Category 3 wells requires special attention. It is very
important to set the production casing before drilling into a prognosed high pressure reservoir.

Drilling wildcat exploration wells one has to take into account the geological uncertainty with respect
to pressure and formation tops. The casing shall be designed based on the maximum prognosed
pressure. Maximum setting depth of the production casing shall be minimum depth of the prognosed
high pressure-depth less twice the geological uncertainty.

The following guideline shall be used:
Max setting depth = Minimum depth of potential over pressurized sand/reservoir - (geological uncertainty * 2)

3.5.1.1  Additional requirements to setting depth selection, Multi lateral wells

A window made in a casing shall be considered a new casing shoe. Well control considerations shall
be based on depth and expected fracture gradient at window depth.
Distance from planned casing exit (window) to any hydrocarbon bearing formation shall be evaluated.

A multilateral well junction shall be placed at sufficient depth to withstand expected formation shut-in
pressure both in a well control situation and in production an injection scenario. In a cemented
junction, but not hydraulically sealed (referred to as “level 4 system”), the cement around the well
junction shall not be regarded as a barrier against internal pressure.
For production wells, the calculated shut-in pressure shall be based on maximum expected pressure
from reservoir to the junction.


3.5.1.2  Additional requirements to setting depth selection, Multi purpose wells

For multipurpose wells additional requirements to formation strength and production and intermediate
casings are:
•  The formation below intermediate shoe, and intermediate casing itself, shall withstand the highest
    expected pressure occurring if the production casing leaks during simultaneous operations.


3.5.2   Approach to the Casing Sizing Determination

•  Determine the size of the production liner/reservoir section.
•  Determine the size of remaining casings/liners based on geometry and hydraulics (ECD/EMW).
•  Relation between down hole equipment and its relevant casing(s).
•  When performing detailed casing design in StressCheck™ and WellCat™ also minimum drift
    diameter requirements vs next casing/tubing OD shall be taken into consideration.

Standard casings [1] and hole sizes shall primarily be used.


3.6    Kick Tolerance

Kick tolerance volume is an estimate of the volume of gas influx at bottom hole conditions that can be
safely shut in and circulated out of the well.

Requirements to minimum Kick Tolerances for the various hole sizes and sections to be drilled are
found in “Drilling & Completion Operation” (WR0436) [6].

•  In situ temperature.

3.8.2    Sensitivities to Well Trajectory (Design Category 2 and 3 wells only)

In engineering work, when one well design is qualifying (several) other(s) design(s), the effects of
difference in well trajectories shall be catered for:
•  Typically a more vertical well yields higher compression loads at Top-of-Cement level, than a
   more deviated well when exposed to same temperature increase.
•  In same manner, a deviated well design yield higher tension loads at casing top when well is
    cooled, than a more vertical design.


3.8.3    Sensitivity to As-Cemented (Initial Conditions) Temperature (Design Cat. 3 wells only)

Drilling operations, or nearby operations, may impose changes in the As-Cemented (or Initial
Conditions) temperature for a casing string.

•  Lower As-Cemented temperature will typically yield higher compression forces at bottom of
    casing string when well is heated, than a higher As-Cemented (Initial Conditions) temperature.
•  Higher As-Cemented (Initial Conditions) temperature will typically yield lower tension forces at
    top of casing string when well is heated, than a lower As-Cemented (Initial Condition)
    temperature.

3.10   Annulus Fluid Expansion


3.10.1  Introduction

Annulus Fluid Expansion (AFE) is the phenomenon that takes place when a constant volume of fluid is
heated without being given the possibility to expand volume-wise or to bleed off pressure.

Situation typically occurs where a casing is cemented into previous shoe, between cement plugs in a
stage cement scenario, whenever heavy components (typically barite) are falling out of a mud system
and form one or more pressure tight plugs, or anywhere else where a fluid-filled volume is being
constrained and subsequently heated.
Observe that upper pressure barrier is frequently the casing or tubing hanger system.

Based on temperature increase, fluid properties and strength of the pressure barriers, potential pressure
build-up can be in the 500 – 1000 Bar region.

Sources for temperature increase related to AFE may be:
•  Heat generated from production flow.
•  Heat generated from circulation from a deeper (and hotter) section of the well.

AFE also causes significant changes in axial loads for the tubular goods in involved section:
•  Outer boundary tubular goods: Increased tension from ballooning.
•  Inner boundary tubular goods: Increased compression from reverse-ballooning.

Pressure increase caused by AFE is determined by:
•  Average temperature increase of constrained volume,
•  Thermal expansion characteristics of constrained fluid,
•  Compression characteristics of constrained fluid,

3.10.2  AFE Philosophy and Design Requirements

Barrier-elements, including possible bleed-off and/or pressure reduction mechanisms shall be capable
of handling AFE-induced loads.


3.10.2.1 AFE Design Requirements for Design Category 1 and 2 Wells

Potential for critical AFE induced loads shall be evaluated. If potential is evaluated to be present, AFE
shall be checked for and included in design as described in this document.


3.10.2.2 AFE Design Requirements for Design Category 3 Wells

Design Category 3 wells shall always be designed to cater for AFE on all annuli.

Special consideration for AFE to be constrained by Production Casing and Intermediate Casing shall
be performed:
•  Actual collapse capacity of Production Casing shall be at least 20% higher than maximum potential
   AFE pressure. (Maximum potential AFE pressure shall in this context mean the maximum pressure
    potential, as a function of bleed-off or pressure reducing mechanisms if planned, or the calculated
   maximum AFE pressure if no bleed-off or pressure reducing mechanisms are planned for.)


3.10.2.3 Time Effects and Requirements to AFE Plug Depths for Design Purposes

Time span from drilling operations to production start-up, or other temperature increasing activity such
as circulation on bottom, is anticipated to be of major significance with regards to mud-particle
produced plugs, and shall be considered and catered for if found applicable.
•  Time-span is an important factor for evaluation of mud deterioration and heavy component fall-
    out.
•  For an AFE-boundary caused by mud heavy particle sagging, the lower plug shall located 50m
  TVD above previous shoe.
•   Potential for, and effects of plugs forming between 50m TVD above previous shoe and wellhead
    shall also be considered.

3.10.3.2 AFE Mitigation Principles

Handling of AFE-induced loads shall be catered for by bleed-off and/or pressure reduction
mechanisms, or by designing relevant barrier elements strong enough to withstand maximum
anticipated AFE-induced loads.

Maximum AFE pressure shall be limited by one or more mechanisms when barrier elements cannot
withstand maximum potential AFE pressure with the relevant minimum safety factor intact.

Pressure limiting mechanism shall be designed in such manner that no barrier element is exposed to
pressure or other forces exceeding maximum allowed for by the design acceptance criteria relevant for
the barrier element in question.

When taking localized Casing Wear into consideration at Collapse Load cases, quite frequently the

alternative collapse design method(s).

Drillpipe with Tungsten Carbide hard-facing is not accepted for drilling in casing or liner.
“New generation” (low casing wear, easily maintained) hard-facing offering low friction/wear factors
shall be used to reduce casing wear.


4.1.3   Casing Wear Log and Post-wear Casing Integrity

It is only pressure testing that shall be considered objective proof of casing integrity.

When abnormal casing wear is detected or expected, running actual “remaining casing wall thickness”
or casing wear log shall be considered to verify the degree of wear and consequently post-wear casing
integrity.

insignificant wear.

The following procedure shall be used to calculate design factors in StressCheck™, and to cater for
mechanical wear and temperature de-ration of the steel.

  #:   Activities, criteria, etc :
  1   Specify the Minimum DF's under "Tubular - Design Parameters".
  2   •  Calculate mechanical casing wear using the casing wear programme "CWear" for
          casing strings where:
            -inclination ≥ 60°.
           -build-up/drop-off rate ≥ 3°/30 meters.
           -the casing will be exposed to extensive drilling hours (i.e. sidetracks, intermediate
             liners, under-reaming etc.)
      •  For the Intermediate and Production Casing in Design Category 3-wells, casing wear
            shall be predicted by using CWear™.
        Wear allowance shall be included for in casing design.
  3   Allow for wall thickness reduction caused by corrosion based on expected conditions and
       design life time.
  4   •   Select “Single External Pressure Profile”, "Temperature deration", “Limit to Frac

  5   Check that "Max allowable wear" listed under "View - Tubular Results" is less than

         If not, evaluate measures for casing wear reduction and/or select a stronger casing.

App C   Additional Design Requirements for Design Category 3 Wells

Introduction:
Main differences between Design Category 3 wells and Design Category 2 wells are the significantly
higher consequences at well failure.
Hence, the design process for Design Category 3 wells shall be much more thorough, basically
investigating every known failure possibility.

Design of Design Category 3 wells casing strings deviates from designing for standard service wells
mainly in the following ways:
•  Thermal effects are much more thoroughly included in the design work.
•  Tubular and Connection loads and stresses below top of cement shall be included in design work.
•   Effects of AFE shall be evaluated and catered for.
•  Conductor casing design shall be included when next section is drilled with riser.
•   Multistring analysis to validate the wells behaviour as “one system” based upon all aspects
    influencing the well tubular stresses and strains.

App F   Material selection Requirements


F.1       Material Selection

Casing material selection, ref Best Practice for the Selection of Materials for Casing and Tubing
TD0139 [9], shall include consideration to the well environment covering the entire well life, including
the abandonment phase. In this respect H2S service or "sour service" shall be included into the design
and the requirements detailed in both Statoil document “Safe Operating Practice for Well Fluids
Containing H2S, TD0079” [8] and NACE MR0175/ISO15156 latest edition shall be followed.

Additional requirements are as follows:


F.1.1      Exploration / Appraisal Wells

The maximum material grade shall be limited to Q-125 type 1 with restricted yield, see table in
appendix F 1.3 note 5. If H2S is to be expected the production casing shall be limited to a H2S resistant
grade, see table in appendix F 1.3 note 5.

For higher strength grades than Q-125, then dispensation for use can be made provided a risk
assessment is performed prior to acceptance for use.

Production casing in Design Category 3 wells shall always be designed for "sour service".


F.1.2      Production / Injection Wells – Development Wells

The maximum material grade shall be limited to Q-125 type 1 with restricted yield, see table in section
F1.3.

For higher strength grades than Q-125, then dispensation for use can be made provided a risk
assessment is performed prior to acceptance for use.

If H2S is to be expected the production casing shall be limited to a H2S resistant grade, see table in
section F1.3.

Production casing in Design Category 3 wells shall always be designed for "sour service".

The liner material, which is exposed to flowing well fluid shall be of the same quality as the well
tubing material.
