deeper intervals (NKP and PK) below

reservoirs  could  contain  significant
hydrocarbon volumes at relatively low
exploration    risk    with    possible
incremental  value  to  the   full  field
development. Once below the Fasila D the pressures are extremely uncertain and tremendous attention will
be needed drilling to the base of the PK formation.

3 SDX-4 Well Objectives & Key Risks

3.1 SDX-4 Well Objectives

3.1.1  Strategic Well Objectives


•    No accidents, No harm to people, No damage to the environment.

•    To provide sufficient reservoir and developability information to support a Stage 2 development
     scheme of +8Bcma

•    To reduce significantly the remaining uncertainty in the scale of Shah Deniz and to improve the
       definition of the scope of further development


3.1.2  Technical Well Objectives


HSE
•   Deliver BP safety performance metrics for wells related activity. (ASA, FOCUS , Safety/ Rep Audits, etc)
•   Full compliance with BP discharge requirements.
•   Full compliance with PSA HSE requirements.

Primary - Appraisal Objectives {Bal VIII, Bal X (SP2), Fasila B (SP3), Fasila D (SP4)}
•   Confirm the GIIP estimates for the West Flank
•  Reduce seismic depth uncertainty
•  Reduce reservoir quality uncertainty
•   Assess pressure communication to the East Flank
•   Evaluate productivity, connectivity and fluids from tests

Secondary - Exploration Objectives (NKP & PK intervals)
•   Acquire sufficient dataset to allow NKP and PK to be included in the Stage 2 development
•   Representative rock and fluid samples
•   Representative well productivity
•   Ensure that the well can be accurately tied to seismic

Developability
•   Quantify southwest Shah Deniz development drillability, time & cost, pore-pressure, wellbore
    stability and fracture pressure

Performance
Drill and evaluate the well in better than 97 (Pmean) days per 10,000ft.
•   Carry out well planning and execution consistent with BP BtB common process

TD for this well will be deeper stratigraphically than any of the Shah Deniz wells drilled to date. Therefore
there is considerable uncertainty around pore pressures and extreme care should be taken when drilling this
unexplored section.

The stratigraphy to Top NKG is expected to be similar to the SDA-01. However, SDX-4 is located further
South (down transport direction) than any of the other Shah Deniz wells and is on the western side of the
structure so  it is possible that the sand content of some of the horizons (especially Balakhany VIII and
Balakhany X (SP2)) could be lower than seen in the other wells. The Fasila B (SP3) and Fasila D (SP4)
should be similar and correlatable with the East Flank wells. The shale intervals should also be easily
correlatable across the structure.

TD decision for this well will be via a staged approach, which will come into play once the well is at, or below,
Base Fasila D (SP4). Assuming everything goes according to plan the TD criteria will be to stop drilling 40m
(a logging sump) below the base of the PK.

7.3 Burst Disk Sub Placement


22” Casing: 1 Burst Disc sub placed below 18 ¾” HP wellhead crossover joint – approximately 20m below
mudline. Burst Disc outward Pressure Rated to 5000psi +/- 5% @ 150oF, 4871psi to 5384psi @ 72oF

18” Liner: No Burst Disc Sub required. Plan to bring TOC to top of liner. As the 13 3/8” will be set above
the weak point in section (Sabunchi Horn) and the 13 3/8” x 13 5/8” will be run inside 18” as opposed to 16”
on predrill wells, there is a realistic chance of bring the TOC as a minimum above the 18” liner shoe.

Even if the TOC is not above the 18” shoe there is no requirement for an 18” burst disc based on the fact that
during the appraisal DST (in case annular pressure build up occurs between 18”/ 13 5/8” annular) the 13 5/8”
casing has a minimum of 2.0SF over its collapse rating and the 18” liner has a minimum of 1.03SF over its
Burst rating.

16” Contingency Liner: 1 Burst Disc sub placed below first full length casing joint below 16” casing hanger.
Burst Disc Pressure Outward Rated to 4500psi @100oF and Inward Collapse Rating to 1300psi to 1500psi
@ 150oF & 1333psi to 1538psi @ 72oF. Plan to bring top of cement to at least the top of the 18” Liner.

9 Primary and Contingency Operations

Best efforts have been made to ensure that this  drilling program complies with  all applicable BP and
Business Unit (BU) policies, as well as, all governmental legislations/ policies.

Priority for safety while drilling SDX-4 shall be in the following order of importance:

    1.  Personnel
    2.  Environment
    3.  The Installation
    4.  Reservoir Integrity
    5.  Well Delivery

The operational procedures in this document should be read in conjunction with the following documents.
The order of hierarchy is as listed below.

    1. BP Drilling and Wells Operations Policy (Issue 5; May 2003)
    2.  SDX-4Section Specific Management of Change documents (MOC)
    3.  SDX-4 Drilling Program
    4.  Shah Deniz Development Drilling Operations Manual version 3, 30th July 2005

Note:

This program has principally been written to cover drilling SDX-4 down through and running of the 7 5/8” Test
Liner across the Primary Appraisal Reservoirs. There will be a further 3 programs issued through the course
of the SDX-4, these being:-

     •    Drilling of the secondary Exploration Interval
     •   Exploration & Appraisal DST Program
     •   Suspension Program

     I.  In the scenario where the 22” does not get down to the ‘Base Plan’ depth and the 18” Liner is

      18” Liner is subsequently set early to achieve the required LOT to drill the section to the to the ‘Base

  III.  In the scenario where there is an early Surakhany pressure surprise following drilling out of the 18”

     resume.

      Deep Surakhany Surprise following problems drilling the shallow section of the well or while drilling
        the Surakhany. In which case the 11 ¾” Liner would be used and set at the 13 3/8” ‘Base Case’
         setting depth.

        predicted “Reasonable Worst Case”, i.e. the pore pressure/ fracture gradient plot. In which case the
       11 ¾” Liner would be used and set when at the point were drilling further is not possible due to

10.4 Conventional Coring


1)  Appraisal (Primary) Target: Fasila B (SP3) (8 ½” hole anticipated).

Coring Objectives

The objective is to take spot cores (heads up coring) in the Fasila B (SP3). It is anticipated that a single core
with a minimum length of 30m will be required (although it is likely a 54m core barrel will be run). As this well
will target a new segment of the field, the core will give important information about porosity and permeability
depth models as well as information on the depositional model, net:gross, sand production and Sw. The core
will also be used to assist in any testing decision and also to help analyse any test information obtained.

Criteria for Coring

It is anticipated that the stratigraphy within this new segment of the field will be broadly similar to that seen in
the cored offset wells in the NE of the field (SDX-2, SDX-3, SDA-01 and SDA-02)
Core point will be decided from correlation with other wells and will incorporate any other drilling information
available. It is anticipated that coring will commence 30m below the top of the reservoir, once sands with
shows have been encountered and correlation with offsets confirm penetration of SP3. The boundary with
overlying shales will NOT need to be sampled in this well.
It is anticipated that the minimum requirement of 30m will sample most if not all of the facies normally found
in the Shah Deniz field. All reservoir formations are expected to be gas bearing at this location. If surprises
are encountered, the requirement for coring will be reconsidered.

Assuming a 54m barrel is run, every effort should be made to fill it – coring should not be terminated once
the 30m minimum requirement has been achieved,  if coring is progressing smoothly. However  if there are
concerns about the hole angle (coring is known to lead to increased hole angle) and our ability to log on
wireline, this requirement will be reviewed.

Further Cores

Optimisation of core barrel length will be required to acquire the core in the least number of runs possible. A
maximum of 2 (two) core runs will be attempted, although it is anticipated that a single run (54m barrel) will
be sufficient (also see note above).

2)  Exploration (Secondary) Targets: Pre-Fasila, NKP and/or PK (6 ½” primary).

Coring Objectives

The objective is to take spot cores (heads up coring) in both the NKP and PK. It is anticipated that a single
core with a minimum length of c.27m will be required in each interval. These are exploration targets and the
core will give important information about log calibration, porosity and permeability depth models as well as
information on the depositional model and net:gross. This information will be vital for building the reservoir
model and incorporating it into the full field. The core will also be used to assist in any testing decision and
also to help analyse any test information obtained.

Criteria for Coring

Core point will be decided from correlation with offset wells (Bahar) and will incorporate any other drilling
information available.  It is anticipated that coring will commence as close as possible to the top of each
reservoir, once sands with shows have been encountered. The boundary with overlying shales will NOT
need to be sampled.
It is anticipated that the minimum requirement of 27m will sample most  if not all of the expected facies. All
reservoir formations are expected to be gas bearing at this location.  If surprises are encountered, the
requirement for coring will be reconsidered.
It is unlikely that the core barrel length run will exceed 27m in this slim hole. However it will still be possible
(though less likely) to achieve the minimum coring requirement in one core run.

30% of the sandstone element of the core will be preserved for special core analysis (SCAL) using the

    •   Rigorous, core handling procedures and controlled trip rates.

    •   Wellsite core plugging
    •  Core using disposable inner barrels (fibretube)
    •   Resin Injection
    •   Possible hotshot core analysis, (poroperm) to aid in testing decision.
    •  LWD Gamma Resistivity as close to the bit as possible (RAB possible?)

Transport of Core

By boat - careful handling of the core at all stages is essential to prevent core damage.

13 Wellhead / BOP System & Casing Test Pressure

All pressure testing operations should be carried out to comply with Section 20 of the “BP Drilling and Well
Operations Policy” (Issue 5 May 2003)

Testing Philosophy:

  ❖ Maximum 13,500psi surface pressure on the 15,000psi BOP stack defines the Maximum Exploration
       Test DST Surface Shut In Pressure
  ❖  MDT’s in the Exploration section will therefore dictate if the Exploration DST will be carried out.
  ❖  Over all impact of the above is that although the well and its components (10” Casing Hanger, 10”
       Csg, 7 5/8” Hanger/ Packer, 7 5/8” Liner & 4 ½” Liner System) is designed to withstand 15,000psi,
        the Final Well Integrity Test will be limited to 13,500psi over SW.
  ❖  As the well is constructed, the casing will only be tested to what is required to continue drilling
       ahead.
  ❖  The final constructed well will be pressure tested to 13,500psi over seawater. This will be the Final
        Well Integrity Test prior to commencing Drill Stem Testing operations.

13.4 Casing Design - Casing Wear Strategy

A strategy to mitigate against and evaluate excessive casing wear was put in place on SD A-01. A baseline
Multi-Finger Caliper Tool (MFCT) was run inside the 10-3/4"x10" casing and a post drilled MFCT run after +/-
500 krevs. The two logs indicated that virtually no wear had occurred during this time. Part of the procedure
was to use CWEAR software to back out wear factors from the casing wear signature. This proved to be
meaningless due to negligible wear occurring.  During drilling and coring of the 8½" section metal returns
from the ditch magnets versus cumulative revs was recorded. This provided a qualitative measure of casing
wear against time and provides a baseline to compare all future wells against.

A final MFCT was also run after the 5-1/2” liner was set on SDA-01 to further evaluate actual wear during
slow drilling 8-1/2” section to TD. Again negligible casing wear was observed.

The above results from SDA-01 and comparison of the SDA-01, 2, 3, 4 trajectories indicate that the 8½"
section on SDX-4 can be drilled without casing wear protection. However, should metal returns seem
excessively higher than expected; casing wear protection will be needed as a contingency.

Recommendations for SDX-4

    •  Run a 60 arm calliper/ USIT in the production casing to obtain a base line production string ID data
         prior to drilling out the 10” shoe track.
    •   Ensure awareness raised on rig with respect to criticality of casing wear and influencing operational
        factors (dogleg severity & RPM).
    •   Ensure ditch magnets are used while  drilling the 8 ½” hole section. Ditch magnets should be
       monitored on a 12 hourly basis and results recorded for rig site trend analysis.  Results should be
         directly compared with those from SDA-01 to look for strong deviations from this trend.
    •     It is recommended that Weatherford's Slick Sleeve tool be used as a contingency should casing
       wear protection be needed.
    •  Run a 60 arm caliper/ USIT in the production casing along with the final cased hole logs once the 4
      ½” Liner has been cemented. Evaluate the final cased hole log against the base line log to get
       assurance around the 10” wall thickness prior to the Drill Stem Tests.

22 Challenges, Risks and Contingencies

22.1 Marine/ Logistics’ Prioritisation

The Istiglal will be operating during the peak demand period for marine vessels and helicopter utilization.
This peak demand period coincides with the move / installation of the TPG500 and the East Azeri Jacket.
Due to the criticality of these time sensitive activities, the Istiglal may incur delays in supply of materials or
dedicated vessels for drill cuttings transport. The utilization of the marine fleet will be actively managed to
minimize any impact to the operation, however no costs have been included in the well cost to cover this risk.

22.2 Shallow Gas

A detailed site survey and hazard assessment has been performed for the proposed SDX-4 well location. 2D
High Resolution seismic data in conjunction with 3D seismic data has been reviewed to assess the likelihood
of the presence of a number of different top hole drilling hazards.

“No amplitude anomalies are observed on the HR or 3D seismic data within the post-Apsheron sequence at,
or close to, location that could be attributed to gas accumulations. Hence, with the exception of the ‘running
sands’ interval, the overall risk of encountering gas in the interval from seabed at a depth of 389.9m (+/-2m)
TVDSS, (531ms TWT), to the Top Apsheron at a depth of 1340m (+/-30m) TVDSS, (1615ms TWT), is
considered to be Negligible.”

22.3 Channels/ Shallow Water Flows/ Running Sands

“The “running sands”, encountered in wells SDX-1, SDX-2, SDX-3 and the E” platform wells can be traced to
the SDX-4 location and might be expected in the interval 865m (+/-16m) – 910m (+/-18m) TVDSS. These
appear as moderate to high amplitude reflectors on the 3D seismic data although of relatively normal
amplitude on the HR data.”

“At the SDX-4 location, the running sands are down-dip from the SDX-3 and SD-6 locations which are 1.1km
apart, and appear somewhat less bright on the seismic data. However given the findings in SD-6 and SDX-3
wells the risks of encountering over-pressured water or gas in minor amounts in the interval 865m (+/-16m) -
910m (+/-18m) TVDSS , must be considered to be Low-Moderate although overall risk to well operations is
considered to be Negligible.”

Specifically for SDX-4 the base plans is to top set the 28” liner above the “running sands” and use a
weighted mud system to drill the “running sands”, setting the 22” surface casing string at approximately
1200m TVD. Both casing strings will be cemented using “fast setting short tail slurry” and “extended lead
slurry” to maintain hydrostatic pressure while the tail is setting up.

22.4 Shallow Faults

No major faults will be intersected by the SDX-4 well trajectory in the shallow section to 2000m TVDSS.

22.5 Mud Volcanoes

The closest mud volcano to the proposed SDX-4 location is the palaeo mud volcano cone, the rim of which
lies approximately 1.56 km to the north-east at closest approach. The closest active mud volcano to the
SDX-4 location lies to the south-west and appears as highly charged, low viscosity salse-like mud pool on
the seabed. The closest approach to the well location of the edge of this pool is 3.75 km.

There are a five thin mud volcano flows that the SDX-4 well trajectory intersects. Numerous thin flows have
been drilled previously and the risk to well operations is considered negligible.

The well trajectory will intersect the outer edge of a palaeo mud volcano cone build-up, in the depth interval
of 1158m TVDSS to 1242m TVDSS. The cone deposits are not bright and are not expected to be gas
charged. The major risk within this section is that it may contain large fragments of harder material that might
cause Drill string deflection, jamming or hang ups when running casing.

22.6 Depth Uncertainties – Geological Sidetrack

Current prognosis has most likely depth uncertainties at top reservoir of +/-200m. There is a potential to have
larger error bars (+/- 500m) due to depth of well, distance from closest well control and the fact that SDX-4 is
positioned on an undrilled side of the Shah Deniz structure. If the larger depth uncertainties due materialise
this will impact delivery of well objectives as the well may end up being TD’ed in the water leg.

There is an opportunity to know the discrepancy early in the 16 ½” hole section, which could mean a
geological sidetrack to the well to mitigate drilling into the water leg and would eliminate any unplanned
corrective directional work beneath the 16” casing, which in itself would introduce additional risk to the well
through increased tortuosity and dog leg severity.

22.7 Pore Pressure/ Fracture Gradient

Shah Deniz is characterized by a complex pore and fracture pressure profile. The depth of the structure plus
the elevation from the crest to flank and the water depth complicate prediction of pore and fracture
pressures.

SDX-4 will be drilled in the Southwest area of the field, which might form a new segment. Whilst the general
stratigraphy and pressure profile is expected to be similar down to the base of Fasilia D (SP4), surprises
cannot be ruled out. Once below the Fasilia D (SP4), the well will drill ahead into the Pre-Fasila as an
exploration well. Pressures are extremely uncertain in this interval and extreme care should be taken whilst
drilling this section. The maximum pressure predicted for the NKG in SDX-4 was projected from the mud
weight used in the deepest part of NKG penetrated in SDA-01.

22.8 Wellbore Stability

Shah Deniz is in a tectonically active area and this is known to cause wellbore stability issues, with breakout
in shale’s and under gauge hole in sandstones. This has led to tight hole problems ant stuck pipe.

The effect of wellbore stability has been modelled using data extracted along the SDX-4 wellbore from the
PPFG cube and a 3-dimensional rock property cube built using the offset well logs. Mud weights have been
predicted in the “BP Well Planning Toolkit” for the zero breakout and the case of limited breakout.

22.9 Conductor Slumping/ Cratering

Analyses of the Exploration & Pre-drill wells suggest that conductor slumping has not been a problem to-
date. On SDX-2 the conductor was run to 230m below mud-line and cemented with conventional Lead/Tail
slurry followed by top-up job and NO conductor slumping problems were encountered. On all 4 Pre-drill wells
the conductor was run +/- 50m below mud-line and cemented in place with conventional Tail slurry followed
with a top-up cement job and again NO conductor slumping problems were evident.

On SDX-4 the 36” Conductor will be cemented with conventional Lead/Tail slurry and will be followed by top-
up cement job. The taail slurry is designed to be brought up a min of 50m above the shoe. Based on the
feedback from Daryl Kellingray it should be noted that the tail slurry alone can support up to 5 million lbs of
weight and the estimated maximum conductor weight with 28” and 22” strings supported is +/- 1mlbs.

The 16m surface sand that is present at the E2 platform location does not exist at SDX-4 location. Therefore
cratering tendency around conductor  is considered to be low at SDX-4 location. To mitigate against
conductor slumping in case of crater develops around the conductor it is planned to fill up the crater around
the conductor with cement to the mud-line.

22.10 Mud Weight Selection for Wellbore Stability/ Overbalance/ Kick
     tolerance/ ECD

All the drilling risks associated with the previous Shah Deniz wells are applicable to SDX-4. These risks are
primarily related to the pressure regime, with the likely presence of regressed and overpressured sandstones
interbedded with overpressured mudstones. These conditions may provide a limited PP-FG window, with the
potential for wellbore ballooning and the possibility of some wellbore breakout. Careful attention to mud
conditions and drilling practices will be essential for management of the mud ECD.

22.11 Mud Losses

Analysis of the offset data suggests that massive mud losses while drilling or logging has not been a problem
to date, but seepage losses have been observed.

Losses while running and cementing casing has been a major issue on the predrill wells and is a strong
possibility on this well given the tight tolerance casing configuration and the resultant clearances. Massive
volumes of mud have been lost specifically on the 13.3/8” x 13.5/8” (3956 bbls) and the 10” x 10.3/4” (1687
bbls) strings.

The losses described above occur due to the Pore/MW/Frac regime experienced on Shah Deniz. Strict
management of ECD, Surge & Swab during drilling and cementing will be essential to minimize losses.

22.12 Rig Reliability

Historically over the 6 wells drilled to date on Shah Deniz the Istiglal has had problems with major pieces of
equipment (Top Drive, BOP’s, Engine’s, Draw works) due to the harsh drilling environment. Although the
Istiglal will have been put through its paces and the cob webs flushed out on SDA-04, this still can not be
discounted as a potential risk for significant downtime.

22.13 Data Acquisition

Historically on Shah Deniz, TD logs have mainly been run on TLC. To date TLC logs have never become
stuck on Shah Deniz. Results when using wireline however have been mixed and fishing jobs have resulted
on many occasions.

However the temptation to continue with TLC logging must be weighed against the cost. Typically a TLC
logging job will take 2 - 3 times as long to perform than conventional wireline. There is therefore a large prize
in terms of rig time and hence cost to have the ability to run wireline logs in these holes.

22.14 Stuck Pipe leading to Sidetrack

Exploration drilling experience has shown that stuck pipe is a major drilling risk especially in the reservoir
section. Two-stuck pipe instances occurred during the exploration programme  - while drilling SDX-1 and
SDX-3 reservoir. The cause of the stuck pipe is believed to be squeezing sand sections. The squeezing
sands in the Lower Surakhany was one of the contributing factor to the stuck pipe event on SDA-03.

Based on learning from the SDX program, SDA program and subsequent WBS studies  it has been
concluded that the best ways to avoid stuck pipe due to these squeezing sands is:

    •   Optimised BHA design (via use of RSS systems, active gauge bits, PDC stabs and rollers reamers)
    •   Control BHA designs through MOC process
    •  Reaming before off-bottom connections
    •   Raise rig site stuck pipe awareness

22.15 Directional Drilling and Rotary Steerable Systems

This will be the  fifth  (first exploration) directional well drilled on the Shah Deniz structure. The original
exploration wells experienced significant directional tendencies in the deeper formations. Directional drilling
could also lead to additional problems with wellbore instability, wellbore breakout and possible additional
trips for directional control and tool failures

The present well design has been optimised to achieve bottom hole location with the well vertical down to
2500m. The well will build angle reaching and holding tangent of approximately 20 degrees before dropping
back to vertical at 5507m TVD prior to drilling through the reservoir targets. This restricts the majority of the
directional drilling to two hole sections.

For the 1st four hole sections a pendulum BHA will be used to drill vertically to kick off point. The offset data
indicates that only 1 out of the 4 wells had any deviation problems in these hole sections.

22.17 Trajectory Design

deviated wells combined with the extensive studies into wellbore stability has resulted in greater confidence
that higher deviations can be planned. SDA–01 is the most recent well drilled to date on Shah Deniz that
bears a resemblance to the proposed trajectory for SDX-4.

Directional drilling on SDX-4 is a necessity to navigate through the expected pore pressure/ fracture gradient

22.18 Drilling Performance/ Drillstring Design

At the start of the 12 ¼” section higher than offset ROP’s were seen on SDA 01 in the 12 ¼” section.
However later in the section, low ROP’s were experienced in the 12 ¼’’ section. This reduction on ROP was
mainly due to drilling string configuration – torque/tension limitations due to not having 6 5/8” drill pipe at the
top of the string. The string was run this way in order to comply with CDC BOP ram configuration policy. For
well SDA 02 the policy was reviewed which resulted in a drill string configuration with 6 5/8’’ DP at top being
acceptable.

22.19 Reservoir Drilling Performance

Analyses of exploration wells indicates that drilling 8 ½’’ reservoir section has taken 1/3 of well time with
ROP’s less than 1m/hr in places. The slow ROP’s appear to be due to squeezing sands resulting in stabiliser
drag.

On SDA 01, higher than offset ROP’s were experienced in the hard formations in the 8 1/2’ section.
However, latterly in the section high shock loads were observed and some LWD failures experienced. Drilling
parameters (WOB/torque) were reduced to manage this problem. The BHA configuration (number and
position of PDC stabs & roller reamers) was also modified to reduce stick/slip/shock characteristics.

22.20 Well Control

Due to over-pressured water bearing sands being common across the Shah Deniz field and  the problems
experienced on exploration wells drilled in the South Caspian, well control awareness will be elevated
throughout the entire drilling operation. Additional emphasis will be placed on the more critical sections (base
of 16 ½” hole through 12 ¼” and 6” sections) where rapid pore pressure increase/regressions and tight
pore/fracture window  will dictate an extremely robust MW Selection and thereafter ECD management
strategy to avoid a Well Control Incident.

TD for this well will be deeper stratigraphically than any of the Shah Deniz wells drilled to date. Therefore
there is considerable uncertainty around pore pressures and extreme care will have to be taken when drilling
this unexplored section.

It is essential that good wellbore finger printing practices are rigorously practiced offshore so that wellbore
ballooning can be interpreted as just that and not a well control incident.

22.21 Casing Wear on 10” Casing

The Well and Casing design has been driven by the requirement for a Pre-Fasila DST (capacity to handle a
15kpsi surface pressure DST). However, in order to deliver the well design conventional conservatisms in
casing design assumptions and limitations required to be challenged by the team and endorsed by EPTG.

One of these conservatisms is the API wall thickness value of 87.5%. The 10” pipe to date provided by
Summitomo has been measured at 94% wall thickness with 99.5% confidence. This is the wall thickness that
has been used to achieve the required objective’s of the 10” casing.
The casing wear on the 10” is going to be critical with regards to compromising the ability to test. Should
there be any considerable casing wear, the integrity of the 10” to withstand the 15k psi tubing leak at surface
design scenario, would make proceeding with testing the Exploration Leg near impossible as well as raising
concerns and lowering confidence around well integrity for the Appraisal Leg test.

The best offset well in terms of well trajectory profile & casing wear is SDA-01. A comprehensive casing wear
evaluation programme was carried out on SDA-01. A similar evaluation program must be carried out for
SDX-4.

22.22 Under Reaming & Hole Opening Considerations

The complex pore pressure and fracture gradient profile of Shah Deniz necessitates the running of numerous
tight tolerance casing and liners. As a result the clearance of the casing/liner is frequently smaller than it is in
the subsequent open hole section.

22.23 Losses While Underreaming 8 ½” Hole

In most, if not all, of the Shah Deniz wells the Balakhany VIII has been drilled with a mud weight greater than
the sand fracture gradient. In most cases this has been done without experiencing losses. It is felt that losses
have not been experienced because native materials  in the mud form stress cages  (barite, calcium
carbonate, graphite, and/or sand). The problem with stress cages is that they have never been successfully
sequentially under reamed without inducing losses. There has never been a problem simultaneously under
reaming (Shah Deniz Offsets).

The logic behind this is, when the pilot hole is drilled stress cage is formed and the fracture slowly collapses
behind the bridge, deforming the formation;  it either forms a much wider deformed mouth or else we have
disturbed the formation behind the bridge to the point that  it does not have the integrity to build a larger
stress cage.

22.24 Sidetracking 6 ½” Hole Section

Should for some unforeseen reason the BHA or coring assembly become differential stuck in open hole and
all fishing attempts to recover fail. There is a considerable likelihood that all the Exploration Leg objectives
will be inaccessible.

To be able to get enough well displacement from the “fish” to prevent re-drilling old hole, while maintaining
large hole sizes, would require milling a window though the heavy wall 10” production string and re-drilling
the lower section of the well. This operation in itself would compromise the integrity of the 10” and eliminate
any chances of performing the Exploration Leg DST as well as putting at risk the Appraisal Leg DST.

22.27 Liner Integrity

compromised either by poor zonal isolation or during the 15k DST.

22.28 High Collapse/ Burst Pressures

High pore pressures, in both the overburden and reservoir, result in many casing strengths being driven by
collapse rather than burst pressures. Within the overburden, pressures are highest within the Sabunchi, up to
12,453psi. Within the reservoir, pressures are highest within the water bearing sand SP1, up to 14,430psi.

High downhole pressures also create high burst pressures whilst drilling. In the event of well control or tubing
leak and gas to surface during the DST Well Head Shut In Pressures of 15,000psi may be seen.

22.29 Wellhead, Liner Hanger Assemblies and Slim Clearances

SDX-4 well design utilizes the MS700 18.3/4” Full-Bore Wellhead System with 36” Conductor. This system
will allow running an additional 28” Drilling Liner prior to the 22” HP wellhead being installed and an 18” liner
inside the wellhead under BOP control.

The number of casing strings and contingency liners result in extremely tight clearances and for this reason,
flush and semi flush integral connections are planned for most of the strings. The downside of this is that if a
contingent liner is run,  it may assist in the current section but leads to more risk in the subsequent hole
sections due to decreased annular drilling, casing & cementing clearances, which in turn can compromise
well integrity and zonal isolation.

HPHT well test equipment and operations are more prone to failure since they are typically operating closer

software tools being less accurate at HPHT conditions, as well as organizational and human failings to fully

amount of potential energy stored in a high pressure gas rich well.

Maintaining tubing integrity is an important requirement for well control. The tubing must be designed to
handle worst-case production loads. A mechanical failure of the tubing string or packer may introduce a leak
from the tubing to annulus. Any loss of integrity imposes serious safety risk

The well will require to be pressure tested to demonstrate well integrity prior to running the firing heads and
perforating the casing.

Clear well kill, contingency and emergency procedures need to be developed for all possible scenarios.

off/ test plug/ valve and being applied to a weaker element must be considered. All reasonable steps must
be taken to monitor for and eliminate such an event. Safety risk is increased when dealing with a packer
failure and these should be covered in the contingency planning procedures mentioned above.

23 Overview of Exploration / Appraisal Wells

23.1 SDX-1, 1Z, 1Y

SDX-1 was the first well drilled to the reservoirs on Shah Deniz. The well was in a mid flank position on the
east flank of the structure and was a gas discovery. Numerous problems were encountered whilst drilling, as
follows:
    •   Tight hole in the Surakhany formation, probably due to tectonics, not recognized as such at the time.
    •   Higher than anticipated pressures in the Surakhany and Sabunchi formations led to several kicks.
    •  A very  large pressure  regression  in the Balakhany formations  leading  to  drilling  with high
        overbalances.
    •   Thin high pressured sand, SP1, sandwiched between two low to moderately pressured reservoirs.
    •  Two sidetracks (SDX-1z, SDX-1y) due to mechanical sticking. The  first was probably due to
         differential sticking or tectonics whilst the second was due to poor directional control leading to stuck
        logging tools and junk in the hole.
    •  Two changes of rig, one planned, to utilize newly refurbished high pressure rated rig and the second
       due to generator problems on the newly refurbished rig.

23.2 SDX-2, 2Z, 2Y

The second well on the structure, SDX-2, was also drilled in the east flank, but in an up dip location. This too
was a gas discovery. The well location proved to be an extremely difficult area at which to drill. This was
mainly due to a tight pore / fracture pressure window and a lack of planning time. Key issues were as follows:
    •   Only one casing string set on depth as planned.
    •   Losses and gains led to the premature setting of the 16”, 133/8” and 95/8” casings and the 7” liner.
    •  11¾” contingency liner had to be run.
    •   Severe lost circulation near to the base of the Surakhany and again at Top Balakhany, due to a tight
        pore-fracture window.
    •  Over 19,000 barrels of mud were lost.
    •  Numerous kicks, including one incidence of cross flow associated with one of the lost circulation
        episodes.
    •  BOP problems.
    •   Logging problems.
    •  The well was spudded before SDX-1 had been completed and therefore before learning from the
         earlier well could be incorporated in the planning.

23.3 SDX-3, 3Z, 3Y, 3X

SDX-3, the third well on the structure, was drilled on the northern flank in a down dip location. It was a dry
hole. Despite the number of sidetracks this was an easier well to drill. Problems encountered were:
    •   Presence of a shallow overpressured sand led to remedial cementing on the 20” casing.
    •   Three stuck pipe incidents leading to sidetracks. All appear to be directly attributable to tectonics.

23.4 SDA-01

SDA-01, the first of pre drilled development wells on the structure, was drilled in a mid flank position on the
east flank of the structure. Key learning’s to date include:
    •   Greater than anticipated pressure and associated gas from 750m, “Q”-4, sand.
    •   22’ cement channeling that led to flow from wellhead grout ports.
    •   Mechanical failure of simultaneous drill and under-reaming of 14.75 x 17.5” interval.
    •   Greater than anticipated pore pressure measured by MDT in top Surakhany.
    •   Greater than anticipated pore pressure and associated gas from Pre Fasila sands below SP-4.

    •  Hughes Christensen DOSRWD Eccentric Pad tool sustained damage when used to drill out 22” shoe
       – dedicated drill-out trip planned for SDA-03.
    •  No repeat of Ander-reamer mechanical failure as seen on SDA-01.

23.6 SDA-03

SDA-03, the third directional well drilled on the Shah Deniz structure with 2.3km step out. SDA-03 drilled the
reservoir further updip than any well previously drilled on the structure. The pore pressure / fracture gradient
was a very tight ‘window’ at Sabunchi level although the effect of this window has been reduced as far as
possible due to the deep kick off and steeper hole angles in this well
Key learning’s to date include:
    •   Overall excellent performance to date.
    •    Drilling and directional performance with the 12 ¼’’ x 19 ½’’ DoSRWD/RSS BHA were excellent
    •   Equipment reliability was an issue in 16 1/2" section (4 Failures), 12 1/4" section (3 Failures) and 8
        ½’’ section (1 Failure) of SDA-03z. Though a modification was made on 16 1/2" section BHA, based
       on SDA-02 and Schlumberger worldwide experiences,  it was not effective in combating shock
        related failures
    •   Casing setting depth was pushed into the Sabunchi horn on this well so crossing the first pressure
       ramp. The pipe became stuck in this ramp region. This TD criteria on SDA-03 was deeper than the
         setting depth on SDA-01 and SDA-02.
