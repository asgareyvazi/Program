K1 Cement Retainer Operation Procedure

K1 Cement Retainer

 Application

The K1 Cement Retainer  is a high-performance,  drillable cement
 retainer ideal for most remedial cementing applications. The K1 was
designed  to function as a  drillable squeeze packer which,  after
 cementing, acts as a plug, trapping squeeze pressure on the cement
below the retainer and isolating the newly cemented area from the
 hydrostatic pressures above the cement retainer.

Advantages

     •  Can be set mechanically on tubing or  drill pipe or run on
        wireline.
     •  Run-in speed is up to the operator
     •  Workstring can be tested before squeezing by picking-up to
       close the valve
     •  Automatic closing of the valve when picking-up the workstring
      ensures lock-in of the squeeze under final pressure as excess
      cement is circulated out
     •   Effectively isolates squeeze from hydrostatic pressure

 Additional Information
The K1 is available for most casing sizes and has a sliding valve
arrangement  for pressure containment. The  tool can be set on
 wireline or threaded pipe.  It has a two-way valve that is controlled
 from surface; no springs to cock or stick; just pick-up workstring to
 close and set-down workstring to open.

K1 Snap-Latch Setting Tool

 Application

The K1 Snap-Latch  Setting Tool  is  designed  to  reliably  set a
Mechanical Set K1 Cement Retainer on tubing or Drillpipe.

Advantages

     •  One trip system.
     •   Easily converted to set either Model K1 Cement Retainers, Model
     K1 Bridge Plugs.
     •  Safe run-in. Upper slip is securely locked in place, and latch is
        solidly threaded into the body of the retainer.
     •  Snap-latch feature latches into the retainer with set-down weight
      and is released with tension.
     •  Controls retainer valve. The stinger opens the sleeve valve with
       set down weight and closes it with upstrain.
     •  Tubing pressure test is possible with latch sleeve valve retainers by
        pulling up to close retainer valve prior to snapping out of retainer.

 Additional Information

The K1  Setting Tool uses a combination  of right-hand  rotation and
 upstrain to set the cement retainer and bridge plug. The tubing and cement
 retainer valve can easily be pressure tested before cementing operations
 being. Since the setting sleeve is fully retracted after the cement retainer
has been set, the K1 Setting Tool becomes the stinger sub for the cement
 retainer. The ability to use the K1 Setting Tool as the setting  tool and
 stinger sub makes  it ideal for a one-trip cementing operations.

Model K1 Mechanical Setting Tool for
"K1" Sleeve Valve Cement Retainers

 Installation and Running Instructions

The Model K1 Mechanical Setting Tool is designed to run and set Model K1 Sleeve
Valve Cement Retainer. Easy to operate and low maintenance are evident in the
 design. The tool incorporates both a stinger seal and built-in snap latch allowing the
 tool to be latched into the retainer with set down weight and released with up- strain
 or right-hand rotation. This tool can be run time after time by simply moving the drive
 housing  (slip nut on smaller sizes) into place and installing new shear screws.
Disassembly is recommended upon returning to the shop. Tool sizes are available
 from 5 to 13-3/8 casing. Fewer moving parts and ease of operation make this tool a
good addition to your line. The Model K1 Mechanical Set Bridge Plug can be run with
 this tool as well by removing a few parts.

 Installation of Retainer or Bridge Plug on the Model K1 Mechanical Setting
Tool

    •  Place the top cone of the retainer or bridge plug in the vise and
        tighten.
    •   Apply grease to the stinger section of the setting tool.

    •  Stab the stinger section of the setting tool into the retainer or plug
       using a quick motion.  If necessary place a block of wood across the
      end of setting tool and strike with a sledge hammer. The stinger needs
        to go in until the latch threads snap into the retainer threads.

    •  Place a pipe wrench on the drive housing (slip nut on smaller sizes)
      and turn to the left screwing the latch farther into the retainer. Stop
     when the holes in the latch align with the holes in the body of retainer.

    •   Install torque screws furnished with the retainer.

    •  Align the holes in the drive housing (slip nut in smaller sizes) with the
      groove in the lower mandrel.

    •   Install shear screws in setting tool.

    •  Place the mechanical slips over the slip nut. With the drag housing
       butted against the stop ring, rotate the slip retaining sleeve down over
       the mechanical slips. Tighten the set screw in slip retaining sleeve.

K1 Cement Retainer Operation Procedure

Running Instructions

    •  The tool should be run at a moderate speed avoiding sudden stops.

    •  Avoid right-hand rotation transmitted to the setting tool. As a precaution, after
       every 10 stands the tubing or drill pipe can be rotated to the left by hand until
       torque is felt.

    •  At desired setting depth, rotate tubing to the right a minimum of seven turns,
       releasing the slips onto the cone.

    •   Pull into the tubing in one continuous pull. See chart below to view the
      recommended tension. It is important to calculate this tension through tubing
        stretch. Do not rely on weight indicators.

    •  After desired pull is reached, lock down the break on rig to allow setting force
        to reach retainer. Hold the tension approximately five minutes, then slack off
       pipe and set approximately five to ten thousand pounds weight down insuring
        retainer or plug is securely set.

           Retainer Size      Minimum Tension     Maximum Tension
                  5”              20,000 lbs.            30,000 lbs.
                  7”              30,000 lbs.            40,000 lbs.
           9-5/8” & 13-3/8”        40,000 lbs.            50,000 lbs.

Test Options - Releasing Retainer – Assembly

Test Options

    •  The tubing or drill pipe can be pressure tested by simply pulling up five
      thousand pounds at the tool and applying pump pressure to the tubing.

    •  The retainer can now be tested for seal-off by applying pressure down the
       annulus or by slacking off five thousand pounds weight on retainer and
       applying pump pressure down the tubing and pumping into formation.

    •  These tests are performed before the setting tool is released from the
        retainer.

    •    If seal-off has not been accomplished, up-strain on the tubing can again be
       applied and the tools can be retested until seal-off is accomplished.

Releasing Retainer

    •  Hold an up-strain of approximately one thousand pounds on the tubing.

    •  Apply torque to the right until torque screws are sheared. Each screw requires
      200 - 400 footpounds.

    •  Continue right-hand rotation for ten turns or until latch is felt releasing.

    •  After releasing from retainer, the setting tool can be relatched into the retainer
       with three to five thousand pounds set-down weight. This stabilizes at two
      thousand five hundred pounds with repetition.

    •  The valve will open when the stinger is fully engaged into the retainer and will
       close with a 2 inch upstroke at the tool. The stinger will remain sealed in the
       bore as long as snap-out force is not exceeded.

Conversion Procedures

The “Basic Unit” shown below must be obtained before conversion to any one of the
 products listed in the following pages can be accomplished. To obtain the “Basic
 Unit”, disassemble the parts from the appropriate tool to be converted as follows:

How to Obtain a “Basic Unit” From “K1” Cement Retainer:

    1. Loosen Set Screw in Guide. Set Screw extends through Body into the Guide
      on Sizes 9-5/8” and larger.

     2. Remove Guide and Lower Slip (Sizes 5” & 7”). DO NOT DROP LOWER
       SLIP.
     a. Insert the appropriate Cement Retainer Tension Mandrel into the Body. Strike
     the exposed end of the Tension Mandrel with a hammer and block of wood to
    push the Latch Sleeve, Seal Spacer and Seal out of the Retainer.

Recommended Drill out Procedures for “K1” Cement Retainers

If conditions are normal and the instructions shown below are followed,“K1” Cement
Retainers are relatively easy to drill up. The materials used in this product must
satisfy various operating specifications and are designed to break up into small
pieces during the drilling-out operation.
It is nearly impossible to cover all the problems that may arise under every condition;
however, there are certain procedures that can be followed in most instances. Thus
the recommendations listed below should be used as a guide only and, under
conditions other than normal past experience and common sense should govern the
drilling-out operation.

Suggested Rotary Operation

    1. A Junk Basket should be installed in the drilling string in order to trap cuttings.
       This will serve two purposes.
        a. It will help to eliminate the condition of continuing to drill on the same loose
        material.
        b. It will trap cuttings that might cause trouble during subsequent operations
        in the well.

    2. A new, high quality, medium hard formation rock bit with medium to short,
        regularly spaced teeth such as a Hughes WO should be used. Select a bit
        that will not “track”, and of a size that will give minimum clearance between
       the bit and the casing. It is recommended that bits with “wings” between the
       outer teeth not be used.

    3. Circulating down the drill pipe or tubing will allow larger pieces of the tool to
      be circulated up into the junk basket rather than reverse circulating. This will
       usually result in faster drill out.

    4. Then feeling for the tool, try to avoid any impact which might break some of
       the teeth on the bit. Experience has shown that rotary speeds between 50
      and 75 RPM will give the best results under normal condition.

    5. Gradually set down 5,000 lbs on the tool and drill for 5-10 minutes. Drill up the
      mandrel which extends above the slips and allow the bit to seat on the full
       diameter. Gradually increase set down weight to about 2,000 lbs per inch of bit
       diameter. If the rate of penetration drops, spudding, increased weight, or
       varying the speed should be tried until the rate of penetration picks up.
