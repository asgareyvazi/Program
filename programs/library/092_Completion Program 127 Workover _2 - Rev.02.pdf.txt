3.  7″ Scraper Run

3.1 Risk Evaluation

     Junk falls in hole
     Debris on top of Liner hanger
    BHA stuck in the hole in perforation interval
     Fish scraper parts in well.

3.2 HSE Focus Points

     Well has to be stable before running the BHA.
     Monitor well for loss/gain over the trip tank.
     Good communication among all parties responsible for well control.
     Pre-job meeting will focus on well control, contingency procedures to be understood by all

       involved parties.
     Volume and well control shall be focused upon throughout running/pulling. Fill out kick sheet

        prior to run in hole.
     On a positive indication of a kick, unexpected pit gains or flow return rate increase, well shall be

       shut in without performing flow check
    A kick stand shall be readily available at all times
     Kick assembly with proper cross over to 3 1/2" and 4 1/2" shall be prepared before starting

       operation.

Note: 7” scraper shall be suitable for 29ppf liner.

3.4 Operation Sequence

    1. P/U & M/U 5 7/8” Bit + 7” Scrapper + 4 ¾” DC or 3 ½” HWDP + enough length of 3 ½” & 5”

     DP
    2.  Scrape packer depth interval 3230m to 3320m Several times while pumping.

    4. POOH to surface while observe the well to make sure well is static. L/D all DPs & DCs.

    5.  Retrieve wear bushing, M/U jet sub and flush BOP stack & wellhead with fresh water.

    6.  Hold a pre – Job safety meeting on rig floor to instruct running speed and other critical

        installation procedures.

    7.  Lift all emergency well control subs on the rig floor and make up to cock valve.

4.  Completion Installation

4.1 Objectives

The objective is to install the upper completion, landing nipple, 4 1/2” & 3 ½” production tubing, tubing

hanger and X-mas tree.

4.2 Risk and Quality

Quantitative Risk Assessment, QRA, has been carried out on all main issues in the planning phase. The

purpose was to guide the work according to identified risks for the operations, as well as to reduce the

probability for incidents with potential HSE or commercial exposure. In the detailed planning phase,

QRA has been an integral part of the work. All potential risks identified have been evaluated and

mitigating actions have been implemented to reduce the risks to acceptable levels.

4.3 Main Concerns

     Junk falls in the well before or during running completions string.
     Well/completion fluid not cleaned prior to running completion string.
     Over-torque tubing connections.
     Missing to tight all stud bolts on X-Tree bottom section
     Tubing head wear/damage while RIH equipment, due to off centered derrick.
     Taking kick/inflow during completions operation (when well is perforated)
     Damaging Tubing Hanger while running through riser/BOP.
     Unnecessary personnel on rig floor while running completion string.
     Use excess amount of dope on each connection, Use of Improper Tong & elevators.
     Damaging DHSV control line with manual.
     Damage to injection lines while running in hole.
     Loosing pressure on DHSV control line while RIH.

4.5 Thread cleaning, compound application and make up

Storage compounds are not suitable as running compounds, so they must be removed and the

connections cleaned prior to application of thread running compound for make-up.

     Never use barite or wire brush to clean connections as damage can occur.
     Never use diesel to clean the connections as this will leave a film that can cause make up

      problems or affect the performance.

Use steam cleaner or water jet to clean the threads.

Rack one row of tubular on pipe rack, open the protectors, clean the box and pin connections out of

storage dope, hand tight the clean protectors and rack another row of tubular on top. Continue the above

5.  Clean up procedure

This program describes the procedure to clean up and displace the cased hole from water based mud

brine as safely and effectively as possible prior to running the completion equipment. Clean working

practices and good housekeeping as always are important. All rig pits, ditches, and lines (including gun

lines) should be cleaned using solvents and detergents. If it is practical, lines should be opened to check

for any mud solids that might have settled in them. All the pits, sand traps, and under the shale shakers

should be cleaned out and washed down using high pressure cleaning equipment. In the pit room, all

gratings should be cleaned, all the lights and beams should be washed down. All cleaning should be

done well in advance wherever possible. To clean pits and lines, 15 m3 of water containing 1 to 2 drums

of CLEAN SURF should be mixed up and circulated through all lines, gun lines, mixing lines etc. This

can also be used to clean the tank system initially.

     Pump should be worked at the maximum safe rate.
     The below check list should be filled by the supervisor in the site.
      It should be noted that all dump valves should seal with grease to ensure they are in good order.
     All ditch gates should be sealed with silicon on each side of the gate. This will need to be replaced

          if the gate is opened.
     Under no circumstances should barite, bentonite or polymers be used to seal anything.
     All pump packing should be examined before starting the cleaning

6.  Displacement Design

To ensure effective mud displacement and water wetting of the casing, the displacement pill needs to be

designed carefully. The use of a weighted push pills with a viscosity greater than that of the mud, will

minimize channeling and ensure good cleaning of the low side of the well bore.

The best fluid for thinning the mud is the continuous phase of the mud. In addition, SPERSEEN CFL

could be used to reduce the yield point and gel strengths. The yield point should be between 8 to10 Pa

and the 10-minute gel to <10 Pa. The actual quantity of material required to do this must be determined

by the pilot testing performed by the mud engineer to determine the most cost-effective treatment.

Throughout the drilling fluid conditioning process, the flow rate used should be the maximum practical

rate.

The drill string should be reciprocated and rotated during the operation. The combined effect of scrapers

being run in the hole, the high flow rate and the reciprocation/rotation will help to remove/disperse large

quantities of mud solids and general debris. The reciprocation stroke should be +/- 10 m. and the rotation

speed should be +/- 60 RPM

During this operation, as much of the surface volume of mud as possible should be removed from the

pits to accommodate the inhibited water and the well clean up pills. Once the mud has been back-loaded,

surface preparation can be done.

At least 150 m of pill/spacer coverage or 3 min contact time at the highest pump rates should be

considered.

Pit levels should be monitor throughout the displacement operation to catch and prevent an influx

entering the well contributing to an incident.

Mix & pump 40bbl fresh Hi-Vis 104pcf CaCO3 Poly-mud followed by 10bbl Hi-Vis 74pcf NaCl Brine, then

7.  Completion Fluid

 7.1 interval Description

      Completion

      The completion fluid for this section will be Calcium Bromide with a scavenger, inhibitor.

7.1.1 Solid – Free CaBr2 System

      Calcium bromide A clear brine fluid, Calcium Bromide (CaBr2) solution is used as single salt
       brine in densities ranging from 11.7 lbs/gal to 15.1 lbs/gal. It is used as a completion, work over,
       gravel pack, and packer fluid. It can also be used as a spike fluid to increase density in fluids up
        to 14.2 lb/gal. Due to its inhibitive properties, CaBr2 can help reduce formation damage induced
      by clay swelling and dispersion.

      Corrosion Control:

      The corrosion rate of this type of fluid (CR) is less tha 5 MPY.

        Pit management will be a key in the successful completion of this interval and sufficient time for
