    3.  Scope of Work

    3.1.  Drilling waste management design

    The contractor is responsible for designing the optimum practices for treating the produced
     waste during the operation. The  initial technical waste management proposal should be
     included the practices for minimizing, treating and recovering of the wastes.

     According to the below categories:

    .     Water based mud containing minerals and natural synthetic polymers, and
    .      Cuttings contaminated with WBM.

    The contractor is encouraged to offer possible practices to minimize the drilling fluids waste
     and its regeneration.

3.1.1.Water based mud containing minerals, naturals and synthetic polymers

    .     Primary operation

    The proposed primary solids control package should be able to control drilling fluid properties,
     reducing of mud  materials usage  against conventional  solid  control  equipment's, and
      significantly reduce the number of excess fluids generated throughout drilling each phase of
      the well.

    .     Secondary Operation

     All excess mud and spillage (water based) should be collected in rig site pits and free surface
      fluid should be treated using a built for propose flocculation station (has to join with centrifuge)
      consisting of integral dosing and pumps along with a centrifuge for efficient, quick and
      controlled separation of the solids before the cleaned fluids is returned to active system or
      buffer tanks.

     This system should be able to remove colloidal sized particles that cannot be removed with
     primary solids control system.

     This system may use to clean up the remaining water-based mud or decrease weight of oil base
    mud at the end of the drilling fluids programmed.

3.1.2.Cuttings contaminated with WBM

    Spud mud and water-based mud cuttings should be fixed and be ready for transport to disposal
     zone. For fixing the cutting, 100-150 kg cement shall be used for each cubic meters of cutting
     as well as 3-4-liter Sodium Silicate for each cubic meter of cutting. (Consumption of cement
     and sodium silicate varies in different formations). Gypsum is not allowed for cutting fixation.
     This method is physically Bonds Contaminants to Prevent Leaching to Environment. Final
     product will pass can test and in case of approval by company man it’ll send to landfill.

     3.2.  Waste discharge and handling

    The Company is responsible for designing of required pits and The Contractor is responsible
      for procedure for burring of waste cuttings in disposal zone; supervision and managing of
     requirement operation for deposit oil waste cutting.

    The contractor is responsible for treating all the generated waste in site without any discharges
      in environment or damages. It is also the responsibility of the contractor for handling/discharge
     of generated waste after treatment.

    The contractor manages and supervises the solids and fluid waste streams loading and off-
     loading on/from  the  designated  areas and  responsible  for  the  loader along with  the
      responsibility of the storage pits, tanks, etc.

    3.3.  Laboratory equipment's and analysis

    The contractor should prepare suitable laboratory analysis equipment's to perform complete
      analysis services of waste matter. The provided equipment's shall make this opportunity to
     study the mentioned environmental policy of waste analysis.

   5.4. High Performance Filter Unit

    Filtration plays an important role in the natural treatment of waste water as it percolates through
   the soil. It is also a major part of most water treatment. Groundwater that has been softened, or
    treated through iron and manganese oxidation, requires filtration to remove floc created by
   coagulation or oxidation processes. Since surface water is subject to run-off and does not
   undergo natural filtration.

     it must be filtered to remove particles and impurities. Filtration can be compared to a sieve or
   micro- strainer that traps suspended material between the grains of filter media. However, since
   most suspended particles can easily pass through the spaces between grains of the filter media,
   straining  is the  least important process in  filtration.  Filtration primarily depends on a
   combination of complex physical and chemical mechanisms, the most important being
   adsorption. Adsorption is the process of particles sticking onto the surface of the individual
    filter grains or onto the previously deposited materials. Forces that attract and hold particles to
   the grains are the same as those that work in coagulation and flocculation. In fact, coagulation
   and flocculation may occur in the filter bed, especially if coagulation and flocculation before
    filtration was not properly controlled. Incomplete coagulation can cause serious problems in
    filter operation.
