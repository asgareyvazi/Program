2      Field and Platform Information


2.1     Introduction

        South Pars is a gas-condensate field located in the Persian Gulf. This field is named South
         Pars on the Iranian side of the border and North Field on the Qatari side. The North Field
        has been on production for some years.

       The reservoir formation is the Triassic, Permian Khuff formation, which consists of
        shallow water carbonate sediments. The Khuff formation is divided into four main units,
        K1, K2, K3 and K4. Top reservoir is at approximately 2680 m TVD MSL at the most
         shallow, Phase 6.

        South Pars 6, 7 & 8 will be developed with three wellhead platforms, normally unmanned
         during the production phase. There will be 16 well slots on each platform, of which 10 are
        planned utilized initially.

      Two jack-up rigs equipped with cantilever, will be in operation to drill and complete a
        planned total of 27 production wells and to tie back three predrilled appraisal wells (SP9,
       SP10 and SP11). It should be noted that adjustments in total number of wells might take
          place.

       As per present plan, the first jacket installation is planned for December 2003, with the
           last two jackets installed back-to-back right after the first. The two jack-up drilling rigs
          will immediately after the jacket installation be moved in place next to the jackets.
         Drilling will continue until late 2004 when the topsides are planned to be installed. The
       two jack-up rigs will then be moved between the three phases/jackets to enable topside
          installations with minimum loss of rig time. The rest of the wells will then be drilled
        through the topside. The drilling and completion program are planned completed by the
        end of 2006.

       The wells are planned drilled with an inclination between 20-60 degrees. The typical gas
        producer will be drilled to just above the gas-water-contact, which is at approximately
       3130 mTVD MSL in K4. The casing program is planned to be 26” x 18 5/8” x 13 3/8” x 9
          5/8”, and the reservoir completed with a 7” 13% Cr non-cemented predrilled liner. The
        completion is planned to consist of a 9 5/8” production packer and 7” tubing with a sub
         surface safety valve. At present the preferred completion design is commingling of all for
         reservoir zones, K1, K2, K3 and K4 by running a predrilled uncemented 7” liner across
         the reservoir section. This is expected to provide sufficient productivity and optimized
         recovery. Perforation and acid stimulation can be applied in case of unsatisfactory
         productivity. After completion the wells will be cleaned up through the x-mas tree and a
         well testing package on the jack-ups. The wells will then be shut in until production start
         up.

       The two first wells are planned used as a guide with respect to correct casing depths, and
           start of a Best Practice process on South Pars. The rest of the wells on each platform are
        planned batch drilled according to a "Main Drilling Program". First completion is planned
          installed during first half of 2004.

           Initial reservoir pressure and temperature is approximately 363 bar and 107 deg C
          respectively. The formation fluids can be regarded as severely corrosive. H2S and CO2 are
        measured to 0.5 % (mol) and 2.0 % (mol) respectively. Formation water is not expected
        produced. However, flow wetted components must be of a material composition able to
        handle small amounts of formation water.


2.2     Partnership

       The development rights for phases 6, 7 and 8 of the South Pars field were awarded to the
         Petropars on 10 July 2000.
        Following negotiations through 2002, Statoil assumed a 40% share in the offshore
        elements of the project. The agreement was signed on 10 November 2002. This share is
         equal to 11.3% of the total CAPEX for the project. Statoil will manage the offshore
        elements of the development.

3    GENERAL DRILLING


3.1     Introduction

        This chapter describes information and general requirements for each hole section for the
        development wells to be drilled on the South Pars field. The individual Well Drilling
        Programs will contain specific requirements for each hole section, which is not described
          in the Main Drilling Program.


3.2     General Drilling Information


3.2.1   32" Hole Section for 26" Conductor

       The 26” conductors will be batch set at 60-80 m below seabed based on the “South Pars
         6,7,8 Soil Description Summery “ performed by Asle Eide.

       The 32" section will be drilled with seawater and viscous pills with return to seabed.
       The hole will be displaced to weighted mud prior to running the 26" conductor.

       The formation throughout the section is soft and uniform with some hard stringers. No
         indications of boulders have been observed in the appraisal wells.


3.2.2    Drilling 24” Hole for 18 3/8” Surface Casing

       The object of the 18 5/8” surface casing is to case off the total loss zones in the Jahrum
        and Sachun Formation prior to entering the Kazhdumi and Dariyan Formation where
         pressures may exceed 1.15 SG. The shoe will be set in the Laffan shale at approximately
       1100 mTVD.
       The section is planned to be batch drilled.
        This hole section will be drilled with seawater and viscous pills with return to the rig.
       The 24" hole will be displaced to weighted mud prior to running 18 5/8" casing.
      No shallow gas was observed in the appraisal wells drilled by Enterprise but 55 ppm H2S
       was observed on well SP-11 during displacing out a hi-vis pill at TD.
        Sulphurous water flow from Halul and Jahrum formation has been observed on the field.

3.2.3    Drilling 16"(17 ½”) Hole Section for 13 3/8" Casing

       The object of the 13 3/8” intermediate casing is to isolate the weak Fahliyan fm. and the
           oil bearing Dariyan fm. The shoe will be set in the Hith anhydrite at +/- 1650 mTVD.

       The section is planned to be batch drilled.

       The 16" (or 17 ½”) section is planned drilled with a KCl/polymer water based mud.


3.2.4    Drilling 12 1/4" Hole Section for 10 ¾” x 9 5/8” Casing

       The object of the 9 5/8” production casing is to seal off the pressured Sudair/Aghar shales
        above the Kangan gas reservoir. This will allow drilling the reservoir with a lower mud
         weight.
       The shoe will preferably be set into the (K1) reservoir.
       The section is planned to be batch drilled.
       The 12 1/4" hole section is planned drilled with a KCl/polymer water based mud system.



3.2.5    Drilling 8 1/2" Hole section

       The wells will be primary be completed with an open hole predrilled liner with a cemented
          liner as a back-up solution. The liner will cover the K1, 2, 3 and 4 gas reservoirs.
        8 ½” sections planned is drilled with a KCl/polymer water based mud system or salt
         saturated (Nacl) system.


3.2.6   Temporary P&A


      No plug will be used for temporarily abandonment of 18 5/8” and 13 3/8” casing.
           If batch drilling of 12 ¼” section the wells will be temporarily plugged after cementing of
       9 5/8”casing, with a mechanical plug set approx. 50 m below 9 5/8” x 10 ¾” crossover.

4   GEOLOGY


4.1     Introduction

       The late Permian Dalan Formation and the early Triassic Kangan Formation is the main
        gas reservoir on the South Pars Field. Oil is discovered in the more shallow Cretaceous
         Formations.

       Kangan and Dalan Formation are divided into four major sequences called K1, 2, 3 and 4.
         In the sequence stratigraphic framework made by Total South Pars these are referred to, as
      KS1 to KS4 witch is slightly different from K1-4. TSP made a detailed sequence
          stratigraphic model based on SP-5 and 6 this is the basis for Robertsons work on the SP-9
         core and are also the basis for our work.

       The reservoir consist mainly of dolomites plus a lower content of limestone and minor
         anhydrites.
        7 major depositional environments are identified spreading from evaporitic/sabkhas flats
          to offshore settings.


4.3     General Geology

         In the late Early Permian, the Arabian Plate is interpreted to have undergone a major phase
         of crustal extension resulting in continental separation. Progressive thermal uplift is
          interpreted to have occurred along the present-day Zagros Suture culminating in the
         regional ‘pre Khuff unconformity’. In the Late Permian, continental spreading took place
         as the Neo-Tethys Ocean started to form.

       Movement on major faults in Abu Dhabi produced substantial, thickness variations within
         the Lower Dalan. Movement on these faults ceased, however, following the deposition of
         the base middle anhydrite. Major shallow water carbonate platform sedimentation
         subsequently prevailed across the entire Arabian Plate, lasting until the late Toarcian.


4.4     Lithology

       The summary given below describes the lithology for the South Pars field. The
         stratigraphy is based on sample descriptions, logs, core descriptions, and correlation
        between wells.

     QUARTERNARY - TERTIARY

     FARS GROUP (thickness ca 180-192 m)
       The Fars Group comprises dominant limestones that pass down in to gypsum and
         anhydrite, finely interbedded with claystones in the deepest part. A sequence of packstone
        and grainstone lies at the base of the formation.

      ASMARI FORMATION (thickness 69-72 m)
       Top Asmari is picked at the first occurrence of dolomite. This becomes the dominant
          lithology, locally grading to dolomitic limestone.

     JAHRUM FORMATION (thickness 270- 424 m)
       The top of the Jahrum Formation marks the unconformable boundary between the lower
       Miocene and the Eocene. Lithologically and petrophysically it is hard to distinguish from
         the overlying Asmari and is picked on depth. The Jahrum Formation contains the Sachun
         Facies Member that has been picked as a slight shift in the gamma ray baseline and just
        below the first occurrence of anhydrite. The sequence is dominated by dolomite. Chert is
      common about 600 m.

       At the base of the Tertiary sequence, the lowermost 20 m is highly distinctive. Although
          lithologically it continues of dolomite, the color becomes darker and it becomes
          characteristically speckled.

     CRETACEOUS

      ILAM / HALUL FORMATION (thickness 73-83 m)
       The Ilam – Jahrum boundary marks the major unconformity between the Tertiary and the
         Cretaceous. As such it is clearly marked, both lithologically and petrophysically as well as
       by a significant drilling break whilst drilling. Lithologically, the sequence consists of
         interbedded dolomite and wackestones – packstones.

     SURGAH / LAFFAN FORMATION (thickness 35-42 m)
       The Surgah / Laffan Formations become increasingly argillaceous through the sequence
        with chalky mudstones passing down into claystones. The top is picked at the first
         indication of an increasing gamma ray. The Laffan itself, being predominantly claystone,
           is represented by a significantly increasing gamma ray profile, very low resistivity values
        and a major shift on the sonic log. The base of the sequence is marked by a significant
        break in the logs, representing the change from claystone / shales to underlying clean
        chalky limestones.

     SARVAK FORMATION  (thickness 56-94 m)
       The Sarvak Formation is sub-divided into three members, the Mishhrif, Ahmadi and the
          thin Mauddud at the base. The upper boundary is unconformable and clearly cuts down
          into the Sarvak in a NW direction as the sequence shows progressive thinning between
         SP-9, SP-10 and SP-11. In SP-9, the Mishrif – Ahmadi sequence is 79 m thick, in SP-10 it
           is 66 m tick and in SP-11, only 41 m tick.
       The Mishrif, Ahmadi Members and Mauddud consists of wackstone – mudstone.

     KAZHDUMI FORMATION (thickness 40-46 m)
       The Kazhdumi Formation is marked by a major break in the logs corresponding to a
         lithology change as the overlaying carbonates pass down into dominant claystones.
         Lithologically, the Kazhdumi comprises of brown claystones interbedded with thin
        wackstones. At the base at the Kazdhumi, two sandstone stringers are present. These are
          thin, being only a few meters thick.

        These claystones and sandstones represent the last significant clastic deposits encountered
          in the sequence until Lower Triassic shales. These thin stringers are oil bearing in SP-11.

      DARIYAN FORMATION (thickness 110-113 m)
       The top of the Dariyan is marked by a lithology change from the overlying dominant
         claystone to carbonates, and as such is also marked by a significant log break.
       The carbonates through the majority of the sequence consist of mudstones. An
         argillaceous section in the middle of the Dariyan grades into a very calcareous claystone,
        which is approximately 30 m thick. At the base of the section the lithology reverts to
        mudstone.
         Oil shows are present in the formation in SP-10 and SP-11.

     GADVAN FORMATION (thickness 70-82 m)
       The top of the Gadvan Formation is picked on a minor log break, where the gamma ray
        becomes increasingly erratic, reflecting the higher argillaceous content of the limestones.
        Thin claystones beds, 1-5 m in thickness appear throughout the formation.

      FAHLYIAN FORMATION (thickness 158-176 m)
       The Fahlyian Formation is the basal section of the Cretaceous on South Pars and
        comprises of wackestones and mudstones, which become interbedded with grainstone and
         dolomites towards the base. The top the Fahlyian Formation is picked below a sharp
      gamma ray spike, representing a claystone horizon at the base of the overlying Gadvan
        Formation.
        Chert becomes quite common in the middle of the section over an interval of around 50 m.
        Dolomites first appear about halfway down the section.

      JURASSIC

      HITH FORMATION (thickness 63-72 m)
       The Hith Formation is one of the major seismic markers in the South Pars field. An abrupt
        change in lithology occurs as the Cretaceous section passes unconformably down into the
          Jurassic. The carbonates of the overlaying sequence give way to evaporites with a
         correspondingly significant log break.
       The Hith Formation consists predominantly of anhydrite, with interbedded stringers of
         dolomite.

     SURMEH FORMATION
       The Surmeh Formation is subdivided into a number of members each having their own
          characteristics.

       Arab Member (thickness 60-64 m)
       The Arab Member consists of interbedded evaporites and carbonates leading to rather
          erratic log response. The unit is picked at the top on the first major dolomite bed to occur
        below the Hith Formation and the last significant anhydrite bed delineates the base.

       Upper Dolomite Member (thickness 46-48 m)
       The Upper Dolomite is a thin unit comprising dominant of dolomite with minor mudstone
          stringers. Lithologically it is quite uniform, with localized fluctuations in porosity causing
         sonic and resistivity variations. The top is picked below the last significant anhydrite bed,
         whilst the base is picked where the dolomite passes into a more uniform mudstone –
        wackestone sequence.

       Upper Limestone Member (thickness 67-95 m)
       The top of this interval is picked at the point where the dolomites are replaced by
        dominant mudstone – wackestone in the sequence. A slight increase of 5 API in the
      gamma ray response can also be observed.

       Cherty Zone Member (thickness 67 m)
       The Cherty Zone Member is so called due to the presence of cherty horizons in some parts
         of the South Pars field. In SP-9, 10 and 11 however no chert was observed. The
          lithological composition of this horizon is an alternating sequence of dolomite and
        mudstone – wackestone.

       Middle Limestone Member (thickness 94-102 m)
       The middle Limestone Member is characterized by an increase in limestone with a
         decrease in dolomite. The member is composed primarily of wackestones and packstone
        with minor dolomite stringers. The top is marked by a slight downward shift in the gamma
         ray of 15 API. The base of this member is marked by the appearance of a distinctive
         mottled grey to black limestone, which straddles the boundary between this formation and
         the underlying Mand Member.

      Mand Member (thickness 94 m)
       The top Mand member is picked within the distinctively mottled wackestone – packstone
        sequence below the base of the claystone stringer identified by a gamma ray spike in SP-9
        and 10. The Mand Member comprises mainly of mudstone and wackestone with minor
         interbedded packstone and dolomite. Further thin horizons of the mottled dark grey to
         black wackestone continue to occur intermittently.

       Lower Limestone Member (thickness 111-142 m)
       The Lower Limestone Member is lithologically very similar to the overlaying Mand
       Member. The top is picked at the upper boundary of a more argillaceous and carbonaceous
          unit that exhibits a corresponding gamma ray increase. The lowermost part becomes
         increasingly argillaceous. Stringers of dolomite occur especially in the middle part.

       Lower Surmeh Shale Member (thickness 41-47 m)
       The Lower Surmeh Shale Member consists of argillaceous and carbonaceous limestones.
      Two 2 m thick gamma ray spikes mark the top of this member. The gamma ray response
         also becomes very erratic, reflecting the finely bedded and varied nature of this member.
       Over the lower half of this formation the limestones become slightly dolomitic,
         representing the top of a thick sequence of dolomite dominated carbonate lithology.

         Lithiotis Bed Member (thickness 24-28 m)
       The Lithiotis Bed Member consists predominantly of dolomite interbedded with
        wackestone.

        Neyriz Shale Member (thickness 13-16 m)
       The Neyriz Shale Member consists mainly of claystone with wackestone stringers and
         exhibits a sharp, gamma ray defined upper boundary.

      TRIASSIC

     DASHTAK FORMATION  (thickness 154-168 m)
       The Dashtak Formation can almost be divided in two halves with the upper 75 m
        comprising of dolomite with minor interbeds of claystone and mudstone, and the lower 90
     m consisting of interbedded anhydrite and dolomite. This lower half of the Dashtak is
         referred to as the Evaporite B. A drop in the gamma ray marks the top of the Evaporite B.
        With depth, the anhydrite becomes less dominant, passing into thicker dolomites and
         claystone stringers.

      SUDAIR FORMATION  (thickness 270-277 m)
       The Sudair Formation is a varied formation that, in gross terms, is divided into Upper and
        Lower. The Lower Sudair (below base Massive Anhydrite Member) has been subdivided
          into ten members, of which seven are the Sudair zones recognized in South Pars.
       The top of the Sudair Formation can be picked by the sharp rise in gamma ray response
        and sonic travel time.

       Upper Shale Member (thickness 45-52 m)
       The Upper Shale Member is marked by the first occurrence of significant claystone in the
          cuttings. Although initially the claystone stringers are quite thin, with depth they become
         progressively thicker, up to a maximum of 8 m.

        Massive Anhydrite Member (thickness 26-28 m)
       The Massive Anhydrite consists of anhydrite beds, ranging in thickness from 2 m to 14 m,
         interlaminated with thin dolomite and minor claystone.

           is not possible.

      gamma ray profile. This passes down into the S7, which is composed of a sequence of
         interbedded claystones and anhydrites. Zones S6 to S4 inclusive comprise mainly of

     KANGAN FORMATION (thickness 180-188 m)
       The Kangan Formation, together with the underlying Permian Dalan Sequence represents
         the primary reservoir objective in the South Pars field.

      UPPER KANGAN (thickness 30 m)
       The uppermost part of the Kangan consists of dolomites interbedded with claystone and
         anhydrite. The claystones are clearly seen on the logs because of the high gamma ray
         values.

      K1  (thickness 107-115)
       The top K1 is picked below a high gamma ray spike. The topmost 23-37 m of the
        sequence consists of dolomite with claystone and anhydrite stringers. This uppermost
         section is generally tight although thin stringers with some better reservoir properties
         appears. Reservoir zone 2 or the middle part of K1 consists predominantly of grainstone,
        mudstone and wackestone with subordinate dolomite stringers. Anhydrite nodules appear
          in the uppermost and lowermost part of the zone. Reservoir zone 2 is regarded as the
         productive part of K1.
         Reservoir zone 3 or the lowermost part of K1 consists of dolomitised grainstone,
        wackestone and mudstone. Anhydrite appears as isolated nodules and as stringers at the
        base of the section.

      K2  (thickness 42-45 m)
        This second reservoir interval, marking the base of the Triassic sequence, displays a
          characteristic low flat gamma ray profile as well as a rather erratic sonic response. K2 is
         divided into three reservoir zones (4,5 and 6) where the two uppermost are the better ones.
         Reservoir zones four and five consist of porous dolomite and limestone with good
         reservoir properties whilst the lowermost part of K2 consists of cemented limestone with
        very low permeability.

6.5     Wellbore stability

     A wellbore stability study for South Pars has been carried out for phases 6-7-8.

        Borehole collapse curves have been calculated for inclinations between 0-60 degrees. In
         the carbonates in and above the reservoir the mud density should be designed only for well
         control and lost circulation purposes. Boreholes are predicted to remain stable in the
         carbonates (without cavings) at all mud densities between the pore pressure and the
          fracturing pressure.

        Borehole collapse will occur in the Sudair and Aghar Shales. It is not possible to stabilize
         the formation with mud weight in high deviated wells as shown in figure 6.2
       The collapse will occur on each side on the wellbore and not from the top and bottom.

       The wellbore stability in a well is dependant of pore pressure, stress regime, hole angle
        and lithology. The wellbore is kept stable from collapsing by sufficient mud density and
         correct chemical composition-inhibition of the drilling fluid.

       As the inclination of a well increases, it is expected that the fracture gradient will decrease.
            It is assumed that the lowest value of the fracture gradient will be equal to minimum
         horizontal stress in the rock. At South Pars the regional orientation of maximum horizontal
          stress is N-S. The prediction is that inclined wells drilled N-S will require a higher mud
         density than wells drilled in an E-W orientation at the same inclination.

        Lost circulation may turn out be an acute problem throughout the drilling campaign. The
        dominating reason for losses appears to be systems of natural fractures, especially in the
        Jahrum and Surmeh formations.

8    DIRECTIONAL DRILLING AND SURVEYING


8.1     Introduction

       The primary objectives of the directional drilling and surveying program are:
      w    Hit geological targets within given tolerances.
      w    Avoid collision with other wells.
      w    Monitor the well trajectory at all times in case a relief well has to be drilled.


8.2     Well Description

       The wells are presently designed with a J-shaped profile, with up-to 60 degrees inclination.
        Rotary and motor BHA will be used from the 24” section to TD.


8.3     Planning

        For any well path, collision avoidance calculations shall be made. Any potential for well
         interference shall be addressed in the Well Drilling Program with a plan showing both the
         horizontal and vertical plan of the proposed well. Also a well separation listing including
           all nearby wells shall be included.
       The probability to drill into an adjacent well shall be less than 2 x 10-3 per well.
       Minimum allowed distance between wells (3D borehole centre-to centre distance) is,
         defined by the calculated clearance factor CF >= 1.
      CF = Actual Distance / Minimum Allowed Distance.

8.5     Operational Requirements

        General requirement:
           If a new well is diverging from the drilling plan and gets closer than the distance defined as
      Minimum Allowed Distance to a producing well, stop drilling, and take the following
         action:
        •  Perform a SJA (Safe Job Analysis)
        •   If the critical point is above the depth of the down hole safety valve, close the valve.
        •   If the critical point is below the depth of the down hole safety valve, a plug shall be set
             in the production tubing below the critical depth.
        •   If possible, slide past the critical point; evaluate plugging back the new well and
              sidetrack.
       Most of the wells in this project will be batched drilled. The requirement of a clearance
          factor, CF >= 1, is therefore not an absolute requirement.


8.6     Directional surveying


8.6.1   Well targets


       The target tolerances shall be evaluated on the basis of the geological uncertainty and the
          directional uncertainty for the available survey tools.
         Ellipsoids of uncertainty shall be calculated for every new well and be compared with the
        given tolerances for the target.


8.6.2   Surveying

         Directional surveying shall be performed according to:
       The “Minimum Curvature” method shall be used for all trajectory calculations.

    MWD survey tools will be used as primary survey tools in open hole sections. Survey
          intervals shall not exceed 100 m.

      An exemption is made in the Jahrum formation due to extremely rough drilling conditions
        and this part of the section will be drilled without MWD.

10.1    Introduction

         In the Well Drilling Program there shall be a cementing program that outlines the
        cementing to be carried out for each casing setting depth.


10.2    Quality control

         All cement compositions (expect for 26” conductor) will be pilot tested by the supplier

10.3     Centralization

     A centralization plan shall be submitted prior to running all casing strings.


10.4   Cementing Program

     A detailed cement program shall be prepared by the Cementing Company prior to drilling
         the well.
         Prior to each cement job an individual cementing program shall be prepared. This program
          shall describe the actual cementing procedure, including  spacer and cement volume,
          slurry and spacer design, displacement rates, mud properties etc. to be used.

10.5.1  Conductor

       The objective for the 26” cement job is:
           •  Perform a good shoe
           •  Anchor for the WH (Min length of good cement based on conductor study is 26m)

       The 26" conductors will be cemented back to the sea floor.
       The cement slurry will be designed to achieve a high early strength at the seabed without
        exceeding the formation strength.

           If insufficient primary cement job, a grouting job has to be planned for.

         pipe above the reservoir and no cementing is necessary. The well will then produce from
         the predrilled liner section.

           If the pressure gradient from the reservoir is higher than the fracture gradient at the 9 5/8”
        shoe and severe losses occurs during drilling, the formation above the reservoir has to be
         sealed with cement to prevent an underground blow out.
         Alternative solutions will then be:


10.5.6.2 Expandable casing

       Open up the hole from the 9 5/8” shoe to the top of the reservoir and use a cemented
        expandable liner to seal off the formation above the reservoir. A 7” predrilled liner will
         then be set trough the reservoir.


10.5.6.3 Stage cementing of a combined blank-pipe and predrilled liner

     A stage-tool with inner-string cementing and an ECP packer as an anchor for the cement
          will be used to cement and seal off the formation above the reservoir. The predrilled liner
          will then be run below the stage tool trough the reservoir.


10.5.6.4 5” predrilled liner trough reservoir

     A 7” liner could be set into the top of the reservoir. A 5” predrilled liner will then be set
        trough the reservoir.

11   WELL CONTROL


11.1    Well Control Equipment

        Well control equipment will constitute one of the minimum two barrier elements required
         during drilling.


11.2    Well Control Drills

           •  Minimum frequency during relevant operations shall be minimum 2 pit drills and 1
                kick drill weekly per crew.
           •   Drills shall be reported on the Daily Drilling Report (DBR)


11.2.1  Flow checks

       Flow checks shall be performed at least prior to the following operations:
           •  When entering the previous casing shoe.
           •   Prior to pulling out of hole.
           •  At all unexpected drilling breaks.
           If hole conditions permit, the check period shall be at least 10 minutes.


11.2.2  Formation Strength Testing

     A formation strength test will be performed after installation of the 18 5/8" surface casing
        and all subsequent casing strings.
       FIT for 8 ½” section will probably not be performed because ECD during cementing of 10
      ¾” x 9 5/8” casing will be much higher than required for drilling 8 ½” section if the casing
           is set into the reservoir and the mud weight is lowered.
       Type of formation strength test and data requirements will be given in each individual
          drilling program.


11.3    Pressure Testing

         Barriers shall be tested at least to maximum anticipated differential pressure. Pressure tests
         are influenced by test volume, gas solubility (OBM) temperature changes, gauge
          sensitivity, etc. The following acceptance criteria may be used:
        •  Low pressure tests: Max 5% deviation
        •   Small volumes: (1-5 m3) Max 2% deviation
        •  Large volumes: Max 1% deviation

12     Health, Safety and Environment (HSE)


South Pars’overall HSE goal is, as for Statoil in general:
• Zero damage to humans or environment
• Zero accidents or losses

During the drilling operations, all necessary steps will be taken to prevent pollution, blowouts,
explosions and other types of accidents harmful to personnel, the installation and/or the
environment.

The SP Wells Unit will establish project specific HSE programs for the duration of the drilling
operations. These will be co-coordinated with the Drilling Contractor's corresponding existing and
future HSE programs, in order to ensure common goals on topics that the programs have in
common.

The programs will cover topics as:
• Working environment
• Environment
• Discharge requirements
• Total Fluid Management (TFM)
• Safety
