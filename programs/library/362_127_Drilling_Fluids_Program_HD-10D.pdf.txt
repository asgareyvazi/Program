1.2  WELL OBJECTIVES


The 26” section will be drilled using Sea water and high viscous sweep to TD at ± 200 m to run 20”
casing.

The well  will be started with a conductor pipe, which already has been piled during the
construction of the well location. The 26” hole section will be drilled through conductor pipe to 200
meters MD. Un‐weighted Hi‐vis Sweep pill (XC‐Polymer) willl be utilized to sweep the hole per each
stand has been drilled prior to stop the circuation to be sure that haole has been cleand and cutting
has been caried over from the hole. HYDRO‐ZAN at 1.5 lb/bbl will be mixed to drill under sub‐base
to avoide whasing out below conductor pipe which cause to un‐stable the rig base. Losses are not
expected. Surface casing will be set at +/‐ 200 m in top hole formation to provide the support for
the surface wellhead and subsequent casing, the casing will be cemented up to the surface. in case
of availability of drill water pre hydreated bentonite at 25 ‐ 30 lb/bbl will be utilized instead of XC‐
Polymer.

The 17 ½" hole section will be drilled from ± 200 meters at 20” casing shoe to ± 1,800 meters of
section TD where drilled out Aghajari and Mishan formation.  The fluid for this section is one
designed to give maximum inhibition to the formations in particular Aghajari and Mishan clays. KCl/
NaCl Polymer will minimize the effects of potential hole wash out, whilst providing partial solids
free density and clay inhibition. KCl is recommended at 3‐5% by weight to prove shale inhibition.

KCl at 15‐20 lb/bbl and NaCl at 30‐50 lb/bbl will be mixed with seawater to got 68‐70 pcf brine as
base fluid. HYDRO‐CAP XP (PHPA) can be utilized at ± 0.5 lb/bbl to encapsulate drill solids prior to
be dispersed in active system. Mishan and Aghajari are un‐permeable formation and seems that
isn’t necessary to control fluid loss, however Starch at 5 lb/bbl and MY‐PAC LV at 2 lb/bbl will be
consumed to pass this interval with fluid loss under control. pH will be kept by Caustic Soda at ± 9.5
and not more to avoid fully hydrolyzing of Partially Hydrolyzed Poly‐acryl Amide (PHPA). HYDRO‐
ZAN will be used as base of Rheology to provide adequate YP and high low shear rate which is the
key factor to ensure adequate hole cleaning. HYDRO‐LUBE can be used because of unique wet‐

The 12¼" hole section will be drilled from 13 ⅜" casing shoe at ± 1,800 meters to an estimated
section TD of 2,300 meters. After setting & cementing 13 ⅜” casing, drill out the shoe & cement
using mud recovered from the last interval, then displace the mud system to salt saturated polymer
mud at 100 pcf.

Whilst drilling, mix Salt Saturated polymer in the reserve volume as per formulation and dilute
system to maintain low gravity solids below 7%vol. Reduce dilution volumes by optimizing screen
size to ensure +/‐ 80% of the screens, are covered by the drilling fluid. Shear premixes volumes
using gun lines & shearing unit to avoid losses over the shakers.

Starch at 5 lb/bbl and MY‐PAC LV at 2 lb/bbl will be consumed to pass this interval with ﬂuid loss
under control. pH will be kept by Caustic Soda at 9.5‐10.5, HYDRO‐ZAN will be used as base of
Rheology to provide adequate YP and high low shear rate which is the key factor to ensure
adequate hole cleaning. HYDRO‐LUBE will be used at ±1% Vol. to decrease the coefficient of
friction, reducing torque and drag.



The 8 ½" hole section will be drilled from 9 ⅝” casing shoe at ± 2,300 meters to an estimated
section TD of ± 3,894 meters in Asmari formation. This section will be drilled directionally to the
liner point where inclination will be around 75 degrees.

After setting & cementing 9 ⅝”, drill out the shoe using mud recovered from the last interval and
then pump 50 bbls high viscous pill as spacer and displace the hole with fresh KCl/ Glycol Polymer
at 70 pcf.

The fluid for this section is one designed to give maximum inhibition to the formations in particular
Asmari Shales and enable drilling through the troublesome Asmari shales and arls. The protection
of water sensitive shales from hydration and dispersion effects is achieved by the use of KCl at 3‐
5%wt and Glycol at 2‐3%vol. The required level of inhibition can be tailored to meet the particular
demands of the formation being drilled. HYDRO‐PLAST has been formulated in range between 2 –
6 lb/bbl to reacts with shale to prevent or stop sloughing and swelling. HYDRO‐PLAST performs a
wide variety of functions in a drilling fluid. It acts as a high temperature fluid loss control agent and
gives thin tough filter cakes. HYDRO‐PLAST reduces torque and drag, inhibits the sloughing and
dispersion of shales and aids in the emulsification of oil.

Fluid loss will be under control below 5 by using Starch at 5 lb/bbl and MY‐PAC LV at 2 lb/bbl, pH
shall be controlled by consumption of Caustic Soda on range ±10. Due to hole going to be drilled
directionally and inclination is in high deviated it is recommended to keep lubricant, HYDRO‐LUBE
in active system at ±1% volume.


The 6 ⅛" hole section will be drilled from Liner shoe at ± 3,894 meters to an estimated well TD of ±
4,037 meters in Dariyan reservoir. This section will be drilled horizontally to the section TD. After
setting & cementing 7", drill out the shoe using mud recovered from the last interval.

Maximum volume of mud from the previous section will be recovered in order to re‐use it in this
section. Prior to drill out the cement in 7" Liner, the whole mud should be pre‐treated with 0.30
ppb of Sodium Bicarbonate.

CaCO3 will be consumed as bridging agent at concentration of 40 – 70 lb/bbl and then mud will be
weighted up by NaCl to reach to desired mud weight as per hole dictation. If the base fluid became
salt saturated and more mud weight has been dictated by hole condition then more concentration
of CaCO3 can be utilized to get it.

HYDRO‐ZAN will be used as base of Rheology to provide adequate YP and high low shear rate
which is the key factor to ensure adequate hole cleaning

HYDRO‐STAR NF at 5.0 ppb and MY‐PAC LV at 1.5 – 2.0 ppb will be used to control the API fluid loss
below 4 cc and keep the filter cake as thin as possible on the reservoir section.

2  HEALTH, SAFETY AND ENVIRONMENT


SAFETY GUIDELINES FOR THE HANDLING OF DRILLING FLUIDS

POTENTIAL RISK AREAS

There are four main areas where personnel may be exposed to drilling mud and chemical
additives:

• Sack room and mud mixing hopper
• Shale shaker house
• Mud tank area
• Rig floor

Sack Room and Mud Mixing Hopper

Bulk chemicals are either pumped or blown into silos on the installation from the supply ship or
crane lifted on, via palletized sack or drum stored loads. Silo stored materials are added directly to
the mud via the mixing hopper but sack or drum stored material is generally added by hand at the
mixing hopper. Dust levels can be high during mixing and when the empty containers are
removed.

Shale Shaker House

Hot muds are fed onto vibrating screen sieves (shale shaker) which are usually open to the
atmosphere. Mud vapors may condense to form a mist in the shaker house. The mechanical
agitation of the shakers  will also generate mist  directly. Personnel exposed here include
roustabouts who maintain and clean up the equipment and the mud engineer, geologist and
service hands who take sample. Considerable exposure may occur when changing shale shaker
screens, especially on the older designs of shale shaker.

Mud Tank Area

After cuttings removal the mud returns to the active mud pit. It is cooler than at the shaker house
but vaporization condensation still occurs. Personnel exposed here are generally roustabouts who
maintain and clean equipment and the mud engineer and derrick man who may spend long
periods in this area taking samples and monitoring the mud condition.

Rig Floor

Drilling involves the making job of joints in the drill pipe with the result that mud gets splashed
aver the rig floor. Consequently contractor personnel can be exposing to substantial quantities of
mud. This may occur for the entire shift. There are of course other operation (involving different
personnel such as maintenance staff, laundry staff, waste disposal personnel), where exposure to
drilling fluids can occur, but these are relatively minor compared with those identified above.

ASSESSMENTS OF HAZARDS FROM DRILLING FLUIDS

The various categories of additives, base oil and well stimulation chemicals are considered for
their health aspect, toxicity and assessment of hazard. From this assessment the following points
are evident.

Weighting Agents/ Viscosifiers

The main mud additives, weighting agents and viscosifiers are not in themselves particularly
hazardous. However impurities such as respirable silica, fibers and lead may be cause for concern.
Experimental data has indicated that the addition of powdered material to the mud at the mixing
bay in the absence of local exhaust ventilation, can lead to the Occupational Exposure Standard
(OES) for nuisance dust being easily exceeded. Consequently the OES's for impurities, which
sometimes have very much lower limits than dust, may also be exceeded.

Thinners and Dispersants

Thinners and dispersants may also contain impurities such as quartz. Additionally, chromates are
used which have very low OES'S. Worst of these is chromolignosulphonate containing chromium in
oxidation state VI which is a suspected human carcinogen. Scomi Oiltools has banned the use of
chromolignosulphonate, and  other chromate  based  additives  because  of  their  potential
carcinogenicity.

Emulsifiers

Primary and secondary emulsifiers and wetting agents contain solvent and base oil which may
have a high aromatic content. One study identified these as the major source of volatile aromatic
hydrocarbons in oil mist/vapor in the working environment even though low aromatics base oil
was used in the mud. The main aromatics found were ethylbenzene, xylene and benzene. Local
exposure standards were not exceeded. However, the oil industry’s move away from moderately
refined to severely refined dearomatised base oil is negated if many additives contain aromatics in
relatively high concentrations. SCOMI OILTOOLS has a range of oil based mud additives based on a
low aromatic solvent.

Flocculants

The free acrylamide content of flocculants containing polyacrylamide, if high (i.e. greater than
0.05%) could be a potential health hazard.

Biocides

Bactericides/biocides are designed to  kill micro‐organism and consequently are hazardous to
humans.  The generally very small quantities used reduce the risk of poisoning. However,
formaldehyde, which has been used widely, is a suspected human carcinogen and has extremely
low OES.

Brine

Brines and well stimulation fluids are generally hazardous in respect of their corrosive action and

Base oils

Base oils when severely refined to reduce the levels of aromatics below approximately 3% are
unlikely to pose a health threat to workers. Most of the mist and vapour liberated from mud is
water vapor. Saturated hydrocarbons (iso‐alkanes and cycloalkanes) are likely to constitute the
main hydrocarbons in oil mist and vapour.



Storage, Housekeeping and Return of Chemicals

Generally sack rooms are never large enough for the amount of chemicals required to run a
modern mud system. This is especially true at the end of a section, when new chemicals also come
on board for the next section. Burst sacks should be put into plastic haversacks and sealed, and
either immediately disposed off or stored so they can be used at the first opportunity. This will be
dependant an the extent of the damage and possible degradation of product quality.  Spillage of
powders should be vacuumed up or hosed away immediately. Spillage of liquids should be hosed
away if non hazardous, or handled using a spill kit with absorbent booms, pads etc. These spillage
kits should be placed in the sack room and hopper area. An area of the sack room should be
changed off, and very hazardous chemicals like caustic and biocides stored in this segregated area.
The state of the contingency chemicals such as loss circulation materials should be checked
regularly and they should be replaced if necessary. It is a good idea to store these in jungle boxes.

Pallets of chemicals should not be stored more than three high. The use of a conveyor to move
chemicals from the sack room to the mixing hopper could be considered as  it would reduce
damage due to forklift handling. Chemicals being returned at the end of a section should be
placed carefully in containers, and immobilised. A safety net at the door must be used. Any
damaged sacks or drum must be correctly and easily identified and noted on the manifest. All
empty drums must be disposed off in the waste skips at the rig site and not be put into containers
for return. Unless specific arrangements have been made with the customer damaged sacks
should be disposed off at the rig site and not retained onshore.

Emergency Provisions

Each Material Safety Data Sheet (MSDS) will state emergency first aid actions in case of chemical
contamination of the skin or eyes, or exposure by ingestion or inhalation. The medic must always
be consulted  if such exposure occurs.  There should be eyewashes in the sack room, mixing
hopper area, shaker house and pit room and a drench shower at the mixing hopper area.

Environmental Aspects

Most of the research carried out on the toxicology of drilling fluids has been with respect to
marine environment rather than worker exposures. There is considerable debate concerning the
long term environmental affects of drilling fluids, and in particular oil based muds, on marine life.

As a result, the level of oil on drilling cuttings which have been allowed to be discharged to the sea

setting discharge limits and the result has also been exerting steady pressure to reduce the use of
oil based muds. This will obviously have an effect on the industrial hygiene aspects of drilling
fluids.


GUIDELINES FOR THE SAFE USE OF OIL BASED MUDS

Introduction

Due to their unique drilling properties, oil muds have been used by operations in demanding
drilling situations for over forty years. The traditional fluid phase of these muds is diesel oil.

3.8   MIXING PROCEDURE


Pre-Hydrated Bentonite

Drill water with chloride less than 1000 mg/L would be used to prepare High viscous pill:

    1.   Fill the pit with Drill Water.
    2. Add 1.5 ppb of Soda Ash to treat out any hardness and get Hardness 100 mg/L.
    3. Add 0.75 ppb of Caustic Soda to have a constant pH in the pit.
    4. Add 50 ‐ 60 ppb of Bentonite and let to hydrate and agitate for at least 6 hrs to reach to
       the maximum Yield.
    5.  Dilute system with sea water to get Bentonite at average concentration of 25 lb/bbl, this
     way will help to minimize the requirement of drill water.

High viscous XC-Polymer

    1.   Fill the pit with sea water.
    2. Add 0.25 ppb of Soda Ash to treat out any hardness below 100 mg/L.
    3. Add 0.25 ppb of Caustic Soda to have a constant pH in the pit.
    4. Add ±2.0 ppb of XC‐Polymer.

4.10 PROPERTIES DISCUSSION

Mud Weight

   In the initial mixing, salt would be the ﬁrst weighting agent up to 68‐70 pcf and then use BARITE
  to increase the weight to desired as per hole condition dictates. That is valid unless the hole
  condition dictates otherwise. Controlling of MW could be done by running SCE and dressing the
  shakers with finest mesh screens & dumping the sand trap and dilution.

Plastic Viscosity

   Plastic viscosity should be maintained as low as possible by extensive use of solids control
  equipment, coupled with whole new mud dilution.

API Fluid Loss Control

  Aghajari and Mishan clays are un‐permeable whoever it is recommended that the API fluid loss
  be maintained at range below 8 ml/30 min at all the time. This may best be achieved by the
  addition of HYDRO‐STAR NF at 5.0 ppb and MY‐PAC LV at 1‐2.0 ppb.

Rheology


  Maintain the Yield point around 20 to ensure adequate hole cleaning. HYDRO‐ZAN will be used
   to provide an optimized rheological profile with elevated low‐shear‐rate‐viscosity and high
   shear‐thinning characteristics.

LCM Pill Spotting Procedure

   1.  After spotting LCM pill, pull the bit above the calculated top of the pill. Wait 30 minutes and
     monitor the annulus.

   2.  After 30 minutes, attempt to ﬁll the annulus, if not already full.  If the hole can be filled, close
      the BOP and squeeze 10 bbls of LCM pill into the formation without exceeding 200 psi.

   3.   If the hole remains stable, continue to drill ahead.

   4.  Should the LCM pill fail to reduce the losses or the hole cannot be filled, then mix and spot
      another 50 bbls of LCM pill.



   5.  Attempt to ﬁll the annulus.  If successful, close the BOP and squeeze 20 bbls of LCM pill into
      the formation without exceeding 200 psi.  If a squeeze pressure develops, hold the pressure
      for 1 hour before staging the drill string back to bottom.

       displacing any LCM pill.

5.3  FORMATION TO DRILL


Gachsaran: Age: Lower – Middle Miocene

Lithology: Mostly consists of white, light grey anhydrite layers and red marl interbedded with grey marl
and Lime stone. Grey and red marls are highly sticky.

Lithology: This evaporitic formation in type section consists of 7 members and 42 key beds, but in
this area it is almost impossible to separate all the members.

Member 7‐4: Mostly consists of white, light grey anhydrite layers and red marl interbedded with
grey marl and Lime stone. Grey and red marls are highly sticky.

Member 4‐2: Mostly consists of halite, red and grey marl with possible thin layers of carbonates
(Member 3).
Member 2: Consists of Halite with minor red marl and an‐hydrate, due to the plastic flow property
of Gachsaran formation, thickness of this member will change rapidly.

5.4  DISCUSSION


The 12¼" hole section will be drilled from 13 ⅜" casing shoe at ± 1,800 meters to an estimated
section TD of 2,300 meters. After setting & cementing 13 ⅜” casing, drill out the shoe & cement
using mud recovered from the last interval and displace the mud system to fresh salt saturated
polymer mud at 115 pcf.

Whilst drilling, mix Salt Saturated polymer in the reserve pits as per formulation and dilute system
to maintain low gravity solids below 7%. Reduce dilution volumes by optimizing screen size to
ensure +/‐ 80% of the screens, are covered by the drilling fluid. Shear premixes volumes using gun
lines & shearing unit to avoid losses over the shakers.

Increase mud weight as dictated by hole condition. Sea water will be weighted up to 75 pcf by
adding NaCl salt and then by addition of Barite to 120 pcf as per hole dictates. Mud weight of 130
pcf may be required prior to TD of the interval as Salt water flows are expected.

Salt will be used up to the Saturation level (110 ppb) to prevent the thin inter bedded layers of
Salt being washed out and act as a strong shale inhibitor in the system.

Drill ahead maintaining the mud property  specifications by  optimizing the  solids  control
equipment and judicious dilution with pre‐mix where applicable to generate new volume. It is
essential to remove drilled solids rather than dilute them to acceptable levels.


Maintain API ﬂuid loss below 4.0 ml by addition of HYDRO‐STAR NF at 5 ppb & MY‐PAC LV at 2.0
ppb. A tight API fluid loss reduces the possibility of coming differentially stuck when running mud
weights at 130 pcf. As less drilled solids are introduced into the system the fluid will have a more
control able API fluid loss.

In case of high viscosity problems, mud would be formulated with HYDRO‐SPERSE CF at 1.0 ppb, a
specially formulated lignosulphonate compound, which is chrome free. HYDRO‐SPERSE CF is used
as deflocculant and fluid loss reducing agent in virtually all water base mud system. HYDRO‐
SPERSE CF is intended for use as a deflocculant and fluid loss additive in all types of water base
systems. HYDRO‐SPERSE CF shows superior deflocculating ability in the presence of contaminants
and increasing temperature.

5.5  SOLIDS CONTROL MANAGEMENT

  ª Shale shakers screens will be the prime means of removing drilled solids. Maintenance of a
         drilling fluid weighted up above 120 pcf using the centrifuges  is limited due to the
        restrictions on the processing volumes of equipments. Hence it is crucial to dress up shaker
       screens as quickly as possible and replace torn screens immediately.

  ª There may be a requirement to dress the shakers with coarser screens for a number of
        circulations in order to minimize loss of mud and to allow full dispersion of hydratable
       polymers.

  ª Ensure shakers are running immediately prior to and during all periods of circulation.

  ª Use all the solids control equipment available on the rig site. The combination of linear
      motion shale shaker with centrifuge proved best results. Both Centrifuges should be run at
          all the time at maximum flow rate on Barite recovery mode to remove low gravity solids
      and keep mud weight & properties in range.

  ª Constant monitoring of the underflow should determine the efficiency of this equipment.
         All eﬀorts should be made to maintain the LGS below 7%.

     ª Deﬂocculants are more effective.

  Measuring of the MBT is the direct indication of formation reactivity. MBT of range of < 10 ppb
    is acceptable. Above this range, mud Rheology will start to shoot up to uncontrollable levels.
  Using fine screens & high speed centrifuges will help in maintaining lower values of MBT by
   discarding the fine solids and consequently minimize the dilution rate.

Chloride


  The chloride content of saturated salt muds is 190,000 mg/l (315,000 mg/L NaCl) at saturation.
  As the temperature of the mud increases, more salt is able to go into solution. This means that
  a ﬂuid which  is saturated under surface conditions may not be saturated at down hole
  temperatures and can cause substantial washout in a salt zone due to increased salt solubility.

Drilling Salt

Drilling in salt and evaporate sequences is a common condition in many parts of the world.
Because salt and evaporate sequences are impermeable, they form an excellent cap rock for
hydrocarbon reservoirs. In addition, the salt, being lighter than surrounding rocks, tends to rise up,
producing dome structures which are ideal traps for hydrocarbons. Sodium, calcium, magnesium
and potassium salts can dissolve into the water phase of a drilling fluid system and result in large
well bore washouts. This can cause two significant problems; hole cleaning and cementing. Other
problems include salt "squeezing" or "salt creeping" and saltwater influxes that require high mud
weights to control.

Causes: A major problem with salt formations is their solubility in the water phase of drilling fluids.
This is further complicated by: Mixed salts, Plastic salts, Salt water influxes, Sub salt shales,
Washing out halite and anhydrite salt formations can occur even with mud systems saturated with
sodium chloride. Although on surface these systems are saturated, under down hole conditions
the temperature of the mud  is higher and the concentration of  salt drops  slightly below
saturation. This means that more salt can dissolve, leading to well bore washouts. Once the hole
has begun to washout, annular velocities decrease and hole cleaning problems such as torque,
drag and packing off may develop. It is more difficult to get a good cement job in a washed out
formation, and this is especially important when drilling a plastic salt formation. When the mud
system cools on surface, the formation salts dissolved down hole may precipitate out. These salts
can be abrasive and a nuisance to drilling operations by reducing the life of pump parts and other
rig equipment, as well as blinding shaker screens.

Mixed Salts: Evaporate sections can contain very soluble potassium and magnesium salts as well
as the common sodium and calcium salts, halite and anhydrite.

Conventional drilling fluids for salt sections are usually saturated only with sodium chloride, but
due to the different solubility’s they still dissolve the potassium and magnesium salts, leading to
large over gauge sections and washouts. One of the best known examples of a complex evaporate
section is the Zech stein formation, found in the Southern North Sea and Northern Europe.

Plastic Salts: Salt formations are sometimes in a plastic state and can be "squeezed" into the well
bore by the pressure of overlying formations, causing the drill string to become stuck. However,
the most significant problem can occur after the well has been drilled. If the hole is washed out
and the casing is poorly cemented, "plastic" salt formations can squeeze in causing the casing to
collapse. Many wells have been lost this way. Improvements in casing design and mud technology
have reduced casing collapse incidents considerably in recent years.

Saltwater Influxes: Influxes of various salt waters and brines from formations can have a very
detrimental effect on fluid properties. These influxes are invariably pressured, requiring high mud
densities to stabilize them.

Sub salt Shales: In the Gulf of Mexico, there is often a zone below the salt formation known as the
"rubble" or "rafted" zone. This consists of an unconsolidated formation, destabilized by the
intrusion of the salt above, which will tend to fall into the well bore. It is very important to remove
this rubble efficiently to prevent packing off and mechanically stuck pipe. If the salt zone above is
washed out, removing this debris becomes a lot more difficult with much higher flow rates
required to clean the hole than in gauge or near‐gauge holes.

Other problems can be foaming and carbonate contamination from anhydrite sequences.
Hydration  of polymers  in saturated  salt systems  is  difficult and  requires higher product
concentrations and subsequent higher fluid costs.

Preventing and Curing Salt Problems: An invert emulsion is the most feasible system to choose if
economic and environmental concerns allow. It is much easier to prevent washout of the salt
section with an oil‐based or synthetic‐based fluid. Water‐based systems have to be saturated to
prevent washout. Use either sodium chloride for drilling halite or anhydrite sequences, or a
mixture of magnesium chloride and potassium chloride salts for drilling mixed salt formations. Salt
inhibitors will prevent re‐crystallization of salts on surface and allow super saturation of the
drilling fluid, keeping it from falling below saturation down hole. If plastic salts are a problem,
increased mud weight may be required to stabilize the formation. Initial stress relief after the salt
is drilled is common. If the drill string becomes stuck, a freshwater pill can be pumped to dissolve
the salt. The pill should be large enough to cover the drill collars with enough excess left to cover
the entire bottom hole assembly (BHA) for several hours while jarring the pipe. It should be noted
that massive halite dissolution in water is slow and enough time (3‐4 hours) should be allowed for
the pill to work. Allow the water to soak around the BHA, pumping only a few strokes every hour
or half‐hour to move the pill slightly. If saltwater influxes are a possible hazard, enough weight
material should be kept at the rig site to allow a rapid increase in mud weight. Water flows can
require densities 3 lbs/gal greater than the salt formation would require under normal conditions.

6.10 PROPERTIES DISCUSSION


Mud Weight

  Controlling of MW could be done between 75‐110 as per hole dictates by adding Calcium
  Carbonate & running SCE and dressing the shakers with finest mesh screens & dumping the
  sand trap and dilution.

Plastic Viscosity

   Plastic viscosity should be maintained as low as possible by extensive use of solids control
  equipment, coupled with whole new mud dilution.



API Fluid Loss

   It is recommended that the API ﬂuid loss be maintained at < 5 ml at all the time. This may best
  be achieved by the addition of HYDRO‐STAR NF at 5.0 ppb and MY‐PAC LV at 1.5 – 2.0 ppb.

Rheology

  Maintain the Yield point around 25 ‐ 30 to ensure adequate hole cleaning, making adjustment
  with fresh mud and use of centrifuges on LGS mode. HYDRO‐ZAN will be used at 1– 2 ppb to
   provide an optimized rheological profile.


Total Hardness

   Total hardness should be maintained below 600 mg/L for optimum polymer performance with
  the addition of Soda Ash and/or Caustic Soda.


pH and Alkalinity

  The pH should be maintained around 9.5 using Soda Ash and/or Caustic Soda.  This will
  maximize chemicals performance and minimize corrosion problems.


MBT


  Measuring of the MBT is the direct indication of formation reactivity. MBT of range of < 10 ppb
   is acceptable. Above this range, mud Rheology will start to shoot up to uncontrollable levels.
  Using fine screens & high speed centrifuges will help in maintaining lower values of MBT by
  discarding the fine solids and consequently minimize the dilution rate.

6.11  DRILLING ISSUES


Stuck Pipe


  Due to the inter‐bedded nature of the formations that will be penetrated, some reservoirs with
  natural pressure and others over pressured, differential sticking problems must be considered.
  The main actions to take in order to avoid differential sticking are tight fluid loss control, and
  continuous movement of the pipe.

  Adding different size of CALCIUM CARB also will decrease the risk of differential sticking. to
  minimize the possibility of differential sticking it is recommended to add CALCIUM CARB 50 to
  the system from the beginning of this section, adding different size of Calcium Carbonate will
  help to drill into fractured limestone of Qom with a low possible of having stuck pipe.

   In the event of stuck pipe, BREAK‐FREE NW can be spotted around the stuck area. For more
  information refer to Stuck Pipe Appendix.



Hole Cleaning


  The XC‐Polymer is the base for Rheology in the KCl polymer system. If additional Rheology is
  required, increase XC‐Polymer concentration to enhance hole cleaning. The driving parameter
  that shows the hole cleaning is the 6 and 3 readings. The 6 reading has to be over 7 all time to
  ensure good hole cleaning. Scomi Oiltools HYPR‐CALC software can be used to determine the
  transport ratio at any hole section.

   Prior to connections, the cuttings should be circulated well above the bottom hole assembly to
  avoid packing off after connections. The hole should be circulated clean before starting to POOH
  or back reaming.

1.  Forget the Bottoms Up calculations based on hole geometry and pump rate. It will take at least
   100% excess circulating to get bottoms up; probably more.

2. Pump as fast as possible. This may require running larger jets, larger liners, and minimizing
   pressure losses inside the drill string.

3.  Spinning drill pipes. Larger drill pipe allows higher AV's by minimizing pressure losses inside the
     drill string and by providing a smaller annular area. In some areas 6 5/8" drill pipe is used.
   Most derricks that can hold 5" DP can also accommodate 5 1/2".

4. The reciprocation & rotation of the drill string, along with proper rheology, will normally
   improve hole cleaning. Also, it seems that rotation of the drill string more effectively removes
    cuttings in a viscous mud vs. a mud where viscosity & gels are insufficient.

   made after the standard viscosity determinations have been compiled with the viscometer.
    After you have determined the last viscosity (3 rpm), shut the viscometer off and measure the
   time it takes for the dial to fall back to zero. Fluids which exhibit an extended relaxation time
    indicate improved hole cleaning and suspension characteristics.

Foaming

  Foaming is generally restricted to surface foam and is not troublesome unless aggravated by
  mechanical agitation. The degree of foaming may sometimes be decreased by increasing the
   alkalinity of the mud (Pm). A de‐foaming agent may be necessary.

Lubricity

   readily biodegradable lubricant and ROP enhancer providing lubricity and thus increased

7.10 PROPERTIES DISCUSSION


Mud Weight

  Base ﬂuid at 75 pcf which has been weighted up non‐solid will be weighted up as per hole
  dictation by CaCO3. Controlling of MW could be done around 94 pcf as per hole dictates by
  adding Calcium Carbonate & running SCE and dressing the shakers with finest mesh screens &
  dumping the sand trap and dilution.

Plastic Viscosity

   Plastic viscosity should be maintained as low as possible by extensive use of solids control
  equipment, coupled with whole new mud dilution.

API Fluid Loss

   It is recommended that the API fluid loss be maintained at < 4 ml at all the time. This may best
  be achieved by the addition of HYDRO‐STAR NF at 5.0 ppb and MY‐PAC LV at 2.0 ppb.

Rheology

  Maintain the Yield point around 18 ‐ 25 to ensure adequate hole cleaning, making adjustment
  with fresh mud and use of centrifuges on LGS mode. HYDRO‐ZAN will be used at 1.0 – 2.0 ppb to
  provide an optimized rheological profile.


Total Hardness

    Total hardness should be maintained below 600 mg/L for optimum polymer performance with
   the addition of Soda Ash and/or Caustic Soda.


pH and Alkalinity

   The pH should be maintained around 9.5 to 10.0 using Soda Ash and/or Caustic Soda. This will
   maximize chemicals performance and minimize corrosion problems.


MBT

   Measuring of the MBT is the direct indication of formation reactivity. MBT of range of < 8 ppb
     is acceptable. Above this range, mud Rheology will start to shoot up to uncontrollable levels.
   Using fine screens & high speed centrifuges will help in maintaining lower values of MBT by
    discarding the fine solids and consequently minimize the dilution rate.

7.11  DRILLING ISSUES


Stuck Pipe

  The main actions to take in order to avoid differential sticking are tight fluid loss control, and
  continuous movement of the pipe.

  Adding different size of CALCIUM CARB also will decrease the risk of differential sticking. To
  minimize the possibility of diﬀerential sticking it is recommended to add CALCIUM CARB 50 to
  the system from the beginning of this section, adding different size of Calcium Carbonate will
  help to drill into fractured limestone of Qom with a low possible of having stuck pipe.

   In the event of stuck pipe, BREAK‐FREE NW can be spotted around the stuck area. For more
  information refer to Stuck Pipe Appendix.

Hole Cleaning


  The XC‐Polymer is the base for Rheology in the KCl polymer system. If additional Rheology is
  required, increase XC‐Polymer concentration to enhance hole cleaning. The driving parameter
  that shows the hole cleaning is the 6 and 3 readings. The 6 reading has to be over 7 all time to
  ensure good hole cleaning. Scomi Oiltools HYPR‐CALC software can be used to determine the
  transport ratio at any hole section.

   Prior to connections, the cuttings should be circulated well above the bottom hole assembly to
  avoid packing off after connections. The hole should be circulated clean before starting to POOH
  or back reaming.

1.  Forget the Bottoms Up calculations based on hole geometry and pump rate. It will take at least
   100% excess circulating to get bottoms up; probably more.

2. Pump as fast as possible. This may require running larger jets, larger liners, and minimizing
   pressure losses inside the drill string.

3.  Spinning drill pipes. Larger drill pipe allows higher AV's by minimizing pressure losses inside the
     drill string and by providing a smaller annular area. In some areas 6 5/8" drill pipe is used.
   Most derricks that can hold 5" DP can also accommodate 5 1/2".

4. The reciprocation & rotation of the drill string, along with proper rheology, will normally
   improve hole cleaning. Also, it seems that rotation of the drill string more effectively removes
    cuttings in a viscous mud vs. a mud where viscosity & gels are insufficient.

5. You might consider the relationship between the pump flow rate & BHA pulling speed when
   making a wiper trip. Relatively slow pulling speeds and high pump rates will normally assist in
   enhancing hole cleaning. Also any cuttings bed remaining when pulling at higher speeds is
    thicker than the bed left when pulling at a slower speed, for the same pump flow rate.

8  COMPLETION PHASE


8.1    DISSCUSSION


Clear,  solids‐free brines are the most commonly used  fluids  in completion and workover
operations to minimize formation damage whilst perforation job and completion phase to
improve production rate.

Completion and workover fluids are specialized fluids used during well completion operations and
remedial workover procedures. These fluids must control not only subsurface pressure with
density, but also must minimize formation damage during completion and workover operations.
The use of fluids that cause minimal formation damage can result in dramatically improved
production. Most reservoirs are sensitive to any fluids other than those contained in them
naturally.

Before complete the well OPTA‐ZYME S which is an Alpha‐amylase designed to remove polymer
damage caused by the starch based products used in the OPTA‐FLO Drill‐in Fluid system primarily
in horizontal and multilateral wells. As the starch is broken down by the enzymes the bridging
agents present in the filter cake will be readily dispersed and may even be removed by additional
treatments. All enzyme treatments should customized in the laboratory before use in the field.

OPTA‐ZYME S can be used to break down the starch components of filter cakes containing salt and
or calcium carbonate as bridging agents. Applicable in water base and monovalent brines including
saturated sodium chloride solutions.

All surface pits need to be cleaned and ﬂush all lines, then prepare CaCl2/ CaBr2 Inhibited brine to
displace hole to be ready for completion the well.

Corrosion: Salt solutions are often highly corrosive. Dissolved oxygen is the primary corrosive
agent in sodium‐, potassium‐, calcium‐chloride‐ or bromide‐brine base completion fluids. The
solubility of oxygen in these brines decreases as saturation with the salt is approached. Anyhow
corrosion inhibitor should to be add in completion fluid to minimize the risk of corrosion.

pH: Corrosion is accelerated in acidic environments (pH <7) and retarded in alkaline conditions (pH
>7) . As the pH (pH = ‐log [H+]) is increased, the concentration of H+ will be reduced, which may
slow the corrosion rate. Conversely, decreasing the pH increases the concentration of H+ ions,
which increases the cathodic reaction and may increase the corrosion rate (acids tend to dissolve
metals faster).

Normally, a pH of 9.5 to 10.5 is adequate to mitigate most corrosion. In some cases, a pH as high
as 12 may be required. High pH values (>10.5) neutralize acid gases and lower the solubility of
corrosion products.

10 HORIZONTAL DRILLING


Horizontal drilling has increased worldwide. It is used to revive production rates in old fields and
reduce the required number of wells in new fields. Drilling horizontal wells is more complicated
than drilling vertical or near vertical wells; therefore the pre‐well planning phase is more involved.
The following areas should be considered when preparing a drilling fluids program:  Reservoir
Protection, Borehole  Stability, Hole  Cleaning,  Lubricity,  Solids  Control, and Environmental
Regulations.

Reservoir Protection: Whenever a  reservoir  is  drilled overbalanced,  especially a depleted
reservoir, there will inevitably be invasion of the formation by mud filtrate and whole mud. This
can cause considerable damage and reduce the production rate (and profitability) of the well.
Fluid invasion is more critical in long open hole reservoir sections. Adequate return permeability
work should be performed on proposed drilling fluids to ensure the formation is adequately
protected.

Borehole Stability: Instability can be induced chemically, mechanically, or both. It is important to
determine the correct mud weight required to give mechanical stability when planning horizontal
wells. It should be noted that the required mud weight can be significantly higher than the mud
weight required drilling a vertical well in the same formation. Mechanical stability is dependent on
the  rock mechanics  of  the  borehole.  Excessive  annular  velocities can cause  erosion  of
unconsolidated formations. Under balanced drilling can also cause well bore instability. The
chemical interaction between a reactive formation and the drilling fluid can also be the cause of
many problems. The types of formations that can give problems are swelling or dispersive
formations, such as shales. Shales weaken when in contact with poorly inhibitive mud’s. This
reduction of strength can lead to mechanical failure. Once a borehole has destabilized, re‐
stabilizing  it can be very difficult and expensive; therefore, effective pretreatment  is always
recommended.

Hole Cleaning: It is important to realize that the horizontal section is not the most difficult to
clean. Problems in hole cleaning usually occur when the hole angle is between 40° and 60°, in the
build section of the well, further up the annulus, or when sliding / steering. Hole cleaning in
horizontal well bores can be summarized as:

   •  Turbulent flow is preferred over laminar flow.
   •  Viscous fluids/sweeps do not increase, and can actually decrease, flow rates below the drill
        string where the cuttings beds accumulate.

To clean these cuttings beds will require mechanical agitation by the pumping of low viscosity
sweeps and by pipe rotation. The low viscosity sweep is often followed by a high density pill to
assist in "floating" cuttings out of the hole. FIBRO‐SEAL pills are another technique which has been
used to follow low viscosity sweeps.

The well bore should be circulated clean on a regular basis.

Lubricity: Drilling torque is usually high, caused by a high degree of drill pipe contact with the well
bore and casing. Cuttings accumulation on the low side adds to the torque, and can frequently be
seen as drag on trips out of the hole and on connections. Maintaining adequate lubricity can be a
problem for medium to long radius wells. Torque and drag are also seen if a formation is not
sufficiently inhibited and is swelling or heaving. Maintaining a system in as clean a state as
possible will aid in keeping torque and drag values to a minimum. Lubricants treatments can help
maintain the ability to slide for directional needs. Torque and drag problems must be identified
before the appropriate lubricant treatment can be recommended.

Solids Control: Solids control is critical in high angle wells, because solids can rapidly build up. This
is because build‐up is mechanically induced by:

∙ "Mortar and Pestle" effect of drill pipe grinding on the low side of the hole.
∙ Longer transportation time of cuttings from bit to surface
∙ Borehole and cuttings erosion in turbulent flow
∙ Bit design coupled with high RPM mud motors.

                     Preventing and Curing Horizontal Drilling Problems

Reservoir Damage: Tight fluid loss control and additions of bridging materials to the mud system
will reduce reservoir invasion by mud and filtrate. OPTA‐FLO DRILL‐IN of fluid offers performance
and production zone protection to prevent invasion and damage to the formation.

Borehole Stability: Correct mud weight and adequate inhibition are the keys to borehole stability
in horizontal wells. In unconsolidated formations, annular velocities may need to be controlled.

Pipe Corrosion:

Run Corrosion Coupons in each hole section, where there will be a minimum of 3 days exposure.
Retrieve Corrosion Coupons and send to Scomi Oiltools laboratory for testing and analysis.
