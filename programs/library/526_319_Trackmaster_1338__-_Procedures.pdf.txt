BEFORE RUNNING THE PACK-STOCK

1     Perform a gauge and scraper run. It is recommended that this be done with the
       following assembly:

       Bit or Window Mill
      Casing Scraper
      2 x Watermelon Mills (or stabs)
    MWD***
        Drill Collars
        Jar
        Drill Pipe

      The above assembly closely resembles the stiffness of the Trackmaster assembly.

Note: Alternatively, a drilling assembly (omitting the scraper & Mills) utilising full gauge
        stabilisers may be used for clean out. This may be prudent in a case where a
        substantial footage of cement requires drilling out.

      * Depending upon the type of MWD system that is to be used, it may also be prudent
        to evaluate the hydraulic impact of the MWD at the operating flow rates. It is then
       possible to alter any system aspect prior to running the Packstock.

Note:  It is strongly recommended that if possible the scraper be run past setting depth by say
      50 Ft then pulled back and circulation commenced. This is to ensure any debris is
      pushed below the setting depth and not circulated back up past the scraper.

      The Trackmaster can be set at any depth. The Trackmaster is set hydraulically, so
        there is no requirement to interact with a cement plug or bridge plug to set the
       whipstock. As with any whipstock sidetrack, the Trackmaster should be set between
      2 casing collars. This is to ensure integrity between the anchoring packer and the
       milling / and drilling assemblies.

 2     Historically a CCL was used to accurately place the Trackmaster with reference to a
        collar; it is now more usual to rely on the casing tally. Should a CCL be used,
        correlation between drill pipe and wireline depth is necessary.


THE MUD SYSTEM

3     High viscosity "milling mud" is not required when Trackmaster side-tracking.

ORIENTATION OF THE PACK-STOCK

4    An MWD Orientation Multi Cycle By-Pass Valve will be used.


OTHER REQUIREMENTS

5       Install ditch magnets. (Protects the pumps from re-circulating cuttings)

6     Approximately 20 gallons of hydraulic oil will be required on rig floor to fill up the
       Trackmaster and Running Tool.

7     Test the By-pass Valve:
       Pick up the following assembly:
       Kelly Cock (closed)
       Multi Cycle By-pass
    MWD Tool
        Drill Pipe

     Run in the hole 3 - 5 stands.
      The Multi Cycling By-Pass Valve will be configured to cycle at 300 - 350gpm.
       Bring up the pumps slowly and assess the minimum flow rate required to activate the
    MWD. Record the parameters. Cycle the pumps with 350 Gpm five times to operate
       the valve. On the sixth cycle bring the pumps up slowly and record the flow rate
       required to close the valve. Once complete, pull back and remove the kelly cock and
      make up the Trackmaster assembly

       Note: When the pressure is bled off after testing the MCBPV will automatically return
        to the open position and is ready to run.


       Safety note: ensure the driller is aware that when the by-pass valve closes he will
      be pumping against a closed valve.

PACK-STOCK RUNNING PROCEDURE

1    Make up following assembly:

       Trackmaster whip and packer
       Trackmaster mill
       Trackmaster running tool
      1x Joint 6 5/8" HWDP                  Customer / Rig supplied.
       Multi Cycle By-Pass Valve
    MWD Tool
      12 x HWDP
        Drill Pipe to surface

      Note: Jars should not be run in this assembly.


2     Pick-up Trackmaster

       Pick up the Trackmaster with a tugger from catwalk. A certified lifting eye is at the
      back of the whipstock. Place the sling around the packer, not around the protector
        sleeve. Set the Trackmaster in the rotary table (rams shut). Leave the tugger
       connected to the certified lifting eye until the whip has been bolted to the mill.


3     Bolt the mill to the Trackmaster Assembly

        Fit in the mill jets and knock off plugs, check they are secure and tight.
       Bolt the whip face to the mill head.
      Connect the hydraulic hose between the whip and mill


4      Fill Running Tool with Hydraulic Oil

     Run the Trackmaster Assembly below the rotary table to the top of running tool.
      Break the connection and top-up with hydraulic oil allowing any air to escape. Fill up
       the oil level to 15" from the top of the tool. Insert the floating piston and make up
       connection again. The floating piston is stored in the toolbox for shipping.

5     Orient Whip Face to MWD Tool

6     Pick up the assembly and run in the hole

       Pick up the BHA and run in the hole slowly (2 minutes slack off time for 1 stand).
       Refer to survey information for doglegs and their position.
      Observe the weight indicator carefully and record up-weights regularly.
      Monitor the trip-tank for proper fill-up. Extreme caution should be taken at the well
       head, casing crossovers etc.

7     Position the Trackmaster assembly at the required setting depth.

8     Orientate and set the Trackmaster with the MWD.

         Fill the pipe slowly (10 spm). Record the up and down weights.
       Slack off to neutral weight at setting depth.
      Once at setting depth, begin circulating to obtain MWD toolface survey. Turn pumps
        off and turn the assembly as required to obtain desired orientation and re-survey.
      Once correctly oriented, cycle the pumps to achieve the closed position on the Multi
      Cycle By-pass Valve. Once the valve is closed reduce the pump rate and slowly
       pressure up to 3,500 psi. Hold and maintain this pressure while going through the
        setting and shearing procedures.

Note: High pressures may be experienced during the setting sequence. All personnel
      must be made aware and all necessary precautions taken.

9     Confirm Packer Set.
       Slack off 10-15k lb. and overpull 10-15k lb. to confirm the packer setting.

10    Shear the Whip / Mill Bolt
       Pick-up 50,000 lb. overpull and shear the bolt. Monitor the pressure drop as the
       hydraulic hose ruptures.

11    Determine and record the free rotating torque.
      At this point the multi cycle bypass valve will have returned to the open position after
       shearing off from the whip. The pumps will have to be cycled to allow the piston to
       close and all flow to go through the millhead. The valve will close on the 6th pumps-
      on cycle when a significant pressure increase will be noticed. Do not switch off the
      pumps but reduce the flow rate to an acceptable pressure level. Proceed to step 12
       without stopping pumping.

12    Lower the assembly until milling torque is observed.
     As the mill contacts the whip face the knock off plugs in the millhead will be sheared
      and a greater flow area exposed. A significant reduction in pressure will be observed.
       Increase the flow rate for milling.

      Flow rate                                          650-750 Gpm
      Recommended weight on mill:                          6,000 -15,000 lbs.,
      Recommended maximum weight on mill:                25,000 lbs.
      Recommended rotary speed:                         80 - 150 rpm

       Mill down the whip face then continue milling the rathole as required.
       30ft is a standard length, which consists of 15ft on the whip/window and up to
       15ft of formation.


13    Milling Completed

      Check the passage of the mills through the window with different parameters,
       recording any drag and torque observed.
        1.     Rotating with pumps on.
        2.    No rotation with pumps on.
        3.    No rotation and no pumps on.

       Circulate bottoms up with the mill above the window then pull out of the hole. A
      Formation Integrity Test, if required, can be performed prior to pulling out of the
        hole.

14    Out of the hole.

       Assess the mill condition to evaluate if a remedial clean up trip is required.

      Allowable mill wear across the 3 mills is:
       3/8" under gauge on the lead and follow mill and
       1/4" under gauge on the watermelon mill.

       Factors affecting the mill wear such as formation type and the length of rat hole
        drilled should be taken into consideration.


15     Drilling Assembly

     Make up and run in the hole with the required drilling assembly to drill ahead.
