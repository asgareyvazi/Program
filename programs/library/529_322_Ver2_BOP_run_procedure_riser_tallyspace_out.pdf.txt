1.1.1 Running the BOP:



Purpose:



The purpose of this procedure is to ensure smooth and safe operation when running the BOP-
Stack on Riser.



Scope:



The following procedure describes the required activities, which need to be carried out prior
and during the running of the BOP- Stack on Riser.



Responsible Persons:



OIM / Toolpusher / Subsea Engineer / Driller / Crane Operator





Procedure:

Safety Precautions and Preparations:



    1.  Hold a Toolbox meeting. Review the procedure and JSA (Job Safety Analysis).
    2.  Acquire weather forecasts and confirm a suitable weather window is available for the
       operation.
    3. The rig is to be moved 30m off location while running the BOP.
    4.  Permit to work fully approved and signed with recommendations.
    5. The clear-call system between the rig floor and the moonpool is to be operational
       before the job begins.
    6. Work vests (correctly donned and secured) must be worn when working in or near the
       side of the moonpool.
    7. PPE worn at all times.
    8.  Safety harnesses attached to fall arrestors must be worn when working from the spider
      beams or from heights.
       Protectors must be correctly secured before any riser is removed from the catwalk.
    9.  Never run an air winch down through the rotary to the moonpool for man riding.
    10. All air hose connections must be secured with "R" clips and whipchecks
    11. Lifting gear fully inspected (check for appropriate colour coding, damage, malfunction,
        etc).
    12. All non essential personnel to keep clear of the drill floor, raised catwalk and riser
       storage areas.
    13. Stand clear in the moon pool when the riser is moving through the rotary.
    14. Ensure good communication between driller, POD reel operator and the crew working in
     moon pool.
    15. The BOP stack should not be run if vessel motion is outside operating limits for BOP
       handling, or weather window  is not sufficient to run and land the BOPs. Crane
        limitations are a maximum of 20 knots wind speed and maximum of 1.5 degrees (full
       amplitude) pitch and roll for handling the slip joint and landing joint.
    16. Serial numbers of the riser and the position of each joint of riser run will be recorded
       while running so that the riser record book can be updated.
    17. Record the string weight after each joint is run to assess the buoyancy condition.
    18. Ensure there is no build up of scale or loose debris in the riser prior to running

BOP / LMRP preparation to be carried out between wells:

During previous drilling phase the following checks to be done prior to running the BOP:



    1. Open all the ram bonnets and clean out the cavities.
    2.  Check wear of the bonnet, seal face and main body of rams.
    3.  Check for damage/wear of the ram piston rods and seals.
    4.  Check the ram rubbers for damage and change as required.
    5.  Pressure test all the BOP hydraulic actuators to 250psi and maximum operating pressure
       except the annular close functions which will only be tested with pipe in them to 250psi
      and 1500 psi.
    6. Change the bonnet gaskets. Oil the bonnet faces and close them. Torque the bolts to
       5000ft/lbs.
    7.  Visual checks on the annular preventers; change the elements as required.
    8.  Adjust the pre-charge pressure on the subsea accumulators including the annular surge
        bottles.
    9.  Check the pre-charge pressure on the surface accumulators’.
    10. Perform function test from both yellow & blue pods.
    11. Function test the wellhead and LMRP connectors and the kill and choke mini connectors.
    12. Operate the rams with 500 psi and check for easy of movement.

Riser Preparation:


       1.  Check the seal condition on all connections (riser, kill, and choke and booster line).
       2.  Check the riser seal-subs and pin and box ends for wear or damage.
       3.  Check the wellbore of the riser pins and boxes for wear or key seating.
       4.  Check riser buoyancy modules for cracks or damage. Ensure the retaining straps are
           tight and in good condition.
       5.  Ensure the buoyancy end stops are secure.
       6.  Check the condition of the POD hose clamps and hydraulic conduit clamps.



       7.  Check the riser telescopic joint packer record to ensure  it will hold for the total
         duration of the well - pressure test the same.
       8.  Check the diverter equipment and support ring.

Preparation:



    1.  Service and check the catwalk, drill floor and moonpool air hoists including the man
        riding winches and check the wires are in good condition.
    2.  Service the riser handling and riser test tools.
    3.  Grease the riser spider and check the gimbal hydraulic system.
    4. Have sufficient air built up in the air accumulator bottles.(Sub Sea to check)
    5.  Check/service all grub screws on the riser protectors.
    6.  Clear the rig floor of any equipment not needed for riser handling.
    7.  Clean and oil or grease the BOP transporter.
    8.  Assure that Position and Riser Angle Indication Beacons are charged and tested.

I) Run the BOP:


    1. Move the rig off the well head.
    2.  Lay down the mouse hole.
    3.  Inform the BCO and Central Control Room that the BOP is about to be transported to
       the well centre.
    4.  Skid the BOP to well centre with the BOP transporter. Unlock the wellhead connector
      from the skid test stump and retract the ring gasket retainers.
    5. Lower the retractable test-stump, remove the old ring gasket from the test stump,
       grease the connector and clean the ring grooves.
    6.  Install a new AX ring gasket on the test stump, extend the test-stump and lock the ring
       gasket retainers.

    7.  Grease the connector, clean the ring groove, remove the cover from the mandrel and
         install a new AX ring gasket.
    8.  Pick up a stand of drill pipe in the elevators and run it down through the BOP ensuring
       the tool joints are clear of the rams.
    9.  Perform a complete BOP function test on both pods. Pull the stand out of the BOP and
       function test the shear rams. Record the time taken and fluid volume for each function.
    10. Perform an EDS function test.(If applicable)
    11. Rack back the stand then change to the 500ton bails and elevators.
    12. Unlock the top drive pipe handler.
    13. Remove the rotary table insert bushings, master bushings and adaptor ring. Take
      measures to prevent personnel from falling through the open rotary.
    14. Install the gimbal, riser spider and control console in the rotary.
    15. Pick up the termination joint with the crane. Place it on the drill floor with the box end
      towards well centre, check the stabs and seals, change as required, oil/grease the stabs
       as required and set it in the spider. Remove the riser handling tool if used
    16. Put the first joint of riser and pick it up.
    17. Engage Hydraulic Running Tool (1500Psi max) or make up the running tool bolts
      (Cameron RF 7500ft/lbs).
    18. Set up reels for running BOP, centre all BOP functions at panel except for pod locks and
        select one pod and use this reel mini panel. Put Riser-Connector to latch, BOP connector

ii) Run Riser Joints from the Aft Side Pipe Deck:



    1.  Lift the riser joint from the storage rack onto the catwalk with the portside crane using a
      two leg spreader and tag lines.
    2.  Shackle the crane and the catwalk air winch to the forward end and a drill floor air
      winch to the aft end.(Air winch only required  if weather does not allow normal
       operation)

    3.  Ensure good communication between the crane operator and air winch driver with the

    4.  Pick up the joint with the crane and swing riser controlled towards catwalk, keeping it

    5.  Land the flange on the drill floor and remove the protector as required. Check the riser,

    6.  Install the hydraulic riser running tool and apply the required pressure (1500Psi), check

    7.  Rotate the elevators to face aft and  lift them with an air winch, at the same time

    8. Lower the elevators onto the riser handling tool and latch them securing the latch with
       rope.
    9. Remove the drill floor air winches from the riser joint and the elevators.
    10. Pick up the joint with the elevators slacking off on the catwalk winch, adjusting the
       height with the crane to bring the box end into the drill floor and continue picking up
        until the joint rests.
    11. Remove the crane and the catwalk air winch.
    12. Check the riser pins and boxes and the kill, choke and booster line stabs. Change any
      damaged seals and oil/grease the stabs as required. Install Riser Seal Sub in joint which
          is in the Spider, assure seals are in good condition and lightly oiled.
    13. Swing the joint into well centre, orientate the  kill, choke and booster line stabs as
       required and stab the joint.
    14. Slack off the full weight of the riser joint to ensure proper engagement. Make up the
       bolts (Cameron RD 7500ft-lbs).
    15. Take a test lift, re-torque the bolts and then take the weight, open the spider and inform
       the moonpool the string is ready to run in.
    16. Lower the string slowly and stop to install the clamps as required.
    17. Fill Kill and Choke lines while P/U the next joint of Riser.

       Pressure testing is defined as applying pressure to a sealed item of equipment for the purpose
        of testing the integrity.
    2.  The permit must describe the equipment to be pressure tested and its location on the rig. It is
       the duty of the permit controller and the persons involved in the task to check that the testing
       does not clash with other operations.
    3.  Hold a Toolbox meeting. Review the procedure, PTW and JSA.
    4.  Upon receipt of the permit, the rig site copy must be displayed at the work site, with the
       equipment testing procedure and JSA.
    5.  The equipment testing procedure and JSA should also be displayed at the pressure test pump.
    6.  Check the calibration and test certificates for the high-pressure pump unit to be used.
    7.  Ensure there is a PRV (pressure relief valve) on the test pump. Check the rating.
    8.  The OIM/ Tool Pusher will check the PRV certification and that  it  is set within the MWP
      (maximum working pressure) of the lowest rated piece of equipment.
    9.  Ensure all pumps in the system to be tested have been electrically and mechanically isolated. All
         local and remote controls will have “Do not operate: isolated equipment” signs displayed.
    10. Ensure any test pressure does not exceed the maximum working pressure of the equipment to
      be tested. Verify that the equipment to be tested is in a suitable condition to be tested and is fit
        for purpose.
    11. All temporary lines must be snubbed to suitable fastenings.
    12. All barriers will be in place and checked, to keep non essential personnel away from test area.
    13. The pressure testing medium will normally be water (seawater or fresh). If any other medium is
       used ensure  it  is safe to use under pressure and additional procedures are in place. Use
        additional PPE (personal protective equipment) as required.



Operation:




    1.  Ensure a permit to work has been made out for the task. This must be checked at shift changes.
    2.  Ensure barriers are in place to prevent non essential personnel entering the area. The pressure
       path from the test pump to the equipment to be tested must also be barriered as required.
    3.  Check all the connections have been correctly made up and the pressure lines have been
       snubbed to a suitable anchorage.
    4.  Ensure there is a relief path, should the pressure pass the equipment being tested.
    5.  Make a P.A announcement that pressure testing is about to commence. This announcement
      must specify the area the testing is taking place and all associated areas.

    6.  Instructions between the test pump operator and the equipment being tested must be clear and
        concise and there must be open lines of communication i.e. Radios or telephone.
    7.  The instructions given to the test pump operator must include the line up being employed, the
       equipment to be pressure tested and the pressure to be applied. It is important to specify the
       above items as it enables the test pump operator to annotate the test chart correctly and avoids
        confusion.
    8.  The test chart must be suitable for both low and high pressure tests (two pens or two chart
        recorders).
    9.  Flush the line to confirm the correct line up. If any above normal pressure is seen, the flushing
         will be shut down.
    10. Commence pressure testing.
    11. Apply a low pressure test to the test pump and pressure line against a closed valve.
    12. When applying the low pressure test do not increase the pressure above the test pressure and
       bleed down.
    13. The low pressure test will be to 200-300 psi and following stabilization a 5 minute straight line
      on the calibrated test chart must be achieved as per API RP53 although this may be extended to
       ensure a straight line is achieved.
    14. Following the low pressure test increase the pressure at a steady rate to apply the high pressure
         test.
    15. Do not apply a pressure greater than the required test pressure and  if the pressure drops
        increase it back up to the test pressure.
    16. Following stabilization a 10 minute straight line on the calibrated test chart must be achieved as
       per API RP53 although this may be extended to ensure a straight line is achieved.
    17. In the event of a leak always bleed the pressure off and get this confirmed by the test pump
        operator. Do not work on or attempt to tighten any fittings that are under pressure.
    18. Make hourly PA announcements during the pressure testing operations. This practice ensures
        that all personnel, not involved with the operation are informed of the ongoing hazard.
    19. At the conclusion of each test, bleed the pressure off, and confirm before lining up for the next
        test
    20. If any equipment is to be tested off the critical path, i.e. stabbing valves or Gray valves, this
       should be carried out using the Sub Sea test pump in an area away from rig floor if possible. This
       area should be clear of any other equipment.
    21. Testing will be carried out in the same manner as in the above procedure.

    1. When all pressure testing has been completed ensure all the lines are bled down to zero before

Ensure good communication between drill floor, pipe deck, crane operator, spider deck area
and pod reels (Radio’s).



The telescopic joint is normally stored on the Starboard Side aft board side of the pipe deck.



    1. Jump the ROV and check the TV monitor on the rig floor is receiving a good picture.
       Position the ROV and carry out a riser, BOP, slope indicator, and wellhead inspection.
       This inspection should be witnessed by the Company Representative.
    2.  Lift the telescopic joint directly from the support to the drill floor with the main block of
       the starboard crane. Install the catwalk air winch on the aft end and a drill floor air
      winch on the fwd end.
    3.  Ensure good communication between the crane operator and air winch drivers with the
      deck foreman in clear view.

vi) Kill Choke Line Pressure Tests:


    1. A permit to work must be in force prior to commencing all high pressure testing.
    2. The kill, choke and booster lines are normally tested every 3 joints. The first test must
      be done once the termination joint and the first riser joint are run.
    3.  Land the riser on the spider and remove the riser running tool (if required).
    4.   Fill up the lines with water until free of air.
    5.  Install the riser plug testing tool.
    6.  Connect the testing manifold to the testing unit which must have a pressure recorder
       suitable for high and low pressure tests.
    7.  Before connecting the testing manifold to the test plugs flush through the lines then
     make up the connections. Make up the coupling bolts (Cameron RF 7500lbs-ft).
    8.  Check the line up and pressure test the line up to testing manifold.
    9.  Pressure test the kill and choke lines to 300 psi for 5 min and 15000psi for 10 min. All
        tests performed against the kill & choke isolation valves.
    10. Bleed off the pressure and remove the riser test tool.

vii) Land the BOP:


    1.  Notify the moonpool and the ROV personnel before lowering the telescopic joint to land
       the inner barrel mandrel on the spider.
    2.  Back out the bolts and remove the riser handling tool.
    3.  Pick up the landing joint (slick joint) from the aft pipe deck and install it on the slip joint.
    4. Make up the coupling bolts (Cameron RF 7500 lbs-ft). Take a test lift and re-torque the
        bolts.
    5.  Pick up the riser string, open the riser spider and notify the moonpool and the ROV
       personnel before lowering the slip joint.
    6.  Lock the load ring onto the telescopic joint with the rotating dogs (6). Visually confirm
          all six dogs are locked. The SSE will witness this operation.
    7. Once the load ring is locked to slip joint, take weight of the load ring (10klb over pull)
      and remove the safety slings from the load ring. Unlock the load ring support dogs.

    9. The last clamps should be installed on the Podhose wire so that they will be just below

    10. Using the drill floor and cellar deck air winches lower the kill line, booster line and choke
        line goosenecks (in that order) from the forward port side of the drill floor and stab

    12. All riser tensioners APV’s must be pressured up well in advance (24 hrs minimum).
    13. Adjust the riser tensioner and CMC pressures as required for landing the BOP (75% on

    14. Spool  off  sufficient  Podhose from  the  reels  to  allow  for  landing and heave
       considerations.
    15. With the BOP 5-10 meters above well head depth move the rig to final position the BOP
      above the wellhead.
    16. Position the ROV to observe the wellhead and the guide posts. Confirm the wellhead
       connector is open, and the ring gasket is in place.
    17. Move the rig into position and land the BOP with 50klb down weight.
    18. Re-position the ROV to observe the wellhead connector position indicator. Lock the

    19. Perform a pick up test with50klb over-pull.
    20. Adjust the riser tensioners as required.(Normal case or Poor Cementing case?)
    21. Record the slope indicator readings with the ROV. Record the ship and ROV headings.
    22. Close the shear rams and pressure test the AX gasket to 500 psi.
    23. Set neutral weight at the telescopic joint lock dogs.
    24. Unlock the telescopic joint hydraulic latch and use the CMC to pull back the landing joint
      and stroke out the inner barrel. Land the crossover in the spider.
