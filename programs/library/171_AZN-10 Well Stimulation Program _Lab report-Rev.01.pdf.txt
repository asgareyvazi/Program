1.  Executive Summary

The main objective of this plan is to stimulate (wash method) the producing interval zone in well AZN-10
vertically completed into Fahliyan Formation with reservoir top at 3987.8mMD. Finally, the well’ potential
and reservoir characteristics are to be determined via well testing analysis.

Staged-Acidizing is adopted to be run from the bottom of perforation (BOP ≈ 4,087 mMD) to the top of
perforation (TOP ≈ 4,027 mMD). Drag CT from toe to the heel while pumping 143bbl of 15% HCl acid with
suitable additives to spot the toe (48.00 gal/ft. as acid index). This is followed by increasing pump rate as
much as possible during operation with pump pressure limited (MAWHP) advised by the Client at wellsite;
then, flow back and POOH CT to surface.

Eventually, samples of reservoir fluid are collected from appropriate wellbore depth where the flowing
pressure is above the bubble point and asphaltene onset pressures. The samples should represent the system
in the reservoir under its initial conditions in order to determine its type, volumetric and phase behaviour and
its composition.  It  is noteworthy mentioning that PVT analysis results are needed for geological and
reservoir engineering evaluation and forecasting, as well as for laboratory studies concerning asphaltene
deposition characteristics and enhanced oil recovery (EOR). Accordingly, the main objectives of this
operation can be defined as follows:

   –  To get 5 litters of qualified sample;

   –  Get downhole pressure and temperature data at sampling depth while taking sample;

   –  Do some basic analysis about the sample, including opening pressure and bubble point.

1.3 Well Status / History

    1. AZNS-10 is a vertical well located near Pad#50 - completed as a cased hole with 7” Liner from
      3,527mMD down to 4,922mMD across the 8 ½” hole section. The well is perforated (from 4027 to 4087
    mMD) in Fahliyan sublayers (Fah-1a, Fah-1b, and Fah-1c) and completed as Fahliyan vertical oil
       producer well.
    2. A 4 ½ * 3 ½ completion string was run in the hole down to the depth of 3665.302mMD. A 7 * 3 ½”
      permanent PKR w/upper seal-bore of 4” was set at 3646.82mMD with minimum tubing restriction of
       2.635”.

3.  Fundamental Geology Characteristic

The Fahliyan formation is the deepest reservoir among the target reservoirs. The reservoir is shallow
marine carbonates and produces light oil, Unlike the North Azadegan, Fahliyan reservoir is important oil
bearing layer in Azadegan. The structural configuration of the Fahliyan is generally similar to the other
overlying Cretaceous reservoirs.

                                   Table 5: Mineralogy Presents in Fahliyan Formation

          Zone Name/Interval MD (TVDSS)                  Calcite%     Dolomite%   Clay%

           Fahliyan Vertical Well/ 4027-4087                     90            8            2.0

Based on the core study in AZNS for early wells, Fahliyan layers are characterized as follow:

Fah-1: It is characterized by deposition of gray packstone/wackestone, medium-low energy depositional

environment. The rock is linen, tight, oil free. The visible dissolution pore are not contained, developed

horizontal fractures & open fractures contain oil can be observed in local cores.

Fah-2: The component of core is mainly packstone & wackestone which represent medium-low energy

depositional environment. The cores are wrapped with light crude oil, they are homogeneous & have

good oil shows & are with visible dissolution pores.

Fah-3: The component of core is mainly blocky packstone & wackestone which represent medium-low

energy depositional environment. The oil-bearing cores show dark color & make a sharp contrast with

the tight surrounding gray rock which showed heterogeneity, the component of cores is tight & oil

saturation is low.

Fahliyan reservoir distribution showed that compared Fah-1, Fah-2 dos not very much in general & has

better reservoir properties than Fah-1. Besides, Fah-3 has the highest mean PHI & K in Fahliyan

formation. Although, this is contrary to the table-2.

6.3  Strategy of Acidizing

Following measures can be adopted in order to enhance the success rate, efficiency of acidizing &

achieve a good effect on increasing productivity based on elementary acidizing theory & test:

    a)  Considering the temperature of the formation to be treated.

    b)  Select appropriate acid additives, to avoid corrosion, sludge & any problem that damage pay

       zone.

    c) Enhance the injecting pressure & flow rate as high as possible to form the “wormhole” as much

       as possible & deepen its depth.

    d) Design effective pre-flush & Overflush treatment for reservoir conditioning & break any formed

        gel.

6.4 Stimulation Main Challenges

Given the high temperature (>250 oF), reaction rate will be definitely reduced during conventional

treatment. Therefore, a de-efficiency factor (0.8) will be considered for overall stimulation efficiency.

6.5 Stimulation Software (Input/Output)

Primary wash-acid model with shut-in method was generated based on the compatibility test results.

Clearly, this model can be tuned through Pressure Matching by using field-data.

6.7  Stimulation Fluids & Stages Introduction

In aim to achieve the best stimulation results following Stimulation fluids are proposed.

Preflush:  Preflush acts as a spacer between reservoir fluid & main fluid, guarantees the water wet

condition of reservoir rock, eliminates the risk of acid/oil sludge formation & facilitates post acid flow

back process.

Main Acid 15% HCl: HCl is the most suitable treatment fluid for stimulation of carbonate (including

dolomite) reservoirs which can dissolve both rock matrix & damage. The dissolution process leads to

clean up of hydrocarbon flowing path.

Over-Flush: Overflush solution contains mutual solvent acting as a multifunctional non-ionic agent

which is soluble in oil, acid & fresh water, which improves the clean-up of spent acid following

stimulation treatment.

7.6 Contingencies

Fire on board:

In case of any kind of uncontrolled fire on board, the stimulation will be stopped, using the following

procedure:

    Close the Wellhead master valve.

    Proceed to extinguish fire with Fire truck

Deterioration of weather condition:

Due to deteriorating weather conditions or failure in the location, the stimulation operations must be

stopped.
In serious emergency situations:

    Stop any Pumping or in Injection.

    Close the master valve of the wellhead.

In other situations:

    Stop pumping the treatment.

    Flush the lines with water.

    Close the master valve of the wellhead.

    Release the pumping coflex hose.

Acid leak at surface while pumping:

    Stop pumping

    Close remote control master valve

    Bleed off acid overboard through emergency bleed off line

   Wash the acid on Location or CTU Surface

    Repair leak
    Pressure test line 15 minutes – 6500psi

   Open slowly remote control master valve.

   Resume treatment.

7.7 Well Flow-Back General Guidelines

The maximum initial choke size should not exceed 1/6th the inside diameter of the surface manifold or

the tubing in the well.

Examples:
     3 1/2 tubing - 1/2 inch or number 32 choke
     2 7/8 tubing - 13/32 inch or number 26 choke
     2 3/8 tubing - 5/16 inch or number 20 choke

During flow back as surface pressure decreases, larger chokes may be used to maintain flow. The
manifold downstream of the choke should be as straight as possible to minimize erosion. Always open
the upstream valve first, then the downstream valve.

Always close the downstream valve first, then the upstream valve. Lo-Torc & other stop valves are not to
be used as chokes. Always assume the presence of combustible hydrocarbons.

  1.  On completion of the acid job, open well for flow back to choke manifold (Choke must be closed

        at this stage for initial reading).

  2.    Prior to opening well ensure downstream lines are open to flare boom

  3.    Initial Opening – Open to flare gradually commencing on a 16/64 adjustable choke & increasing

      choke as required. Continue flowing to burner boom until clean.
     Ensure downstream pressure does not exceed pipe specifications (1,000psi as guideline)
     Note: Record all parameters every 5 minutes during the cleanup & then every 15 minutes when

       the well is stable on all choke settings.

Criteria for stable flow:
     Well head temp is changing at less than 2 deg per hour
     Well head pressure is changing less than 5psi per hour
    BS&W at choke is less than 1%

  4.   Record BS&W every 30 minutes at choke manifold, check for H2S, CO2, pH & Chlorides. (If

       required take & document fluid samples)

  5.   Once the clean-up has been established close the well at the tree & bleed down lines

7.8 CT Acidizing Procedure

    1. RIH CT to the maximum depth as per above procedure.

    2.  Confirm the volume of stimulation fluids with Client representative to be mixed based on the

       access interval in the perforations.

    3.  Stop operations, & have a safety meeting prior to pumping:
     Determine wind direction & nominate a safe assembly point in the event of an emergency
     Discuss chemical & flammable hazards & state the location of the fire extinguishers, first aid kits,
      eyewash stations, & other safety equipment.
     Ensure that MSDS of all chemicals are available onsite.
     Clearly identify maximum pressures for both coiled cubing & injection pressures. CT pump
       pressure should not exceed 6,500 psi as per the line test
     Ensure water hose is accessible in the event of an acid spill.

    4.  Once CT reach to the maximum depth.

    5.  Start mixing the volume of stimulation fluids as per the attached recipe.

     Add Anti-Sludge Agent to the mixing solution & mix thoroughly
     Add Non-Emulsifier Agent to the mixing solution & mix thoroughly
     Add Iron Control Agent to fresh water & mix the solution thoroughly
     Add Corrosion Inhibitor to the solution while continue mixing
     Add H2S Scavenger Agent to the mixing solution & mix thoroughly
      Finally, add raw HCl to the solution & continue mixing for 15 minutes

    6.  Reset the volume counter & start pumping proposed acid treatment as per “Pumping Schedule”

        section.

    7.  Ensure good communication is kept between the pump operator & coiled tubing supervisor &

        that regular check-ins are made regarding pump rate, total volume pumped etc. to ensure program

         is being followed as per design.

    8. When the CT is full of stimulation fluid & preflush is at the nozzle, close well return (CT

       annulus), monitor & record the pumping rate & pressure.

    9.  Start pumping proposed acid treatment as per “Pumping Schedule” section (Pump Rate, Volume

     Pumped vs. POOH schedule).

                                      (High Temperature Test)

Material and Laboratory Equipment

    1.  Laboratory scale with 0.0001 gr precision.

    2.  Standard corrosion coupons (N80)

    3.  Graduated cylinder with a minimum volume of 250 ml.

    4.  Nylon string for hanging the coupon.

    5.  High Temperature – High Pressure Corrosion Tester Apparatus.

    6.  Hydrochloric acid (supplied by E.C.C.S), Acetone (Merck Millipore), Isopropyl Alcohol 99.7% (Sigma-

         Aldrich), Commercial soap.

    7.  Mixing water provided by O.E.O.C Company for HCl dilution.

Test Method

    A.  Corrosion Test

         1.  200 ml’s of HCl 20% containing required amounts of additives were prepared (As listed in tables) in

          such a way that the total volume of acid and additives is equal to 200 ml.

         2.  The corrosion coupon (N-80) was suspended inside the prepared solution while being hanged exactly

              in the middle of the container using a nylon string. A timer was started as soon as the coupon was

           completely submerged inside the acidic solution.

         3.  Next, the corrosion cell was transferred into the HTHP Corrosion Tester Apparatus immediately. The

           device was sealed, and pressure (1000 psi incrementally) was applied on the sample using nitrogen

           gas. The temperature was increased to 250 oF (121 oC).

         4. When the temperature reached 121 oC the second timer as started which was set as starting point of

            the test. From this point, the temperature was kept at 121 oC and the pressure at 1000 psi for 360

           minutes (6 hours).

         5.  After 360 minutes, the temperature was decreased gradually (without changing the pressure) until a

           temperature of less than 70 oC was reached. Then, pressure was decreased slowly until reaching

           ambient pressure.

         6.  The corrosion cell was removed from the HTHP apparatus, the corrosion coupon was taken out of the

            acid solution, washed with soap-water solution and isopropyl alcohol, and finally the coupon was

            rinsed with acetone.

         7.  The coupon was weighted, and its corrosion rate was calculated using below formula:

Test Method

    A. A solution of HCl 2% containing 500 ppm’s of Fe3+ was prepared by dissolving FeCl3.6H2O in diluted HCl

       were recorded.

   added to the HCl 2%.

    Note: Acid solutions containing ferric ions are yellow to orange colored depending on their ferric ion content.

     All solutions are expected to become colorless after adding proper dosages of iron reducing agents to the

    solution. The solution must remain colorless for the duration of six hours.

    F. A pH electrode was placed inside the solution to record the pH.

   G. Sodium hydroxide (2N) was added until the pH of 4 was reached. The beaker was incubated at 93 oC for

         six hours and results were reported.
