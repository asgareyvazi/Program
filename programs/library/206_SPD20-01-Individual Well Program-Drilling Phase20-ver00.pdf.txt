4.  Health, Safety, Environment and Quality (HSE & Q)

    4.1  HSE

The South Pars Phases 20/21 goals are:

        Zero harmful spill to sea
        Zero red incidents
        Zero dropped objects
        Zero serious well control situations
        Rig prepared for H2S
        High standard on housekeeping
       Hands on Safety focus

For this well, the special HSE focus points will be:

       Crane lift and falling objects
        Safe Job Analysis (SJA) shall be used for planning of all non-standard or rare operations
       Good communication is the key to good teamwork. Radio communication should be used between

       crewmembers to improve communication. Use headsets in noisy areas
        Pre-job meetings shall be held prior to drilling out shoe, running casing and cementing operations
        Rig and crew will be prepared for H2S. Regular drills will be held
        Derrick will be inspected regularly for loose objects or potential falling objects. In addition inspections

        should be performed after severe vibrations/jarring operations
         All incidents and non-conformances shall be reported in OIEC system by the drilling supervisor

       4.1.1   Risk assessment

During the detailed planning of the first well, the different groups involved have prepared risk logs for each hole

section. The risks are classified using a South Pars risk matrix.

The risk logs  will be checked before the start of each section, and continuously updated  if new risks are

identified. The risk logs shall be used offshore on a daily basis during the operations.

    4.2   Quality

As a part of the Quality Control of the first drilling programme, a Drill Well on Paper exercise will be performed.

For this well, special quality focus points will be:

         Ensure that relevant experiences from the appraisal and production wells on the South Pars Field is

          captured and included in the operation programmes and procedures
         Checklists prepared for all equipment, available for OIEC prior to start of operations
         Procedures planned for all operations, available for OIEC prior to start of operations
         High focus and follow up of KPIs (Key Performance Indicators).
          Drilling Risk Assessment

  4.2.1   Stuck pipe

Stuck pipe prevention shall be a focus point in all down hole operations.  All key personnel both onshore and

offshore shall have attended a stuck pipe course.

The following operations require special attention on stuck pipe:
            Drilling of shoe track and rathole
           26” section

           Poor hole cleaning (due to losses)

            Big formation pieces jamming the BHA
          16 ” section

            Tight hole due to reactive and swelling shale in Kazhdumi and Fahliyan Fm.
          12 ¼” section

             Differential sticking in Upper Surmeh loss zone

            Chert in Surmeh (PDC bit prone to demolish)

           Poor hole cleaning

            Formation pieces jamming the BHA in the Aghar and Sudair shale (specially in long wells)
          8 ½” section

             Differential sticking in the depleted reservoir
          Back reaming
           Running casing

These operations will be discussed under the different hole section chapters.

5.  Geology and Formation Evaluation

    5.1   Well objectives

Well SPD20-01 is one of a total number of 11 planned production wells (including appraisal well) that will be

drilled in Phase 20 on South Pars. Also this well is one of the eight wells with short displacement that will be

drilled as second batch drilling (five short wells and three long wells).

This is according to the main drainage strategy for phases 20/21 (ref. SP20/21 Reservoir Development Plan).

The primary objective of the gas/condensate producer SPD20-01 is to ensure sufficient production from the K1,

K2, K3 and K4 Formation.

The location of well SPD20-01 is based on the total drainage strategy in phases 20/21. The followings criteria

have been used in addition to the drainage strategy to locate the first well:

        Located in an area with seismic control
       No faults has been interpreted in this area

    5.2   Geological and geophysical evaluation
       5.2.1   Seismic data

Seismic interpretation, structural mapping and depth conversion of the South Pars area have so far been limited.

The 2D seismic from 1988 was first made available to Statoil late August 2003. This seismic was the basis for

enterprise positioning of well SP9, SP10 and SP11. The 2D seismic is also the basis for the current structural

depth maps.

The first 3D seismic survey was performed by DELFT Geophysical for National Iranian Oil Company in 1994.

This data acquisition covered an area of 85 square kilometers including the area between wells SP-01 and SP-

01. This survey was carried-out to improve the structural definition of the field structure for Phase 1 development

area. The second 3D seismic survey carried out by Global Geo Services ASA Company (GGS) for AGIP over an

area of 1,015 square kilometers, covered Phases 4, 5, 6, 7 and 8 development areas. The third and the most

recent 3D seismic survey was carried-out by GGS for POGC over an area of 2,665.9 square kilometers

processed by SPECTRUM.

The main structural results found from seismic interpretation are:

This field is part of a huge shallow elongated northeast-southwest dome which has a more gentle dip on the

southeast flank (0.5°) as compared with the northwest flank (1°). The thickness of the Kangan/U.Dalan reservoir

was found to be almost constant. The K1 top surface varies from a minimum depth of 2,580m (MSL) in the

southwest part of Phases 2 and 3 areas, to a maximum depth of 2,780m (MSL) in the northeast of these blocks.

    5.7   Pressure Prognosis
       5.7.1   Pore Gradient

The pore gradient profile was inferred from mud data except in the reservoir section which was determined from

RFT/MDT recordings.

Surface formations down to the Jahrum are hydrostatically pressurized  (i.e. the formation gradient is 1.03

atm/10m or 0.46 psi/ft). The Jahrum formation is dolomitic limestone with large fractures from near vertical

limestone slabs causing total losses even when sea water is used as drilling fluid. At times, shows of sulphurous

water reach the surface thus, accounting for this fact,  it is believed that the formation gradient may be slightly

higher than hydrostatic, probably in the range of 1.05-1.06 atm/10m (0.470 to 0.475 psi/ft). Circulation losses

alternated to sulphurous water shows are common throughout the Cretaceous formation down to the Hith

anhydrite. Approaching the bottom of the Fahliyan formation, the pore gradient is ought to slightly and constantly

increase throughout the Jurassic and Triassic formations, reaching an estimated pore gradient of 1.36-1.38

atm/10m (0.61 to 0.62 psi/ft) in the Aghar shale cap rock.

During Drilling the well SP-7 through the Upper Sudair formation (top at 2,385m), high-pressure water kick was

experienced (first flow detection was at 2,428m with the possibility of the actual flowing top-zone being slightly

higher, at between 2,412m to 2,415m, as inferred from the ROP). This event was specific for this well as there

       5.7.2   Fracture Gradient

The Fracture Gradient was calculated as a function of the estimated pore and overburden gradients (a concise

illustration of the applied method is given hereinafter). Based on the actual experience, different Poisson's ratios

have been applied depending on the nature of the formation, resulting in a differentiated fracture gradient profile.

Regardless the formation, the fracture gradient estimated herein refers to the following criteria:

"Should the liquid phase of the drilling mud deeply invade the formation" (which is the case in permeable

formations at shallow depth, coarse sands, fractured formations, etc.) the fracture gradient is better expressed

by the following equation:

          Pfr = Pp + 2v * (Pov -Pp)

        Where:

          Pfr = fracture pressure in atm

       Pp = pore pressure in atm

       Pov = overburden pressure in atm

         v = Poisson modulus (ratio) for a given formation.

In this case different values, ranging from 0.1 to 0.28 were assumed depending on the nature of the formations

7.7.3   Stuck pipe strategy
      Jarhum and Sachun: Stuck pipe  often occurs due  to  total  losses, pack  off, poor hole

       cleaning and tight hole
         It is important to keep a high flowrate and pump hi-vis pills frequently
      Use of roller reamer and slick assembly will help to avoid stuck pipe
      Stuck pipe situations occurs due to poor hole cleaning (due to losses) and larger pieces of

       formation jamming the BHA  Mechanical sticking
       In Jahrum: Set back one single  prior to connection to enable  jarring down  if stuck  after

       connection.
         If over pull/ pack-off occur when reaming upwards, move pipe down and reduce pump rate

       immediately to avoid fracturing formation
         If mechanical stuck be patient, first try to use rotation of DP to crush the formation pieces, start

         jarring at a low force to prevent getting jammed up. Do not start jarring with max force
      Never apply full overpull before allowing the jar to hit
      Never jar up with torque in the string.
      Always hit jar in opposite direction of getting stuck (most likely the jar should be hit downwards)
       Driller shall be aware of max pull on jar and drill string
      Use jar in combination with accelerator in all BHAs.

       9.7.5   Hole stability

Borehole collapse curves have been calculated for inclinations between 0-60 degrees. In the carbonates in and

above the reservoir the mud density should be designed only for well control and lost circulation purposes.

Boreholes are predicted to remain stable in the carbonates (without caving) at all mud densities between the

pore pressure and the fracturing pressure.

Borehole collapse  will most likely occur in the Sudair and Aghar Shales.  It is not possible to stabilize the

formation with mud weight in high deviated wells. The collapse will occur on each side on the wellbore and not

from the top and bottom. The mechanism may not be reactive shale. There are reports suggesting either a geo-

pressured shale or unstable fractured shale since both these shales are of Trias age.

The following comments and recommendations are provided based on the wellbore stability sudy carried-out by

Eni for Phases 4 and 5.

          The wells drilled along the NW/SE direction can be stable for any angle, but for hole

                     inclination higher than 50º high mud density values are required to keep the hole stable with

               a potential risk of seepage/losses increase.

              The K4 reservoir could be depleted with approximately 500psi, resulting in 1000-1450 psi

               overbalance. Also, K4 has the highest permeability.
                 It  is always recommended to control mud weight to avoid the increasing of over balance

               pressure thru reservoir sections and to keep the fluid losses in range (<2.5ml/30 min) which

               helps to minimize the risk of differential sticking mishaps.
                 If differential sticking occurs,  it will be considered to pull out with the MWD/LWD BHA and drill

               the remaining with a rotary BHA (without MWD/ LWD)
              Minimize survey in K4(Take one survey in top K4 and one in bottom K4)
              Over pull data sheet for every BHA shall be updated and kept in driller's room
               Fire the jar first upward then apply maximum over pull. Never hit Jar upward when torque is

               trapped in string.
              Enough amounts of stuck pipe spotting agents and 28% HCL acid shall be kept on board at all

                time.

      To prevent bad hole cleaning and high ECD, the following parameters should be focused:

      Only seepage losses have been reported for the SPD1 and SPD2 wells, except for SPD2-05

      where significant losses were observed in the K1.
      When breaking circulation, increase pump strokes in steps and move pipe slowly up to prevent

       breaking down the formation.
         If over pull/ pack-off occur when reaming upwards, move pipe down and reduce pump rate

       immediately to avoid fracturing formation

10.8.3  Stuck pipe strategy
       Differential sticking

     The K4 reservoir could be depleted with approximately 600 psi due to production on other

         phases and the North Field (Qatar).

     Keep the fluid loss below 2 ml to avoid sticking filter cake

      Minimize connection time

      Always rotate/reciprocate/pump if stop in operationsBe aware of ledging. DD to be on rig floor
         If differential sticking occurs,  it will be considered to pull out with the MWD/LWD BHA and drill

       the remaining with a rotary BHA.
      Keep acid (HCl) on the rig in case a differential sticking situation occurs.
         If differential stuck, start jarring at max hit on the jar to prevent worsening the situation.
      Never apply full over pull before allowing the jar to hit.
       After the jar has hit, max DP over pull to be applied.
      Never jar up with torque in string.
      Always hit jar in opposite direction getting stuck (most likely the jar should be hit downwards)
       Driller shall be aware of max pull on jar and drill string
      Use jar in combination with accelerator in all BHAs.
