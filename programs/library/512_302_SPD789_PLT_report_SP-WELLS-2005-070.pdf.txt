1  Introduction and Background

      Upon request from POGC and in agreement with Statoil/Petropars, two PLT
        survey were run on SouthPars phases 6,7 & 8. One in well SPD7-09 and one
         in well SPD9-15. They were carried out immediately after completion and
        clean-up in October (SPD7-09 on Sagadril-2) and November (SPD9-15 on
       RaniWoro.

       Both wells were logged with Sclumberger MPSP (Memory Production
        Service Platform). Log interpretations and analysis was done using the
        Emeraude-software by Statoil in Norway.

2 Summary

       Rig operations and data acquisition were carried out successfully on slick-
         line for both PLT-logged wells. To minimize risk of surface wash-out,
      maximum flow rates were limited to 85 MM Scf/d.

         Productivity in PLT-logged wells are excellent – same as all other open hole
        completion in phase 6,7 & 8. Gas rates of approximately 85 MMScf/d
        produced with 11-14 bar (180 psi) drawdown.

        For both wells logged productivity is surprisingly high in upper part of the
       pay zone (K1 and K2). In well (SPD7-09) flow contribution from K1 is
        approximately 80 % out total flow. In well SPD9-15 K2 has around 80 %
        flow contribution.

          Initial PLT’s give no or marginal flow contribution from lower levels of
        wellpath (K3 and K4). This is caused by liquid-overbalance against lower
         levels during logging with relative moderate production rates (85/86 MM
         Scf/d).

        After some weeks/months of high and stable production, wellbore and
         reservoir contribution will go into equilibrium. I.e. according to the KH-
         relationship, flow history and boundaries in the different layers of the
         reservoir.

     A four hours build-up was carried out right after flowing for both wells.
        Information from the buildup tests are discussed in the report. They both
        prove high KH-relationship.

3 PLT in SPD7-09

3.1 Summary and Conclusions

Well SPD 7-09 was logged with a production logging tool string from Schlumberger
(PSP), with memory on slikcline. The operation was performed after initial clean up
and consisted of logging at 3 different rates and at shut in conditions. Logging data
were acquired during 3 up and 3 down passes at each rate and shut in. Stationary data
were recorded for transient buildup test analysis at shut in conditions.

Logging in flowing well gave the following principal flow contributions:

       - More than 80% from upper K1 formation
       - Approx 20% from K2 and upper K3 formation

The flow profiles are shown in figures 4.1 and 4.2. Tabulated profiles at each rate is
shown in Chapter 4.2 ‘Interpretation’.

Maximum production depth during production logging was approximately 3635
mMD RT, determined from temperature data. The reason why lower parts of the well
are not contributing to flow is because of overbalance against formation at logged
rates. This is due to 1) drilling mud that has not yet been lifted out of the well and/or
2) because rate (and drawdown) is relatively low.

At maximum drawdown/rate there is overbalance almost up to 3670 mMD RT in the
K3 formation. Pressure versus vertical and measured depth is shown in figures 4.3 and
4.4 respectively.

Logging at shut in conditions indicated no cross-flow activity in the well.

3.2 Data quality

The production logging data acquired have overall good quality and can be used for
conclusive analysis. Raw data is shown in figures 4.9 through 4.16.

Spinner data
Quality of the fullbore spinner data is good. However, the fluid and flowing
conditions in the well complicate the interpretation in parts of the interval. The
spinner response and characteristics (slopes and thresholds) vary with depth,
depending on the fluid phases present. Also, recirculation effects are evident from
comparison of spinner data with other data in the transition zone of gas and
mud/condensate at different rates.

Quality of the inline spinner data is bad. Spinner is completely or partly blocked in
most of the logging passes. Inline spinner was completely blocked in shut in well.
This does not compromise the interpretation, since fullbore spinner data are used for
analysis.

Caliper data
Quality of data is satisfactory. Measured data are reasonably consistent with
completion dimensions.

Holdup data
Quality of data is reasonably good. Down passes are best. The data indicate the levels
of different fluid phases in the well; gas, transition zone and mud zone in the well.
The probes will be missing the hydrocarbon (gas) phase where gas is flowing on the
high side of the well, in the annulus.

Pressure data
Quality of the data is good, and they repeat well. Data have been used to derive fluid
density. Derived gas density is in accordance with expected downhole density of gas.

Temperature
Quality of the temperature data is good in gas phase and repeat well. Data are
reasonably good in the transition zone of gas and mud/condensate. Where mud is
dominating the wellbore in the lower part of the well, there is a large discrepancy
between the passes, depending on logging direction and logging speed. This is
probably caused by viscous and high density mud being too viscous to keep the
temperature sensor clean and exposed to ‘fresh’ fluid while logging. This is seen
when comparing temperature data from different log passes, up/down and at different
logging speeds. Also, segregated flow effects and recirculation effects may together
with varying temperature sensor positioning have an effect on the temperature
measurements. Temperature from fast down passes seem to be the most representative
of fluid temperatures at allocated depths. See fig 4.18 and 4.19 for temperature plots.

Auxiliary data
Gamma ray data show good quality and all the PL data are easily correlated on depth.
CCL (casing collar locator) also show good data quality and indicate the positions of
the liner joints.

3.3 Interpretation


3.3.1  General

The well is completed with a predrilled liner in open hole. In addition to tubular flow,
flow occur in the annulus on the outside of the predrilled liner. This is seen from
spinner raw data and from interpreted velocity. Flow merges into the liner from the
top section of predrilled liner, above top reservoir. The schematic rate data and

There is a transition zone in flowing well, with a mix of gas and mud. Spinner data in
the transition zone are not reliable for quantitative interpretation due to segregation of
fluid phases in the wellbore and recirculation heavy phase, causing complex spinner
responses.

Top of the transition zone at end of logging passes at each rate:
(max depth of pure gas, detected from pressure and pressure derived density)

Max rate: 3380 mMD RT (moving slightly up during logging period)
Med rate: 3380 mMD RT (constant)
Min rate: 3345 mMD RT (constant)
Shut in:  3500 mMD RT (constant)

In the mud column below the transition phase, the spinner response characteristics are
varying with the properties of the mud, with decreasing slope and increasing threshold
with mud density. Mud density, and probably viscosity, is increasing with depth.
Fullbore spinner data from lower part of the well are shown in fig 4.17.

For rate interpretation, single phase gas is assumed in flowing well. Both gas and
liquid (condensate/mud) are present in the well, but the flowing phase above top of
the transition zone is pure gas. Gas holdup is close to 100% where spinner data have
been used for rate interpretation. Gas production below top of the transition zone is
taken as gas rate estimated immediately above top of transition zone.

Spinner and temperature data indicate production down to at least 3635 mMD RT at
maximum and medium production rate.

Plot of pressure versus vertical and measured depth respectively is shown for each
flow rate and at shut in conditions in fig 4.3 and fig 4.4. Extrapolation of the shut in
pressure curve in gas phase indicate the effective drawdown (or overbalance) at each
rate versus depth, assuming uniform pressures. At the intersection, there is pressure
balance between formation and well and hence no drawdown. Below this point, there
is overbalance and no possibility of flow (except segregation and phase change out).
Above the intersection there is drawdown and possibility of flow.

Depths of the intersection, i.e. the balance point of zero drawdown is approximately
the following at each rate:

Max rate:    3670 mMD RT (K3 formation)
Med rate:    3655 mMD RT (K3 formation)
Min rate:     3605 mMD RT (K3 formation)

3.3.2  Flowing


High rate
The pressure plot indicate drawdown down to approximately 3670 mMD RT. Logged
data indicate inflow from formation down to at least 3635 mMD RT.

Spinner data in transition zone approx. 3385-3550 mMD RT, are not reliable for fluid
velocity interpretation due to segregated flow and recirculation effects. Data below
3550 mMD RT are interpretable with respect to velocity, but must be interpreted with
variable spinner slopes and threshold. Also, these data do not reflect average fluid
velocity. Interpreted velocity is partly negative, which indicate ‘apparent downflow’;
i.e. recirculation effects due to gas flowing on the high side of the wellbore.

From 3550 to bottom logging interval, velocity is calculated from all passes, with a
variable spinner calibration but fixed ratio of positive slope versus negative slope.
Velocity data have been interpolated through the transition zone.

Main flow entry is in formation K1 down to approx 3380 mMD RT. Inflow below this
point is probably limited to immediately beneath and from the interval approximately
3625-3635 mMD RT, based on temperature profile.

3.3.3  Shut in

Raw data and velocity calculated from fullbore spinner do not indicate any flow
activity in shut in well.

Due to varying fluid properties, spinner characteristics and quality of data in the liquid
zone below 3520 mMD RT, velocity has been calculated schematically within the
liquid zone in intervals with good spinner data, from two fastest up and down passes
with variable spinner calibration and fixed ratio of positive/negative slope. In the gas
phase, conventional spinner analysis with fixed slopes and thresholds were used.
Maximum calculated velocity is less than 6 m/min and is within the uncertainty of the
calculations.

Based on pressure and pressure derived density, the well has segregated phases of
fluids, with gas (density 0,25 g/cc) down to 3498 mMD RT, condensate (0,67 g/cc)
down to 3515 mMD RT and mud below this depth. Density of the mud increases
successively with depth to a maximum of 2,3 g/cc at 3830 mMD RT (maximum
logging depth).

5 PLT in SPD9-15

5.1 Summary and conclusions


Well SPD 9-15 was logged with a production logging tool string from Schlumberger
(PSP), with memory on slikcline. The operation was performed after initial cleanup
and consisted of logging at 3 different rates and at shut in conditions. Logging data
were acquired during 3 up and 3 down passes at each rate and shut in. Stationary data
were recorded for transient buildup test analysis at shut in conditions.

Logging in flowing well gave the following principal flow contributions:

- 20-25% from K1 formation
- 60-70% from upper half of K2 formations
- 10-11% from lower K2 and possibly top of K3
- Insignificant production from lower K3 and K4 formation.

The flow profiles are shown in Figures 6.1 and 6.2.

The reason why lower parts of the well is hardly contributing to flow is that
drawdown on the deep formations is only marginal during cleanup and test. This is
again due to high density drilling mud that has not yet been lifted out of the well. At
maximum drawdown/rate there is overbalance almost up to top of K4 formation.

Density and temperature data indicate that there is flow contribution from lower K3
and top of K4 at highest logged rate, but this is not quantitatively interpreted. Pressure
versus vertical and measured depth is shown in Figures 6.3 and 6.4 respectively.

Logging at shut in conditions indicated no cross-flow activity in the well.

5.3 Interpretation


5.3.1  General

The well is completed with a predrilled liner in open hole. In addition to tubular flow,
flow occur in the annulus on the outside of the predrilled liner. This is seen from
spinner raw data and from interpreted velocity. Flow merges into the liner from the
top section of predrilled liner, above top reservoir. The schematic rate data and
tabulated results have been corrected for this by multiplying with a factor equal to the
ratio of velocity above top section of predrilled liner divided by the velocity below.
Match factors were found to be 1.15, 1.13 and 1.17 for maximum, medium and
minimum logging rates respectively. The average factor of 1.15 was used for each
logging rate.

For rate interpretation, single phase gas has been assumed in flowing well. Both gas
and liquid (mud/condensate) are present in the well, but for interpretation purposes
liquid phase can be neglected. Gas holdup is close to 100% where spinner data have
been used for rate interpretation.

At medium and minimum flow rate, depth of the transition zone from gas to mud was
stable. At maximum rate, however, the max depth of pure gas was moving slightly
upwards during logging (8 meters during the logging passes). This was caused by the
decreasing well pressure and thereby increasing drawdown on the lower zones, such
that these zones were activated.

Comparison of density and temperature from the different passes indicate that there
was flow activity down to formation K4 at maximum logging rate. There was a
density and a temperature drop, down to approx. 3773 mMD RT. This could be
caused by inflow of gas, thus reducing the average fluid density and cooling down the
formation/fluid.

  6   Pressure Buildup in SPD9-15

       More than four hours of pressure buildup data were collected after the low-rate logging run, with the
         tool stationed at 3577 m MD RT, between K2 and K3. This data set has been used to determine flow
         properties and the skin effect.

       With mud in the lower part of the well, the thickness contributing to flow is uncertain, especially in
        view of the production files shown in Chapter 6. The challenge is that only K1 and K2 appeared to
         contribute to the flow, with most the inflow coming from K2. It is therefore likely that the flow
         capacity (kh product) obtained from the analysis only represents K1 and K2.

         Fig. 7.1 shows an overview of the downhole pressure and temperature data collected from the PLT
         shut-in period, with about two minutes of data from the preceding low-rate flow period also
         included.





         Fig. 7.1 – Downhole pressure and temperature data from the PLT shut-in period.


         Fig. 7.2 shows the downhole pressures from the previous figure together with wellhead pressures.
        For this test the quality of the wellhead data is of better quality, mainly because formation properties
         for this well is lower compared to SPD7-09. Even so, it is still difficult to obtain a good estimate of
        flow properties from wellhead data, but an error less than 50% might be obtained if both reduction
         periods and the shut-in period are used.
